## 2. What the source says — exact transcriptions

**p. 117 (abstract, closing sentences):** "A conjecture is made about the behavior of the matrix product on the line which has consequences for the behavior of the scalar product on the line. […] The conjecture implies the Riemann hypothesis for all Dirichlet zeta functions."

**p. 118 (bottom):** "A formulation of the Riemann hypothesis can be made in the theory of such Hilbert spaces of entire functions [4]. Assume that the defining function E(z) of a space ℋ(E) satisfies a functional identity, stating that E(z − i) and E*(z) are linearly dependent. Then the zeros of E(z) are symmetric about the line iz̄ − iz = −1. The Riemann hypothesis for Hilbert spaces of entire functions then concludes that the zeros of E(z) lie on the line iz̄ − iz = −1. An equivalent assertion is that E(z) has no zeros in the half-plane iz̄ − iz > −1. Some hypothesis is needed for the desired conclusion."

**p. 119 (top — THE positivity condition, ℋ(E) form):** "A sufficient condition is that every element of the space ℋ(E) be of the form F(z) + F(z + i) for an element F(z) of the space such that F(z + i) belongs to the space and that the real part of ⟨F(t + i), F(t)⟩_ℋ(E) is nonnegative for every such element F(z)."

**p. 119 (second paragraph — de Branges' own doubt, in 1994):** "The positivity condition so formulated applies to a large class of functions, some of which resemble zeta functions, for which the analogue of the Riemann hypothesis is true. **But the positivity condition is in doubt in the context of Dirichlet zeta functions.** Additional constructions from factorization theory are introduced to obtain the desired conclusion in that case. The positivity condition remains a link in the logical chain which is desired to conclude in a proof of the Riemann hypothesis for Dirichlet zeta functions. The condition applies to intermediate Hilbert spaces of the entire function, associated with the gamma function, which are used to construct the spaces associated with zeta functions." (Bolding added.)

**p. 119 (bottom — the ℱ(W) form with κ):** "Of particular interest are spaces ℱ(W) which satisfy the positivity condition for a given number κ, 0 < κ ≤ 1. Every element of the space is of the form F(z) + F(z + iκ) for an element F(z) of the space such that F(z + iκ) belongs to the space, and the real part of ⟨F(t + iκ), F(t)⟩_ℱ(W) is nonnegative for every such element F(z). An equivalent condition is that W(z + iκ)/W(z) is analytic and has a nonnegative real part in the half-plane iz̄ − iz > −κ." Also p. 119, mid-page, defining ℱ(W): "the weighted Hardy space ℱ(W) is defined to be the set of function F(z), **analytic in the upper half-plane**, such that F(z)/W(z) has bounded type in the upper half-plane and has square integrable boundary values on the real axis" — **ℱ(W) is NOT a space of entire functions** (source-confirms the §4-flagged paraphrase fix).

**p. 120, THEOREM 1 (namespace hazard — this is NOT Conrey–Li's Theorem 1):** "THEOREM 1. If W(z) = Γ(½v + ½ − ½iz) Γ(½v̄ + ½ − ½iz) for a complex number v with nonnegative real part, then W(z + iκ)/W(z) is analytic and has nonnegative real part in the half-plane iz̄ − iz > −κ when 0 < κ ≤ 1." (Gamma-weight positivity — a space where the condition HOLDS.)

**p. 124, THEOREM 2 (same hazard):** "THEOREM 2. Define W(z) = Γ(½v + ½ − ½iz) Γ(½v̄ + ½ − ½iz) where v + v̄ ≥ 0. If c > 0 and if 0 < κ ≤ 1, then every element of the double Sonine space of order v and parameter c is of the form F(z) + F(z + iκ) for an element F(z) of the space such that F(z + iκ) belongs to the space, and the real part of ⟨F(t + iκ), F(t)⟩_ℱ(W) is nonnegative." (Double-Sonine positivity — again a HOLDS case, not the zeta case.)

**p. 183 (the actual route to RH in this paper):** "The convergence hypothesis implies the Riemann hypothesis for all Dirichlet zeta functions." Followed by "THEOREM 12*. If χ is any character modulo r, the Dirichlet zeta function ζ_χ(s) = Σ_{n=1}^∞ χ(n) n^{−s} has no zeros in the half-plane Re s > ½." The star marks conditionality on the convergence (Euler-product) hypothesis — the "conjecture" of the title — **not** on the positivity condition.

**p. 184 (references — internal provenance).** w-05b's own list: [1] Self-reciprocal functions, JMAA 9 (1964); [2] "Escapes [sic — Espaces] Hilbertiens de Fonctions Entières," Masson, Paris, 1972; [3] Modular spaces of entire functions, JMAA 44 (1973); **[4] The Riemann hypothesis for Hilbert spaces of entire functions, Bull. Amer. Math. Soc. 15 (1986), 1–17; [5] The convergence of Euler products, J. Funct. Anal. 107 (1992), 122–210**; [6]–[8] Rovnyak–Rovnyak/Rovnyak. So p. 118's "formulation of the Riemann hypothesis … [4]" points at **BAMS 1986** and p. 119's "A source of positivity is found in the properties of certain weight functions [5]" points at **JFA 107 (1992)** — i.e., w-05b itself attributes the formulation and the source of positivity to the two earlier papers.

## 4. Fidelity verdict: FAITHFUL — and the largest residual risk is DISCHARGED

**(a) The attribution is faithful.** The positivity condition Conrey–Li test is stated verbatim, as a *sufficient hypothesis*, in de Branges 1994 pp. 118–119 (transcriptions above), in both the ℋ(E) form (their (3.1)) and the ℱ(W)/κ form (their (3.3); de Branges' equivalent condition Re[W(z + iκ)/W(z)] ≥ 0 matches CL Theorem 2's Re[W(z)/W(z + i)] ≥ 0 conclusion up to the reciprocal, which preserves the sign of the real part). "De Branges' positivity conditions [2] [3] [4]" is an accurate description, and the theorem-level "essentially due to de Branges (cf. [2] [3])" is *precisely* right: w-05b itself sources the RH formulation to BAMS 1986 and the positivity to JFA 107 (its own refs [4], [5]).

**(b) The counterexamples hit de Branges' LITERAL condition, not a Conrey–Li strengthening.** This was the open question of FETCH-LIST-RESPONSE §4 ("whether the counterexamples hit de Branges' literal conditions or a Conrey–Li strengthening is unverified"). Resolution from the side-by-side texts: (i) CL's added regularity hypotheses are not extra assumptions but *true facts* of E = ξ(1 − iz) — no real zeros (Hadamard–de la Vallée Poussin non-vanishing on Re s = 1), Ē(z̄) = E(z − i) (the functional equation, ε = 1), strict monotonicity of |E(x + iy)| in y (the ρ-product formula) — all verified in CL §3.1; (ii) CL's pointwise conclusion ℜ{Ē′(w)E(w + i)/2πi} ⩾ 0 at zeros is a *necessary consequence* derived from the positivity hypothesis alone (F = K(w,·) in their proof), so its numerical failure (3.2) falsifies the positivity conjunct itself; (iii) de Branges' extra clause "every element of the space be of the form F(z) + F(z + i)" is one conjunct of his condition — falsifying the positivity conjunct falsifies the conjunction a fortiori. **Therefore de Branges' literal sufficient condition fails for H(ξ(1 − iz)) and ℱ(1/ξ(1 − iz)), and (CL §3.2 + Sarnak) for the χ₄ analogues.** The "essentially" marks genuine but risk-free reshaping: prose-to-theorem, the added (true) regularity hypotheses, the added pointwise necessary condition, the dropped conjunct.

**(c) Bonus fidelity fact — de Branges had already hedged.** p. 119: "the positivity condition is in doubt in the context of Dirichlet zeta functions." The 1994 paper never asserts that the zeta spaces satisfy the positivity condition; its route to RH is the **convergence (Euler-product) hypothesis** (Theorems 11*/12*, pp. 182–183). Consequently: **Conrey–Li falsified a hypothesis its author had himself marked doubtful, and they did NOT refute the 1994 paper's actual conjecture.** No program file was found claiming "CL refuted de Branges' conjecture" (checked: all direction files, BARRIER-ZOO III.5) — no drift of that kind exists; keep it that way in future write-ups.

**(d) Namespace hazard (new, record it when citing).** "Theorem 1"/"Theorem 2" *of w-05b* (pp. 120, 124) are gamma-weight/double-Sonine positivity theorems — cases where the condition HOLDS — and are unrelated to "Theorem 1"/"Theorem 2" *of Conrey–Li* (the "essentially due to de Branges" pair).
