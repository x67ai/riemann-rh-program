## 4. Content claims — read from the full text

All four were checked against `pdftotext -layout` of arXiv v2, which is the referee-revised text.

### (i) "3-level correlations give 96.14% multiplicity ≤ 2 (GRH, support 2)" — **CONFIRMED**

The number is theirs, it is under GRH, it is the n = 3 row, and support 2 is the m = 1
specialization of their admissibility constraint. It is **Theorem 1**, the paper's main theorem
(p. 2):

> **Theorem 1.** Let L(s, π) be the L-function attached to an irreducible cuspidal automorphic
> representation π of GL_m/Q. Assuming GRH for L(s, π) we have
>
>     lim inf_{T→∞} Z_n(T)/N(T)  ≥   0.9614  if n = 3 and m = 1,
>                                    0.2997  if n = 3 and m = 2,
>                                    0.9787  if n = 4 and m = 1,
>
> where the results for (n, m) = (3, 2), (4, 1) hold under the additional assumption that certain
> series of rational functions are summed correctly using Maple; see Section 6.

The translation from "n = 3" to "multiplicity ≤ 2" is their own definition, one page earlier:

> Let Z_n(T) count the number of nontrivial zeros of L(s, π) with multiplicity at most n − 1 up to
> height T.

So n = 3, m = 1 is exactly "at least 96.14% of the zeta zeros have multiplicity ≤ 2, under GRH".
The report's companion figure, "97.87% of multiplicity ≤ 3", is likewise confirmed: n = 4, m = 1.

"Support 2" is confirmed as the m = 1 case of the Rudnick–Sarnak admissibility window (p. 2):

> supp(f̂) ⊆ {x ∈ R^n : |x_1| + … + |x_n| < 2/m}  (in the distributional sense)

and, after reduction to n − 1 variables (p. 4), `supp(ĝ) ⊆ (2/m) H_{n−1}`. At m = 1 the bound is
2. One caveat for the reconciliation paragraph: the constraint is a **total** ℓ¹ support bound
across all n coordinates, not a per-variable one, so "support 2" should not be read as the
pair-correlation "support (−2, 2)" of the Montgomery literature without saying which is meant.

Two further facts from p. 2-3 that the reconciliation paragraph will want:

> As far as we know, the bounds in Theorem 1 are all new. Using a different method, it has been
> shown that at least 95.5% of the nontrivial zeros of the zeta function are simple or double
> zeros [12]. Our (n, m) = (3, 1) case improves on this result.

and, immediately relevant to A4's "adds exactly nothing" wording,

> For (n, m) = (2, 2) we show in Section 3 our method does not produce a positive lower bound, but
> as shown above for (n, m) = (3, 2) it does.

That second sentence is the sharpest form of "for them the third-level datum pays" and is a better
anchor for the reconciliation than the N_n remark discussed under (iii).

### (ii) "Their eq. (5) is the atom half of [A4's] Theorem 3.4" — **CONFIRMED in substance**

Their equation (5), on p. 5, is:

>     N_n(T) = Σ_{k=1}^{n} S(n, k) M_k(T),                                (5)
>
> where S(n, k) are the Stirling numbers of the second kind.

with the two families defined as the report describes — a power sum and a falling-factorial sum.
From p. 5:

> As an alternative to M_n(T) we could also use the parameters
>     N_k(T) = Σ_{j≥1, γ_j≤T} m_{ρ_j}^{k−1},
> which satisfy N(T) = N_1(T) and N*(T) = N_2(T).

and from p. 3:

>     M_n(T) = Σ_{j_1,…,j_n ≥ 1 distinct, γ_{j_1}=…=γ_{j_n}≤T} 1
>            = Σ_{j≥1, γ_j≤T} (m_{ρ_j} − 1) ⋯ (m_{ρ_j} − n + 1).
> Then M_1(T) = N(T) and M_2(T) = N*(T) − N(T).

I checked the arithmetic rather than trusting the report. Because the index j runs over zeros
*repeated according to multiplicity*, `M_k = Σ_{zeros} m(m−1)⋯(m−k+1)` and
`N_k = Σ_{zeros} m·m^{k−1} = Σ_{zeros} m^k`. At n = 3, with S(3,1) = 1, S(3,2) = 3, S(3,3) = 1,
equation (5) reads

    Σ m³  =  Σ m  +  3 Σ m(m−1)  +  Σ m(m−1)(m−2),

which rearranges to `Σm³ − 3Σm² + 2Σm = Σ m(m−1)(m−2)`. A4's Theorem 3.4 (`results/arxiv/a4-no-go/main.tex`,
`\label{thm:blind}`; Theorem 3.4 in the built PDF, "cubic blindness on isolated blocks") states

>     C − 3F + 2 Mass = Σ_{zeros} m(m−1)(m−2) + 6 Σ_{pairs} m_p²(m_p−1) A_p²

with the atom block charges (mass m, Frobenius m², cubic m³), i.e. Mass = Σm, F = Σm², C = Σm³ on
atoms. The atom half is therefore **the n = 3 instance of the same elementary Stirling identity**.
The report is right.

Two precisions the executing session should carry into the reconciliation paragraph, because the
report's gloss is slightly loose:

* Equation (5) as printed runs power-sum-from-falling-factorials (N from M); A4's Theorem 3.4 uses
  the rearranged direction. Same identity, opposite reading order. It would be wrong to write
  "their eq. (5) *is* C − 3F + 2·Mass"; correct is "their eq. (5) is the Stirling conversion whose
  n = 3 case is the atom half of Theorem 3.4".
* The report's parenthetical "including the consequence A4 draws (vanishing on marks {1,2})" is
  also right, and for their reason: M_n(T) is a count over n-tuples of coincident zeros, so it
  vanishes identically when every multiplicity is ≤ n − 1. That is why they can target
  "multiplicity ≤ n − 1" at all. So the vanishing is not a coincidence of two papers; it is the
  same observation.
* Equation (5) is **not** the n-level correlation asymptotic. That is their equation (1)
  (the Hejhal / Rudnick–Sarnak limit with Dyson's `W_n(x) = det(sin π(x_i−x_j)/π(x_i−x_j))`).
  If the citing sentence in A4 says "their eq. (5) is the correlation identity", that is wrong;
  eq. (5) is pure combinatorics of the multiplicity statistics.

### (iii) "their N_n remark is a published negative of the same species" — quote **CONFIRMED**, report's gloss **partly wrong**

The remark exists and the report quotes it verbatim. Full context, p. 5, immediately after (5):

> Since S(n, k) ≥ 0 for all n and k, we can use our bounds on c_{n,m}, computed Section 5 and 6, to
> obtain
>
>     lim sup_{T→∞} N_n(T)/N(T)  ≤   2.0597  if n = 3 and m = 1,
>                                    5.8984  if n = 3 and m = 2,             (6)
>                                    3.8834  if n = 4 and m = 1,
>
> provided the hypothesis of Theorem 1 hold. **We also adapted the problem in (4) to bound N_n(T)
> directly, but we were not able to improve over the results in (6) in this way.**

Three corrections to how the report describes this:

1. **Location.** The adjudication block says "Their **§1** also remarks". It is **§2**
   ("Derivation of the optimization problem"), at the end, after equation (6), on p. 5. Cite it as
   §2 or as "the remark following (6)".
2. **The brief's framing "is it a statement that a higher-level row adds nothing?" — no.**
   N_n and M_k here are *the same level n*; what failed was a **different formulation of the same
   problem** (optimizing the SDP (4) directly against the power-sum functional, versus deriving the
   power-sum bound from the falling-factorial bounds through (5)). It is a negative about
   formulation, not about information content of a higher correlation level. A4 must not
   paraphrase it as "GdLL also found the higher level adds nothing" — that would misstate their
   paper, and a referee who knows it will catch it.
3. **"Same species" is defensible but only loosely.** Both are "we tried the extra/direct route and
   it bought nothing", and it is fair to cite it as a precedent for publishing such a negative. It
   is not evidence for A4's actual claim. If A4 wants a GdLL negative that is genuinely about a
   level not paying, the better one is the (n, m) = (2, 2) statement quoted under (i) — "our method
   does not produce a positive lower bound" at n = 2 for GL_2 — which is a genuine level-indexed
   negative, and which they then *overturn* at n = 3. That cuts against A4 rather than for it,
   which is precisely why the reconciliation paragraph is needed.

### (iv) "Do they use semidefinite programming / linear programming, and at what support?" — **SDP yes; LP no**

Semidefinite programming, explicitly, and it is the paper's method — the abstract names it
("in conjunction with semidefinite programming bounds"), and their problem (4), p. 5, is:

>     minimize    ν_n(g)
>     subject to  g(0) = 1                                                  (4)
>                 X ⪰ 0
>
> Here X ⪰ 0 means X is a positive semidefinite matrix of appropriate size. Since we optimize a
> linear functional over positive semidefinite matrices with affine constraints, this is a
> semidefinite program, which can be solved efficiently in practice.

built from the sum-of-squares parametrization `g(x) = Σ_{i,i'} X_{i,i'} g_i(x) g_{i'}(x)` (eq. (3)).
`grep -ni "linear program"` over the full text returns **nothing** — there is no LP anywhere in the
paper. (§4 "solves linear systems", but that is symmetry reduction via Schur's lemma plus Pataki's
rank bound, not linear programming.) MSC 90C22 is "Semidefinite programming", consistent.

Supports actually computed: `supp(ĝ) ⊆ (2/m) H_{n−1}` with
`H_{n−1} = {x : |x_1| + … + |x_{n−1}| + |x_1 + … + x_{n−1}| ≤ 1}`. Results are given at
**m = 1 (support 2)** for n = 3 and n = 4, and at **m = 2 (support 1)** for n = 3. That m = 2 /
support-1 row is worth noting in A4's reconciliation paragraph, since A4's own claim is at
"effective support 1".
