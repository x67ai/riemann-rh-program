### Content claim (b) — Theorems 17.11 (RH-true) and 17.14 (RH-false) in one chapter

Chapter 17 is **"Optimality of the dlVP Remainder Term"**, pp. 195–228. Its chapter summary
(p. 195) already announces both examples:

> Summary. Can one establish a better PNT remainder estimate for a discrete g-number system
> than that of de la Vallée Poussin? Here we construct an example showing that the answer is
> in general, No. Also, we show, in the other direction, that there exist discrete g-number
> systems for which the analogue of the Riemann Hypothesis holds.

and §17.1 Background, pp. 195–196, names the theorem numbers explicitly:

> (DLVP) to establish the optimality of (17.1) and (17.2), apart from the numerical value of
> the constants c; the result is given in **Theorem 17.14**.
> (RH) to give, on the other hand, a g-number system for which the Riemann Hypothesis holds
> for the associated zeta function and the prime counting function satisfies
> π(x) = li(x) + O(x^{1/2}). This result, which is slightly better than the classical
> conditional estimate, is given in **Theorem 17.11**.

**Theorem 17.11 (p. 205, §17.3.2 "Riemann Hypothesis example"), verbatim:**

> **Theorem 17.11.** There is a system of Beurling primes P_R such that
> (i) the associated Beurling integers satisfy N_R(x) = k_1 x + O(x^{1/2} exp{c(log x)^{2/3}})
>     with k_1 > 0;
> (ii) the associated zeta function ζ_R(s) is analytic for σ > 1/2 except for a simple pole at
>     s = 1 with residue k_1;
> (iii) the function ζ_R(s) has no zeros on the half plane σ > 1/2;
> (iv) the prime counting function π_R(x) satisfies π_R(x) = li(x) + O(x^{1/2}).

Clause (iii) is exactly the analogue of RH, and it is **true** for this system.

**Theorem 17.14 (p. 208, §17.4 "Generalized primes with large oscillation"), verbatim:**

> **Theorem 17.14.** There is a system of Beurling primes P_B such that
> (i) the resulting Beurling integers satisfy N_B(x) = k_2 x + O(x^{1/2} exp{c(log x)^{2/3}})
>     with k_2 > 0;
> (ii) the associated zeta function ζ_B(s) is analytic for σ > 1/2 except for a simple pole at
>     s = 1 with residue k_2;
> (iii) the function ζ_B(s) has **infinitely many zeros on the curve σ = 1 − 1/ log t, t ≥ e²**,
>     and no zeros to its right;
> (iv) the Chebyshev function ψ_B(x) associated with P_B satisfies
>     limsup (ψ_B(x) − x)/(x exp{−2√(log x)}) = 2 and liminf (…) = −2.

Clause (iii) puts infinitely many zeros strictly to the **right** of σ = 1/2 (on the classical
zero-free-region boundary curve), so RH is **false** for this system — while (i) keeps
N_B(x) − k_2 x as small as in the RH-true case.

The two theorems live in the same chapter, and the book itself notes the point (p. 208):
"Landau's argument applies to both P_B and the system P_R of Theorem 17.11 and produces the
same remainder term (17.2)."

**Claim (b): CONFIRMED. Theorem numbers 17.11 and 17.14 are exactly right.**
(Chapter 17 has sections 17.1–17.11, the last being "Notes"; §17.11 is a *section* number, not
a theorem number, and must not be confused with Theorem 17.11.)

### Content claims — the actual axiom list

The paper has two sections. §1 "Weil cohomologies over F̄_p" recalls Serre's supersingular
argument. §2 "'Weil-'cohomologies over spec Z" states, pp. 2–4, "the basic expected properties
of H^n(spec o_k, C) and its operator θ" as numbered items **2.1 through 2.15**. Note that these
are not all axioms: 2.1–2.4 are the stated basic expected properties, 2.5 and 2.8–2.9 are
derived, 2.6 and 2.10–2.12 are expectations, 2.7 is flagged as "much more optimistically",
2.13 is the theorem, and 2.14–2.15 are the closing suggestion. The labels, verbatim:

> **2.1.** θ is a derivation with respect to cup-product.
>
> **2.2.** H⁰(spec o_k, C) = C with θ = 0.
>
> **2.3.** H¹(spec o_k, C) is infinite dimensional with the eigenvalues of θ being the
> non-trivial zeroes of ζ_k(s) with their multiplicities. Here the eigenvalues α are counted
> with their algebraic multiplicities dim H¹(spec o_k, C)^{θ∼α}. …
>
> **2.4.** There is a θ-equivariant trace isomorphism
> tr : H²(spec o_k, C) →∼ C(−1) where C(−1) = C with θ = id.
>
> **2.5.** h₁ ∪ h₂ = θ(h₁ ∪ h₂) = θh₁ ∪ h₂ + h₁ ∪ θh₂.
>
> **2.6.** the pairing ∪ : H¹(spec o_k, C)^{θ∼α} × H¹(spec o_k, C)^{θ∼1−α} —∪→ H²(spec o_k, C)
> —tr→ C is perfect. In particular, α is an eigenvalue of θ on H¹ if and only if 1 − α is an
> eigenvalue and the (algebraic) multiplicities are equal. Note that via 2.3 this is compatible
> with the functional equation of ζ_k(s).
>
> **2.7.** There is a canononical [sic] anti-linear automorphism ∗ of H¹(spec o_k, C) with
> ∗² = −id, ∗ ∘ θ = θ̄ ∘ ∗ and such that we get a scalar product by setting
> ⟨h, h′⟩ = tr(h ∪ ∗h′) on H¹(spec o_k, C).

**(a) "its item 2.3 is a trace-reading axiom" — REFUTED.**
2.3 is the **spectral / Hilbert–Pólya axiom**: H¹ is infinite dimensional and the eigenvalues of
θ are the non-trivial zeros of ζ_k. There is no trace in it. The **trace axiom is 2.4** ("There
is a θ-equivariant trace isomorphism tr : H²(spec o_k, C) →∼ C(−1)"). If the m0 note wants the
trace-reading axiom, it must cite **2.4**; if it wants the spectral reading, 2.3 is right but
must not be called a trace axiom. This is a real, executable correction.

**(b) "its 2.6 corresponds to a duality/transpose axiom" — CONFIRMED as duality; the word
"transpose" is not Deninger's.**
2.6 is the perfect cup-product pairing between the α- and (1−α)-generalized eigenspaces of θ on
H¹, landing in H² and composed with the trace of 2.4 — i.e. a Poincaré-duality-type perfect
pairing, and Deninger notes it reproduces the functional equation of ζ_k(s). Nothing in the
paper calls this a "transpose" axiom. Cite it as a duality / perfect-pairing axiom.

**(c) "its 2.7 sets up a chain of consequences" — CONFIRMED (in substance).**
2.7 is the positivity / ∗-operator hypothesis, introduced with "Much more optimistically, one
could hope for the following property." The chain the paper draws from it, verbatim (p. 3):

> Note that 2.5 and 2.7 imply the relation ⟨h, h′⟩ = ⟨θh, h′⟩ + ⟨h, θh′⟩. Hence θ − ½ would be
> skew-symmetric on H¹(spec o_k, C) and its eigenvalues therefore purely imaginary. Using 2.3
> the Riemann hypotheses would follow. Of course this argument follows the lead of Serre [16].
> Also note that if 2.7 is true, skew-symmetry of θ − ½ would imply that the algebraic
> eigenspaces H^{θ∼α} of θ are the usual ones H^{θ=α}.

So 2.7 + 2.5 + 2.3 ⟹ RH; and 2.7 also yields the quaternionic structure used at the end of the
paper. **Caution for the executing session:** 2.7 is *not* what Theorem 2.13 refutes — see (d).

**(d) "its Theorem 2.13 is the impossibility statement" — CONFIRMED. Verbatim (p. 4):**

> **Theorem 2.13.** For a number field k there is no functorial real valued cohomology theory
> H^•(spec o_k, R) with endomorphism θ_R such that H^•(spec o_k, C) := H^•(spec o_k, R) ⊗_R C
> with θ := θ_R ⊗ id satisfies property 2.12.

where 2.12 is the immediately preceding expectation

> **2.12.** dim_C Hom_G(V_π, V_k) = ord_{s=1/2} L(V_π, s),  with V_k = H¹(spec o_k, C)^{θ∼½}.

The proof takes k/Q Galois with G having an irreducible **symplectic** representation V_π of
root number W(π) = −1 (e.g. a quaternion field with G = H₈), so ord_{s=1/2} L(V_π, s) is odd by
the functional equation; a real form V_k^R would decompose over R in a way forcing V_π to occur
with **even** multiplicity — contradiction. Deninger closes: "There is thus no real valued
'Weil-'cohomology for arithmetic curves."

The load-bearing property is therefore **2.12** (equivalently the Galois-equivariant refinement
of 2.3 through 2.10–2.11), **not** 2.3 or 2.7. Any sentence in the m0 note that says Deninger
rules out a real theory satisfying "his trace axiom" is wrong on two counts and must be
rewritten to say: satisfying the Galois-equivariant order-of-vanishing property 2.12.

### Content claim — "the §3.3 Lefschetz-tie axiom IS Deninger's conjecture, in print for 28 years"

Now what Deninger 1998 actually prints. Three things, all conjectural, all on the record:

**(i) The explicit formula recast as a Lefschetz trace formula over Spec Z** (§3, p. 171,
following eq. (5) which reads
`Σ_i (−1)^i Tr(θ | H^i("Spec Z", R))_dis = Σ_p Σ_{k≥1} log p · δ_{k log p} + ⟨(1 − e^{−2t})^{−1}⟩`):

counting data on the other.

**(ii) The dynamical Lefschetz trace formula, stated as an expectation** (§4, p. 179):

> It seems reasonable to expect a dynamical trace formula of the form
> Σ_i (−1)^i Tr(φ^{t∗} | H̄^i(X, F)) = Σ_γ l(γ) Σ_{k=1}^∞ ε_γ(k) Tr(φ^{kl(γ)∗} | E_x) δ_{kl(γ)}

the explicit admission (p. 179–180):

> Apart from these cases which do not involve stationary points the formula (20) does not seem
> to be established. … It seems to be quite a challenge to establish such dynamical trace
> formulas in any generality and also for more general foliations.

**(iii) The zeros↔flow and primes↔closed-orbits dictionary made explicit.** §2 (p. 168–169):
θ acts on H¹("Spec Z", R) with eigenvalues the zeta zeros, and

> hence that θ = ½ + A where A is a skew-symmetric endomorphism of H¹("Spec Z", R). Hence the
> Riemann conjecture would follow.

§5 (p. 182), primes as orbit lengths, verbatim:

> For "Spec Z" the period group Λ must contain the numbers log p as they should be lengths of
> closed orbits.

and the bridge sentence at the end of §3 (p. 176):

> In the next section we consider trace formulas for dynamical systems on foliated spaces which
> bear striking formal similarities with (5) and its generalization (18).

**Verdict on the content claim: CONFIRMED in substance, with the report's verb over-stated.**

What is verified: Deninger 1998 does state, conjecturally and in print, a dynamical
Lefschetz-type trace formula tying the non-trivial zeros of zeta/L-functions (as eigenvalues of
the flow generator θ on H¹) to the closed orbits of a flow (whose lengths are the log p), and he
says outright that it is expected rather than established. Publication year 1998; the current
date is 2026, so "in print for 28 years" is **arithmetically correct** (28 years as of 2026).

What is **not** verified, and what the executing session must not write: that the m0 note's
§3.3 axiom **is** Deninger's conjecture. Deninger's setting is leafwise cohomology of a foliated
dynamical system; the m0 note's §3.3 is about counting data of an arithmetic correspondence D
read against the diagonal in a Yuan–Zhang-style arithmetic intersection calculus. They are the
same *move* — posit the Lefschetz tie because it cannot be proved over Q — not the same
statement. Recommended phrasing for the note, defensible on the evidence above:

> Positing such a tie as an axiom rather than proving it is not new: Deninger [Den98] already
> put a conjectural Lefschetz-type trace formula of exactly this shape in print in 1998, tying
> the zeros of zeta and motivic L-functions, as eigenvalues of a flow generator on H¹, to the
> closed orbits of a foliated dynamical system, and noted that "the formula (20) does not seem
> to be established" [Den98, §4].

If the note wants a citation for "in characteristic p that tie is a THEOREM", that is the
Grothendieck–Lefschetz trace formula, which Deninger 1998 also names (p. 169: "The Grothendieck
Lefschetz trace formula in characteristic p …") — but that should be cited to SGA/Grothendieck,
not to Deninger.
