### CONTENT CLAIM 1 — "equivalent to RH (non-vacuous but circular)": CONFIRMED, and here is the precise form

The equivalence is stated three times, sharpening each time. Quote whichever the note needs.

**(a) 2011, abstract (arXiv abs page, verbatim):**

> "For an operator of a certain class in Hilbert space, we introduce axioms of an abstract
> intersection theory, which we prove to be equivalent to the Riemann Hypothesis concerning the
> spectrum of that operator. In particular if the nontrivial zeros of the Riemann zeta-function
> arise from an operator of this class, the original Riemann Hypothesis is equivalent to the
> existence of an abstract intersection theory."

**(a) 2011, Theorem 3.5 (PDF p. 6, verbatim):**

> "Theorem 3.5. Let A: H ⊃ dom(A) → H be an operator satisfying (OP1–5). The following
> conditions are equivalent. (i) The Riemann Hypothesis holds for A. (ii) There exist an R-linear
> space V, a symmetric bilinear R-valued form β on V, a mapping ṽ_δ of dom(ṽ_δ) ⊂ F into V, and
> an R-algebra homomorphism Φ̃ of dom(ṽ_δ) ⊂ F into End_R(V) giving vectors v_01, v_10, h_a,
> Φ^n ṽ_δ = Φ(Y)^n ṽ_δ(Y) (Y > 0) in V so that axioms (INT1–3) of the abstract intersection
> theory hold."

**(b) 2015, Theorem 5.2 (PDF, verbatim):**

> "Theorem 5.2. Let A: H ⊃ dom(A) → H be an operator satisfying (OP1), (OP2), (OP3a), (OP4) and
> (OP5). (1) If there exists an abstract intersection theory AIT (in the sense of §3) for A, then
> the Riemann hypothesis holds for A. (2) Suppose further that A satisfies (OP3-b). Then, there
> exists a standard model AIT_m for A if and only if the Riemann hypothesis holds for A and A is
> semi-simple."

**(b) 2015, Theorem 5.4 — the sharpest, and the one to quote (PDF, verbatim):**

> "Theorem 5.4. Let A: H ⊃ dom(A) → H be an operator constructed in [U] corresponding to the
> Dirichlet L-function L(s, χ) associated with a congruence subgroup Γ of SL_2(Z). Then
> (1) L(s, χ) satisfies the Riemann hypothesis and its all nontrivial zeros are simple if and only
> if there exists a standard model AIT_m for A. (2) L(s, χ) satisfies the Riemann hypothesis and
> its all nontrivial zeros are simple if and only if there exists a GNS model AIT_GNS for A."
>
> "Remark 5.1. In the above theorem if Γ = SL_2(Z) then the Dirichlet L-function L(s, χ) reduces
> to the Riemann zeta-function ζ(s)."

**(c) 2021, introduction (PDF p. 1, verbatim):**

> "We proposed what we call abstract intersection theory, which implies the RH for the Riemann
> zeta-function ([BU1], [BU2]). In [BU2] we also developed a model for this theory, which we call
> a standard model. The existence of this model is equivalent to the RH (plus the simple zero
> conjecture)."

**So the precise statement of the equivalence is:**

> For an operator A obtained from automorphic scattering theory attached to L(s, χ) — for
> Γ = SL_2(Z), to ζ(s) itself — Banaszak and Uetake prove that **RH for L(s, χ) together with
> simplicity of all its nontrivial zeros holds if and only if a standard model (equivalently, a
> GNS model) of their abstract intersection theory exists for A** (2015, Thm 5.4). One direction
> alone — axioms ⇒ RH — needs no semi-simplicity (2015, Thm 5.2(1)). The authors state the
> resulting circularity themselves: "The existence of this model is equivalent to the RH (plus
> the simple zero conjecture)" (2021, §1).

That is exactly "non-vacuous but circular" in the report's phrase, and it is now sourced. Note the
extra rider the report omitted: **the equivalence is to RH *plus simple zeros*, not to RH alone**,
in the 2015/2021 form. Print the rider — it is the sharper and more honest statement, and it is
the authors' own.

### Adjudication of the content claim: CONFIRMED IN SUBSTANCE, but the word "graph" must go

* **CONFIRMED:** the paper does treat `z ↦ z^n` (i.e. `ζ ↦ ζ^n` on roots of unity, `e(γ) ↦ e(nγ)`
  on Q/Z) as the Frobenius over F_1 — §6.1 says so explicitly for every integer n, and
  Theorem 6.2(b) upgrades it to literal coincidence with the classical Frobenius correspondence
  for `n = p^ℓ` after reduction to a perfect field of characteristic p. The endomorphisms are the
  Bost–Connes endomotive's own, and they compose as `σ_nm = σ_n σ_m`, exactly the note's (PF3).
* **NARROW THIS:** the word **"graph" appears nowhere in the paper** (`grep -i graph funf1.txt`:
  **zero hits**). CCM work with the endomorphism `σ_n` on the pro-variety `μ(∞) = lim μ(n)`,
  `μ(n) = Spec Z[Z/n]` — roots of unity — not with a graph correspondence `Γ_{z↦z^n} ⊂ P¹ × P¹`,
  which is the m0 note's own construction on a surface. The honest statement is that the note's
  family, **restricted to the roots-of-unity locus**, is CCM's Frobenius family; the surface and
  the graph packaging are the note's.
* **ALSO NARROW:** "coincide with the Frobenius correspondence" is asserted by CCM only for
  `n = p^ℓ` over a perfect field of characteristic `p > 0` (Thm 6.2(b)). For general n the claim
  is the weaker §6.1 one — the `σ_n` are the Ẑ-powers `ψ^α` of the Frobenius of `F_1^∞/F_1`. Both
  support "treated there as Frobenius over F_1"; only the first supports "*is* the Frobenius
  correspondence".

**Suggested sentence for the note**, saying exactly what is sourced:

> The family `{Ψ_n}` of §3.2 is not new even as a Frobenius family: on the roots-of-unity locus
> `z ↦ z^n` is precisely the Bost–Connes endomotive's endomorphism `σ_n : e(γ) ↦ e(nγ)`, whose
> composition law `σ_nm = σ_n σ_m` is our (PF3), and Connes, Consani and Marcolli treat exactly
> these maps as Frobenius correspondences over F_1 — "the endomorphisms σ_n describe the
> Frobenius correspondence, in the sense that on the algebra Z[Q/Z] ⊗_Z K, for K a perfect field
> of characteristic p > 0, the endomorphisms σ_n, n = p^ℓ (ℓ ∈ N) coincide with the Frobenius
> correspondence" [CCM09, Thm 6.2(b)]. What §3.2 adds is only the graph packaging on P¹ × P¹,
> which is what makes the degree bookkeeping computable — and, as §3.2 shows, worthless.

### (d) How a converse theorem hosts an exclusion — one sentence, and one caveat

**The logical shape**, asserting only what the paper supports:

> Theorem 1.1 is a saturation statement — Hecke relations + Ramanujan + Voronoi already *force*
> automorphy on GL(3) — so its contrapositive is an exclusion: **any coefficient sequence that is
> not the Fourier-coefficient set of a GL(3) automorphic form must fail at least one of the three
> hypotheses**, and since the Voronoi formula is (by Kiral–Zhou, as Blomer–Leung note) equivalent
> to the twisted functional equations, a non-automorphic object possessing the full functional-
> equation package must fail the *Hecke relations* — i.e. must fail multiplicativity in both
> arguments, which is exactly the Euler-product/Hecke axiom the Davenport–Heilbronn function
> lacks.

**Caveat the executing session must respect.** Blomer and Leung prove this for **GL(3)**. The
Davenport–Heilbronn function is a degree-2 object, and their theorem does not literally apply to
it. What the note may legitimately say is that Blomer–Leung is the **sharpest formal home of the
*shape* of the exclusion** — a converse theorem whose hypothesis list is tight enough that
dropping the Hecke/Euler axiom is the only escape — not that it excludes DH. The zoo I.1 entry's
present wording ("the sharpest formal home") is already correctly hedged; keep that hedge, and add
the same hedge in the m0 note if the m0 note asserts more. Do not write that Blomer–Leung "exclude
