the Riemann hypothesis is the conclusion.** Positivity upstream, RH downstream. The report's gloss

> an algebraic endomorphism is again algebraic, and I(X x X, L_X (x) 1 + 1 (x) L_X) implies that
> Tr(u' o u) > 0 if u != 0 (compare, Serre [11]). Two celebrated consequences ensue: the
> semisimplicity of the ring of algebraic endomorphisms; the 'Riemann hypothesis' of the absolute
> value of the zeta function's zeros and poles."

**(f) Remark 3.10, quoted verbatim** (`kleiman.txt` line 1238):

> **3.10. Remark.** Let X be a complex variety. Then X satisfies I(X, L) by the Hodge index
> theorem. Hence, homological equivalence of algebraic 1-cycles on X is equal to numerical
> equivalence by 3.8, 3.1 and 3.4. Furthermore if X is an abelian variety or has dimension <= 4,
> then by 2.[11] or 3.1 and 3.4, X satisfies A(X, L); hence, by 3.9, homological equivalence on X
> is always equal to numerical equivalence.

**This does not say what the report says it says.** It is about complex varieties; its conclusion is
D(X) (homological = numerical), not RH. What it *does* illustrate — and this is a genuine, smaller
point the note may still want — is that Kleiman treats the Hodge index theorem as a **theorem that
is an input**, from which other statements are derived, exactly as the note's §8 does.

**(g) The remark the note actually wants: Remark 4.5** (`kleiman.txt` line 1506, p. 385):

> **4.5. Remark.** By the Lefschetz fixed-point formula, chi = (Delta^2), where Delta is the
> diagonal class on X x X. Hence, the functional equation, derived with the aid of a Weil
> cohomology, is independent of cohomology. **Furthermore, this derivation does not depend on any
> unproved conjectures.**

That final sentence is Kleiman keeping an explicit dependency ledger — separating what is proved
outright (rationality, the functional equation) from what is conditional on the standard
conjectures (Theorem 4.7, RH). It is the 1968 ancestor of exactly the bookkeeping the note is
doing, and it is a far stronger pin than 3.10.

1. Extend the imprint to the full one (series + both publishers).
2. Keep **Theorem 4.7** for "Hodge-type positivity is logically upstream of RH".
3. **Drop Remark 3.10 from that sentence.** Either cite it separately for the narrow
   characteristic-zero point (index theorem => hom = num), or replace it with **Remark 4.5**, which
   is the actual dependency-ledger remark.

### What is right and what is wrong about the gloss

Right: this is a **published, refereed instance of exactly the bookkeeping the note performs** —
professional algebraic geometers, in a top journal, pausing to record whether their proof of a
Hodge-type positivity statement consumes the Weil conjectures, precisely because that positivity
statement implies the Weil conjectures.

Wrong: "the published non-circularity audit" reads as *a demonstration that the proof is
non-circular*. The sentence says the opposite — they **did not attempt** to avoid it. If the note
cites this as an audit whose verdict was "clean", the citation is misdescribed and the note is
vulnerable. Recommended wording for the note:

> Ito, Ito and Koshikawa flag the same dependency question explicitly, and answer it in the
> negative for their own proof: "However, we did not attempt to avoid the Weil conjecture in our
> proof of Theorem 1.2" [IIK, Remark 1.3].

That is stronger for the note than the original gloss, because it establishes both that the
question is a recognized one **and** that answering it is not automatic.

### Caveat that must travel with this citation

The remark number **1.3** is verified in **arXiv:2206.10086v1 only**. There is no v2 on arXiv, and
the published version (J. Algebraic Geom., online 3 Oct 2024) is behind the AMS paywall and could
not be read this session. Numbering *usually* survives copy-editing, but this quote is going into
the note verbatim, so the safe form is to attribute the remark to the arXiv version explicitly.
The house-style string below does that.

### Ready-to-paste citation

    K. Ito, T. Ito and T. Koshikawa, *The Hodge standard conjecture for self-products of K3
    surfaces*, J. Algebraic Geom. **34** (2025), no. 2, 299-330; doi:10.1090/jag/840;
    arXiv:2206.10086. Remark 1.3 (numbering as in arXiv:2206.10086v1).
