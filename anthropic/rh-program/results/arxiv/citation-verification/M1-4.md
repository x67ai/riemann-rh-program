> (a) the full Tate conjecture for abelian varieties over F; (b) the standard conjecture of Hodge
> type for abelian varieties in characteristic p.

> **Theorem 3.** The full Tate conjecture for algebraic varieties over F implies Grothendieck's
> standard conjectures over F.

> **Theorem 1.4.** Assume that the Lefschetz standard conjecture holds for algebraic varieties over
> finite fields. Then all absolute Hodge classes on abelian varieties over fields of characteristic
> zero are almost-algebraic.

> **Theorem 3.2.** Assume that the Lefschetz standard conjecture holds for algebraic varieties over
> F and l-adic etale cohomology (some l != p). Then the full Tate conjecture holds for abelian
> varieties over finite fields of characteristic p.

- Journal-ref: **none.** No journal reference or DOI row on the abs page; zbMATH holds it only as

- Content claim: confirmed. The abstract states which form is known and on what (the Weil
  conjectures) and which is conjectural; every theorem in the body is stated with its hypothesis in
  the statement. That is exactly the bookkeeping the note appeals to. Strictly, the *abstract alone*
  does the known/conjectural split; the "which results it assumes" bookkeeping is carried by the
  theorem statements and the introduction. If the note leans on the abstract alone, widen the
  pointer to "abstract and the statements of Theorems 1-3".

**Ready-to-paste citation.**

## CONTENT: what the arithmetic Hodge index theorem asserts, and in which codimension

This was read, not inferred. Three primary sources, all agreeing.

**Source 1 -- Yuan-Zhang I (arXiv:1304.3538v1), Section 1.1, Theorem 1.1, verbatim:**

> **Theorem 1.1 ([Fal, Hr, Mo1]).** Let K be a number field, and pi : X -> Spec O_K be a regular
> arithmetic variety, geometrically connected of relative dimension n >= 1. Let M-bar be a Hermitian
> line bundle on X, and L-bar be an ample Hermitian line bundle on X. Assume that
> M_K . L_K^{n-1} = 0 on the generic fiber X_K. Then the arithmetic intersection number
>
>     M-bar^2 . L-bar^{n-1} <= 0.
>
> Moreover, if L is ample on X and the metric of L-bar is strictly positive, then the equality holds
> if and only if M-bar = pi^* M-bar_0 for some Hermitian line bundle M-bar_0 on Spec O_K.
>
> The result was due to Faltings [Fal] and Hriljac [Hr] for n = 1, and due to Moriwaki [Mo1] for
> general n.

and, from the same paper's opening paragraph:

> The Hodge index theorem for divisors on arithmetic surfaces proved by Faltings [Fal] in 1984 and
> Hirijac [Hr] in 1985 is one of the fundamental results in Arakelov theory. ... In 1996, Moriwaki
> [Mo1] extended the Hodge index theorem for codimension one cycles on high-dimensional arithmetic
> varieties, and then confirmed the codimension one case of the arithmetic standard conjecture
> proposed by Gillet and Soule in [GS3]. Despite its fundamental importance in number theory and
> arithmetic geometry, ... the high-codimensional case of the Gillet-Soule conjecture is still
> wide open.

(The misspelling "Hirijac" is Yuan-Zhang's, in the original; the correct spelling is Hriljac.)

**Source 2 -- Moriwaki, arXiv:alg-geom/9403011v4, Introduction, verbatim.** He states the
Gillet-Soule conjecture in all codimensions and then proves only p = 1:

> In [GS], H. Gillet and C. Soule conjectured that
> **Arithmetic Analogues of Grothendieck's Standard Conjectures.** For a suitable choice of k,
> if 2p <= d+1, then
> (a) The homomorphism L^{d+1-2p} : CH^p-hat(X)_R -> CH^{d+1-p}-hat(X)_R is bijective, and
> (b) If x in CH^p-hat(X)_R, x != 0 and L^{d+2-2p}(x) = 0, then
>     (-1)^p deg-hat(x . L^{d+1-2p}(x)) > 0.

> **Theorem A.** ... (1) L^{d-1} : CH^1-hat(X)_R -> CH^d-hat(X)_R is injective.
> (2) If x in CH^1-hat(X)_R, x != 0 and L^d(x) = 0, then deg-hat(x L^{d-1}(x)) < 0.

> Theorem A is a consequence of the following higher dimensional generalization of
> Faltings-Hriljac's Hodge index theorem on arithmetic surfaces (cf. [Fa] and [Hr]).

**Source 3 -- Kuennemann, Compositio Math. 99 (1995), p. 115, verbatim (from the Numdam scan):**

> Gillet and Soule have shown in [7] that the conjecture holds when p = 0 or when X is an arithmetic
> surface. In the second case, the conjecture is a consequence of the Hodge index theorem of
> Faltings and Hriljac. Moriwaki has shown in [11] that part ii) holds in general in codimension
> one.

**The one sentence the note can source.**

> The arithmetic Hodge index theorem is a statement about **codimension-one** arithmetic cycles
> (divisors / Hermitian line bundles): on a regular arithmetic variety X -> Spec O_K of relative
> dimension n, if M-bar is a Hermitian line bundle whose generic-fiber class is primitive against an
> ample L-bar (that is, M_K . L_K^{n-1} = 0), then M-bar^2 . L-bar^{n-1} <= 0, with equality exactly
> when M-bar is pulled back from the base -- proved by Faltings (1984) and Hriljac (1985) for
> arithmetic surfaces (n = 1) and by Moriwaki (1996) for all n. [Yuan-Zhang, arXiv:1304.3538v1,
> Thm. 1.1; Moriwaki, Math. Res. Lett. 3 (1996), Thms. A and B; Kuennemann, Compositio Math. 99
> (1995), p. 115.]

**Why that is not the missing piece for a surface-level Weil positivity, and the record backs the
claim.** Three points, each with a source:

1. The theorem is an inequality of **one sign only, in codimension one**: it says the arithmetic
   self-intersection form is *negative* semi-definite on primitive divisor classes (Moriwaki's
   Theorem A(2): `deg-hat(x L^{d-1}(x)) < 0`), which is the p = 1 instance of the Gillet-Soule sign
   `(-1)^p deg-hat(...) > 0`. It is the Arakelov-theoretic shadow of the classical Hodge index
   theorem for divisors, not a positivity statement about a cycle-pairing in the middle dimension.
2. Everything above codimension one -- which is where a Weil-positivity argument for a surface would
   need to live -- is **open**: "the high-codimensional case of the Gillet-Soule conjecture is still
   wide open" (Yuan-Zhang, arXiv:1304.3538v1, p. 2), and Kuennemann's 1995 survey confirms only
   p = 0, arithmetic surfaces, and Moriwaki's codimension-one part ii).
3. Faltings-Hriljac is *itself* the arithmetic-surface case (Kuennemann, p. 115: "when X is an
   arithmetic surface ... the conjecture is a consequence of the Hodge index theorem of Faltings and
   Hriljac"), so it cannot supply anything beyond what the codimension-one inequality already gives
   on a surface.
