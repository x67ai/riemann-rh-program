## Findings by item (filled at harvest; each entry cites its report)

### Item 1 — Cantwell–Conlon, Duminy's theorem (report `a01-12-warsaw.md`, COMPLETE)
- **Standing hypotheses (p. 225, verbatim):** "(M, 𝓕) a transversely oriented, C²-foliated manifold of codimension one, with M compact and oriented. We assume each component of ∂M, if any, is a leaf of 𝓕."
- **Semiproper (pp. 225–226):** a leaf F is semiproper if "at least one side of F is proper, so we include the case in which F is a proper leaf."
- **Theorem 1.1 (Duminy):** "If the leaf F of 𝓕 is semiproper and if it accumulates on the exceptional LMS X, then ℰ^X(F) is homeomorphic to the Cantor set" — X an exceptional *local* minimal set (an exceptional minimal set of 𝓕|U, U open saturated). **Corollary 1.2** is exactly Hurder's sentence (F ⊂ X minimal ⟹ ℰ(F) is a Cantor set). Theorems 1.3, 1.4 and the proof structure are transcribed in the report.
- **Versus Hurder r3s-29 §5:** C², codimension one, compact M agree; Hurder omits the orientability / transverse-orientability and boundary-leaf hypotheses and quotes the corollary, not Theorem 1.1. Hurder's Problem 5.4 is printed on p. 226 as the authors' own conjecture ("true for Markov LMS but unknown in general"), alongside a weaker generic-leaf conjecture and the remark that the generic leaf has one end or a Cantor set of ends. The paper's §8 prints the Dippolito, Hector and measure-zero questions (= Hurder 5.1–5.3).
- **What it settles for Q-S4⁗:** the exact statement with its hypotheses is now on disk; the manifold half still waits on Ghys 1995 (item 2). No verdict changed this session.

### Item 2 — Ghys 1995, *Topologie des feuilles génériques* (report `a02-ghys1995.md`, COMPLETE)
- **Identity:** exact match. Ann. of Math. (2) 141, no. 2 (Mar. 1995) 387–422; PDF page = printed − 385 (PDF 1 = JSTOR cover). Text layer strips accents, runs words together, garbles math — every quotation in the report was transcribed by vision. French throughout.
- **Theorem A (p. 387):** an orientable 2-dimensional lamination of a compact space with no compact leaf has *uncountably many* leaves homeomorphic to one of six surfaces: plane, Loch Ness monster, cylinder, Jacob's ladder, Cantor tree, blooming Cantor tree (characterized on p. 389 by the end space having 1 or 2 points or being a Cantor set, with the planar-end set empty or everything). Three further surfaces in the non-orientable case (p. 411).
- **Theorem A′ (p. 388):** the union of leaves that are compact or one of the six is a Borel set of full measure for *every harmonic measure*. "Generic" = harmonic-measure-a.e. — explicitly NOT Baire, and not restricted to invariant transverse measures (which "exist only rarely"; an invariant transverse measure yields a "completely invariant" harmonic measure, p. 401). **Theorem B (p. 389):** in any dimension a harmonic-a.e. leaf has 0, 1, 2 or a Cantor set of ends. Theorems C, D (pp. 390, 392) characterize two ends / ≥ 2 ends (compact extension of a measured 1-dim foliation; Kakutani-equivalence to a non-trivial HNN extension).
- **Hypotheses:** compact metrizable space, C³ leaves, transversally only continuous, no codimension restriction (p. 400). A minimal set of a C² codimension-one foliation of a closed 3-manifold is a lamination in this sense, so everything applies to it by restriction.
- **Minimal sets and non-generic leaves:** the paper constrains ONLY a.e. leaves, never all leaves and never a named leaf. Duminy, "semiproper" and "exceptional minimal set" do not occur; Cantwell–Conlon is cited only for endset/leaf-prescription examples. Ghys's Theorem B and Duminy's theorem are logically independent.
- **Verdict for Q-S4⁗ §5 step 2:** the paper is SILENT on both (a) and (b): no obstruction to a plane leaf (the plane is one of the six generic types), nothing about flows, non-transverse sets or scaling measures. It passes as a consistency check only. Read literally, (b) is answered YES by the irrational linear foliation of T³ (every leaf a plane) — the agent's inference, flagged as such; the intended reading (exceptional minimal set) is not addressed. **The manifold half of Q-S4⁗ rests on item 1 (Duminy) and Hurder's Problem 5.4, not on this paper.** Wording nit for the next program session: the adjudication calls the paper's subject "which surfaces occur as leaves in a minimal set"; the actual subject is harmonic-a.e. leaves of any compact lamination.
- §4 of the report lists further leads (e.g. Cass, *Minimal leaves in foliations*, Trans. AMS 287 (1985); the 5-manifold example with invariant volume and non-generic leaves with countably many ends).

### Item 3 — Kopei 2011, foliated Arakelov theory (report `a03-kopei.md`, COMPLETE)
- **Identity:** exact match (DOI 10.1007/s12188-011-0061-4; received 24 Sep 2010, online 20 Sep 2011). 49 PDF pages, printed = PDF + 140. Text layer clean; formula-bearing quotations vision-verified. Version of record; no arXiv version (the precursor math/0605184 is his ref. [25]).
- **The class (Definition 2.1, p. 143):** compact connected smooth (2n+1)-manifold, codimension-one foliation by complex manifolds, boundary (if any) a union of leaves; a smooth foliated flow transverse to the foliation off finitely many flow-invariant compact leaves. **Nothing else** — no fixed-point, non-degeneracy, metric, conformality or transverse-measure hypothesis. Remark 2.5 (p. 144): "we do not need these fixed points for any proof and therefore do not require that the flow has fixed points at all. However, if the flow has fixed points, by transversality they have to be in the compact leaves."
- **No theorem on fixed points, trace formulas, periods or transverse measures.** Only: the Introduction's summary of Deninger (p. 142), Remarks 2.5 and 2.7, one sentence on ω inducing a transverse measure when there are no compact leaves (p. 156), Remark 2.44 "foliated trace formulas … are known only in the transverse case" (p. 156), and the Reeb-flow construction Lemma 4.2 / Cor. 4.3 (p. 180). The word "period" does not occur.
- **Relation to Deninger:** "extending the work of Deninger" (p. 142) but diverging on three printed points: manifolds not foliated spaces (Rem. 2.7); compact leaves, not fixed points, as infinite primes (Rem. 2.5); boundary leaves for real places instead of Deninger's orbifold embedding (Rem. 2.5). Cites Deninger [8]–[14], Deninger–Singhof [15] (= round-4 item 5) and [16]; bibliography entries transcribed in the report.
- **Verdict on N-A, N-D, N-F, N-G: nothing moves.** No printed finite-generation/rank statement, no transverse-measure theorem, no compact-leaf/fixed-point analysis. **Two corrections for the next program session (NOT enacted):** (1) the list's and the adjudication's description "compact non-transverse leaves *carrying fixed points*" is wrong for Kopei — the class is defined without fixed points; the N-F contrast sentence must be weakened to "fixed points, if any, lie in compact leaves". (2) Agent's inference: Kopei's own Reeb example (Lemma 4.2) has a compact torus leaf with a fixed-point-free flow for α+β ≠ 0 (an instance of N-F's arrangement) and length group (1/α)ℤ (consistent with N-G) — unremarked by Kopei.
