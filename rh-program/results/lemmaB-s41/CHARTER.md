## 1. The target
**Theorem 1.6 (proved, dual-read).** A Beurling system with R(u) := N(u) − ρu ≥ r₀ > 0 for all u ≥ 1 and R(u) = O(u^θ), θ < r₀/(r₀ + ρ), has a real zero of ζ_P in [r₀/(r₀ + ρ), 1); so α ≥ r₀/(r₀ + ρ). Discreteness is not used.
**Conjecture U (the program's own):** every DISCRETE Beurling system has α ≤ max{½, 2β}. It is refuted by ANY discrete system with
  (A) N(u) − ρu ≥ r₀ for all u ≥ 1, with r₀ > ρ; and (B) N(u) − ρu = O(u^θ) for some θ < ½·r₀/(r₀ + ρ).
S8(ρ), ρ < ¼, has (A) by construction (r₀ = ½ − ρ); (B) is **Lemma B_ρ** (needed: θ < ½ − ρ; numerically E ≈ 0.05 log²x at ρ = π/16 to 10¹¹). NOT proved. Not even E = o(x) is proved.

## 2. What the orchestrator worked out today (use it; check it)
**(a) Lindley form.** Lattice points x_k = 1 + (k − ½)/ρ, k ≥ 1. Let c_k = number of composites in (x_{k−1}, x_k] and e_k = N(x_k) − (k + 1) ≥ 0 after the decision at x_k. Then e_k = max(e_{k−1} + c_k − 1, 0), and a g-prime is placed at x_k exactly when e_{k−1} = 0 and c_k = 0. S8 is a single-server queue with one service per lattice step; composites are the arrivals; g-primes are the idle steps. E = e_k up to 1.
**(b) Price of the direct route.** By Prop. 2.1, B_ρ is a statement of the strength "every interval [x, x + x^θ] contains a g-prime, θ < ½ − ρ". For a zeta function with a zero at real part ≥ 1 − 2ρ > ½, zero-density methods give gaps x^{1−1/A+ε} with N(σ, T) ≪ T^{A(1−σ)}; even A = 2 gives only ½. So no bootstrap through zero-density estimates can reach B_ρ for S8 as it stands. Route (iii) of the queue is priced out unless the needed exponent is raised (see (d)).
**(c) Why random discretization is not enough.** Generic or randomly discretized systems have integer error x^{½+o(1)} (Poisson-like local statistics of g-integers); U needs θ < ½. A sub-square-root integer error needs either additive structure (ℕ) or feedback (the greedy rule). The measured arrivals of S8 are sub-Poissonian.
**(d) Design freedom.** Theorem 1.6 holds for ANY never-undershooting system. Threshold τ → 0 gives r₀ = 1 − ρ − τ: the needed exponent rises toward θ < (1 − ρ)/2. Placing a g-prime EARLY never breaks (A). The positions of g-primes need not be on one lattice. The rule may change with scale. Composites up to p₁·x are known when the g-primes below x are placed, so a rule may look ahead.
