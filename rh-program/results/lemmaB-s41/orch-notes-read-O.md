## 1. O1: Theorem 1.6 with a mean-square bound. Verdict: ✓ (the closing sentence is corrected in §4)

Setting as in O1. P is any Beurling system (N nondecreasing, right-continuous, unit jumps) and E(u) := N(u) − ρ(u − 1) − 1 ≥ −c with c ∈ [0, 1). Hypothesis (M): ∫_X^{2X} E² du ≤ K·X^{1+2θ₂} for all X ≥ 1, with θ₂ < 1.

**1.1 Dyadic Cauchy–Schwarz [proved here] ✓.** Take σ > −1. Then u^{−σ−1} ≤ X^{−σ−1} on [X, 2X], so ∫_X^{2X}|E|u^{−σ−1}du ≤ X^{−σ−1}·X^{1/2}·(K X^{1+2θ₂})^{1/2} = K^{1/2}X^{θ₂−σ}. Summing over X = 2^j gives ∫_1^∞|E|u^{−σ−1}du ≤ K^{1/2}Σ_j 2^{j(θ₂−σ)} < ∞ for σ > θ₂, uniformly on σ ≥ θ₂ + ε. This matches O1's display.

**1.2 Continuation [proved here] ✓.** (i) N(X) = O(X): N is nondecreasing, so N(X) ≤ X^{−1}∫_X^{2X}N ≤ 2ρX + 1 + X^{−1/2}(K X^{1+2θ₂})^{1/2} = O(X). Hence, for Re s > 1, ζ_P(s) = ∫_{1−}^∞u^{−s}dN = s∫_1^∞N u^{−s−1}du (the boundary term N(U)U^{−s} tends to 0). Also s∫_1^∞(ρ(u − 1) + 1)u^{−s−1}du = ρs/(s − 1) − ρ + 1 = (s − 1 + ρ)/(s − 1) = ζ_c(s), which is Lemma 1.5. So ζ_P = ζ_c + sÊ for Re s > 1. (ii) By 1.1, Ê(s) = ∫_1^∞E u^{−s−1}du converges absolutely and locally uniformly on Re s > θ₂, so it is analytic there. The right side is therefore the continuation of ζ_P to Re s > θ₂: meromorphic, with its only pole the simple pole at s = 1 (residue ρ). (iii) θ₂ ≥ 0 automatically, as in Theorem 1.6. N is integer-valued, so E(u) ≡ −ρ(u − 1) (mod 1), hence |E(u)| ≥ ‖ρ(u − 1)‖ (distance to ℤ), and ∫_X^{2X}E² ≥ X/12 − O(1/ρ).

**1.3 The one-sided inequality [proved here] ✓.** For σ ∈ (θ₂, 1) with σ > 0: ζ_P(σ) = 1 − ρ/(1 − σ) + σ∫_1^∞E u^{−σ−1}du ≥ 1 − ρ/(1 − σ) − cσ∫_1^∞u^{−σ−1}du = 1 − c − ρ/(1 − σ). The right side vanishes at σ₀ = 1 − ρ/(1 − c). The inequality is strict because E > −c off a countable set: E decreases strictly between jumps.

**1.4 The real zero [proved here] ✓.** Fix σ₁ ∈ (θ₂, 1). The function |E|u^{−σ₁−1} dominates on [σ₁, 1], so Ê(σ) → Ê(1), which is finite, as σ → 1⁻, while ζ_c(σ) = 1 − ρ/(1 − σ) → −∞. ζ_P is real and continuous on [σ₀, 1) when σ₀ > θ₂, and ζ_P(σ₀) > 0. The intermediate value theorem then gives a zero σ* ∈ (σ₀, 1).

**1.5 The last step: a zero at σ* forbids ψ_P(x) − x = O(x^a), a < σ* [proved here] ✓.** For Re s > 1, −ζ′_P/ζ_P(s) = s∫_1^∞ψ_P u^{−s−1}du, which converges absolutely because ψ_P(u) ≤ N(u) log u = O(u log u). Suppose ψ_P(u) − u = O(u^a) with a < σ*. Then H(s) := s∫_1^∞(ψ_P − u)u^{−s−1}du is analytic on Re s > a, and −ζ′_P/ζ_P = s/(s − 1) + H on Re s > 1. Let Ω := {Re s > max(a, θ₂)} minus the zeros of ζ_P and the point 1. The zero set is discrete because ζ_P ≢ 0 is meromorphic, so Ω is open and connected. Both sides are analytic on Ω and agree on Re s > 1, so they agree on Ω (identity theorem). Near σ* (σ* > max(a, θ₂), σ* ≠ 1), the left side is −m/(s − σ*) + (analytic), where m ≥ 1 is the order of the zero, while the right side is analytic at σ*. This is a contradiction. So α ≥ σ*. The step uses only the meromorphic continuation of ζ_P to Re s > θ₂ and the zero. No pointwise bound on E enters, so it holds verbatim under (M).

**1.6 For S8 [proved here] ✓.** c = ½ (Lemma 1.1, E > −½) gives σ₀ = 1 − 2ρ. A mean-square exponent θ₂ < 1 − 2ρ therefore gives a real zero in (1 − 2ρ, 1) and α > 1 − 2ρ, as O1 states. The finite certificates of Cor. 1.7(iii) also survive: the tail σ₁∫_X^∞E u^{−σ₁−1}du ≥ −½X^{−σ₁} uses only E > −½ and convergence (θ₂ < σ₁). So ζ_P(0.79) > 0 for π/16 (F_{10⁷}(0.79) = +0.022231) and ζ_P(0.89) > 0 for π/32 (F_{10⁶}(0.89) = +0.0434) hold under (M) [quoted: theory NOTE §0, §3.0].

**1.7 The mean-square form of Conjecture U, exactly, and what O1's consequence is worth [proved here].**
Definitions. β(P) := inf{θ : E = O(x^θ)} and β₂(P) := inf{θ₂ : (M) holds}. α is as in U (the exponent of ψ_P(x) − x). Since ∫_X^{2X}E² ≤ X·sup_{[X,2X]}E², we have β₂ ≤ β.
**U₂ (mean-square form):** every discrete Beurling system has α ≤ max{½, 2β₂}. Because max{½, 2β₂} ≤ max{½, 2β}, **U₂ ⇒ U**: U₂ is the stronger conjecture. O1's consequence, that θ₂ < ½ − ρ (ρ ≤ ¼) refutes U₂, is correct: α > 1 − 2ρ ≥ max{½, 2θ₂} ≥ max{½, 2β₂}. ✓ But refuting U₂ leaves U standing, so a mean-square bound at θ₂ < ½ − ρ is NOT a route to refuting U. O1 does not claim it is. Its closing sentence calls the mean-square bound a "target", and that word needs this qualification (§4, pair 1).
**Lemma L (one-sided Lipschitz: L² controls L^∞; any Beurling system) [proved here].** E(y) − E(x) = (N(y) − N(x)) − ρ(y − x) ≥ −ρ(y − x) for y ≥ x. If (M) holds, then for X ≥ X₀: sup_{[X,2X]}|E| ≤ (8ρK′)^{1/3}X^{(1+2θ₂)/3}, with K′ := 9K (from ∫_X^{4X}E² ≤ K(1 + 2^{1+2θ₂})X^{1+2θ₂} and ∫_{X/2}^{2X}E² ≤ 2K X^{1+2θ₂}). Hence **β ≤ (1 + 2β₂)/3**.
*Proof.* Let x ∈ [X, 2X] and E(x) = M > 0. For u ∈ [x, x + M/(2ρ)], E(u) ≥ M − ρ(u − x) ≥ M/2.
Case M ≤ 4ρX: the window lies in [X, 4X], so ∫_X^{4X}E² ≥ (M/2)²·M/(2ρ) = M³/(8ρ). By (M) on [X, 2X] and [2X, 4X], ∫_X^{4X}E² ≤ K′X^{1+2θ₂}.
Case M > 4ρX: then E ≥ M − 2ρX > M/2 on [x, x + 2X] ⊂ [X, 4X], so ∫_X^{4X}E² ≥ M²X/2 > 8ρ²X³. This contradicts (M) for X ≥ X₀, since θ₂ < 1.
If instead E(x) = −M < 0, use the window [x − M/(2ρ), x], on which E ≤ −M/2, and argue the same way. ∎ (For S8, E > −½, so only the first case is needed.)
**Consequence: a mean-square bound refutes U itself below an explicit threshold [proved here, from Lemma L, §1.6 and §1.5].** Let ζ_P(σ₁) > 0 at some σ₁ ∈ (θ₂, 1) with σ₁ ≥ ½. Then α > σ₁. If moreover θ₂ < (3σ₁ − 2)/4, Lemma L gives β ≤ (1 + 2θ₂)/3 < σ₁/2, so max{½, 2β} < σ₁ < α, and **U is false.**
Admissible σ₁: (a) any σ₁ < 1 − 2ρ, by §1.3. This gives the threshold θ₂ < (1 − 6ρ)/4, which is nonempty for ρ < 1/6: 0.1027 for π/32, nothing for π/16. (b) The certified points of §1.6: θ₂ < 0.0925 for S8(π/16) (σ₁ = 0.79) and θ₂ < 0.1675 for S8(π/32) (σ₁ = 0.89).
For comparison, O7 measures rms E ≈ 0.09·log x for π/16 (a mean-square exponent of 0 on the observed range) [quoted: ORCH-NOTES O7]. A mean-square target therefore exists that bears on U, but its threshold is about a quarter of the pointwise one: 0.0925 against 0.395. In logical strength it is a sufficient condition for Lemma B in its certified form, not a weaker target. Its use is methodological: L²/Parseval tools apply to it (see §5 item A2).

**1.8 O1's closing sentence is FALSE as stated [proved here].** By Prop. 2.1(i)–(ii), on [p_k, p_{k+1}): E(x) = ½ + C(p_k, x] − ρ(x − p_k) ≤ ½ + (ρg_k − 1) − ρ(x − p_k) = ρ(p_{k+1} − x) − ½. Hence ∫_{p_k}^{p_{k+1}}(E⁺)² ≤ ρ²g_k³/3. With E > −½, this gives
  ∫_X^{2X}E² ≤ (ρ²/3)·Σ g_k³ + X/4 (the sum runs over the gaps that meet [X, 2X]).
So what implies a mean-square bound is a **third-moment bound on g-prime gaps**, Σ g_k³ ≪ X^{1+2θ₂}. That bound implies "almost all intervals of length X^{θ₂+ε} contain a g-prime", because Σ_{g_k>h} g_k ≤ h^{−2}Σ g_k³ = o(X). The converse fails. A single gap of length X^{0.9} in [X, 2X] changes the measure of bad x only by O(X^{0.9}) = o(X), yet it adds X^{2.7} to the third moment. A mean-square bound on E also implies nothing about gaps (Prop. 2.1's own remark: composites spread over a gap keep E small). So the "almost all" statement is neither sufficient for the mean-square bound nor implied by it. The correct description is: an averaged gap statement (third moment), stronger than "almost all" and weaker than "every gap" (G ≪ X^θ gives Σ g_k³ ≤ G²·2X ≪ X^{1+2θ}).

## 2. O2: hypothesis (A) weakened to Mellin-average positivity at one point. Verdict: ✓ (correct as stated)

**2.1 Re-derivation [proved here].** Write R(u) := N(u) − ρu = 1 − ρ + E(u). For Re s > 1, ζ_P(s) = s∫_1^∞(ρu + R)u^{−s−1}du = ρs/(s − 1) + s∫_1^∞R u^{−s−1}du (theory NOTE §2.2). Under (B), or under (M) of §1, the integral ∫_1^∞R u^{−s−1}du = (1 − ρ)/s + Ê(s) converges for Re s > max(0, θ). At real σ₁ ∈ (θ, 1):
  ζ_P(σ₁) = −ρσ₁/(1 − σ₁) + σ₁∫_1^∞R(u)u^{−σ₁−1}du,
so **ζ_P(σ₁) > 0 ⟺ σ₁∫_1^∞R u^{−σ₁−1}du > ρσ₁/(1 − σ₁)**, which is O2's condition, exactly. With ζ_P(σ) → −∞ as σ → 1⁻ (§1.4) and continuity on [σ₁, 1), there is a real zero σ* ∈ (σ₁, 1), and α ≥ σ* > σ₁ by §1.5. Theorem 1.6 uses (A) only to make this quantity ≥ 0 at σ₀; one point suffices, and the location of the zero then becomes "(σ₁, 1)" instead of the explicit "(σ₀, 1)". O2 is correct. The weight σ₁u^{−σ₁−1}du is a probability measure on [1, ∞), so "Mellin average" is accurate. The conclusion "undershoot on a thin set is allowed" is correct and understates what is allowed, since only the average matters.
**2.2 What O2 is, and a usable quantitative form [proved here].** O2 is the statement "ζ_P(σ₁) > 0" rewritten. It is the principle of Cor. 1.7(iii) (a finite certificate decides the sign), not new information. The weight puts mass 1 − U^{−σ₁} on [1, U] (0.42 on [1, 2] and 0.84 on [1, 10] at σ₁ = 0.79), so the condition is decided mostly by the start of the system. A form a design unit can use: if R ≥ r₀ on [1, U] and R ≥ −r₁ on [U, ∞), then the average is ≥ r₀(1 − U^{−σ₁}) − r₁U^{−σ₁}. So a real zero in (σ₁, 1) is forced as soon as r₀(1 − U^{−σ₁}) − r₁U^{−σ₁} > ρσ₁/(1 − σ₁), together with the continuation ((B) or (M) with exponent < σ₁). A rule may therefore undershoot by a bounded amount at all large scales. O2 does not touch the hard half, Lemma B (the upper bound that gives the continuation).

**3.2 The step marked [recalled]: zero density ⇒ g-primes in short intervals. Verdict: NOT valid as recalled for a Beurling ζ_P with only Landau's region.**

*Against the recalled form* [quoted]: "a zero-density estimate such as (1.1) alone does not suffice to obtain the PNT in short intervals for any λ < 1" (p. 4, l. 169–170). Prop. 4.2 (p. 13, l. 784–789): for every θ ∈ (½, 1) there is a θ-well-behaved system with lim sup > 1 and lim inf < 1 of (ψ(x_K + h_K) − ψ(x_K))/h_K, h_K ≍ x_K^λ, for every λ ∈ [4/5, 1), and hence (l. 798–799) for every λ < 1. *Mechanism [proved here, from Thm 4.1].* Take T = x^a. Each zero contributes |((x + h)^ρ − x^ρ)/ρ| ≤ h·x^{β−1}(1 + o(1)), and Σ_{β≥b} x^{β−1} = x^{b−1}N(b, x^a) + log x ∫_b^1 x^{σ−1}N(σ, x^a)dσ (BD (4.2), l. 836–844). With N(σ, T) ≪ T^{A(1−σ)}(log T)^L and aA < 1, the integral is ≪ (log x)^L·x^{−(1−aA)η} at the edge 1 − η of the zero-free region. Under Landau's region, η ≍ 1/log T, so x^{−(1−aA)η} is a CONSTANT, and the log^L factor is not beaten. The zeros near the 1-line can then contribute ≍ h. This is exactly the obstruction behind Prop. 4.2.
*The real zero is harmless [proved here].* σ* is fixed and lies in (1 − 2ρ, 1). Its term is −((x + h)^{σ*} − x^{σ*})/σ* = −h·x^{σ*−1}(1 + o(1)) = o(h), and it adds 1 to N(σ, T) for σ ≤ σ*, which every bound T^{A(1−σ)} allows. The charter's §2(b) wording ("For a zeta function with a zero at real part ≥ 1 − 2ρ > ½, zero-density methods give …") misplaces the difficulty. The obstruction is the lack of a zero-free region near Re s = 1, not the real zero.
*For S8 specifically.* Under (B) only Landau's region σ > 1 − C(1 − θ)/log|t| is known. DMV showed that it cannot be improved in general for θ ∈ (½, 1) [quoted: BD p. 2, l. 86–89, "for θ ∈ (1/2, 1) this zero-free region was shown to be optimal, apart from the precise value of d"]. For θ ≤ ½ optimality is open (Diamond–Zhang p. 196, as quoted in theory NOTE §5). No Littlewood-type region and no log-free density estimate is known for S8. **So the passage gives nothing for S8 as it stands: no λ < 1 at all.** The recalled exponents hold only in the best hypothetical case: a Littlewood-type region with an arbitrarily large constant (BD Cor. 4.4, p. 15, l. 867–877, "λ > (θ + 3)/4"; this is 1 − 1/A with A = 4/(1 − θ)). The almost-all form is not in BD at all. For ℕ, the exponent 1 − 2/A is classical [recalled, unverified].

**3.3 The correct exponent map, pointwise [proved here from the quoted statements].** The input is E = O(x^θ). The output is θ′ := inf{λ : every interval [x, x + x^λ] contains a g-prime for large x}, converted by Prop. 2.1 (E ≤ ρG − ½) into an integer exponent. Then:
- *S8 as known* (Landau's region; density bounds with log factors): **no output**. Neither Thm 4.3(1) nor 4.3(2) applies, so θ′ = 1.
- *If a Littlewood-type region with constant d₁ were known*: θ′ = max{b, 1 − d₁(1 − θ)/(4d₁ + 9(1 − θ))} + ε, with b ≥ (2 + θ)/3 (BD; b ≥ (1 + θ)/2 with Broucke's Thm 1.2). As d₁ → ∞ this is **θ′ = (3 + θ)/4**, O8's map with c = 4 (BD Cor. 4.4).
- *If a log-free density bound ≤ KT^{A(1−α)} were known* (Landau region constant d₂): the Chebyshev form gives θ′ = max{b, 1 − d₂/(Ad₂ + max{log 2K, Ad₂})} ≥ 1 − 1/(2A) = 1 − (1 − θ)/(2c), which is **(7 + θ)/8 for c = 4**. This is worse than O8's map.
- *In every case* θ′ > b > θ. **The output exponent always exceeds the input exponent, for every density function A(σ).** The cause is the error term O_b(x^b), b ∈ (θ, 1), in the only printed explicit formula: intervals shorter than x^b cannot be reached. In general ζ_P is not known to continue to the left of Re s = θ, which is why b > θ.
*O8's fixed-point sentence.* The map f(θ) = 1 − k(1 − θ)/c satisfies 1 − f(θ) = (k/c)(1 − θ). Its fixed points are θ = 1 alone when c ≠ k, and every θ when c = k. The intended content, "f(θ) < θ iff c < k", is true for the bare map. The cap removes it: with the cap the output is max{b, f(θ)} > θ, even where f(θ) < θ. The escape "c < k" is not idle: Broucke's Thm 5.2 gives c → 8B near σ = 1 under a subconvexity bound B, and Cor. 5.3 gives T^ε zeros right of (3 + θ)/4 under Lindelöf. ## 5. Additions

**A1. Lemma L and a mean-square threshold for U itself** (§1.7) [proved here]. For every Beurling system, β ≤ (1 + 2β₂)/3, from the one-sided slope E(y) − E(x) ≥ −ρ(y − x). For S8 this gives: θ₂ < 0.0925 (π/16) or θ₂ < 0.1675 (π/32), together with the certified ζ_P(0.79) > 0 and ζ_P(0.89) > 0, refutes U. The bound with general p: β ≤ (1 + pβ_p)/(p + 1) for the L^p exponent β_p, by the same window argument.
**A2. The mean-square target as an L² statement on ζ_P [proved here, except the converse].** For σ > θ₂, set u = e^v. Then E(e^v)e^{−σv} lies in L¹ ∩ L²(0, ∞) (by §1.1 and the dyadic bound), so Plancherel gives
  ∫_{−∞}^{∞}|ζ_P(σ + it) − ζ_c(σ + it)|²·|σ + it|^{−2}dt = 2π∫_1^∞E(u)²u^{−2σ−1}du.
Conversely, finiteness of the right side at σ gives (M) with θ₂ = σ, since ∫_X^{2X}E² ≤ (2X)^{2σ+1}·(right side). So β₂ = inf{σ : the right side is finite}. The converse in analytic form (a continuation of ζ_P to Re s > σ₀ with uniformly bounded line integrals forces ∫E²u^{−2σ₀−1} < ∞) is Paley–Wiener for H² of a half-plane [recalled, unverified; not load-bearing]. Lemma B₂ is therefore a statement about ζ_P itself, not about 1/ζ_P: its zeros, the real one included, play no role. It is the quantity in Broucke's moment hypotheses (Thm 5.1, p. 12), taken on lines to the left of the continuation that a bound on E provides. A unit with an independent handle on ζ_P (an identity the greedy rule imposes on ζ_P across scales) would attack this, not the primes.
**A3. Charter §2(b)** carries the same unqualified passage, and two of its wordings need the same repair. (i) "B_ρ is a statement of the strength 'every interval [x, x + x^θ] contains a g-prime'": by Prop. 2.1, G_ρ implies B_ρ, not conversely (Prop. 2.1's own remark). (ii) "For a zeta function with a zero at real part ≥ 1 − 2ρ > ½, zero-density methods give …": the real zero is harmless (§3.2), and the obstruction is the missing zero-free region near Re s = 1 together with the cap b > θ.
**A4. Where the pricing sits relative to print.** Broucke–Debruyne (p. 15) ask whether every θ-system has Chebyshev bounds in intervals x^λ for some λ < 1. Lemma G_ρ for S8 (every gap ≤ x^θ with θ < ½ − ρ) asks less in kind (existence, not ≍ h) but far more in scale. No printed method reaches it for any θ-system with θ > 0 and only Landau's region [a reading of BD p. 15 and of the sources in §3.1, not a survey].
