## §2 The global class 𝒦_L(π) and the gap trichotomy (H2) — the literature at the page, the count, and what is proved / counted / inferred

**Verdict on H2: (i) REFUTED as stated; (ii)–(iii) REPRICED.** The gap theorem the brief names does not pin anything on supports of Riemann–von Mangoldt density, because the density it uses is a global Beurling–Malliavin-type interior density (with an energy condition) of the support of a FINITE measure, and for supports whose local density grows like log τ that characteristic is infinite. What survives of the trichotomy is a stratification of the strip BY HEIGHT at fixed L, given by a count, with the "pinned" side a count and not a theorem, and the "free" side exhibited numerically in a window (§1.4) but not constructed exactly.

### §2.1 Poltoratski, "Spectral gaps for sets and measures", arXiv:0908.2079v2 (Acta Math. 208 (2012) 151–209) — read at the page (arXiv PDF, 43 pp., SHA-256 378856e9…; page numbers of the arXiv version)

* **The object (§2, eq. (2.1), p. 5).** "Let M be a set of all finite Borel complex measures on the real line. If X is a closed subset of the real line denote G_X = sup{a | ∃ μ ∈ M, μ ≢ 0, supp μ ⊂ X, such that μ̂ = 0 on [0, a]}." Proposition 1 (p. 5): G_μ = G_{supp μ} — the gap characteristic depends on the support only. **Finite complex measures.** `[read at the page]`
* **The density (§5 "Main definition", pp. 8–9).** "Let Λ = {λ_n} be a sequence of real points. We write C_Λ ≥ a if there exists a short partition {I_n} such that Δ_n ≥ a|I_n| for all n (density condition) (5.3) and Σ_n[Δ_n² log|I_n| − E_n]/(1 + dist²(0, I_n)) < ∞ (energy condition) (5.4), where Δ_n = #(Λ ∩ I_n) and E_n = E(Λ ∩ I_n) = Σ_{λ_k,λ_l∈I_n, λ_k≠λ_l} log|λ_k − λ_l|. If X is a closed subset of ℝ we put C_X = sup{a | there exists a sequence Λ ⊂ X such that C_Λ ≥ a} (5.5)." A partition is short if |I_n| → ∞ and Σ|I_n|²/(1 + dist²(0, I_n)) < ∞ (p. 8). Remark 6 (p. 9): "The density condition says simply that the lower (interior) density of the sequence in the sense of Beurling and Malliavin, is at least a" (four equivalent definitions d₁–d₄ listed, pp. 9–10; "A closely related notion of exterior density appears in the Beurling-Malliavin Theorem on completeness of exponential functions in L² on an interval, see [5] or [21]"). Example 1 (p. 10): for separated sequences (|λ_n − λ_{n+1}| > δ > 0) "the energy condition disappears" and the gap characteristic equals the interior density (printed "G_Λ = d_i(Λ)"; Theorem 2 and Lemma 2 carry the factor 2π — the normalization is immaterial below, where everything is infinite). `[read at the page]`
* **The theorem (§6, Theorem 2, p. 10).** "G_X = 2πC_X." `[read at the page]`
* **The Beurling–Malliavin side (§7, p. 32).** "If Λ is a real sequence we define its Beurling-Malliavin density as d_BM(Λ) = sup{d | ∃ long {I_n} such that #Λ ∩ I_n ≥ d|I_n| ∀n} if Λ is discrete and as ∞ otherwise." Lemma 1: G_{X∪Λ} ≤ G_X + 2πd_BM(Λ). Lemma 2 ("a version of the first Beurling-Malliavin theorem"): if there is a long sequence of intervals with #(Λ ∩ I_n) ≤ a|I_n| for all n then G_Λ ≤ 2πa. Intro (p. 2): "the Beurling-Malliavin problem is equivalent to the problem of triviality of the kernel of a Toeplitz operator with the symbol φ = exp(−iax)θ … whereas the gap problem is equivalent to the triviality of the kernel of the Toeplitz operator with the symbol φ̄ = exp(iax)θ̄". `[read at the page]`

### §2.2 What the theorem gives for the honest class — and what it does not

Let ν₁, ν₂ ∈ 𝒦_L(π) and μ := ν₁ − ν₂, a signed integer-atomic measure with μ̂ = E_{ν₁} − E_{ν₂} = 0 on (−L, L) (§0.3): a spectral gap of length 2L, symmetric. Its support X ⊂ Z₁ ∪ Z₂ has local density ≤ 2C₁log(3 + |x|) (axiom RvM), and for configurations carrying the archimedean density (every element of the class, by F1(a)) the local density is ≍ (1/2π)log|x| → ∞.

**(P1) The gap theorem gives no upper bound on gaps for such supports; it gives the opposite.** `[derivation from the printed definitions]` Let X be the support of any configuration whose counting function satisfies N_X(R) ≥ (1 − o(1))(R/2π)log R (ζ's zeros, or any element of the class). For any a > 0 choose a subsequence Λ_a ⊂ X that is separated with spacing in [1/(2a), 1/a] beyond some height R_a (possible because the mean gap 2π/log R → 0, with the finitely many exceptional stretches absorbed by taking |I_n| → ∞ — the count in I_n is (|I_n|/2π)log R_n(1 + o(1)) ≫ a|I_n|); take the short partition |I_n| = √(dist(0, I_n)) beyond ±R_a and one initial interval on each side of 0 long enough that its count exceeds a times its length (N_X(R) ≥ 2aR once log R ≥ 4πa + o(1)). Then (5.3) holds for all n, and (5.4) holds because Λ_a is separated (Example 1, as printed). Hence C_Λa ≥ a, C_X = ∞, and by Theorem 2 **G_X = ∞: the support of every element of the class carries finite complex measures with arbitrarily long spectral gaps.** The brief's H2(i) mechanism — "the union of two RvM configurations has density ≤ ℓ/π per unit τ at height T, so a nonzero μ with gap (−αℓ, αℓ) needs a gap longer than the gap theorem allows" — inserts a LOCAL density at ONE height into a theorem whose density is a global supremum over subsequences on partitions going to infinity; the theorem's density for these supports is infinite. **H2(i) is refuted as an application of Poltoratski's theorem.** (Lemma 2, the first-BM-type bound, needs a LONG sequence of intervals on which the count is ≤ a|I_n|; for these supports no such a exists.)

**(P2) The theorem does not cover μ either way.** μ is an infinite tempered measure with integer weights, not a finite complex measure; the measures Theorem 2 produces on X have complex weights in ℓ¹ (or L¹(|σ|)) and say nothing about whether an integer-weighted, positivity-constrained difference ν₁ − ν₂ with the RvM bound can have a gap (−L, L). Poltoratski's Proposition 1 makes the finite-measure gap problem a property of the support; the class's problem is a property of the WEIGHTS (integers, ν_i ≥ 0) and of the growth. **No statement in the paper decides it.** `[read at the page]` — the absence is the Grossmann condition (zoo V.5): it is not evidence that the class is a singleton and not evidence that it is large.

**(P3) Beurling–Malliavin's completeness radius: same conclusion, other side.** The completeness radius R(Λ) of {e^{iλu}}_{λ∈Λ} in L²(−a, a) is π times a BM exterior density (the first BM theorem; the precise statement is `[recalled, unverified]` at the page — Koosis, *The Logarithmic Integral* II, and Havin–Jöricke are not on disk; what is on disk is Poltoratski's §7 definition of d_BM, his Remark 6 pointing to the exterior density, and his Lemma 2 in the gap normalization). Completeness in L²(−a, a) is the statement that no nonzero f ∈ L²(−a, a) has f̂ vanishing on all of Λ — the annihilator side, dual (Poltoratski's intro: the two Toeplitz symbols e^{−iax}θ and e^{iax}θ̄) to the gap side. For Λ of density → ∞ the radius is +∞ (ζ's zeros are a uniqueness set for every Paley–Wiener space, `[recalled, unverified]` as a printed sentence; it follows from d_BM = ∞ and the first BM theorem once that theorem is opened). Neither characteristic is finite; neither pins.

**(P4) The Landau–Pollak–Slepian count.** `[recalled, unverified]` at the page (Slepian–Pollak 1961, Landau–Pollak 1961/62, Bell System Tech. J. — not on disk); used here only as a COUNT, in the form: a function on a window of length W band-limited to (−L, L) has ≈ 2L·W/(2π) = LW/π real degrees of freedom (the prolate-spheroidal eigenvalues are ≈ 1 up to that index and then decay exponentially). Its finite-window instance was MEASURED in §1.4 (`[computed]`): the fit's transition from 10⁻⁹-relative to 10⁻²-relative residual sits at count ≈ number of free positions, and the V.4 control switches from "other positions" to "exact recovery" between count 25 and count 37 for 40 positions.
