## §2 The global class 𝒦_L(π) and the gap trichotomy (H2) — the literature at the page, the count, and what is proved / counted / inferred

**Verdict on H2: (i) REFUTED as stated; (ii)–(iii) REPRICED.** The gap theorem the brief names does not pin anything on supports of Riemann–von Mangoldt density, because the density it uses is a global Beurling–Malliavin-type interior density (with an energy condition) of the support of a FINITE measure, and for supports whose local density grows like log τ that characteristic is infinite. ### §2.1 Poltoratski, "Spectral gaps for sets and measures", arXiv:0908.2079v2 (Acta Math. 208 (2012) 151–209) — read at the page (arXiv PDF, 43 pp., SHA-256 378856e9…; page numbers of the arXiv version)

* **The density (§5 "Main definition", pp. 8–9).** "Let Λ = {λ_n} be a sequence of real points. ### §2.2 What the theorem gives for the honest class — and what it does not

Let ν₁, ν₂ ∈ 𝒦_L(π) and μ := ν₁ − ν₂, a signed integer-atomic measure with μ̂ = E_{ν₁} − E_{ν₂} = 0 on (−L, L) (§0.3): a spectral gap of length 2L, symmetric. Its support X ⊂ Z₁ ∪ Z₂ has local density ≤ 2C₁log(3 + |x|) (axiom RvM), and for configurations carrying the archimedean density (every element of the class, by F1(a)) the local density is ≍ (1/2π)log|x| → ∞.

**(P1) The gap theorem gives no upper bound on gaps for such supports; it gives the opposite.** `[derivation from the printed definitions]` Let X be the support of any configuration whose counting function satisfies N_X(R) ≥ (1 − o(1))(R/2π)log R (ζ's zeros, or any element of the class). For any a > 0 choose a subsequence Λ_a ⊂ X that is separated with spacing in [1/(2a), 1/a] beyond some height R_a (possible because the mean gap 2π/log R → 0, with the finitely many exceptional stretches absorbed by taking |I_n| → ∞ — the count in I_n is (|I_n|/2π)log R_n(1 + o(1)) ≫ a|I_n|); take the short partition |I_n| = √(dist(0, I_n)) beyond ±R_a and one initial interval on each side of 0 long enough that its count exceeds a times its length (N_X(R) ≥ 2aR once log R ≥ 4πa + o(1)).
