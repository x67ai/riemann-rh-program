### V.4 The negative-control rule (a falsification test is trusted only after it has fired on a case known to fail and stayed silent on a case known to hold) — NEW, Session 21 (extracted from B2's Phase-4 finding, Session 3)

A test never run on its controls is not a test; it is a hypothesis about a test. A channel that would fire on the positive control is inverted, and a brief whose channel has not been exercised on both controls has no falsification channel at all — whatever its prose says.

Both controls must sit in the detector's visible regime (IV.9): a control run below the visibility threshold proves nothing about the channel. A brief that offers a falsifier without its two control runs is RETURNED, not refuted.
