## §6 Statement (E) — the Kronecker sharpness of the pointwise prime-sum wall — with its proof and its instance numbers

**Setting.** Fix L > 0 and X = e^{L}. By §1, P_X(g_t) = Σ_{n≤X} w_n cos(t log n) with the t-INDEPENDENT real coefficients w_n := 2Λ(n)n^{−1/2}a(log n) (w_n = 0 unless n is a prime power; there is no "t-independent part" of P_X(g_t) to subtract — PRICING §3(b)'s phrase is void, since log n ≠ 0 for every n with Λ(n) ≠ 0). Group by primes: for each prime p ≤ X put φ_p(θ) := Σ_{k≥1, p^k≤X} w_{p^k}cos(kθ), a real trigonometric polynomial of degree ⌊L/log p⌋ (degree 1 for p > √X). Then, with θ_p(t) := t log p,

  P_X(g_t) = Σ_{p≤X} φ_p(θ_p(t)) = Φ(θ(t)),   Φ(θ) := Σ_p φ_p(θ_p) on the torus 𝕋^N = (ℝ/2πℤ)^N, N = π(X).   (6.1)

**Theorem (E)** (Bohr 1913, through the "Bohr's reduction argument" of Weber §3; **Turán 1960**, *Acta Sci. Math. Szeged* 21, 311–318, Lemma p. 313, for the frequencies {log p}, with the effective window e^{17ωN log² N}; **Weber**, *Unif. Distrib. Theory* 4 (2009) 97–116 = arXiv:0806.3990, Theorem 1 and (3.17)–(3.18), in general and effective — Turán and Weber read at the page, §11; the proof below is reproduced for this coefficient system, and the published form is effective where this one is not)**.** Let H ≥ 1. For every T ∈ ℝ,

  sup_{t∈[T,T+H]} P_X(g_t) → Σ_p max_θ φ_p(θ) = sup_𝕋 Φ  and  inf_{t∈[T,T+H]} P_X(g_t) → Σ_p min_θ φ_p(θ) = inf_𝕋 Φ  as H → ∞, uniformly in T;

in particular sup_{t∈ℝ}|P_X(g_t)| = sup_𝕋|Φ| = max(Σ_p max φ_p, Σ_p max(−φ_p)). For primes p > √X, max φ_p = max(−φ_p) = |w_p|; hence sup_t|P_X(g_t)| ≥ Σ_{p>√X}|w_p| + Σ_{p≤√X}max(±φ_p) and ≤ Σ_n|w_n| (the ℓ¹ norm), the gap being the prime-power alignment defect, which is computed below (2.4 % at L = 10, 0.08 % at L = 20).

**Proof** (one and a half pages; the only inputs are unique factorization, Fejér's theorem on the torus and a one-line mean-value computation — Weyl's route to Kronecker's theorem, written out because the "finite version by Dirichlet's simultaneous approximation" of the pricing does not exist as stated: Dirichlet's theorem gives simultaneous approximation of the (tω_p) to INTEGERS, i.e., returns to the origin of the torus, whereas the supremum needs the INHOMOGENEOUS target θ*, which is Kronecker's statement and needs the linear independence; see §11).

*Step 1 (independence).* If Σ_p m_p log p = 0 with integers m_p, then Π_{m_p>0}p^{m_p} = Π_{m_p<0}p^{−m_p}, and unique factorization forces every m_p = 0. So for every m ∈ ℤ^N ∖ {0}, λ_m := Σ_p m_p log p ≠ 0. (Steuding, LNM 1877, p. 17, states the same sentence and Lemma 1.8(i) there is the equidistribution form of what follows.)

*Step 2 (mean values, uniform in T).* For m ≠ 0 and every T, H > 0: |(1/H)∫_T^{T+H}e^{iλ_m t}dt| = |e^{iλ_m H} − 1|/(H|λ_m|) ≤ 2/(H|λ_m|), a bound independent of T.

*Step 3 (Fejér on the torus).* For M ≥ 1 let K_M(x) := Σ_{|k|≤M}(1 − |k|/(M+1))e^{ikx} = (1/(M+1))·(sin((M+1)x/2)/sin(x/2))² ≥ 0 on ℝ/2πℤ, with (1/2π)∫K_M = 1 and, for 0 < ρ ≤ |x| ≤ π, K_M(x) ≤ 1/((M+1)sin²(ρ/2)). Put F_M(x) := Π_{j=1}^N K_M(x_j)/(2π)^N on 𝕋^N: F_M ≥ 0, ∫F_M = 1, and ∫_{max_j|x_j|≥ρ}F_M ≤ Σ_j (1/2π)∫_{|x_j|≥ρ}K_M(x_j)dx_j ≤ N/((M+1)sin²(ρ/2)) → 0 as M → ∞. For a continuous χ on 𝕋^N (uniformly continuous, modulus ω_χ) the convolution σ_Mχ := χ ∗ F_M is a trigonometric polynomial with the same constant term as χ (the constant term of F_M is (2π)^{−N}) and |σ_Mχ(θ) − χ(θ)| ≤ ∫|χ(θ − x) − χ(θ)|F_M(x)dx ≤ ω_χ(ρ) + 2‖χ‖_∞N/((M+1)sin²(ρ/2)); choosing ρ then M makes this < any given η > 0. (This is Fejér's theorem; the four lines are its proof.)

*Step 4 (a trigonometric minorant of a bump at the maximizer).* Φ is continuous on the compact torus; let θ* be a maximizer, S := Φ(θ*) = Σ_p max φ_p (a sum of functions of separate variables is maximized coordinatewise). Given ε > 0 choose ρ₀ > 0 with |Φ(θ) − S| < ε/2 whenever max_p|θ_p − θ*_p| < ρ₀, and let χ(θ) := Π_p max(0, 1 − |θ_p − θ*_p|/ρ₀) (a product of tent functions; continuous, 0 ≤ χ ≤ 1, supported where max_p|θ_p − θ*_p| ≤ ρ₀, with constant term c₀ := (2π)^{−N}∫χ = (ρ₀/2π)^N > 0). By Step 3 choose M with ‖σ_Mχ − χ‖_∞ < c₀/2 and put Q := σ_Mχ − c₀/2. Then Q ≤ χ pointwise, Q is a trigonometric polynomial Σ_{|m_j|≤M}q_m e^{im·θ} with constant term q₀ = c₀ − c₀/2 = c₀/2 > 0, and Q(θ) > 0 forces χ(θ) > 0, i.e., max_p|θ_p − θ*_p| < ρ₀, i.e., Φ(θ) > S − ε/2.

*Step 5 (a good t in every long window).* With θ(t) = (t log p)_p, Q(θ(t)) = Σ_m q_m e^{iλ_m t}. By Step 2, for every T, (1/H)∫_T^{T+H}Q(θ(t))dt ≥ q₀ − Σ_{m≠0}|q_m|·2/(H|λ_m|) ≥ c₀/4 once H ≥ H₀ := (8/c₀)Σ_{m≠0}|q_m|/|λ_m| — a finite sum over |m_j| ≤ M, so H₀ < ∞, and H₀ depends on (ε, the w_n, X) but not on T. Hence for every window [T, T + H] with H ≥ H₀ there is a t in it with Q(θ(t)) > 0, hence with P_X(g_t) = Φ(θ(t)) > S − ε/2. (Measure remark, added in the fix pass: since Q ≤ χ ≤ 1, ∫_T^{T+H}Q(θ(t))dt ≥ (c₀/4)H forces meas{t ∈ [T, T + H] : Q(θ(t)) > 0} ≥ (c₀/4)H, so the heights with P_X(g_t) > S − ε/2 have lower density ≥ c₀/4 > 0 in every window of length ≥ H₀ — positive, not effective, c₀ = (ρ₀/2π)^N; Steuding's Lemma 1.8(i), p. 17, gives the exact density (ρ₀/π)^N of the ρ₀-box over (0, T) as T → ∞, without the window uniformity. ## §8 The conditional-detector paragraph (repair 6): the Lindelöf lock, SHARED; the single-defect regime as a computation

Under a hypothesis H of Lindelöf strength — Z(t) = O_ε(t^ε) (Farmer arXiv:2211.11671v4, §9.3, p. 44, Conjecture 9.7, (9.1)–(9.2), read at the page), equivalently Backlund's form N(σ, T + 1) − N(σ, T) = o_σ(log T) for every σ > ½ (Farmer §9.3, p. 44, Theorem 9.8, (9.4)–(9.5): "for any fixed A > 0 and any fixed-width strip around the critical line, a negligible proportion of the zeros with T ≤ γ ≤ T + A lie outside that strip") — what improves is the **t-AVERAGED** control of the datum: the number of off-line zeros in a unit window is o(log T), so a proportion o(1) of the ZEROS in [T, 2T] lie at depth ≥ δ, and the datum at a height sampled from the zeros sits within the kernel's reach ≈ 5/L of a deep orbit with probability o(1) — the normalization matters (check-O.md §6b): as a Lebesgue proportion of [T, 2T] at FIXED L the statement does not follow, since Backlund's form allows T·o_δ(log T) such zeros in [T, 2T], each capturing a set of heights of measure ≈ 10/L, and (10/L)·o(log T) is unbounded at fixed L; the o(1) is a proportion of the zeros, equivalently the reach 5/L measured in mean spacings 2π/log(t/2π) (the §4 reading "≈ 2 zeros at L = 10 and t = 10⁶") — and the on-line noise N_Z and the off-line contamination are controlled in mean over windows — zero-density territory, the campaign residue's own regime (C2 line 64: "graceful degradation is t-averaging, which collapses back to known density-type territory"; the Lindelöf lock (`directions/A4-lindelof-lock.md`; named at zoo IV.6 and in the §0 protocol item 5(b); the Schatten-3 route to it decided at IV.7's Session-6 closure; the prime-side form is `results/full-map.md` line 203) is the second-order system's name for the same wall at the proven operating point).
