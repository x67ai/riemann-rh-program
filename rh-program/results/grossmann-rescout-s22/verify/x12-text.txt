                                              This paper is motivated by an application to the geometrization of the local Langlands corre-
                                           spondence [FS21], namely to [FS21, Conjecture I.9.5] on the independence of ℓ of the Langlands

                                           generalization, diamonds [Sch17]. Étale cohomology only works well with torsion coefficients prime
                                           to the characteristic, naturally leading to ℓ-adic coefficients for some auxiliary prime ℓ ̸= p.
                                           Grothendieck’s vision was that these different ℓ-adic cohomologies should naturally be shadows
                                           of an underlying “motive”. While a full theory of motives remains elusive, much progress was made
                                           by Voevodsky et. al. through the introduction of a stable ∞-category Dmot (S) of motivic sheaves

on S, for any scheme S, equipped with all six functors [Ayo07]. Having six functors with all usual
properties is sufficient for many applications, although usually one eventually runs into the issue
that the categories Dmot (S) remain inexplicit, even when S is a geometric point. Namely, rationally
morphisms in this category are governed by rational K-theory groups and these remain challeng-
ing to compute, being the subject of the Hodge and Tate conjectures, and also the Beilinson–Soulé
vanishing conjecture. Notably, endowing Dmot (S) with the “motivic t-structure” remains unknown.
   For the applications to local Langlands, one however needs a variant of this theory that applies to
some generalization of rigid-analytic varieties, namely diamonds: Quotients of perfectoid spaces by
pro-étale equivalence relations. Even more generally, the formalism should apply to v-stacks. Such
a theory of motives has been constructed through the works of Ayoub [Ayo15], Vezzani [Vez19]
and Ayoub–Gallauer–Vezzani [AGV22]. Their approach is to handle rigid-analytic varieties via
formal models, eventually reducing to algebraic motives. General v-stacks need to be reduced to
rigid-analytic varieties in turn.
   The purpose of this paper is to observe that one can also build the theory from scratch, in a
language that is very close to the one used to discuss étale cohomology of diamonds [Sch17] – in
particular, making use of very fine Grothendieck topologies like the v-topology, and strictly totally
disconnected spaces. We decided, however, to write the results in the Berkovich setting instead of
the adic setting, for the following reasons:
    (1) The theory works similarly in the archimedean and non-archimedean settings, which can
        be nicely combined in the Berkovich setting.
    (2) The cancellation theorem holds true over any base, making the category of motivic sheaves
        more concrete.
    (3) For many X, the stable ∞-category Dmot (X) of motivic sheaves on X is rigid dualizable.
    (4) We believe that the symmetric monoidal presentable (∞, 2)-category naturally built from
        the 6-functor formalism classifies certain “normed ring stacks” in a suitable sense, and yields
        a nontrivial example of a 2-rigid symmetric monoidal presentable (∞, 2)-category.
   However, as shown in [AGV22], there is a parallel theory for adic spaces.
   Let us now explain our results in some more detail. We work in the category of Banach rings,
which are rings A equipped with a norm | · |A : A → R≥0 such that A is Cauchy-complete. Any
ring A admits the discrete norm |a|A = 1 for a ̸= 0, embedding discrete rings into Banach rings.
For any Banach ring A, Berkovich has defined the Berkovich spectrum M(A), which is a compact
Hausdorff space, classifying maps from A to Banach fields. Here, a Banach field is a Banach ring
(K, | · |K ) such that K is a field and | · |K is multiplicative. There are three types of Banach fields:
    (1) Discrete fields.
    (2) Archimedean Banach fields, which are by Gelfand–Mazur just R or C, with norm a power
        | · |α , α ∈ (0, 1], of the standard norm | · |.
    (3) Nonarchimedean Banach fields (K, | · |K ). These have as ring of integers a rank 1 valuation
        ring OK .
  We sidestep the problem of defining a geometric category of Berkovich spaces and instead directly
endow (the opposite of) the category of Banach rings with a very fine Grothendieck topology, and
will proceed by looking at the corresponding category of stacks. The following notion is analogous
to the notion of v-covers used in [Sch17], but as in Berkovich spaces one only records rank 1
valuations it is closer to the arc-topology defined for schemes in [BM21].

uncountable regular cardinal κ, one can restrict to κ-small Banach rings, meaning those whose
underlying set has cardinality less than κ; and it is easy to see that when increasing κ, the corre-

a small colimit of Marc (A)’s. Equivalently, these are those arc-sheaves that also commute with

                         2. Banach rings and Berkovich spectrum
  We begin by recalling some basic definitions and facts about Banach rings and Berkovich spectra
that we will use. We follow closely Berkovich’s work [Ber90, Chapter 1].
Definition 2.1. A seminormed ring is a commutative unital ring R equipped with a map
                                          | · |R : R → R≥0
satisfying the following properties.
     (i) One has |0|R = 0 and |-1|R ≤ 1.
    (ii) For all x, y ∈ R, one has |xy|R ≤ |x|R |y|R .
   (iii) For all x, y ∈ R, one has |x + y|R ≤ |x|R + |y|R .
   A map of seminormed rings (R, |·|R ) → (S, |·|S ) is a map of commutative unital rings f : R → S
such that |f (x)|S ≤ |x|R for all x ∈ R.
8                                           PETER SCHOLZE

   Any seminorm on R induces a ring topology on R, and we will often implicitly endow R with
this topology.
Definition 2.2. A Banach ring is a seminormed ring that is complete with respect to the induced
topology, i.e. any Cauchy sequence has a unique limit.
  In particular, in a Banach ring (R, | · |R ), the seminorm | · |R is actually a norm, i.e. if |x|R = 0
then x = 0. As usual, the inclusion of Banach rings into seminormed rings has a left adjoint, the
completion.
Remark 2.3. If one applies (ii) to y = 1 one gets |x|R ≤ |1|R |x|R , which shows that if |1|R < 1
then in fact |x|R = 0 for all x ∈ R, and so the completion of R is zero. However, one cannot ask
|1|R = 1 as this would exclude R = 0 from being a Banach ring.

of Banach rings has all colimits.

Definition 2.10. A Banach field is a Banach ring (K, | · |K ) such that K is a field and |xy|K =

Lemma 4.4. Let A be an analytic Banach ring. Then there is a quasi-pro-étale cover A → B such

                                      12. Relation to v-stacks
   In this final section, we comment on the relation between the Berkovich-arc-stacks used in this
paper to the adic v-stacks used in [Sch17] and [FS21].
   To match the setup of [Sch17], we focus on the characteristic p situation, and only work with
perfect analytic Banach rings R over Fp , i.e. perfectoid Fp -algebras equipped with a chosen norm.
We already see that the first difference between the settings is that here we fixed a norm, which we
did not do in [Sch17]. This has the consequence that in the setting of this paper, the point ∗ is a
qcqs arc-sheaf, while in [Sch17] it is not quasiseparated. In the setting of [Sch17], one can however
consider the v-sheaf N parametrizing norms; this is a canonical R>0 -torsor N → ∗ over the point.
We will thus compare the arc-stacks of this paper with the v-stacks over N .
   Now we have a natural forgetful functor (R, R+ ) 7→ R from perfectoid Huber pairs (R, R+ ) with
chosen norm towards Banach-Fp -algebras. This has both adjoints. The right adjoint takes R to
(R, R◦ ) while the left adjoint takes R to (R, Fp^ + R◦◦ ) where Fp^+ R◦◦ ⊂ R◦ denotes the integral
closure of Fp + R .◦◦

   In the following proposition, we ignore the set-theoretic issues, which are resolved as usual by
first fixing a cutoff cardinal κ, and then taking a union over all κ.

   Many naturally occuring v-stacks are partially proper. This includes analyticifications of al-

are virtually never partially proper. Such quasicompact open subsets play a key technical role in

proceed by reduction to compact objects. In the Berkovich setting, compact generation is not true
anymore, but the categories are usually still compactly assembled, i.e. (in the stable setting) dual-

arguments involving reduction to compact morphisms.
