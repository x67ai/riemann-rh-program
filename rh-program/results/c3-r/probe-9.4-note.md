## 2. What the local principle actually says (from the sources)

Setting ([x-03] §§13–14, pp. 78, 89): X₀ = Spec o_{K₀}, K₀ finite over Q_p; X = Spec o_K, K = algebraic closure, G = Gal(K/K₀); coefficients o = a p-adically complete rank-1 valuation ring with algebraically closed quotient field C of characteristic 0 and residue field k of characteristic p (example: o = o_{C_p}). Crucially, **the acting monoid is reduced**: "In the following we only consider the monoid N₀ generated by p i.e. the F_p = ( )^p action" ([x-03] §14 opening, p. 89).

The principle is a three-stage construction on the o-valued points X•(o) = W_rat(X)(o) (triples (x, y, P): a point, a specialization, a multiplicative map — Def. 14.1, p. 90):

- **Stage 1 — continuity:** restrict to X•_c(o), the triples whose maps factor over p-adically continuous P̂_y (Def. 14.2, p. 90).
- **Stage 2 — completion:** pass from X̌_c(o) = colim_{F_p} X•_c(o) to X⋄_c(o) by admitting ALL continuous multiplicative maps on the projective limits, not only those factoring over a projection ([x-03] intro pp. 5–6). Deninger, verbatim: "the process of 'completion' to pass from X̌₀(o) to X⋄₀(o) was necessary to obtain something interesting" (intro p. 6, notation adapted), and — decisive for this probe — "**Incidentally, if we consider points of W_rat(X) with values in rings without 'small multiplicative subgroups' like the complex number field C this process does not give more points**" (intro p. 5).
- **Stage 3 — the selection:** Y⋄_α = the locus in X⋄_c(o) where the additivity defect is small: Y⋄_α = {(x,y,P̂_y) : |P̂_y(r+s) − P̂_y(r) − P̂_y(s)| ≤ α for all r,s in the tilted domain} (Def. 14.12 = (183), p. 99; the uncompleted version Def. 14.5, p. 94). Intro, verbatim: "The answer is simple, Y⋄ consists of all the diagrams in X⋄_c(o) whose maps are not only multiplicative but mod p also additive" (p. 6, discovered via the question of which points carry an action of A_inf(X) = W_p(Γ(X,O)^∧♭)).

Structural features consumed below:

- **(F1) The bound is in the target's intrinsic valuation.** For a rank-1 valuation ring the valuation is definable from the ring structure (divisibility), so every ring automorphism preserves it; Aut(o)-equivariance costs nothing.
- **(F2) Frobenius self-improvement normalizes the constant.** F_p(Y̌_α) = Y̌_α and Y̌_α ⊆ Y̌_{1/p} for every 0 < α < 1, hence Y̌_α = Y̌_{1/p} for α ≥ 1/p (Prop. 14.7, p. 94, proof read: the p^ν-power estimate is ultrametric, |(defect)^{p^ν} + pc| ≤ max(α^{p^ν}, 1/p)); same for Y⋄ (Prop. 14.13, p. 100). The threshold 1/p is dynamically forced — this is where the local principle's canonicity comes from, and the proof is ultrametric through and through.
- **(F3) Selection = extension of the function algebra.** Mod-p-additive multiplicative maps correspond exactly to ring homomorphisms on tilts (Prop. 14.14, pp. 100–101, via the [CD14] presentation W_p(R) = lim ZR/Iⁿ with I generated by [r+s]−[r]−[s]); on Y⋄_α and only there, the ring A_inf(X) acts as o-valued functions ((172), (174), (184); Prop. 15.7 p. 115). The selection principle IS: "the points where the multiplicative evaluation ZR → o is continuous for the additive-defect (I-adic) filtration, so that the Witt ring acts."
- **(F4) The output.** Theorem 15.6 (pp. 113–114): Y⋄ = Hom_cont(ô♭_K, o♭) equivariantly; Y⋄₀ = Y⋄/G is in bijection with H_{κ₀}(o♭)/o×_{K₀} (Lubin–Tate description), identified via untilts with the closed points of the Fargues–Fontaine curve (parts 4–5); and — **the property S4 covets** — part 6: "The only periodic (i.e. finite) orbit of the F_p-action on Y⋄₀ is Y⋄₀_{s₀}. It has order log_p N(π₀) = r." One periodic orbit per closed point, of the correct length after suspension. The uncompleted Y̌₀ is tiny and boring (Prop. 15.8, p. 116: one F_p-fixed point plus one infinite orbit).
- **(F5) Equivariance is part of the theorem.** Thm 15.6(1) is a "G × Aut(o) × ⟨F_p⟩-equivariant identification" — the principle is natural in every automorphism of the coefficient data.
- **(F6) The global side, in Deninger's own words.** [x-06] §4 p. 11: the raw system "has too many periodic orbits, since the N-space W_rat(X₀)(C) does not know enough about the addition in O_{X₀}. **In the local p-adic situation below, we know the right modification to make. ## 4. The local principle in packet coordinates: it selects the Teichmüller class (Lemma A)

**Packet coordinates recalled** ([x-03] §5, pp. 31–33, read verbatim): fix x over x₀ = (p) (X₀ = Spec Z from here on), κ(x) ≅ F̄_p; fix ι: μ(K) ↪ μ(C); χ_x = ι∘i_x^{-1}: κ(x)^× ↪ C^× the resulting injective character; every (Tors)-character is χ_x·(a,ν) = χ_x∘( )^a∘( )^ν with (a,ν) ∈ Ẑ×₍p₎ × N₀, fibers (a,ν) ~ (a′,ν′) iff ν′ = νpⁿ, a = pⁿa′ ((34)–(35)); the packet C_{x₀} ≅ (Ẑ×₍p₎/Nx₀^Ẑ) ×_{p^Z} Q₀^{>0} fibers over B_p := Ẑ×₍p₎/p^Ẑ = Aut(F̄_p)^×/Aut(F_p)^, "and the fibres are the Q₀^{>0}-orbits" ((38)–(39) and following line, p. 33). So each closed orbit in Γ_p has a well-defined **base class** [a] ∈ B_p, all Frobenii fix the base class, and distinct base classes give distinct orbits.

**Lemma A (derived; consistency check against Thm 15.6 below).** Let o be as in §2 (residue field k algebraically closed of char p), κ an algebraically closed field of characteristic p, and P: κ → o multiplicative with P(0) = 0, P(1) = 1, whose restriction to κ^× is a group homomorphism into o (values automatically in μ^{(p)}(o), the prime-to-p roots of unity, since κ^× is prime-to-p torsion). Then

> |P(r+s) − P(r) − P(s)| ≤ |p| for all r, s ∈ κ **iff** P = [·]∘τ for a unique field embedding τ: κ ↪ k, where [·] is the Teichmüller section of the reduction μ^{(p)}(o) ≅ μ^{(p)}(k).

*Derivation.* (Reduction is injective on prime-to-p roots of unity: if ζ ≡ 1 mod m with ζ^d = 1, d prime to p, then 0 = ζ^d − 1 = (ζ−1)(d + x) with x ∈ m, and d + x is a unit since |d| = 1, so ζ = 1. Surjectivity onto μ^{(p)}(k): an injective homomorphism between groups abstractly ≅ ⊕_{ℓ≠p} Q_ℓ/Z_ℓ has divisible, hence full, ℓ-primary images. So [·] exists and is unique.) (⇒) The defect lies in p·o ⊆ m, so r ↦ P(r) mod m is additive, multiplicative, unital: a ring homomorphism κ → k, injective since κ is a field and 1 ↦ 1; call it τ. For r ∈ κ^×, P(r) and [τ(r)] are prime-to-p roots of unity with the same reduction, hence equal. (⇐) [a] + [b] − [a+b] ∈ V W(k) = p W(k) in the Witt ring W(k) ⊆ o (first ghost/component coordinates agree; V F = p on Witt vectors of a perfect ring), so the defect of [·]∘τ has absolute value ≤ |p|. ∎

*Consistency:* this matches Theorem 15.6's closed-point fiber Y⋄_s = Hom(κ, k) (p. 114) — the mod-p-additive points over the closed point are exactly the field embeddings, Teichmüller-lifted; and Hom(κ₀, k) is a single F_p-orbit of size r = deg(κ₀/F_p), which suspends to one closed orbit of length r·log p = log N(π₀) — Thm 15.6(6).

**Consequence 1 — why the local principle succeeds.** The o-valued unit-exponent characters at a char-p point form a torsor under Aut_group(κ^×) = Ẑ×₍p₎; imposing mod-p additivity cuts this to the torsor Hom_ring(κ, k) under Aut_ring(κ) = Ẑ = the closure of Frobenius. Locally, the surviving ambiguity is EXACTLY one Frobenius-orbit closure — the symmetry orbit coincides with the dynamics orbit, so no choice is left dangling. Globally, the C-valued packet base is B_p = Ẑ×₍p₎/p^Ẑ = coker(Aut_ring ↪ Aut_group) — the part of the ambiguity that additivity would have killed and nothing else can. (This is the same cokernel the adjudication identified as the packet-forcing group coker(lk_p), §2 there.) **The packet degeneracy W4 and the local fix W9 are two faces of one identity.**

**Consequence 2 — the transport reading collapses to Theorem C (D3).** The condition in Lemma A is o-valued. To impose it on the global C-valued characters one must first transport values: choose an isomorphism j: μ^{(p)}(C) → μ^{(p)}(C_p) (equivalently, by Lemma A, choose which base class is "Teichmüller"). For each j, the class {χ_x·(a,ν) : j∘(that character) is mod-p additive} cuts the packet to the single base class determined by j; varying j over its torsor sweeps every base class. So the faithful transport of the local principle **is** the adjudicated Theorem-C cut E(a₀), with j in place of a₀: it exists, it is one arbitrary point of a Cantor torsor per prime, it is not admissible-with-theory (forfeits every certified theorem), and it is not N-invariant — precisely [x-03]'s p. 29 Remark. Nothing new is gained; the known dead end is re-derived from the local principle itself. [Derived; anchors: Lemma A + adjudication §4 item 5.]

## 5. Proposition 1: the equivariance no-go (D1)

The local principle is equivariant under every automorphism of its coefficient data (F5), and its selection bound uses only ring-intrinsic structure of o (F1). The global analog of that naturality is stability under Aut(C). It cannot coexist with packet-cutting:

**Lemma C (derived; Steinitz).** The restriction map Aut(C) → Aut(μ(C)) = Ẑ× is surjective for C the complex numbers (or any algebraically closed field of characteristic 0 containing Q̄ with infinite transcendence degree). *Derivation:* u ∈ Ẑ× defines an automorphism of Q(μ_∞) (cyclotomic theory); extend to Q̄ (isomorphism extension), then to C along a transcendence basis of C over Q̄ and to the algebraic closure C of the lifted subfield (Steinitz; uses AC). ∎

**Lemma D (derived).** Let σ ∈ Aut(C) act on X̌(C)_{E_tors} by post-composition, (x, P) ↦ (x, σ∘P). Then: (i) σ commutes with the G-action (pre-composition), with every F_ν, hence with the Q^{>0}-action, and descends to the suspension X₀^{E_tors} commuting with the flow φ^t and fixing the R-coordinate; (ii) σ preserves each example class E_tors, E_max, E_f, E_fg, E_fd, E_fd0 (kernels are unchanged — ker(σ∘P) = ker P — and images map by σ, preserving torsion and ⊗Q-dimension; Def. 4.1's operations are pre-compositions, which commute with σ); (iii) on the packet over p, σ maps the point with coordinates (a, ν) to the point with coordinates (u_σ a, ν), where u_σ ∈ Ẑ×₍p₎ is σ's action on prime-to-p roots of unity: σ∘χ_x = σ|_{μ}∘χ_x = χ_x∘( )^{u_σ} because χ_x is an isomorphism onto μ^{(p)}(C) (injective character between groups ≅ ⊕_{ℓ≠p}Q_ℓ/Z_ℓ, divisible-image argument as in Lemma A) and power maps commute with any group homomorphism. Hence σ moves the base class [a] ↦ [u_σ a] in B_p and maps closed orbits of length log p to closed orbits of length log p in the same packet. ∎

**Proposition 1 (derived).** Let E be any Aut(C)-stable class (all named example classes qualify) and let S ⊆ X₀^E be Aut(C)-stable and flow-invariant. If S contains one periodic point over the prime p, then for EVERY base class [c] ∈ B_p, S contains a closed orbit of length log p with base class [c]. In particular S contains uncountably many closed orbits over p (B_p is an infinite profinite group, hence uncountable — adjudication §2, re-derived there), and **no Aut(C)-stable selection achieves one orbit per prime**. *Proof:* by §4's coordinates the periodic point lies on an orbit γ with some base class [a]; for [c] = [ua] pick σ with u_σ = u (Lemmas C–D); σ(γ) ⊆ S is a closed orbit of the same length with base class [c]; distinct base classes lie on distinct orbits since the fibers of the (38)-fibration are the Q^{>0}-orbits ([x-03] p. 33). ∎

**Corollary (the no-go for the transplant).** The local principle's coefficient-naturality (F1/F5) cannot be carried to the global setting: any global selection with the analogous naturality — in particular, any selection defined uniformly from the abstract field C and the scheme data, since Deninger's construction takes an abstract algebraically closed C as input ([x-03] §5, "Let C be an algebraically closed field which satisfies the conditions…") and transport of structure then makes every uniformly-defined locus Aut(C)-stable — retains the full packet. **A viable global selection must break Aut(C)-symmetry**, i.e., must consume structure on C beyond its abstract field structure.

*Scope and honesty.* (a) The bridge "canonical ⇒ Aut(C)-stable" is a definability schema, not a theorem; it is exactly the sense in which Thm 15.6 is canonical, and the corollary states its precise reach. (b) Wild automorphisms of C are discontinuous (a continuous automorphism is the identity on R by density of Q and continuity, hence id or conjugation — two lines), so Proposition 1 does NOT constrain selections using C's analytic structure. That door — the one Deninger's p. 29 Remark points through ("rephrased in terms of absolute values") — is addressed next. (c) Consistency check: the Theorem-C cuts E(a₀) are not Aut(C)-stable (σ(χ^{a₀}) = χ^{u a₀} is outside E(a₀)'s reachability set a₀·p^Ẑ for u ∉ p^Ẑ — adjudication §4 item 5b), so Proposition 1 and Theorem C are compatible, and Proposition 1 conceptually explains [x-03] Thm 5.2's full-packet phenomenon: every certified (kernel-defined, hence Aut(C)-stable) class must carry full packets.

## 6. Lemma B: the archimedean defect bound selects nothing (D2)

The naive translation of Stage 3 to the global C-valued system is: fix ε < 1 and select the points where the multiplicative evaluation has archimedean additivity defect ≤ ε — i.e., where the evaluation ZΓ(X,O) → C is continuous for the additive-defect filtration, the verbatim (F3) formulation with |·|_∞ in place of the valuation.

**Lemma B (derived).** For X₀ = Spec Z, at every point of every packet (any prime p, any character P in any class E ⊆ E_tors), the test r = s = 1 gives
> |P(1̄+1̄) − P(1̄) − P(1̄)| = |P(2̄) − 2| ≥ 1,
since P(2̄) is a root of unity or 0 (κ(x)^× is torsion; 0 occurs iff p = 2). Hence the class "archimedean defect ≤ ε at char-p points" is **empty on the periodic locus for every ε < 1**. Moreover the (F3) extension mechanism requires the defect ideal to land in a topologically nilpotent set (|·| < 1, so that lim ZR/Iⁿ receives the evaluation — (172), p. 94); with a defect bounded below by 1 there is no completion and no A_inf-analog action. ∎

Two supporting observations, both from the sources: the completion Stage 2 is ALREADY invisible over C ("this process does not give more points", [x-03] intro p. 5 — C has no small multiplicative subgroups), and it was the completion that made the local principle non-trivial (Prop. 15.8 vs Thm 15.6). So over C, Stage 2 is trivial and Stage 3 is empty: **two of the local principle's three stages die independently at the archimedean coefficient field.** The ultrametric inequality is load-bearing in (F2)'s self-improvement as well; no part of the mechanism is characteristic-blind.

*What Lemma B does NOT close:* conditions on the archimedean defect PROFILE other than a uniform threshold (averaged, asymptotic, or comparative conditions). ## 7. What survives: three roads, first obstacles named

**Road 2 — renounce selection; Haar-average the packet (new in this note).** Proposition 1 kills selections but not measures: the packet base B_p is a compact group, its Haar probability measure is translation-invariant, and by Lemma D(iii) Aut(C) acts on B_p by group translations — so **Haar measure is canonical AND Aut(C)-equivariant: it passes exactly the naturality test that every selection fails.** The flow fixes the base (§4), so the measure is flow-invariant. At the formal level the T1 count then comes out right: the packet's aggregate orbit contribution is ∫_{B_p}(single-orbit term) dHaar = the single-orbit term, since the integrand is constant (all orbits in Γ_p have length log p — [x-06] Thm 4.2). What it does not buy: the substrate defects W1/W6/W12 (infinite-dimensionality, not-a-foliated-space, non-Hausdorff along packets) are untouched, and the trace-formula frameworks on disk exclude orbit continua by hypothesis (ALKL H4 simple/isolated orbits — ledger R4; ALKM's setting per ledger R13).
