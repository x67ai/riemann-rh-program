### 3.2 (B2) The splitting Q̄^× = μ(Q̄) × V with V uniquely divisible

μ(Q̄) is divisible (every root of unity has an n-th root in the algebraically closed field Q̄); a divisible abelian group is injective as a Z-module, hence a direct summand of any abelian group containing it [this is the standard Baer criterion consequence; [x-03] itself uses exactly this on p. 28, Lemma 4.3's proof, verbatim: "The sequences 1 → μ(k) → k^× → k^×/μ(k) → 1 and 1 → μ(C) → C^× → C^×/μ(C) → 1 are both split since μ(k) and μ(C) are divisible. ... Here k^×/μ(k) and C^×/μ(C) are uniquely divisible abelian groups and therefore Q-vector spaces."]. So Q̄^× = μ(Q̄) × V for a (non-canonical) complement V ≅ Q̄^×/μ(Q̄). V is divisible (quotient of the divisible Q̄^×) and torsion-free (if v^n ∈ μ then v is torsion, so v ∈ μ, so v = 1 in the quotient), hence uniquely divisible, hence a Q-vector space. Its dimension is countable (Q̄ is countable) and infinite (3.3). **Verified**; the splitting is the same device [x-03] uses in Lemma 4.3 and again in Lemma 8.3's proof (p. 51, (71)).

### 3.3 (B3) Q-linear independence of the V-components of distinct primes

Fix distinct rational primes ℓ₁, …, ℓ_n and write ℓ_i = ζ_i · v_i with ζ_i ∈ μ(Q̄), v_i ∈ V (unique for the fixed splitting). Suppose ∑ k_i v_i = 0 in V (additive notation) with k_i ∈ Z not all zero (a Q-relation clears to a Z-relation). Multiplicatively, ∏ v_i^{k_i} = 1, so ∏ ℓ_i^{k_i} = ∏ ζ_i^{k_i} ∈ μ(Q̄). The left side is a positive rational number; μ(Q̄) ∩ Q^× = {±1}; so ∏ ℓ_i^{k_i} = 1, and unique factorization in Z forces all k_i = 0. **Verified.** (Positivity even excludes −1; the note's "{±1}" is fine.)

### 3.4 (B4) The characters Ψ_t; (Tors), (Image), E_max, unitarity

Complete v₁, …, v_n to a Q-basis (v_i)_{i≤n} ∪ (w_j)_j of V (V has a basis; countable dimension, so no choice beyond the countable is needed). For t ∈ [0, ½]^n define Ψ_t: Q̄^× → S¹ by Ψ_t(ζ) = ι(ζ) for ζ ∈ μ(Q̄) (ι: μ(Q̄) ↪ μ(C) the fixed injection of §5, p. 31; over Q it is an isomorphism, p. 47 "Fix an isomorphism ι: μ(K) → μ(C)"), Ψ_t(v_i^a) = e^{2πi t_i a} for a ∈ Q, Ψ_t(w_j^a) = 1, extended multiplicatively. Well-definedness: a ↦ e^{2πi t_i a} is a homomorphism (Q, +) → S¹; the basis lines are independent, so the product of the line homomorphisms is a homomorphism V → S¹; the product with ι on μ is a homomorphism on μ × V = Q̄^×. Values in S¹. **Verified.**

Membership. The conditions, verbatim from p. 27 (read visually): "(Tors) the group ker(χ)_tors = ker(χ|_{μ(κ)}) is finite and |(ker χ)_tors| ∈ N₀." — here ker(Ψ_t|_μ) = ker ι = 1, finite, and 1 ∈ N₀ = N. "(Image) Only if char κ > 0. If χ(κ^×) is torsion, then κ^× is torsion as well, i.e. κ^× ⊗ Q ≠ 0 implies χ(κ^×) ⊗ Q ≠ 0." — κ = Q̄ has characteristic 0, so (Image) imposes nothing. The note's §1.2 observation that over Spec Z the E_max- and E_tors-loci coincide is confirmed: for residue fields F̄_p, κ^× is torsion so the conclusion of (Image) holds; for Q̄, (Image) is silent. So (x_η, Ψ_t) ∈ X̊(S¹) ∩ X̊(C)_{E_max}, and its image in X̊₀(S¹) ∩ X̊₀(C)_{E_max}. **Verified.**

### 3.8 (B8) Compact-to-Hausdorff, and the dimension step — exact hypotheses

*Compact-to-Hausdorff.* A continuous injection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space is a closed map (closed subsets of the compact domain are compact, their images are compact hence closed in the Hausdorff target), hence a homeomorphism onto its image. Standard; no source needed. So **if** the subspace Θ([0,½]^n) ⊂ X₀ is Hausdorff, Θ is an embedding. The note conditions this on S being Hausdorff; it suffices that the cell image be Hausdorff, which Lemma F.1 (§3.11) supplies unconditionally.

*Dimension monotonicity — what the note invokes, and what it needs.* The note writes: "If S is metrizable ... subspace monotonicity of covering dimension in separable metric spaces (Hurewicz–Wallman, classical) gives dim S ≥ n; more generally, for compact Hausdorff S the cell image is closed and closed-subspace monotonicity in normal spaces applies." Neither Hurewicz–Wallman nor Engelking is on disk; what I can say with weight is the following.

(i) **Small inductive dimension ind is monotone on arbitrary subspaces of arbitrary topological spaces** — proved here, not recalled. Definition: ind ∅ = −1; ind X ≤ n iff for every x ∈ X and every open U ∋ x there is an open V with x ∈ V ⊆ U and ind ∂_X V ≤ n − 1. Claim: M ⊆ X ⇒ ind M ≤ ind X. Induction on n = ind X. For x ∈ M and U′ open in M containing x, write U′ = U ∩ M with U open in X; choose V as in the definition for X; then V ∩ M is open in M, contains x, lies in U′, and ∂_M(V ∩ M) ⊆ ∂_X V ∩ M ⊆ ∂_X V (a point of M in the closure of V ∩ M relative to M but not in V ∩ M is in cl_X V ∖ V); by induction ind ∂_M(V ∩ M) ≤ ind ∂_X V ≤ n − 1. ∎ No separation axiom is used.

(ii) **ind [0,1]^n = n** [RU — Brouwer/Lebesgue; Hurewicz–Wallman Thm IV 1]. This is the only recalled input, and it is bedrock.

(iii) Covering dimension dim. For a **metrizable** ambient space, dim is monotone on all subspaces [RU — Katětov–Morita / Dowker; Engelking Dimension Theory Thm 4.1.7]; for separable metric spaces dim = ind [RU — Hurewicz–Wallman Thm V 8]. For a **normal** ambient space (e.g. compact Hausdorff), dim is monotone on **closed** subspaces [RU — Čech; Engelking Thm 3.1.4]; for a merely Hausdorff, non-normal ambient space, closed-subspace monotonicity of dim can fail [RU — Engelking, Ch. 3 examples]. The cell image is compact, hence closed in any Hausdorff S; so the note's second route needs S normal, which holds for compact Hausdorff S and for metrizable S but is not implied by "Hausdorff" alone.

**Consequence for the statement as printed.** "(b) ... every closed invariant S ... whose subspace topology is Hausdorff ... has covering dimension ≥ n" is justified for compact Hausdorff S and for metrizable S; for a Hausdorff S that is neither, the note has no argument for *covering* dimension. With ind in place of dim the statement is fine for every Hausdorff S — indeed for every S whatsoever, once the cells are known to be genuine (Lemma F.1). **Finding F3 (MAJOR: statement must change).** Given F1 (the Hausdorff clause is vacuous anyway) the repair is folded into the rewritten (b) in §4.

### 3.11 (B11) Filling F2: the cells are genuine (Lemma F.1)

(v) Hence (P_β, u_β) → (P, u) along the sub-subnet. ∎

**Consequence (fills F2).** Θ([0,½]^n) ⊂ Σ₁, which is Hausdorff (metrizable); Θ is a continuous injection from a compact space; so Θ is a homeomorphism onto its image, **unconditionally**. Every closed flow-invariant S ⊆ X₀^{E_max} meeting every packet contains, for every n, a subspace homeomorphic to [0,1]^n, contained in the metrizable slice Σ₁. Moreover, by 3.8(i)–(ii), ind S ≥ ind Θ([0,½]^n) = n for all n, so ind S = ∞; and S ∩ Σ₁ is a metrizable space containing n-cells for all n, so dim(S ∩ Σ₁) = ∞ [RU for dim-monotonicity in metrizable spaces; unconditional for ind]. This is the non-vacuous content of Theorem B(b), and it applies in particular to Deninger's Y₀ = closure of the periodic set (§8, p. 49: "we will show that the system Y₀ is still infinite-dimensional"): Y₀ contains genuine n-cells for every n, in its subspace topology, for X₀ = Spec Z.
