## 3. The topology in which closures are taken, settled from the sources (assignment items 1 and 2)

Throughout: X₀ = Spec Z, X = Spec Z̄ (the normalization of Z in Q̄; note Z̄ is countable), G = Gal(Q̄/Q), C = ℂ, N₀ = ℕ so Q₀^{>0} = Q^{>0}. Everything is affine, so Lemma 7.1 and Cor. 7.8 apply directly.

**3.1 The chart X•(C).** By definition (p. 40) X•(C) is the set of multiplicative maps P: Z̄ → ℂ of Remark 3.4 with the topology of pointwise convergence, i.e. the subspace topology from ℂ^{Z̄}; metrizable (Z̄ countable), hence Hausdorff. A point over the prime x of Z̄ above p is (x, P×) with P×: κ(x)^× = F̄_p^× → ℂ^× a homomorphism; as a map on Z̄ it is r ↦ 0 for r ∈ p_x and r ↦ P×(r̄) otherwise. Since F̄_p^× = μ^{(p)} is torsion of prime-to-p order (every r̄ ≠ 0 has finite order m prime to p) and homomorphic images of torsion are torsion, the values P×(r̄) are roots of unity of prime-to-p order. Hence, at a **fixed** x, a net (x, P_α×) converges pointwise to (x, P×) iff P_α×(ζ) → P×(ζ) for each ζ ∈ μ^{(p)}, and since the values lie in the discrete-in-ℂ set of m-th roots of unity for ζ of order m, iff for each ζ the values **agree eventually**. This is the "pointwise evaluation" of the note, and on a fixed-x set in chart 1 it is exactly the right description. Nets whose x varies are governed by Lemma 7.1 (the prime ideal P^{-1}(0) can only grow in the limit) and by the Galois step §4.2(R3); they are not described by evaluation at one x.

**3.2 The Galois quotient X•₀(C) = X•(C)/G.** Quotient topology (p. 42); π: X•(C) → X•₀(C) is continuous and open (p. 43: "since G acts by homeomorphisms, also open"); X•₀(C) is metrizable, hence Hausdorff (Cor. 7.8); the action map X•(C) × G → X•(C) is jointly continuous (Prop. 7.5), and G is a compact (profinite) group.

**3.3 The colimit X̌(C), X̌₀(C): "finer than pointwise"?** X̌(C) = colim_ℕ X•(C) carries the final topology for the inclusions F_ν^{-1}|_{X•(C)} (p. 43): Z is closed (open) iff F_ν(Z) ∩ X•(C) is closed (open) in X•(C) for all ν. Write U_ν := F_ν^{-1}(X•(C)) ⊂ X̌(C). ## 4. Line-by-line re-derivation of the converse inclusion

**4.0 Normalization of the orbit (the note's Step 1, checked).** Let γ ⊂ Γ^E_p be a periodic orbit. By Thm 6.1 γ = {[P₀, w] : w ∈ R^{>0}} for some P₀ ∈ C^E_{x₀}, x₀ = (p), with isotropy p^Z. By (38) (p. 32) P₀ = F_r π(x, χ^{a}) for some unit a ∈ Ẑ×_{(p)} and r ∈ Q^{>0}, where χ = χ_x is the injective reference character and (x, χ^a) := (x, χ ∘ ( )^a); since [F_r P, w] = [P, rw] (the orbit relation (P, rw)·r = (F_r P, w)), γ is also the orbit of π(x, χ^a), and π(x, χ^a) ∈ X̌₀(C)_E because the E-locus is Q^{>0}-invariant (Prop. 4.2) and contains P₀. So without loss of generality **P₀ = π(x, χ^{a₀}) with a₀ ∈ Ẑ×_{(p)}, a point of the chart X•₀(C)_E.** Two facts used repeatedly, both from p. 32 and p. 35: (i) Hom(κ(x)^×, ℂ^×) = Hom(μ^{(p)}, μ^{(p)}(ℂ)) ≅ End(μ^{(p)}) = Ẑ_{(p)} via b ↦ χ ∘ ( )^b =: χ^b (every homomorphism of the torsion group μ^{(p)} ≅ ⊕_{ℓ≠p} Q_ℓ/Z_ℓ into ℂ^× lands in the prime-to-p roots of unity, which χ identifies with μ^{(p)}; End(⊕_{ℓ≠p} Q_ℓ/Z_ℓ) = ∏_{ℓ≠p} Z_ℓ since Hom(Q_ℓ/Z_ℓ, Q_{ℓ′}/Z_{ℓ′}) = 0 for ℓ ≠ ℓ′); (ii) F_n(x, χ^{b}) = (x, χ^{bn}) for n ∈ ℕ.

### 4.1 Route I — the packet is a fiber of a continuous flow-invariant map (any arithmetic scheme, any admissible E)

**Proposition (closedness of packets).** Let X₀ be an arithmetic scheme, E admissible, C as in [x-03] (conditions before Cor. 4.4), N₀ with char N₀ ⊇ char X₀, x₀ ∈ X₀ a point with finite residue field, and X₀ = X̌₀(C)_E ×_{Q₀^{>0}} R^{>0} with the quotient topology. Then Γ^E_{x₀} is a closed, flow-invariant subset of X₀. Consequently cl_{X₀}(γ) ⊆ Γ^E_{x₀} for every periodic orbit γ ⊂ Γ^E_{x₀}, and cl(γ) meets neither the generic stratum nor Γ^E_{y₀} for any y₀ ≠ x₀.

*Proof, step by step.*
1. The map Φ: X̌₀(C)_E × R^{>0} → X₀, (P₀, u) ↦ pr_{X₀}(P₀), is continuous (first projection followed by the continuous pr_{X₀}, §3.6) and constant on Q₀^{>0}-orbits: Φ((P₀, u)q) = pr_{X₀}(F_q P₀) = pr_{X₀}(P₀) (p. 27). By the universal property of the quotient topology it descends to a continuous **Π: X₀ → X₀, Π[P₀, u] = pr_{X₀}(P₀)**.
2. Π ∘ φ^t = Π, since φ^t[P₀, u] = [P₀, ue^t] (p. 38). So every fiber of Π is flow-invariant.
3. Π^{-1}(x₀) = q(pr_{X₀}^{-1}(x₀) × R^{>0}) where pr_{X₀}^{-1}(x₀) is taken in X̌₀(C)_E. By p. 31, C_{x₀} = pr_{X₀}^{-1}(x₀) in X̌₀(C)_{Etors} (and = ⋃_ν F_ν^{-1}pr₀^{-1}(x₀), the two descriptions agreeing because pr_{X₀}(F_ν^{-1}P₀) = pr_{X₀}(P₀)). Since every admissible E satisfies (Tors) (Def. 4.1), X̌₀(C)_E ⊆ X̌₀(C)_{Etors}, hence pr_{X₀}^{-1}(x₀) ∩ X̌₀(C)_E = C_{x₀} ∩ X̌₀(C)_E = C^E_{x₀} (Thm 5.2 / p. 38). Therefore Π^{-1}(x₀) = q(C^E_{x₀} × R^{>0}) = Γ^E_{x₀} (definition, p. 38).
4. {x₀} is closed in X₀ (Zariski). Proof for a general arithmetic scheme: choose an affine open Spec A ∋ x₀, x₀ = 𝔭; the domain A/𝔭 embeds in its fraction field κ(x₀), which is finite, so A/𝔭 is a finite integral domain, hence a field, so 𝔭 is maximal and {x₀} is closed in Spec A. If y ∈ cl_{X₀}{x₀} and V ∋ y is any affine open, then V meets {x₀}, so x₀ ∈ V and y ∈ cl_V{x₀} = {x₀}. For Spec Z this is just "(p) is maximal".
5. Hence Γ^E_{x₀} = Π^{-1}(x₀) is closed and flow-invariant. If γ ⊂ Γ^E_{x₀}, cl(γ) ⊂ Γ^E_{x₀}. The generic stratum is Π^{-1}(η₀) and Γ^E_{y₀} = Π^{-1}(y₀), both disjoint from Π^{-1}(x₀). ∎

This is the whole converse, in five lines, and it does not use Theorem A, Hausdorffness of anything, or any computation of limits. It does use the quotient topology on X₀ (step 1) — a coarser topology on X₀ would break the descent of Φ — which is why the topology convention had to be pinned down (§3.5).

### 4.2 Route II — the explicit chart-by-chart computation (X₀ = Spec Z), proving both inclusions at once

This route re-derives the note's own instrument (pointwise limits of F_n P₀) and carries it correctly from the chart to the suspension. Notation: O(P₀) := {F_r P₀ : r ∈ Q^{>0}} ⊂ X̌₀(C)_E (the Q^{>0}-orbit), S := {F_n P₀ : n ∈ ℕ} ⊂ X•₀(C)_E, S̃ := {(x, χ^{a₀n}) : n ∈ ℕ} ⊂ X•(C) (so π(S̃) = S by fact (ii)).

**(R1) Reduce from X₀ to X̌₀(C)_E.** q^{-1}(γ) = q^{-1}(q({P₀} × R^{>0})) = ⋃_r ({P₀} × R^{>0})·r = ⋃_r {F_r P₀} × r^{-1}R^{>0} = O(P₀) × R^{>0}. By (L2) for the open q (§3.5): q^{-1}(cl_{X₀} γ) = cl(O(P₀) × R^{>0}) = cl(O(P₀)) × R^{>0} (closure of a product is the product of the closures), and q is surjective, so
  **cl_{X₀}(γ) = q(cl_{X̌₀(C)_E}(O(P₀)) × R^{>0}).**

**(R2) Reduce from the colimit to chart 1.** The E-charts U^E_ν = F_ν^{-1}(X•₀(C)_E) are open in X̌₀(C)_E and cover it (§3.4), and F_ν: U^E_ν → X•₀(C)_E is a homeomorphism. By (L1), cl(O(P₀)) = ⋃_ν cl_{U^E_ν}(O(P₀) ∩ U^E_ν) = ⋃_ν F_ν^{-1}(cl_{X•₀(C)_E}(F_ν(O(P₀) ∩ U^E_ν))), and F_ν(O(P₀) ∩ U^E_ν) = F_ν(O(P₀)) ∩ X•₀(C)_E = O(P₀) ∩ X•₀(C)_E because O(P₀) is Q^{>0}-stable. Which orbit points lie in chart 1? Write r ∈ Q^{>0} as r = p^k·m/m′ with m, m′ ∈ ℕ coprime and prime to p; F_p P₀ = P₀ (isotropy p^Z, Thm 5.2/(38)), so F_r P₀ = F_{m/m′}P₀. Now F_{m/m′}P₀ = F_{m′}^{-1}F_m P₀ lies in X•₀(C) iff F_m P₀ ∈ F_{m′}(X•₀(C)) = π(F_{m′}(X•(C))) iff (by (51), p. 42, applied to the representative (x, χ^{a₀m})) χ^{a₀m} is trivial on the image of μ_{m′}(K) in κ(x)^× — which is μ_{m′} itself (m′ prime to p, (32)) — iff m′ | a₀m in Ẑ_{(p)}, i.e. (a₀ a unit, gcd(m, m′) = 1) iff m′ = 1. (Machine check C3; the p-part of the denominator is always harmless, since F_{p}^{-1}P₀ = P₀.) Hence O(P₀) ∩ X•₀(C)_E = {F_m P₀ : m ∈ ℕ} = S, and
  **cl(O(P₀)) = ⋃_ν F_ν^{-1}(cl_{X•₀(C)_E}(S)).**
This already answers the note's Q-c: the closure in the colimit is the union of the chart closures, and in each chart it is the F_ν^{-1}-transport of the chart-1 closure.

**(R3) Reduce from X•₀(C) to X•(C) at the fixed x (the Galois step).** π: X•(C) → X•₀(C) is open (p. 43), so by (L2) cl_{X•₀(C)}(S) = π(cl(π^{-1}S)), and π^{-1}(S) = S̃·G. For a compact group G acting jointly continuously (Prop. 7.5) on a space, cl(A·G) = cl(A)·G: ⊇ because each x ↦ xg is a homeomorphism; ⊆ because if a_i g_i → z, a subnet has g_i → g ∈ G, and then a_i = (a_i g_i)g_i^{-1} → zg^{-1} by joint continuity, so z ∈ cl(A)·g. Hence
  **cl_{X•₀(C)}(S) = π(cl_{X•(C)}(S̃)),** and the closure in the subspace X•₀(C)_E is π(cl S̃) ∩ X•₀(C)_E.

**(R4) The closure of S̃ in X•(C) is a copy of Ẑ_{(p)}.** Define β: Ẑ_{(p)} → X•(C), b ↦ (x, χ^{a₀b}) (a point of X•(C) for every b by fact (i)). β is **continuous**: in the coordinate r ∈ p_x the value is 0; in a coordinate r ∉ p_x with r̄ of order m (prime to p) the value χ(r̄)^{a₀b} depends only on a₀b mod m, i.e. on b mod m, and Ẑ_{(p)} → Z/m is continuous; so each coordinate is locally constant and a map into a product is continuous iff each coordinate is. β is **injective**: χ^{a₀b} = χ^{a₀b′} forces ( )^{a₀(b−b′)} = id on μ^{(p)} (χ injective), i.e. a₀(b − b′) = 0 in End μ^{(p)} = Ẑ_{(p)} (faithful), so b = b′. Ẑ_{(p)} is compact and X•(C) is Hausdorff (§3.1), so β is a homeomorphism onto the compact, hence closed, set K := β(Ẑ_{(p)}). ℕ is dense in Ẑ_{(p)} = lim_← Z/M (M prime to p): a basic open set is a residue class mod M and every class contains a positive integer (machine check C1). Therefore
  **cl_{X•(C)}(S̃) = β(cl_{Ẑ_{(p)}} ℕ) = K ≅ Ẑ_{(p)}.**
So "every limit of {F_n(P₀)} along any subnet is P₀^b for some b ∈ Ẑ_{(p)}" (the note) is correct **as a statement about S̃ in X•(C)** — and the converse also holds: every b occurs. (The note's "by compactness of Ẑ_{(p)} and the same pointwise evaluation" is this argument compressed; the exact eventual agreement is check C5.)

**(R5) Which points of K are in the space at all — the exact (Tors) criterion.** For b = (b_ℓ)_ℓ ∈ Ẑ_{(p)}, the kernel of χ^{a₀b} on μ^{(p)} = ⊕_{ℓ≠p} μ_{ℓ^∞} is the kernel of the endomorphism a₀b, namely ⊕_{ℓ≠p} μ_{ℓ^∞}[a₀_ℓ b_ℓ], and μ_{ℓ^∞}[c] = μ_{ℓ^{v_ℓ(c)}} for c ≠ 0, = μ_{ℓ^∞} for c = 0. Hence (a₀ a unit)
  **ker χ^{a₀b} is finite ⟺ b_ℓ ≠ 0 for all ℓ and v_ℓ(b_ℓ) = 0 for all but finitely many ℓ ⟺ b ∈ ℕ·Ẑ×_{(p)}** (b = ν·a with ν = ∏ ℓ^{v_ℓ(b_ℓ)} ∈ ℕ prime to p and a a unit).
The excluded b form two classes: (i) some b_ℓ = 0 (kernel ⊇ μ_{ℓ^∞}); (ii) all b_ℓ ≠ 0 but infinitely many non-units, e.g. b = (ℓ)_{ℓ≠p} (kernel ⊕_ℓ μ_ℓ, infinite). Both violate (Tors) and lie in no X•(C)_E for admissible E; both are points of Deninger's un-cut X•(C). (Machine check C2.) For b ∈ ℕ·Ẑ×_{(p)}, (x, χ^{a₀b}) is a (Tors)-character at x, i.e. a point of pr^{-1}(x) ∩ X•(C)_{Etors}, and by (35)–(36) these are all of them. Hence, intersecting with the E-locus (G-invariant, Def. 4.1/Prop. 4.2):
  **cl_{X•₀(C)_E}(S) = π(K) ∩ X•₀(C)_E = pr₀^{-1}(x₀) ∩ X•₀(C)_E.**
(⊆: a point of π(K) in the E-locus is π(x, χ^{a₀b}) with χ^{a₀b} ∈ E ⊆ E_tors, so b ∈ ℕ·Ẑ×_{(p)} and it lies over x₀. ⊇: a point of pr₀^{-1}(x₀) ∩ X•₀(C)_E is a G-orbit of E-points over x₀; G is transitive on the points over x₀ (p. 32), so the orbit contains a point (x, Q) at our x, in X•(C)_E by G-invariance; Q = χ^{c} for some c ∈ Ẑ_{(p)} by fact (i), so (x, Q) = β(a₀^{-1}c) ∈ K.) Note the ⊇ here **is Theorem A in chart 1** — every packet point at x is a limit of F_n P₀ — re-derived by the compact-homeomorphism β rather than by an explicit CRT sequence.

**(R6) Assemble.** By (R2) and (R5), cl(O(P₀)) = ⋃_ν F_ν^{-1}(pr₀^{-1}(x₀) ∩ X•₀(C)_E) = ⋃_ν (F_ν^{-1}pr₀^{-1}(x₀) ∩ U^E_ν) = (⋃_ν F_ν^{-1}pr₀^{-1}(x₀)) ∩ X̌₀(C)_E = C_{x₀} ∩ X̌₀(C)_E = C^E_{x₀}, using F_ν^{-1}(A ∩ X•₀(C)_E) = F_ν^{-1}A ∩ U^E_ν, U^E_ν = F_ν^{-1}X•₀(C) ∩ X̌₀(C)_E (Prop. 4.2), and C_{x₀} = ⋃_ν F_ν^{-1}pr₀^{-1}(x₀) (p. 31). By (R1),
  **cl_{X₀}(γ) = q(C^E_{x₀} × R^{>0}) = Γ^E_p,** both inclusions at once. ∎

Consistency of the two routes: Route I gives cl(γ) ⊆ Γ^E_p directly; Route II gives equality and, through (R4)–(R5), the complete list of limit points upstairs. They agree.

## 5. Break attempts (each failed; each pins down a hypothesis)

**5.1 Drop (Tors): the un-cut suspension.** In X₀^{uncut} := X̌₀(C) ×_{Q^{>0}} R^{>0} (all characters, no class E — not an admissible system), (R1)–(R4) go through unchanged and (R5) degenerates to cl_{X•₀(C)}(S) = π(K); so cl(γ) = q((⋃_ν F_ν^{-1}π(K)) × R^{>0}) ⊋ Γ_p. Concretely, n_k := ∏_{ℓ≤k, ℓ≠p} ℓ^k → 0 in Ẑ_{(p)} (v_ℓ(n_k) = k for k ≥ ℓ; check C4), so F_{n_k}P₀ = (x, χ^{a₀n_k}) → (x, 1) pointwise (ζ^{a₀n_k} = 1 as soon as ord(ζ) | n_k), and [π(x, 1), u] ∈ cl(γ). The point π(x, 1) has F_q π(x,1) = π(x,1) for every q ∈ Q^{>0} (F_n(x,1) = (x,1), hence F_{1/n}(x,1) = (x,1) in the colimit), so [π(x, 1), u] is a **fixed point of the flow** and is not in Γ_p. **The equality fails without (Tors); (Tors), which every admissible E carries by Def. 4.1, is exactly the hypothesis that makes it true.** This also confirms that the note's exclusion sentence, though incomplete (§6.1 F1), pointed at the right hypothesis. Answer to assignment item 3: the limits with a zero component (and those of class (ii)) are excluded **by (Tors)**, i.e. by the class E, not by the topology — they are honest pointwise limits in the un-cut chart; for the closure computed **in X₀** it does not matter *how* they are excluded, only that they are not points of X₀ (closure in a subspace is the trace of the ambient closure), and (R5) shows that every limit that *is* a point of X₀ is a packet point.

**5.2 Drop the quotient topology.** If X₀ carried a topology strictly coarser than the quotient topology, Route I's step 1 (descent of Φ) could fail and Route II's (R1) (openness of q) would have to be re-argued. No such topology is used anywhere in [x-03], [x-06] or [r3s-08] (§3.5), so this is not a break but the reason the convention must be stated.

**5.3 Subnets with unbounded denominators.** Could a net F_{r_α}P₀ with r_α = m_α/m′_α and the prime-to-p part of m′_α → ∞ converge in X̌₀(C)_E to a point outside C^E_{x₀}? No: any limit z lies in some open chart U^E_ν, so the net is eventually in U^E_ν, which forces (prime-to-p part of m′_α) | ν eventually ((R2)), contradicting unboundedness. And a net inside one chart cannot leave it (charts are closed as well as open). This is the assignment's "colimit finer than pointwise" worry, and it is harmless by (L1).

**5.4 Other strata.** Could cl(γ) meet the generic stratum or another prime's packet (assignment item 4)? No, by Route I: cl(γ) ⊂ Π^{-1}((p)). Also by Route II: every limit point of S̃ upstairs is β(b) at the **same** x. **5.5 A Galois-twisted limit outside the packet.** Could the G-saturation in (R3) create a limit at a *different* x′ over p that is not in pr₀^{-1}(x₀)? No: every point over x′ is G-conjugate to a point over x, and pr₀^{-1}(x₀) is by definition the set of all G-orbits over x₀ (p. 32).

**5.6 Vary E within admissibility.** For E ⊉ E_f the packet E-locus is a proper subset of C_{x₀}, and one might fear that the closure of an E-orbit picks up C_{x₀}-points outside the E-locus. It cannot: such points are not in X̌₀(C)_E, and closures are taken there ((R5), intersection with the E-locus). The E-dependence is entirely in which orbits exist, not in the closure law.

**5.7 Vary N₀ (outside the item, recorded for scope).** The ⊇ direction (Theorem A, and the ⊇ of (R5)) uses the density of N₀ in Ẑ_{(p)}, which holds for N₀ = ℕ and, by Dirichlet's theorem on primes in progressions [RU — not needed for the item], whenever char N₀ contains almost all primes; it fails for N₀ = p^ℕ (then S = {P₀} and cl(γ) = γ: every orbit closed, the packet a genuine (profinite) × (circle)). Route I's closedness of Γ^E_{x₀} holds for every N₀. So: closedness of packets is universal; the equality cl(γ) = Γ^E_{x₀} needs the Frobenius monoid to be dense, as it is for Spec Z.
