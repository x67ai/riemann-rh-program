## 3. The adjudicator's re-derivation (line by line, from the sources in §2)

Notation: X₀ the scheme; 𝕏₀ := X̌₀(C)_E ×_{Q₀^{>0}} R^{>0} the suspension; q : X̌₀(C)_E × R^{>0} ↠ 𝕏₀; π : X•(C) ↠ X•₀(C), π̌ : X̌(C) ↠ X̌₀(C) the Galois quotients; U_ν := F_ν^{-1}(X•₀(C)_E) ⊂ X̌₀(C)_E the charts.

### 3.1 The topology on 𝕏₀, settled

(a) *Chart.* X•(C) for affine X₀ = Spec R₀ carries pointwise convergence on multiplicative maps R → C (p. 40): Hausdorff, metrizable. For X₀ = Spec Z, R = Z̄. At a point x over (p), κ(x)^× = μ^{(p)}; every r ∉ 𝔭_x has r̄ of finite order prime to p; so at fixed x pointwise convergence of (x, P_α^×) is eventual agreement of P_α^×(ζ) for each ζ ∈ μ^{(p)} (values in a finite set of roots of unity, discrete in ℂ). ✓
(b) *Galois quotient.* X•₀(C) = X•(C)/G with the quotient topology (p. 42); π open (p. 43); G acts by homeomorphisms (Lemma 7.3) and jointly continuously (Prop. 7.5). ✓
(c) *Colimit.* X̌(C) has the inductive limit topology; X•(C) is clopen in it (Prop. 7.4 a) and each F_q is a homeomorphism (7.4 b), so every chart F_ν^{-1}(X•(C)) is clopen and F_ν maps it homeomorphically onto X•(C); the charts are nested (F_μ(X•(C)) ⊂ X•(C)) and cover (p. 24). X̌₀(C) = X̌(C)/G ≅ colim X•₀(C), π̌ open (p. 43). **Closure in the colimit is chart-local (L1 below), and on each chart the colimit topology IS the pointwise topology transported by F_ν.** A net converging to a point of the open chart U_ν is eventually inside U_ν; a net that is not eventually inside any chart has no limit. So no subnet limit exists "pointwise but not in the colimit" or conversely inside a chart. ✓
(d) *E-loci.* Subspace topologies on X•(C)_E, X•₀(C)_E; inductive limit = subspace topology on X̌(C)_E, X̌₀(C)_E because the charts are open (p. 47; re-derived: U_ν = F_ν^{-1}(X•₀(C)) ∩ X̌₀(C)_E by Prop. 4.2's backward invariance, open in the subspace topology, and covering). "All preceding results in this section remain true …" (p. 47). Closures in E-spaces are ambient closures intersected with the E-locus. ✓
(e) *Suspension.* [x-03] §6 (p. 38) defines 𝕏₀ as a set only. ### 3.4 Break attempts and necessity of the hypotheses

* *Drop (Tors).* In the un-cut suspension X̌₀(C) ×_Q R^{>0}, (R1)–(R4) go through and (R5) becomes cl(S) = π(K): with n_k := ∏_{ℓ≤k, ℓ≠p} ℓ^k → 0 in Ẑ_{(p)} (check C4), F_{n_k}P₀ → π(x, 1) and [P₀, n_k u] = [F_{n_k}P₀, u] → [π(x,1), u], a point whose Q^{>0}-isotropy is all of Q^{>0} (F_n(x,1) = (x,1), so F_{1/n}(x,1) = (x,1) in the colimit), hence not in Γ_p (isotropy p^Z, p. 32). Its R^{>0}-isotropy is Q^{>0} ⊂ R^{>0} — dense, but it is **not** a fixed point of the flow (contra referee F §5.1's phrase; Deninger's "There are no fixed points of the flow", p. 3, refers to the cut system). **Equality is false without (Tors).** Every admissible E carries (Tors): a scope fact, not an escape. Both referees found this (F §5.1, O B1).
* *Coarser topology on 𝕏₀.* Would void Π's continuity (Route I) and q's openness (Route II). Not an escape: the quotient topology is the sourced reading (§3.1 e). Recorded as the one hypothesis a reader must accept.
* *Unbounded denominators / Galois-created limits / other strata.* A net F_{r_α}P₀ converging in X̌₀(C)_E converges into some open chart U_ν, so the prime-to-p parts of its denominators eventually divide ν ((R2)) — no escape; Galois nets are absorbed by (L3) or by O's Lemma O.3; other strata are excluded by Route I (cl(γ) ⊂ Π^{-1}((p))). No counterexample exists to the statement as scoped.
* *N₀ not dense in Ẑ_{(p)}* (F §5.7, O B5). For X₀ = Spec Z this cannot arise: char X₀ is the set of all primes, so p. 24's standing assumption forces N₀ = ℕ; for X₀ flat of finite type over Spec Z, N₀ contains almost all primes (p. 62). Only for schemes whose residue characteristics form a proper subset of the primes (e.g. N₀ = p^ℕ over F_p-schemes) can the equality half fail while the closedness half (Route I) survives. Recorded as a precision on both referees' scope statements; outside the item.
* *Consistency with Cor. A.2 and Theorem A.* A compact circle dense in a strictly larger closed set, with uncountably many disjoint such circles, is impossible in a Hausdorff space and possible here precisely because 𝕏₀ is not Hausdorff along the packets. Since packets are closed AND (for E ⊇ E_f) compact, the Hausdorff failure of 𝕏₀ is not the "compact but not closed" pathology at packet level; it is internal to each packet (referee O §6.2 item 3, re-derived: compactness in §4 finding 11). The single-orbit case: if C^E_{x₀} is one Q-orbit (probe A's Theorem C(b) cut classes, Session-8 adjudication §4 item 5(b)), then Γ^E_p = γ is a **closed** orbit by Route I, so "no periodic orbit is closed as a subset" holds only for E whose packet E-locus contains at least two orbits (finding 12).

## 10. Adjudicator's observation (run 4; UNCERTIFIED; outside the item): the subspace topology of every packet, and of every periodic orbit, is indiscrete

Setting as in §3.3: X₀ = Spec Z, C = ℂ, N₀ = ℕ, E admissible, 𝕏₀ with the quotient topology (§3.1 (e)); γ = q({P₀} × R^{>0}) with P₀ = π(x, χ^{a₀}), a₀ ∈ Ẑ×_{(p)}, normalized as in §3.3; F_p P₀ = P₀ (pp. 32–33).

**Claim O-1 (a single orbit).** For all u, s > 0: [P₀, u/s] ∈ cl_{𝕏₀}({[P₀, u]}).
*Proof.* Put M_k := ∏_{ℓ ≤ k, ℓ ≠ p} ℓ^k, choose j_k with p^{j_k} > k·M_k/s, and choose an integer n_k ≡ 1 (mod M_k) in the interval (s·p^{j_k}(1 − 1/k), s·p^{j_k}(1 + 1/k)), whose length 2s·p^{j_k}/k exceeds M_k (check C9). Then n_k → 1 in Ẑ_{(p)} and r_k := n_k/p^{j_k} → s. Now (P₀, u)·r_k = (F_{r_k}P₀, u/r_k) = (F_{n_k}P₀, u/r_k), because F_{r_k} = F_{n_k} ∘ F_p^{−j_k} and F_p P₀ = P₀; and F_{n_k}P₀ = π(β(a₀ n_k)) → π(β(a₀)) = P₀ in the open chart X•₀(C)_E, hence in X̌₀(C)_E ((R4): β continuous, π continuous). So (P₀, u)·r_k → (P₀, u/s) in X̌₀(C)_E × R^{>0}. Let W be any open subset of 𝕏₀ containing [P₀, u/s]. Then q^{−1}(W) is open and Q^{>0}-saturated and contains (P₀, u/s), hence contains (P₀, u)·r_k for k large, hence contains (P₀, u); so [P₀, u] ∈ W. ∎
Consequently every open subset of 𝕏₀ that meets γ contains all of γ: **the subspace topology on every periodic orbit of 𝕏₀ is indiscrete.** No periodic orbit is a Hausdorff — or even a T₀ — subspace of 𝕏₀, for every admissible E (Deninger's "circles R^{>0}/N x₀^Z", p. 38, are statements about the R^{>0}-set, and Thm 7.10's continuous bijection from the Hausdorff space X•₀(C)_{p,in} ×_{p^Z} R^{>0} is, on each orbit, a bijection onto an indiscrete space — Remark 2 p. 47 in its most extreme form). Finding 8's non-wandering witness is the case s = 1.

**Claim O-2 (the packet).** For every unit b with χ^{a₀b} ∈ E and every s > 0: [π(x, χ^{a₀b}), u/s] ∈ cl{[P₀, u]}. *Proof.* As for O-1 with n_k ≡ b (mod M_k), so that F_{n_k}P₀ → π(x, χ^{a₀b}). ∎ Every point of Γ^E_p is of the form [π(x, χ^{c}), w] with c a unit and χ^c ∈ E (§3.3 normalization; take b = c/a₀, s = u/w), and Γ^E_p is closed (Route I). Hence **cl{z} = Γ^E_p for every z ∈ Γ^E_p** — this is run-1 referee O's Cor. O.7 (`B-corA1-O-run1-0809.md` line 374), re-derived here without its irrational s and Ẑ-subsequence — and therefore **the subspace topology of Γ^E_p is indiscrete**: an open subset of 𝕏₀ that meets Γ^E_p contains it (if z′ ∈ W ∩ Γ^E_p, then z′ ∈ cl{z} for every z ∈ Γ^E_p, so W ∋ z).
*Consistency.* This contradicts nothing in the sources: [x-03] p. 64's T₁ statement (via [LR00, Lemma 3.1]) concerns the generic-stratum model Y = Q^{>0}Ẑ× ×_{Q^{>0}} R^{>0} over the full ideles, where |r_k|_p = p^{j_k} → ∞ prevents the accumulation; the packets sit over the p-component-zero part Q^{>0}(Ẑ×_{(p)} × 0) of (64)/(68) (p. 49), where p is a unit and that constraint is absent (run-1 O's "dichotomy", re-derived). Cor. A.2's two-limit witness is a special case; "compact packets" (pp. 2–3) and "minimal set" (Cor. A.1) become trivial statements about an indiscrete space, while Prop. A.1′ (closedness in 𝕏₀) and the equality cl(γ) = Γ^E_p keep their content as statements about the topology of 𝕏₀.

**Consequences (all conditional on certification).** (i) **Q-a of the Session-8 record (§5) would be answered NO outright.** A subspace Y ⊂ 𝕏₀ that is Hausdorff — or merely T₀ — in its subspace topology meets each packet in at most one point: if γ ⊆ Y ∩ Γ^E_p, the subspace topology on γ induced from Y equals the one induced from 𝕏₀ (transitivity of subspace topologies), which is indiscrete by O-1, while a subspace of a T₀ space is T₀ — impossible for |γ| ≥ 2. So no compact Hausdorff-in-itself flow-invariant Y meets each packet in an orbit; more generally no subspace of 𝕏₀ that is a lamination (compact metrizable) in its subspace topology contains a periodic orbit, and the subspace half of Deninger's question ([x-03] p. 40) is NO in every reading — not only the closed one (Cor. So, once (i) is certified, Q\* collapses to Q-b. (iii) For general arithmetic schemes the same argument runs with N x₀ in place of p and N₀ dense in Ẑ_{(p)}; not written out here.
