## 3. Lemma A — the local principle in packet coordinates selects the Teichmüller class

### 3.1 The statement as printed (note §4)

> "**Lemma A (derived; consistency check against Thm 15.6 below).** Let o be as in §2 (residue field k algebraically closed of char p), κ an algebraically closed field of characteristic p, and P: κ → o multiplicative with P(0) = 0, P(1) = 1, whose restriction to κ^× is a group homomorphism into o (values automatically in μ^{(p)}(o), the prime-to-p roots of unity, since κ^× is prime-to-p torsion). Then |P(r+s) − P(r) − P(s)| ≤ |p| for all r, s ∈ κ **iff** P = [·]∘τ for a unique field embedding τ: κ ↪ k, where [·] is the Teichmüller section of the reduction μ^{(p)}(o) ≅ μ^{(p)}(k)."

Setting from [x-03] p. 89 / pp. 105–106 (A15): o is a p-adically complete rank-one valuation ring, C = Quot(o) algebraically closed of characteristic 0, m the maximal ideal, k = o/m algebraically closed of characteristic p; hence p ∈ m, |p| < 1, and every integer d prime to p is a unit of o (|d| = 1). "Defect ≤ |p|" means P(r+s) − P(r) − P(s) ∈ po (in a valuation ring, |x| ≤ |p| ⟺ x ∈ po).

### 3.2 Step 0 — the Teichmüller section on prime-to-p roots of unity (re-derived)

*Injectivity of reduction on μ^{(p)}(o).* Let ζ ∈ o with ζ^d = 1, d prime to p, and ζ ≡ 1 mod m. Then 0 = ζ^d − 1 = (ζ − 1)(ζ^{d−1} + ⋯ + ζ + 1), and the second factor is ≡ d mod m, a unit. Hence ζ = 1. So reduction is injective on the prime-to-p torsion of o^×. ✓ (the note's argument, checked.)

*Bijectivity onto μ^{(p)}(k).* For each n prime to p, C algebraically closed gives |μ_n(o)| = n (roots of unity are integral, hence in o), and k algebraically closed gives |μ_n(k)| = n; an injective map between two n-element sets is bijective. Taking the union over n: reduction μ^{(p)}(o) → μ^{(p)}(k) = μ(k) is an isomorphism of groups. (The note's "divisible-image" argument — an injective homomorphism between groups ≅ ⊕_{ℓ≠p} Q_ℓ/Z_ℓ has divisible, hence full, ℓ-primary images — is also correct: the only divisible subgroups of Q_ℓ/Z_ℓ are 0 and itself, since every proper subgroup is finite cyclic.) The inverse is the Teichmüller section [·]: μ(k) → μ^{(p)}(o), extended by [0] = 0. ✓

### 3.4 The stated generality is not correct — MAJOR A-1, with a counterexample

The lemma is stated for "κ an algebraically closed field of characteristic p". Three things go wrong as soon as κ is not algebraic over F_p (i.e. κ^× is not torsion — e.g. κ an algebraic closure of F_p(t)):

(a) *The parenthetical hypothesis is false.* "values automatically in μ^{(p)}(o) … since κ^× is prime-to-p torsion" — κ^× is not torsion, and if P is mod-p additive then P mod m = τ is a field embedding κ ↪ k, so for t transcendental over F_p the value P(t) reduces to the transcendental element τ(t) ∈ k and is therefore not a root of unity (roots of unity of o reduce to roots of unity of k, which are algebraic over F_p).

(b) *The right-hand side is not defined.* The note defines [·] only on μ^{(p)}(k) (Step 0). For τ(κ) ⊄ μ(k) ∪ {0} the expression [·]∘τ has no meaning. So as printed the biconditional is ill-formed for such κ. (For o = o_{C_p}, k = F̄_p, no field embedding τ: κ ↪ k exists at all, and by Proposition 14.14 no mod-p-additive P exists either; the statement is then vacuous, not false — but the parenthetical remains false and the lemma still asserts something about a "Teichmüller class" that does not exist.)

(c) *Under the natural completion of the definition the (⇒) direction is false.* Complete [·] to the general Teichmüller lift ♯∘s: k → o for the coefficient-field section s of [x-03] p. 106. Take k = an algebraic closure of F_p(T) and o = the valuation ring of the completion C of an algebraic closure of Quot W(k) (a p-adically complete rank-one valuation ring with algebraically closed quotient field of characteristic 0 and residue field k, as in §2 of the note; that the completion of an algebraically closed valued field is algebraically closed is **[RU-standard]**, Krasner). By A15, C^♭ is algebraically closed of characteristic p, and k = s(k) ⊂ o^♭ is a subfield. Pick ϖ ∈ m^♭ ∖ {0} with (1 + ϖ)^♯ ≠ 1 (for instance ϖ = p^♭ = (p, p^{1/p}, p^{1/p^2}, …) mod p: from x^♯ ≡ x̃_1^p mod p^2 one gets (1+p^♭)^♯ − 1 ≡ p + (terms of valuation ≥ 1 + 1/p) mod p^2, of absolute value exactly |p| ≠ 0). Put T′ := s(T)(1 + ϖ) ∈ (o^♭)^×. For every nonzero f ∈ F_p[X], f(T′) ≡ f(T) ≠ 0 mod m^♭ (T is transcendental over F_p in k), so |f(T′)| = 1: the valuation of C^♭ is trivial on F_p(T′) ≅ F_p(t). Let F ⊂ C^♭ be an algebraic closure of F_p(T′) inside the algebraically closed field C^♭; the valuation is trivial on F (every z ∈ F and z^{−1} is integral over the trivially valued F_p(T′), hence |z| ≤ 1 and |z^{−1}| ≤ 1). So F ⊂ o^♭ is an algebraically closed subfield of characteristic p with F^× not torsion. Set κ := F and χ^♭ := the inclusion κ ↪ o^♭, a ring homomorphism, and P := ♯∘χ^♭: κ → o. By Proposition 14.14 (A = κ), P is multiplicative, P(0) = 0, P(1) = 1, P(κ^×) ⊂ o^×, and P is additive mod p: **its defect is ≤ |p| everywhere.** Its reduction τ = P mod m is the reduction map F → k, a field embedding with τ(T′) = T. But P(T′) = (s(T)(1+ϖ))^♯ = s(T)^♯ · (1+ϖ)^♯ ≠ s(T)^♯ = [τ(T′)]. Since any τ′ with P = [·]∘τ′ must equal P mod m = τ, **P is mod-p additive and is not [·]∘τ for any field embedding τ: κ ↪ k.** The (⇒) direction fails. (What is true in general is exactly [x-03] Proposition 14.14: mod-p-additive multiplicative maps κ → o are the composites ♯∘χ^♭ with χ^♭: κ → o^♭ a ring embedding — a set that, for κ transcendental over F_p, is strictly larger than the Teichmüller class and depends on a non-unique coefficient field.)

*Break-vs-fill record.* Break: succeeded in the stated generality (above). Fill: the only fill is the hypothesis restriction κ = F̄_p, equivalently "κ^× torsion", equivalently "κ an algebraic closure of a finite field" — and for X₀ = spec Z every point x over a prime p has κ(x) ≅ F̄_p ([x-03] p. 31: "the residue field κ(x) is an algebraic closure of κ(x₀)"), which is the only situation the note applies Lemma A to (§4 Consequence 1, Consequence 2, and the consistency check). No downstream statement of the note consumes the general-κ case. Had the note used Lemma A at a non-closed characteristic-p point of a higher-dimensional scheme (κ(y) transcendental over F_p — e.g. [x-03] §14's points (x, y) with y a non-closed point of X ⊗ F_p), the defect would be FATAL for that use.

The gap is filled by a one-clause restriction of hypothesis, the restricted lemma is proved from on-disk sources, and nothing in D1–D3 rests on the unrestricted version. ## 6. Lemma B — the archimedean defect bound selects nothing on the periodic locus (D2)

### 11.2 What is now established at referee grade, and its precise scope
