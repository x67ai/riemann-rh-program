## 2. Lemma A — the local principle in packet coordinates selects the Teichmüller class

### 2.1 The note's statement (§4, quoted)

> Let o be as in §2 (residue field k algebraically closed of char p), κ an algebraically closed field of characteristic p, and P: κ → o multiplicative with P(0) = 0, P(1) = 1, whose restriction to κ^× is a group homomorphism into o (values automatically in μ^{(p)}(o), the prime-to-p roots of unity, since κ^× is prime-to-p torsion). Then |P(r+s) − P(r) − P(s)| ≤ |p| for all r, s ∈ κ **iff** P = [·]∘τ for a unique field embedding τ: κ ↪ k, where [·] is the Teichmüller section of the reduction μ^{(p)}(o) ≅ μ^{(p)}(k).

The o of the note's §2 is [x-03]'s §15 o (anchor A12): p-adically complete rank-one valuation ring, algebraically closed quotient field C of characteristic 0, algebraically closed residue field k of characteristic p. I take |p| < 1 throughout (if p were a unit of o the p-adic completeness hypothesis would be empty and nothing in §14–15 would make sense; Deninger's ω = P_y(p) ∈ m at p. 91 presupposes it).

### 2.2 Preliminaries re-derived

**(P0) Integers prime to p are units of o.** For a prime ℓ ≠ p, 1 = aℓ + bp with a, b ∈ Z; if |ℓ| < 1 then |1| ≤ max(|aℓ|, |bp|) < 1, absurd; so |ℓ| = 1 and hence |d| = 1 for every integer d prime to p. (Elementary; no Ostrowski needed.)

**(P1) Reduction is injective on μ^{(p)}(o).** Let ζ ∈ o, ζ^d = 1 with d prime to p, ζ ≡ 1 mod m; write ζ = 1 + y, y ∈ m. Then 0 = ζ^d − 1 = y·(d + C(d,2)y + … + y^{d−1}) = (ζ − 1)(d + x) with x ∈ m; by (P0) d + x is a unit, so ζ = 1. This is the note's argument; it is correct, and it is exactly where (P0) is used.

**(P2) Reduction μ^{(p)}(o) → μ^{(p)}(k) is surjective.** μ^{(p)}(C) ≅ ⊕_{ℓ≠p} Q_ℓ/Z_ℓ (C algebraically closed of characteristic 0) and every root of unity has |ζ| = 1, so μ^{(p)}(o) = μ^{(p)}(C); likewise μ^{(p)}(k) ≅ ⊕_{ℓ≠p} Q_ℓ/Z_ℓ (k algebraically closed of characteristic p). The reduction is a homomorphism, injective by (P1); its image is a divisible subgroup (homomorphic image of a divisible group) of each ℓ-primary component Q_ℓ/Z_ℓ; the only divisible subgroups of Q_ℓ/Z_ℓ are 0 and the whole (every proper subgroup is finite cyclic, and a finite nonzero group is not divisible); the image is nonzero by injectivity; hence the reduction is onto. This is the note's "divisible-image argument"; correct. [The isomorphism type ⊕ Q_ℓ/Z_ℓ of the prime-to-p roots of unity of an algebraically closed field is RU-classical.] Consequently the Teichmüller section [·]: μ^{(p)}(k) → μ^{(p)}(o) exists and is unique, and it is a group isomorphism.

### 2.3 Where the statement is false, and where it is true — finding A1 (MAJOR)

**The parenthetical is false in the stated generality.** An algebraically closed field κ of characteristic p has torsion multiplicative group if and only if κ is an algebraic closure of F_p: if κ ⊋ F̄_p then κ contains an element t transcendental over F_p (the elements algebraic over F_p form the subfield F̄_p), and t has infinite order. So "κ^× is prime-to-p torsion" holds iff κ ≅ F̄_p.

**The proof breaks without it.** The (⇒) step reads: "For r ∈ κ^×, P(r) and [τ(r)] are prime-to-p roots of unity with the same reduction, hence equal." For non-torsion r, P(r) is a unit of o (P(r)P(r^{−1}) = 1) with no reason to be a root of unity, and "[τ(r)]" is not even defined by the note's [·] (a section over μ^{(p)}(k) only). The (⇐) direction likewise needs [τ(r)] for all r ∈ κ, i.e. a Teichmüller map on all of k, which the note supplies only via the unjustified "W(k) ⊆ o" (finding A2).

**Break attempt — the lemma is false as stated (counterexample, constructed by the referee; RU-classical inputs flagged).** Take an o in Deninger's class whose residue field k contains an element u transcendental over F_p (for instance the valuation ring of the completion of an algebraic closure of Q_p(T) for the Gauss norm |Σ a_i T^i| = max |a_i|; the completion of an algebraically closed valued field is algebraically closed [RU-classical, Kürschák–Ostrowski], and its residue field is F̄_p(t)^{alg}-type, algebraically closed of characteristic p). Let κ := k and τ := id_k. By anchor A12 there is a ring embedding s: k ⊂ o^♭ with k ⊂ o^♭ → k the identity, and by anchor A16 (Prop. 14.14 with A = κ, so lim_{( )^p} A = A^♭ = κ because Frobenius is bijective on the perfect field κ) every ring homomorphism φ: κ → o^♭ yields a multiplicative P_φ := ♯∘φ: κ → o with P_φ(0) = 0, P_φ(1) = 1 and P_φ(I) ⊂ p·o, where I is generated by the elements [r+s] − [r] − [s] (anchor A13); i.e. |P_φ(r+s) − P_φ(r) − P_φ(s)| ≤ |p| for all r, s.
