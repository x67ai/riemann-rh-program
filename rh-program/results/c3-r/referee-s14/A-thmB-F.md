### 3.0 The topology on X₀, stated exactly (what continuity must be checked against)

By A9, A11, A14, A7 the space X₀ is built in four stages, each with a definite topology:
1. X̊(C)_E ⊂ X̊(C) ⊂ ℂ^{Z̄}: the topology of pointwise convergence of multiplicative maps P : Z̄ → ℂ (Tychonov subspace), restricted to the E-locus (subspace topology). Metrizable (Z̄ countable). — A9, A14.
2. X̊₀(C)_E = X̊(C)_E/G with the quotient topology, which equals the subspace topology of X̊₀(C) = X̊(C)/G; the projection π is continuous and open; X̊₀(C) is metrizable, separable, Hausdorff. — A11, A12, A14.
3. ### 3.8 Step B8 — from continuous injection to homeomorphic n-cube: the gap, and its filling (Lemmas F.0–F.2)

The note: "So Θ is a continuous injection of the n-cube into S. If S is Hausdorff in its subspace topology, Θ is a homeomorphism onto its image (compact to Hausdorff), so S contains an n-cell."

The note's conditional is correct: a continuous injection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space is a closed map (compact subsets go to compact, hence closed, subsets), hence a homeomorphism onto its image [RU, elementary; re-derived in this sentence]. **But the theorem's first sentence asserts the homeomorphic copy without the hypothesis** — MAJOR 1. The gap is filled by the following three lemmas, which I state and prove in full; they use only anchors A2, A7, A10, A11, A12, A14 and Fact 3.0.1.

**Lemma F.0 (kernel lemma).** Let P̃ = (x_η, Ψ) ∈ X̊(C) with Ψ injective on μ(Q̄), let P = π(P̃) ∈ X̊₀(C), and let q = ν/ν' ∈ Q>0 with ν, ν' ∈ N coprime. If F_q P lies in the stratum X̊₀(C) ⊂ X̌₀(C), then ν' = 1, i.e. q ∈ N.
*Proof.* F_q P = F_{ν'}^{-1} F_ν P (the Q>0-action on the colimit, A2/A11). It lies in the stratum F_1^{-1} X̊₀(C) iff there is Q ∈ X̊₀(C) with F_{ν'}^{-1}(F_ν P) = F_1^{-1} Q, iff (colimit equality rule, A2 p. 25) F_1 F_ν P = F_{ν'} Q, i.e. F_ν P = F_{ν'} Q in X̊₀(C). Lifting to X̊(C) and using that the G-action commutes with the F's (p. 22): F_ν P̃ = (F_{ν'} Q̃)σ = F_{ν'}(Q̃σ) for some σ ∈ G and some lift Q̃. By (51) (A10), F_{ν'}(X̊(C)) = {P' ∈ X̊(C) | P'(μ_{ν'}(K)) = 1}, so F_ν P̃ kills μ_{ν'}(Q̄): Ψ(ζ^ν) = 1 for all ζ ∈ μ_{ν'}(Q̄) (for the generic point, P̃(ζ) = Ψ(ζ) with no reduction). Since gcd(ν, ν') = 1, ζ ↦ ζ^ν is a bijection of μ_{ν'}(Q̄), so Ψ kills μ_{ν'}(Q̄). Ψ is injective on μ(Q̄), so μ_{ν'}(Q̄) = {1}, i.e. ν' = 1. ∎

**Lemma F.1 (Θ is a topological embedding).** Θ : [0, ½]^n → X₀ is a homeomorphism onto its image K_n := Θ([0, ½]^n), with K_n carrying the subspace topology of X₀ (equivalently, of any S ⊇ K_n with its subspace topology). No separation property of X₀ or of S is used.
*Proof.* Θ is continuous (3.6) and injective (3.7). It remains to show: if (t_α) is a net in the cube with Θ(t_α) → Θ(t) in X₀, then t_α → t. Suppose not. Then there are an open neighborhood N of t and a subnet (t_β) with t_β ∉ N; by compactness of the cube a further subnet (still written t_β) converges to some t* ∈ cube ∖ N, so t* ≠ t; and still Θ(t_β) → Θ(t) (subnets of convergent nets converge to the same limit). Let W := X̊₀(C)_{E_max} × R>0 ⊂ X̌₀(C)_{E_max} × R>0; W is open (open stratum, A11/A14). Let O' be any open neighborhood of π(P_t) in X̊₀(C)_{E_max} and O := O' × (½, 2) ⊂ W, open in the product. By Fact 3.0.1, q(O) is an open neighborhood of q(π(P_t), 1) = Θ(t). Hence eventually Θ(t_β) ∈ q(O): there is (P', u') ∈ O with q(P', u') = Θ(t_β) = q(π(P_{t_β}), 1), i.e. (P', u') = (π(P_{t_β}), 1)·q_β = (F_{q_β} π(P_{t_β}), q_β^{-1}) for some q_β ∈ Q>0 (A7). Since P' ∈ X̊₀(C)_{E_max} and Ψ_{t_β} is injective on μ(Q̄), Lemma F.0 gives q_β = ν_β ∈ N; then u' = 1/ν_β ∈ (½, 2) forces ν_β = 1. So (P', u') = (π(P_{t_β}), 1) and π(P_{t_β}) ∈ O'. As O' was an arbitrary neighborhood: π(P_{t_β}) → π(P_t) in X̊₀(C)_{E_max}, hence in X̊₀(C). But also π(P_{t_β}) → π(P_{t*}) in X̊₀(C) by continuity of t ↦ π(P_t) (stages 1–2 of 3.6) and t_β → t*. X̊₀(C) is Hausdorff (A12, Cor. 7.8; Spec Z is affine), so limits are unique: π(P_t) = π(P_{t*}). By 3.7(iii)–(iv) (which used only equality in X̊₀(C)), t = t*, contradicting t* ≠ t. ∎

**Lemma F.2 (K_n is closed in X₀).** The image K_n = Θ([0, ½]^n) is a closed subset of X₀.
*Proof.* Let A := {π(P_t) : t ∈ [0, ½]^n} ⊂ X̊₀(C)_{E_max} ⊂ X̌₀(C)_{E_max}. A is compact (continuous image of the cube) in the Hausdorff space X̌₀(C)_{E_max} (A12 Cor. 7.9 with A14), hence closed there. K_n = q(A × {1}), and q^{-1}(K_n) is the Q>0-saturation Sat = ∪_{q∈Q>0} F_q(A) × {1/q}. We show Sat is closed in X̌₀(C)_{E_max} × R>0. Let (F_{q_α} π(P_{t_α}), 1/q_α) → (P, u) be a convergent net with (P, u) in the product. Then q_α → 1/u in R>0. ### 3.9 Step B9 — the dimension-monotonicity step: exact theorem and hypotheses

The note: "If S is metrizable — every candidate lamination is — subspace monotonicity of covering dimension in separable metric spaces (Hurewicz–Wallman, classical) gives dim S ≥ n; more generally, for compact Hausdorff S the cell image is closed and closed-subspace monotonicity in normal spaces applies. As n was arbitrary, dim S = ∞."

What is needed and what is available (no dimension-theory text is on disk; the following is labeled [RU] and is universally standard, but by standing order 5 it carries no more weight than the note's own "classical" flag):
- (D1) dim [0,1]^n = n for the Čech–Lebesgue covering dimension (and for ind, Ind, which agree with dim on separable metric spaces). [RU: Hurewicz–Wallman, *Dimension Theory*, Thm IV 1 (I^n has dimension n) with Thm V 8 (coincidence on separable metric spaces); the "≥ n" half is the Lebesgue covering theorem.]
- (D2) Monotonicity. Two versions: (D2a) for **closed** subspaces F ⊆ Z of an arbitrary topological space, dim F ≤ dim Z when dim is defined by finite open covers and open refinements of bounded order — this one is **re-derived here**: a finite open cover {V_i} of F has V_i = U_i ∩ F with U_i open in Z; {U_i} ∪ {Z ∖ F} is a finite open cover of Z (F closed); any open refinement of it of order ≤ k+1 traces on F to an open refinement of {V_i} of order ≤ k+1. (D2b) for arbitrary subspaces of metrizable spaces (or separable metric spaces) — [RU: Hurewicz–Wallman Thm III 1 for separable metric; Engelking, *Dimension Theory*, for metrizable]. The Katětov–Smirnov definition of dim for Tychonoff spaces via finite **cozero** covers is not monotone on closed subspaces of non-normal spaces in general [RU]; this is the only reason "normal" appears in the standard statement, and it is why the note's phrase "closed-subspace monotonicity in normal spaces" is the standard citation.
- (D3) Compact-to-Hausdorff: re-derived in 3.8.

With Lemmas F.1–F.2, K_n ⊆ S is **closed** in S (closed in X₀, hence in S) and homeomorphic to [0,1]^n, whatever S is. So by (D1) + (D2a): **for the finite-open-cover covering dimension on arbitrary spaces, dim S ≥ n for all n, with no separation hypothesis at all**; for metrizable S all standard dimension functions agree and give the same. A separation-free corollary that is more useful than "dim S = ∞" (because S is never Hausdorff, see 3.10): **S admits no continuous injection into a metrizable space M of finite covering dimension** — a continuous injection f : S → M restricts on the compact K_n to a continuous injection into a Hausdorff space, hence an embedding, so M contains a closed copy of [0,1]^n and dim M ≥ n by (D1)+(D2a); n is arbitrary. In particular S is not, and does not inject continuously into, a finite-dimensional compact metrizable lamination.

**Assessment of B9 as written.** The note's two routes are both valid under their stated hypotheses (metrizable S; compact Hausdorff S). The precise theorem names/hypotheses were not stated; they are stated above (MINOR m4). But the hypotheses are never satisfied — next item.
