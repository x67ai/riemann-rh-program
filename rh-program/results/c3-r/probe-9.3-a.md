## 0. VERDICT (stated first): **UNDECIDED-WITH-PROGRESS** — with two halves DECIDED

## 1. Setup: the objects, reconstructed from the sources

Throughout, X₀ = Spec Z, K₀ = Q, K = Q̄, X = Spec Z̄ (normalization of X₀ in K), G = Aut(Q̄/Q), C = ℂ, N₀ = N. All references in this section were read on disk.

**1.1 Points.** For affine X₀, the points of X̊(C) are identified with multiplicative maps P: Z̄ → ℂ with P(0) = 0, P(1) = 1, p := P⁻¹(0) a prime ideal, and P factoring through Z̄/p, equivalently pairs (x, P×) with x ∈ X and P×: κ(x)^× → ℂ^× a character ([x-03] Remark 3.4, p. 23). The topology is pointwise convergence on Z̄ (Tychonov subspace; metrizable since Z̄ is countable) ([x-03] §7, p. 40). X̊₀(C) = X̊(C)/G with the quotient topology; for affine X₀ it is metrizable, separable, Hausdorff ([x-03] Prop. 7.7 and Cor. 7.8, pp. 44–45). X̌(C) = colim_N X̊(C) along the injections F_ν (P ↦ P ∘ ( )^ν; injectivity: [x-03] Prop. 4.2, p. 27), with the inductive-limit topology; the strata F_ν⁻¹X̊(C), F_ν⁻¹X̊₀(C) are **open** in X̌(C), X̌₀(C), and the same holds for the E-subspaces with their subspace topologies ([x-03] §7, p. 47). X̌₀(C) is Hausdorff for affine X₀ ([x-03] Cor. 7.9, p. 45).

**1.2 Admissible classes.** E is a class of characters χ: κ^× → ℂ^× on algebraically closed fields with: χ ∈ E ⟺ χ∘σ ∈ E (σ ∈ Aut κ), χ ∈ E ⟺ χ^ν ∈ E (ν ∈ N), and (Tors) (finite kernel on roots of unity) ([x-03] Def. 4.1, p. 27). Examples E_f ⊂ … ⊂ E_max ⊂ E_tors ([x-03] p. 28). **Observation (used below):** for X₀ = Spec Z the condition (Image) is vacuous — it constrains only characters of fields κ of positive characteristic with κ^× not torsion, and every closed-fiber residue field here is F̄_p, whose multiplicative group is torsion; char-0 fields are exempt by definition ([x-03] p. 27). So over Spec Z, E_tors and E_max impose identical conditions fiberwise.

**1.3 The dynamical system.** X₀ = X̌₀(C)_E ×_{Q>0} R>0, the quotient of X̌₀(C)_E × R>0 by (P₀, u)q = (F_q(P₀), q⁻¹u); flow φ^t[P₀, u] = [P₀, u e^t] ([x-03] §6, p. 38). The quotient map q is continuous by definition of the quotient topology, and **open** (saturations of opens are unions of homeomorphic translates). Closures in X₀ can be computed upstairs: for A ⊆ X₀, the closure of A equals q(closure of q⁻¹(A)), because the closure of a Q>0-invariant set is Q>0-invariant (the group acts by homeomorphisms), hence saturated and closed. This elementary fact is used without further comment.

**1.4 Packets ([x-03] §5–6).** Fix the prime p, x₀ = (p) ∈ Spec Z, a point x ∈ X over p (κ(x) = F̄_p), and ι: μ(Q̄) ↪ μ(ℂ). The reduction isomorphism i_x: μ^{(p)}(Q̄) → κ(x)^× gives the injective character χ = χ_x = ι ∘ i_x⁻¹: κ(x)^× ↪ ℂ^× ([x-03] (32) and following, pp. 31–32). Every character of F̄_p^× with finite cyclic kernel is χ ∘ ( )^a ∘ ( )^ν =: χ^{aν} for (a, ν) ∈ Ẑ×_{(p)} × N, with (a, ν), (a′, ν′) giving the same character iff ν′ = νpⁿ, a = pⁿa′ ([x-03] (35), p. 32; identically [r3s-08] (2.2.7), p. 16). Galois acts through Gal(κ(x)/F_p) ↪ Aut(κ(x)^×) = Ẑ×_{(p)}, with image the closed subgroup p^Ẑ (topological closure of ⟨p⟩) ([x-03] (34), p. 32); this inclusion **is** Morishita's arithmetic linking homomorphism lk_p ([r3s-08] (1.2.1)–(1.2.3) and p. 16: "the map lk_p is nothing but the arithmetic linking homomorphism"). Consequences ([x-03] (38)–(40), pp. 32–33; Thm 5.2, p. 34; §6 pp. 38–39; Thm 6.1, p. 39):

- C_{x₀} ≅ (Ẑ×_{(p)}/p^Ẑ) ×_{p^Z} Q>0 as Q>0-sets; every point has isotropy exactly p^Z.
- Γ_{x₀} = C_{x₀} ×_{Q>0} R>0 ⊂ X₀; all R-orbits in Γ_{x₀} are circles of length log p; Γ_{x₀} fibres over the compact group **B_p := Ẑ×_{(p)}/p^Ẑ = Aut(F̄_p^×)/lk_p(Gal(F̄_p/F_p))** with fibres the orbits. The fibration map depends on the choices (x, ι); the phase projection C_{x₀} → Q>0/p^Z is canonical ([x-03] (40), p. 33).
- Theorem 6.1: the set of points of X₀ with nontrivial isotropy is exactly ∐_{x₀} Γ^E_{x₀}, and Γ^E_{x₀} = Γ_{x₀} whenever E ⊇ E_f ([x-03] p. 39 and Thm 5.2).
- B_p is an infinite compact metrizable totally disconnected group — infinite because it surjects, for every finite set T of odd primes ≠ p, onto (∏_{ℓ∈T} (Z/ℓ)^×/squares)/⟨image of p⟩ ≅ (C₂)^{|T|}/⟨one element⟩ of order ≥ 2^{|T|−1}; an infinite profinite group is perfect (homogeneity: one isolated point would force discreteness, hence finiteness) and so B_p is **a Cantor set** (Brouwer characterization, classical), in particular uncountable. Morishita states the homeomorphism Γ_p ≅ Ẑ×_{(p)}/p^Ẑ ×_{p^Z} R+ (mapping torus of multiplication by lk_p(p), which is trivial on the quotient B_p, so Γ_p ≅ B_p × S¹ as a flow space, the flow rotating the circle factor and fixing the base) ([r3s-08] Thm 2.2.9(1)(2), pp. 17–18, citing [x-03] §§5–7; [x-03] itself states the corresponding statements as bijections). Nothing below depends on the homeomorphism claim; it is used only descriptively.

**1.5 What "the packet fibration is Galois-monodromy-forced" is taken to mean.** Morishita's §2.3 makes "monodromy" precise at the level of coverings: in the Deninger system X_L for L ⊇ Q(μ^{(p)}), the preimage of one closed orbit γ_{p,a} ⊂ Γ_p ⊂ X_Q is a disjoint union of **lines** γ_{P,a} ≅ R+, and the deck monodromy around γ_{p,a} is multiplication by p, i.e. by lk_p(σ_p) on the unwound base Ẑ×_{(p)} ([r3s-08] Thm 2.2.8, Thm 2.3.1, pp. 16–19). Downstairs in X_Q this monodromy is already quotiented out — it is *why* the orbits close up at length log p at all — and the packet base is exactly the **cokernel of lk_p**: B_p = Ẑ×_{(p)}/lk_p(p^Ẑ). The ledger's kill hypothesis ("every subsystem inheriting the orbit of one prime must inherit its whole packet closure") is therefore formalized here as a statement about **closed flow-invariant subsets of X₀** (notion N1 below), and separately about **equivariant maps into X₀** (notion N2), because S4 per [x-03] §6 p. 40 allows both readings:

- **(N1) Embedded subsystem:** a closed, flow-invariant subset Y₀ ⊆ X₀ (with subspace topology). "Closed" is the honest reading of "sub-dynamical system"; the compact laminations S4 asks for are Hausdorff and metrizable, and a compact subset of a Hausdorff ambient is closed, so if X₀ is Hausdorff (open question, see G1 in §7) N1 covers all compact candidates; if X₀ is not Hausdorff, Theorems A and B below still apply verbatim to all *closed* invariant candidates.
- **(N2) Mapped-in system:** a compact 3-dimensional lamination Y₀ with its own R-flow and a continuous R-equivariant map f: Y₀ → X₀ such that for every prime p some closed orbit of Y₀ of length log p maps onto a periodic orbit in Γ_p.

The ledger's candidate mechanism attributed the forcing to "minimality of the translation flow on the fiber". That is not the actual mechanism: **the flow acts trivially on the base B_p** (it rotates each circle fiber; §1.4), so no orbit is dense in its packet *under the flow semigroup alone*, and individual orbits are honest compact circles. The true mechanism, proved next, is the density of the Frobenius monoid: the suspension identifications [P₀, u] = [F_m(P₀), u/m] (m ∈ N) twist the packet-base coordinate by m, and N is dense in Ẑ_{(p)} = ∏_{ℓ≠p} Z_ℓ, whose unit group sweeps all of B_p. In Morishita's language: not the monodromy lk_p of the one prime p (that is already the reason the base is B_p rather than Ẑ×_{(p)}), but the joint monodromy of **all** Frobenii — the linking of p with all other primes at once — closes up the packet.

## 2. THEOREM A (the packet-closure law)

**Theorem A.** Let X₀ = Spec Z, let E be **any** admissible class ([x-03] Def. 4.1) with E ⊆ E_max, and let X₀ = X̌₀(C)_E ×_{Q>0} R>0. Let p be a prime, and let γ be any periodic orbit contained in the packet Γ^E_{(p)}. Then the closure of γ in X₀ contains the entire packet Γ^E_{(p)}. Equivalently: **all periodic orbits in one packet have the same closure, and every closed flow-invariant subset of X₀ containing one of them contains all of them.**

**Proof.**

*Step 1 (normalization).* By [x-03] (35)/(38) (pp. 32–33) and Thm 5.2 (p. 34), every point of C^E_{(p)} lies on the Q>0-orbit of a point (x, χ^{a₀}) with a₀ ∈ Ẑ×_{(p)} an injective character exponent: indeed each isotropy point has character χ^{aν} = F_ν(χ^a), and F_ν preserves Q>0-orbits; χ^a ∈ E ⟺ χ^{aν} ∈ E by admissibility (Def. 4.1). So we may write γ = {[P₀, w] : w ∈ R>0} with P₀ = (x, χ^{a₀}) ∈ X̊₀(C)_E, a₀ ∈ Ẑ×_{(p)}, χ^{a₀} ∈ E. (The orbit is the circle R>0/p^Z since [P₀, u] = [F_p(P₀), u/p] and F_p(P₀) = (x, χ^{pa₀}) = (x, χ^{a₀} ∘ Frob) = P₀ in X̊₀(C) — Frobenius lies in the Galois image p^Ẑ; this is Thm 6.1's isotropy statement.)

*Step 2 (the target).* An arbitrary point of Γ^E_{(p)} has, by the same normalization, the form [P₁, v] with P₁ = (x, χ^{c}), c ∈ Ẑ×_{(p)}, χ^c ∈ E, v ∈ R>0. Fix such (c, v).

*Step 3 (Frobenius approximation).* Since N is dense in Ẑ_{(p)} = lim_{(M,p)=1} Z/M (Chinese remainder theorem), choose a sequence of positive integers m_n → c·a₀⁻¹ in Ẑ_{(p)}. Consider the points on γ:
  z_n := [P₀, m_n v] ∈ γ.
Using the defining relation with q = m_n ∈ Q>0: (P₀, m_n v)·m_n = (F_{m_n}(P₀), v), so z_n = [F_{m_n}(P₀), v], with F_{m_n}(P₀) = (x, χ^{m_n a₀}).

*Step 4 (pointwise convergence upstairs).* Claim: (x, χ^{m_n a₀}) → (x, χ^{c}) in X̊(C). The topology is pointwise convergence of the multiplicative maps P_n: Z̄ → ℂ, P_n(r) = χ(r̄^{m_n a₀}) for r̄ := r mod x ≠ 0 and P_n(r) = 0 for r ∈ x ([x-03] Remark 3.4 p. 23, §7 p. 40). Fix r ∈ Z̄. If r ∈ x both sides are 0. If r̄ ≠ 0 then r̄ ∈ F̄_p^× has finite order d prime to p; since m_n → ca₀⁻¹ in Ẑ_{(p)} and d is invertible-compatible (d | M for suitable moduli M prime to p), eventually m_n ≡ ca₀⁻¹ (mod d), hence m_n a₀ ≡ c (mod d) and χ(r̄^{m_n a₀}) = χ(r̄^{c}). So P_n(r) is eventually equal to P_∞(r) := value of (x, χ^c); pointwise convergence holds (indeed eventually-pointwise-constant).

*Step 5 (convergence in the right spaces).* Every point (x, χ^{m_n a₀}) lies in X̊₀(C)_E: (Tors) holds (kernel = μ_{m_n'} with m_n' the prime-to-p part of m_n, finite), and χ^{m_n a₀} = (χ^{a₀})^{m_n} ∈ E by admissibility. The limit (x, χ^c) lies in X̊₀(C)_E by hypothesis (χ^c ∈ E; note c is a unit so the character is injective — for E ⊇ E_f membership is automatic). Applying the continuous quotient X̊(C) → X̊₀(C) ([x-03] §7 p. 41–44) and passing to the subspace X̊₀(C)_E ([x-03] p. 47), we get convergence in X̊₀(C)_E, hence in X̌₀(C)_E (the stratum is an open subspace, [x-03] p. 47). Therefore (F_{m_n}(P₀), v) → (P₁, v) in X̌₀(C)_E × R>0.

*Step 6 (push down).* The quotient map q: X̌₀(C)_E × R>0 → X₀ is continuous, so z_n = q(F_{m_n}(P₀), v) → q(P₁, v) = [P₁, v]. Since z_n ∈ γ for all n, every neighborhood of [P₁, v] meets γ, i.e. [P₁, v] ∈ closure(γ). As (c, v) was arbitrary, closure(γ) ⊇ Γ^E_{(p)}. ∎

**Remarks.** (i) No Hausdorffness of X₀ is used: only continuity of q and the metrizable topology upstairs. (ii) The proof works verbatim for any closed point x₀ of any arithmetic X₀ (replace p by Np and Ẑ×_{(p)}/p^Ẑ by Ẑ×_{(p)}/Np^Ẑ, [x-03] (39) p. 33); it is stated for Spec Z because that is the S4 case. (iii) Deninger's simile "the compact packets Γ_{x₀} are reminiscent of invariant tori" ([x-03] p. 2) is thus given exact content: relative to the ambient X₀, a packet behaves like a single quasi-orbit — the orbit space of Γ_p is the Cantor group B_p as a topological space, but the orbit-**closure** relation restricted to the packet is total. (iv) Neither [x-03], [x-06], nor [r3s-08] states this; [x-03] §8 computes only the closure of the union of **all** periodic orbits, and [r3s-08] only the covering monodromy. Checked on disk this session; the ledger's §1 prior-art scan (2026-08-26) found no other literature on these spaces.

## 3. THEOREM B (the embedded kill)

**Theorem B.** Let X₀ = Spec Z and E_f ⊆ E ⊆ E_max ([x-03]'s designated window: Γ^E = Γ by Thm 5.2/6.1, and connectedness/H⁰ hold for E ⊇ E_f by [x-03] §§9–10). Let S ⊆ X₀ be closed and flow-invariant, and suppose that for every prime p, S contains at least one periodic orbit of Γ_{(p)}. Then:

**(a)** S contains **every** periodic orbit of every packet — in particular, for each p, uncountably many pairwise distinct closed orbits of primitive length log p (parametrized by the Cantor group B_p).

**(b)** If moreover E = E_max (equivalently E_tors over Spec Z, §1.2), then S contains, for every n ≥ 1, a homeomorphic copy of the n-cube. Hence every closed invariant S as above **whose subspace topology is Hausdorff** (in particular: every candidate compact metrizable lamination) has covering dimension ≥ n for all n, i.e. is infinite-dimensional.

**Corollary.** (i) For every E in the window E_f ⊆ E ⊆ E_max, no closed flow-invariant subsystem of X₀ meeting every packet can serve as the S4 object: by (a) its closed-orbit count per prime is uncountable, violating the target coefficient 1 (ledger §3, T1) and the ALKL finiteness hypothesis H4 (finitely many closed orbits with lengths in a compact interval; ledger §2) — this is independent of any dimension consideration. (ii) For E = E_max (= the system in which [x-03] §8 operates) and for the unitary system Y₀^Den = X̌₀(S¹) ×_{Q>0} R>0 — the two spaces the ledger's §9.3 names — there is in addition **no** compact finite-dimensional (in any dimension, in particular 3) lamination Y₀ ⊆ X₀, closed and flow-invariant, containing at least one periodic orbit in Γ_{(p)} for every p, by (b). The first alternative of Deninger's question ([x-03] §6 p. 40) has answer **NO**.

**Proof of (a).** Immediate from Theorem A: S contains one orbit of Γ_{(p)}, is closed and invariant, hence contains closure(γ) ⊇ Γ_{(p)} = Γ^E_{(p)} (packets are full for E ⊇ E_f, [x-03] Thm 5.2 p. 34). By [x-03] Thm 6.1 (p. 39) the union ∐_p Γ_{(p)} is exactly the set of periodic points. The orbits are parametrized by B_p ([x-03] (39) p. 33), which is an infinite compact perfect totally disconnected metrizable group, hence uncountable.

**Proof of (b).** By (a), S ⊇ ∐_p Γ_{(p)} = all periodic points of the system (suspended; [x-03] Thm 6.1). S closed ⟹ S contains the closure of this set in X₀^{E_max}. Now [x-03] Thm 8.2 (p. 50; unconditional for Spec Z — Claim 8.1 is known for number rings via [Per11, Theorem 1], [x-03] p. 50) states X̌₀(C)_per‾ = X̌₀(S¹): every unitary-character point is a limit of periodic points, and the approximants produced by its Lemma 8.3 (pp. 50–53) have finite-kernel characters, hence lie in X̌₀(C)_{E_f} ⊆ X̌₀(C)_{E_max}. So for any unitary target point that itself lies in the E_max-locus, the approximation takes place inside the subspace X̌₀(C)_{E_max} (a sequence in a subspace converging in the ambient space to a point of the subspace converges in the subspace topology). Suspending (§1.3): S contains [Ψ, v] for every unitary generic-fiber point Ψ in the E_max-locus and every phase v.

Now construct the cells. Let x_η ∈ X be the generic point ((0) ⊂ Z̄, κ(x_η) = Q̄). Write Q̄^× = μ(Q̄) × V with V uniquely divisible (μ(Q̄) is divisible, hence a direct summand), a Q-vector space of countable dimension. Fix distinct primes ℓ_1, …, ℓ_n; their V-components v_1, …, v_n are Q-linearly independent (a multiplicative relation ∏ℓ_i^{k_i} ∈ μ(Q̄) ∩ Q^× = {±1} forces k = 0). Complete to a Q-basis and, for t ∈ [0, ½]^n, define the unitary character Ψ_t: Q̄^× → S¹ by: Ψ_t = inclusion on μ(Q̄) ⊂ S¹; on the line Q·v_i: Ψ_t(v_i^{a}) := e^{2πi t_i a} (a ∈ Q); trivial on the remaining basis lines. Each Ψ_t is a well-defined character, unitary, with ker Ψ_t ∩ μ(Q̄) = 1, so (Tors) holds; (Image) is vacuous in characteristic 0 ([x-03] p. 27). Hence (x_η, Ψ_t) ∈ X̊₀(S¹) ∩ E_max-locus for every t, and by Thm 8.2 each [Ψ_t, 1] lies in the closure of the periodic set, hence in S.

The map Θ: [0, ½]^n → S, t ↦ [Ψ_t, 1], is continuous: for each r ∈ Z̄∖{0}, decomposing r = ζ_r · ∏v_i^{a_i(r)} · w_r in the fixed splitting, Ψ_t(r) = ι(ζ_r)·e^{2πi⟨t, a(r)⟩}, continuous in t; pointwise convergence is the topology upstairs, and the maps to X̊₀ and to the suspension are continuous.

Θ is injective: suppose [Ψ_t, 1] = [Ψ_{t'}, 1]. Then (Ψ_{t'}, 1) = (F_q Ψ_t, q⁻¹) for some q ∈ Q>0, and comparing phases forces q = 1, so Ψ_{t'} and Ψ_t are equal as points of X̌₀(C). Since the colimit is along injections F_ν ([x-03] Prop. 4.2, p. 27), each stratum embeds, so equality holds already in X̊₀(C): Ψ_{t'} = Ψ_t ∘ σ for some σ ∈ G. Evaluate at the **rational** points ℓ_i (fixed by every σ ∈ G): Ψ_{t'}(ℓ_i) = Ψ_t(ℓ_i), i.e. c_i e^{2πi t'_i} = c_i e^{2πi t_i} with the constant c_i = ι(ζ_{ℓ_i}); since t_i, t'_i ∈ [0, ½], t' = t. 

So Θ is a continuous injection of the n-cube into S. If S is Hausdorff in its subspace topology, Θ is a homeomorphism onto its image (compact to Hausdorff), so S contains an n-cell. If S is metrizable — every candidate lamination is — subspace monotonicity of covering dimension in separable metric spaces (Hurewicz–Wallman, classical) gives dim S ≥ n; more generally, for compact Hausdorff S the cell image is closed and closed-subspace monotonicity in normal spaces applies. As n was arbitrary, dim S = ∞. ∎

**Scope notes (honesty).** (i) For E strictly between E_f and E_max the cell characters Ψ_t (which have infinite kernel) may fall outside E; part (a) — which already kills the counting — holds for the whole window, while (b) as stated needs E = E_max or the unitary system Y₀^Den = X̌₀(S¹) ×_{Q>0} R>0 itself (where the cells live natively and the identical argument applies). Both spaces named by ledger §9.3 ("X₀, or X̌₀(S¹)×_{Q>0}R_{>0}") are covered. (ii) The Hausdorffness hypothesis in (b) is on the **subsystem**, not on X₀; every lamination in the sense of S4 is metrizable, so nothing is lost for the kill. (iii) Classical inputs: CRT-density of N in Ẑ_{(p)}; direct-summand property of divisible subgroups; compact-to-Hausdorff homeomorphism criterion; subspace monotonicity of covering dimension. All standard; flagged for the referee.

## 4. THEOREM C (the admissible-class mechanism, completely analyzed — a correction found mid-probe)

An earlier draft of this section claimed that no admissible class can cut a packet below two orbits; re-derivation of the compressed step showed that claim is **false**, and the truth is sharper in both directions. Everything below was re-derived twice.

**4.1 The reachability computation.** Fix the reference injective character χ = χ_x of F̄_p^× (§1.4). Every character of F̄_p^× is χ^{c} for a unique c ∈ Ẑ_{(p)} = ∏_{ℓ≠p} Z_ℓ: indeed F̄_p^× ≅ ⊕_{ℓ≠p} Q_ℓ/Z_ℓ, and Hom(Q_ℓ/Z_ℓ, ℂ^×) = Hom(Q_ℓ/Z_ℓ, Q_ℓ/Z_ℓ) = Z_ℓ (every homomorphic image of an ℓ-divisible ℓ-torsion group consists of ℓ-power-order roots of unity). The kernel of χ^c is finite iff c ∈ N·Ẑ×_{(p)} (finitely many ℓ with v_ℓ(c) > 0, all finite). The generating moves of admissibility ([x-03] Def. 4.1) act on exponents as: (1) σ-twist: c ↦ cσ with σ ∈ p^Ẑ (Aut F̄_p = Gal(F̄_p/F_p), image in Aut(F̄_p^×) = Ẑ×_{(p)} equal to the closure p^Ẑ — [x-03] (34) p. 32, = lk_p per [r3s-08] p. 16); (2) power: c ↦ cν, ν ∈ N; (3) root: c ↦ c/ν, allowed exactly when c/ν ∈ Ẑ_{(p)}, and the root character is then **unique** (Hom(F̄_p^×, μ_ν) = 0 since F̄_p^× is divisible). Hence the minimal admissible class E(χ^{a₀}) containing an injective χ^{a₀} consists (in residue characteristic p) of the characters with exponent in
  { a₀ · σ · (m/n) : σ ∈ p^Ẑ, m, n ∈ N, v_ℓ(m/n) ≥ 0 for all ℓ ≠ p }.
The **injective** members have exponents with all valuations zero, forcing m/n = p^j, i.e. exponent ∈ a₀·p^Ẑ: **a single base class [a₀] ∈ B_p.** (In particular the unit parts u(ℓ) of primes ℓ ≠ p are NOT reachable — this is exactly where the earlier draft erred, and it is why the closure in Theorem A strictly exceeds the admissibility class: the closure uses limits m_n → β ∈ Ẑ×_{(p)}, which are not admissibility moves.)

**Theorem C.** (a) For every admissible E with E ⊇ E_f, every nonempty packet is full: Γ^E_{x₀} = Γ_{x₀}, with uncountably many closed orbits ([x-03] Thm 5.2 p. 34, "If e.g. E ⊃ E_f then C^E_{x₀} = C_{x₀}"). This covers every class Deninger designates by name (E_f, E_fg, …; [x-06] §4 p. 11) and every system for which [x-03] proves connectedness (§9, stated for E ⊃ E_f), H⁰_F = R (§10), or the periodic-closure identification (§8). (b) Conversely, the minimal admissible class E(χ^{a₀(p)}) (one chosen a₀(p) per prime, arbitrary; empty or arbitrary in characteristic 0) is admissible in the sense of Def. 4.1 and its system X₀^E has **exactly one closed orbit of length log p per prime** — the Q>0-orbit of (x, χ^{a₀(p)}), since the E-locus of the packet is the single orbit's own point set (the σ-twists are Galois-identifications and the powers/roots move along the same Q>0-orbit).

**Proof.** (a) is [x-03]'s statement. (b) is the reachability computation: C^E_{(p)} = C_{(p)} ∩ E-locus = the set of points with exponents a₀σ(m/n) as above; all of these lie on the single Q>0-orbit of (x, χ^{a₀}) — σ-twists give the same point of X̊₀(C) (Galois quotient), and exponent m/n·p^j-moves are the F_{m/n}-action. Isotropy and length are as in Thm 6.1. ∎

**4.2 Why (b) is not an S4 substrate — the price list.** The cut system forfeits, at one stroke, every property [x-03] certifies: the connectedness proof (Thm 9.x needs density of the E_f-generic fiber), H⁰_F = R (Thm 10.2 rides on §9), the closure identification (Thm 8.2's approximants are arbitrary finite-kernel characters, almost none of which survive the cut), and Cor. 4.4's surjectivity argument. Whether any of these can be re-proved for a cut class is completely open; Deninger's printed complaint that his minimal workable E "does not look natural" ([x-03] intro p. 5) and his remark that the correct local selection ("P additive mod p") **"is not N-invariant"** ([x-03] §4 Remark, p. 29) both live exactly here. Note the choice content: one arbitrary point a₀(p) of the Cantor group B_p for every prime — no canonical selection is provided by anything in the framework (consistent with ledger R15: no canonical transverse measure/selection exists in [x-03]).

**4.3 The cut system still dies as an embedded substrate, modulo G1.** The cut space X̌₀(C)_{E(a)} is a flow-invariant subspace of the full E_max-space, but by Theorem A it is **not closed** in it (the closure of its one orbit at p contains the full packet, which the cut space omits). Then Y₀ is a compact subset of the Hausdorff space X₀^{E_max}, hence closed there; Theorem A applied **in the ambient system** forces Y₀ ⊇ Γ_{(p)} (the full packet), contradicting Y₀ ⊆ X₀^{E(a)}. With Theorem B(b) the same ambient argument makes any compact invariant subset of X₀^{E_max} meeting every packet infinite-dimensional, whatever sub-class it was drawn from. **So the entire admissible-class escape route reduces to the single topological question G1**; only a failure of Hausdorffness of the suspension could keep it alive, and then only through compact-but-not-closed pathology that no lamination-based S4 reading intends.

## 5. What this decides, in the ledger's own terms

- **"If every subsystem inheriting the orbit of one prime must inherit its whole packet closure, S4 dies"** (ledger §9.3). The antecedent is now a **theorem for embedded subsystems** (Theorem A), and the consequence for them is total (Theorem B): the embedded reading of S4 is dead — not merely "no 3-dimensional one", but no finite-dimensional one, and none with countable orbit multiplicity. This also upgrades ledger rows R4/R6/W7: Deninger's open question [x-03] §6 p. 40 is now half-answered (the "Y₀ ⊂ X₀" half: NO), by an argument not present in [x-03], [x-06], or [r3s-08].
- **"Whether the map to X₀ must be injective on orbit closures":** for any candidate with closed image the question is moot — by Theorem A a closed invariant image containing one packet orbit contains the whole packet, so **injectivity on a packet is impossible; the restriction of any such map over a packet is all-or-nothing.**
- **"Whether a section of the packet fibration can be flow-equivariant, and whether lk_p obstructs continuous sections":** the fibration Γ_p → B_p is (per [r3s-08] Thm 2.2.9) a trivial Cantor-bundle of circles, so continuous sections exist and lk_p poses no obstruction to sections; "flow-equivariant section" is degenerate since the flow is trivial on B_p. The obstruction is **not** at the section level. It is at the closure level (Theorem A) — the ambient topology of X₀ glues the packet into one indecomposable closed invariant piece. The ledger's phrase "Galois-monodromy-forced" survives with corrected content: the forcing monodromy is the closure of the full Frobenius monoid (joint linking data of all primes; N‾ = Ẑ_{(p)}), of which lk_p accounts only for the already-quotiented p^Ẑ.

## 6. Why the MAPPING half does not die by this mechanism (the escape, taken seriously)

Let f: Y₀ → X₀ be continuous and R-equivariant, Y₀ a compact metrizable finite-dimensional flow space (the S4 lamination). Equivariance gives: the image of an orbit of Y₀ is exactly one orbit of X₀, and images of minimal sets are minimal sets. Two honest observations, one negative and one positive for the kill:

**6.1 What IS forced.** If X₀ is Hausdorff (open, G1 below), then f(Y₀) is compact hence closed and invariant, so by Theorems A/B: f(Y₀) ⊇ Γ_p for **every** p (once it meets each packet), and f(Y₀) contains the n-cells of Theorem B. Consequences for Y₀: for each p and each b ∈ B_p, pick y with f(y) ∈ γ_{p,b}; the orbit closure of y contains a minimal set M, and f(M) is a minimal subset of the minimal circle γ_{p,b}, hence f(M) = γ_{p,b}. Distinct b give disjoint M (two minimal sets coincide or are disjoint, and their images differ). So **Y₀ must contain, for every p, an uncountable family of pairwise disjoint flow-minimal sets mapping onto the Cantor family of packet circles**, while carrying (per S4's counting) only ONE closed orbit of length log p. 

**6.2 Why this is not a contradiction.** None of the following cheap kills closes:
- *Dimension:* Γ_p ≅ B_p × S¹ is a 1-dimensional compact space (Cantor × circle); it embeds in R³. A 3-dimensional lamination can contain solenoid-like families of uncountably many disjoint minimal sets (e.g. any foliation by invariant tori or a solenoidal sub-lamination). The infinite-dimensionality of f(Y₀) forced by 6.1 does **not** bound dim Y₀: continuous surjections raise dimension arbitrarily when fibers are large (an arc maps onto the Hilbert cube), and equivariance constrains only the flow direction. No dimension-theoretic contradiction is available without a bound on fiber size, which nothing in S4 provides.
- *Periodicity:* an aperiodic minimal set can equivariantly surject onto a circle of length log p (any minimal flow with eigenvalue 2π/log p; e.g. an irrational 2-torus flow onto its circle factor). So covering the packet does not force extra closed orbits in Y₀; the one-closed-orbit count on Y₀ is untouched.
- *Rank:* the ledger already records (§9.3) that period-group rank cannot kill: 1-dimensional adelic solenoids carry closed orbits of length log p for all p simultaneously.
- *Non-Hausdorff loophole:* if X₀ is NOT Hausdorff, f(Y₀) need not be closed, Theorem A does not apply to it, and even 6.1's forcing evaporates — the escape only widens.

**6.3 The mapping half and the cut classes.** For the mapping reading, the admissible-class analysis of §4 changes nothing: f maps into whichever X₀^E is fixed as the target, and if the target is a certified system (E ⊇ E_f) the analysis of 6.1–6.2 applies as is. If instead one targets a cut system X₀^{E(a)} (Thm C(b)), then f(Y₀) lands in a space with one closed orbit per prime and the packet-forcing is vacuous *inside that target* — but the target itself is a non-closed invariant subspace of X₀^{E_max} (§4.3), so "mapping to Deninger's X₀" in the ledger's sense is then satisfied only by composing with a non-closed inclusion, and the composite image's closure in the real X₀ again contains full packets (Theorem A). The mapping half is thus insensitive to the choice of admissible class: its open core is G2 regardless.

**Conclusion of §6:** the monodromy-forcing mechanism proves the all-or-nothing law on the X₀ side but cannot, by itself, refute the existence of a compact 3-dimensional lamination mapping to X₀ as in S4/[x-03] §6 p. 40 (second alternative). An attempted kill along these lines must answer G2 below, which is genuinely open.

## 7. The remaining gap, as decidable sub-questions

- **G1 (topology of X₀; decidable, medium difficulty).** Is the suspension X₀ = X̌₀(C)_E ×_{Q>0} R>0 Hausdorff (X₀ = Spec Z, E ⊇ E_f)? [x-03] proves Hausdorffness for X̊(C), X̊₀(C), X̌₀(C) (Cor. 7.8/7.9, p. 45) but nowhere for the suspension, and notes twice that the Q>0-action is **not properly discontinuous** (end of §7, at (68): "The Q>0-action on Ȟ_{E_tors} × R>0 is not properly discontinuous. In section 10, we will see that this works to our advantage"; and again in §12: "not properly discontinuous if the rank of Q₀ is greater than one"). Partial analysis this session: the orbit equivalence relation is closed against nets whose Q>0-parameters converge in R>0 **and** whose points stay in one colimit stratum with bounded denominators (then the parameter net is eventually constant and closedness follows); the unresolved failure mode is nets with unbounded character-kernel jumps. A clean answer either way is a publishable lemma about these spaces; if Hausdorff fails at packet points, even the embedded-kill statement (which does not need it) should be re-examined for the *compact-but-not-closed* pathology.
- **G2 (the honest blocker for the full kill; open, hard).** Can a compact metrizable **finite-dimensional** R-flow (a fortiori a 3-dimensional lamination with a foliated flow) admit a continuous equivariant surjection onto Y₀^Den = X̌₀(S¹) ×_{Q>0} R>0 — equivalently (granted G1-Hausdorff) onto any closed invariant subset of X₀ meeting every packet? By §6.1 such a Y₀ must contain uncountably many disjoint minimal sets per prime and equivariantly cover cells of every dimension. A NO answer (e.g. a dimension-theoretic obstruction exploiting equivariance plus the specific product structure of the cells transverse to the flow, or a mean-dimension / minimal-set-complexity argument), **combined with a YES on G1** (which is what forces f(Y₀) to contain the whole packet system in the first place), would close the mapping half and, with Theorems A–C, fire the S4 kill in full. A YES answer (an abstract construction) would show the monodromy mechanism can never kill S4 and would redirect the kill burden entirely to the archimedean/α=1 requirements (ledger S3, R12–R13).
- **G3 (positive side, = probe 9.4, sharpened by Theorem C).** The selection problem now has an exact shape: one-orbit-per-prime selections inside the admissible framework exist (Thm C(b)) but are non-canonical (an arbitrary point of B_p per prime) and forfeit every certified theorem of [x-03] (§4.2), while every *certified* system (E ⊇ E_f) has irreducibly uncountable packets (Thm C(a)) and obeys the all-or-nothing law (Thm A). A viable S4 selection must therefore be **canonical and theory-preserving**, and the only mechanism on the books is Deninger's own non-N-invariant local condition ("additive mod p", [x-03] p. 29) transplanted globally — probe 9.4 exactly. Any 9.4 progress should be tested first against 4.2's price list (connectedness, H⁰, closure identification).
