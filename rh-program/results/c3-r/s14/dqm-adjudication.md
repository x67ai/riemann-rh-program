## 1. FINAL BINDING VERDICT (stated first)

**DQ-M = NO, as a theorem in the model world. Road 2 (Haar-average the packet) is CLOSED. The re-scoping of Q\* to "meeting each packet in a Haar-measured orbit family" is NOT adopted. The C3 kill-criterion does NOT fire.**

Precisely — DQ-M (`probe-9.4-note.md` §8 item 2) asked, for a [Den05]-§7-type suspension Y = M̄ ×_Λ R whose periodic set is a continuum B × S¹ of closed orbits of common length ℓ, B a Cantor group with Haar probability measure μ: *does the distributional trace of the flow on leafwise forms exist, with orbital side equal to ∫_B (single-orbit contribution) dμ = the single-orbit contribution?* The adjudicated answer has three parts, all decided:

**(V1) Existence — YES in the leafwise configuration, NO in the transverse one.** For every [Den05] §7.7 suspension with M̄ = M a closed manifold (a mapping torus Y = M ×_h R of any diffeomorphism h, Λ = ℓZ), the trace of φ\* on each reduced leafwise cohomology H̄^n_F(Y) exists as a distribution **with no non-degeneracy hypothesis whatsoever**, and equals ℓ Σ_{k∈Z} tr((h\*)^k | H^n(M;C)) δ_{kℓ}; its supertrace is ℓ Σ_k L(h^k) δ_{kℓ}, L the Lefschetz number (§3: **the shared core, Theorem L of F = Theorem A of O, re-derived here — three independent derivations**). In this class every orbit family is parametrized by a compact subset of the base manifold M (the closed orbits of period kℓ are canonically homeomorphic to Fix(f^k) ⊂ M, also for infinite profinite Γ̄ — O's Lemma 5.1, re-derived §5.2), so a packet base sits in the **leaf** direction. If instead the Cantor base is a **transverse factor** (Y = B × X, dim F = 0 or a product with a Deninger solenoid), the Hörmander/Deninger trace does not exist: S_j(α) = 1 ⊗ S_j^X(α) is never trace class (F's Theorems T1/D = O's Prop. 4.3, re-derived §5.3), and the Guillemin flat-trace regularization diverges at every accumulation point of a fixed set (F's Theorem D′, re-derived §5.4).

**(V2) The orbital side is NOT the Haar average — THEOREM.** In the leafwise configuration the aggregate contribution of a continuum B of closed orbits of common length ℓ at t = kℓ is
  ℓ · L(h^k) · δ_{kℓ} = ℓ · ind(h^k, B) · δ_{kℓ},
the Lefschetz number of the leafwise return map = the total fixed-point index of B (an integer), never a mass. The Haar proposal predicts ℓ·1·δ_{kℓ} for every probability measure on B (the integrand is constant — O's Remark 4.0′, re-derived §2), so a single model with L(h^k) ≠ 1 refutes it for every measure at once. Fully re-derived counterexamples (§4): probe F's model (T², h the time-one map of f·∂_x with f ≥ 0 vanishing exactly on a Cantor set C meeting every horizontal circle; periodic set exactly C × S¹, every orbit maximally degenerate, weight **0**); probe O's models (B = S²: **2**; B = T² with Haar: **0**; B = Σ_g: **2−2g**; a Cantor set in S² with an otherwise aperiodic flow: **2**; two normally hyperbolic circles in T²: **0**; a genuine solenoid with Γ̄ = Z_2, M = T², f = (2x, y), B ≅ S¹: **0**, modulo a Künneth lemma re-checked in §5.7). Equality with one orbit's worth holds **iff ind(h^k, B) = 1 for all k** (O's Cor. C3), which O's Construction 12.1 (a closed disk of degenerate orbits plus one simple ε = +1 orbit on S²) realizes — so an orbit continuum *can* weigh exactly one orbit, for topological reasons, never for measure-theoretic ones. Where the packet's Haar canonicity comes from (a transitive translation symmetry, 9.4 Lemma D(iii), referee-pending), the index is forced to **0**: an infinite profinite group carries no nonzero translation-invariant Z-valued finitely additive measure on its clopen algebra (O's Lemma 9.2, re-derived §5.6), so a packet-symmetric continuum has fixed-point index 0 (O's Theorem D(i), unconditional modulo classical index theory; the trace-formula form is a theorem when the continuum is the whole period-kℓ set, proposition grade otherwise — §5.6).

**(V3) The one trace that returns the Haar identity is a different object and cannot carry the arithmetic.** F's Theorem M (re-derived §5.5): for any fixed-point-free continuous flow on a compact metric space with a finite invariant measure ν, the transverse-measure (type II) trace along the *orbit* lamination exists as a positive Radon measure T_ν = ν(Y)δ_0 + Σ_{k≠0}(m_k)_\*σ, and a packet with ν = μ ⊗ ds contributes ℓ μ(B) Σ_{k≠0} δ_{kℓ} — exactly ∫_B(single-orbit term)dμ. But (F's Theorem N, re-derived §5.5): Σ_p log p · μ_p(B_p) ≤ ν(Y) < ∞, so Haar *probability* on every packet is impossible on any compact substrate; T_ν has bounded mass per unit interval while the prime side has mass ≥ log p; T_ν is even in t while T2's weights are p^k for k ≤ −1; T_ν ignores every ν-null orbit; and the infinite-measure regime has an infinite δ_0-coefficient, no trace-class operator, a non-Radon measure, and an arithmetic-free "union of circles" orbital identity. O's §4.4 computes the same Haar answer in the dim F = 0 trivial model and correctly names it an artifact of leaves being points; F's construction shows the artifact is a genuine (but arithmetically useless) trace on the orbit lamination. No conflict (§6).

## 2. The question made precise (definitions fixed before anything is proved)

**2.1 The single-orbit contribution.** From [Den05] Thm 5.3 p. 22 and [ALKL] Thm 1.3.10 p. 8 (read verbatim, they agree): a closed orbit c of least period ℓ(c) contributes ℓ(c) Σ_{k∈Z^×} ε_c(k) δ_{kℓ(c)}, ε_c(k) = sgn det(id − φ_\*^{kℓ(c)} | T_pF) ∈ {±1} ([ALKL] p. 3; [Den05] p. 21 with T_xX/RY_{φ,x} ≅ T_xF). In the solenoidal/α-twisted setting the k ≤ −1 weights carry the extra factor det(−T_xφ^{kℓ}|T_xF) ([Den05] Thm 7.8 p. 35) or e^{kℓ(γ)} ([x-22] Cor. 3.4 p. 18). The "single-orbit contribution" of DQ-M is this with ε ≡ +1: ℓ Σ_{k≠0} δ_{kℓ}. The Haar proposal replaces Σ_{orbits in the continuum} by ∫_B (ℓ Σ_k ε δ_{kℓ}) dμ(b) = ℓ Σ_k ε δ_{kℓ}, because μ is a probability measure and the integrand is constant in b (every orbit of the continuum has length ℓ; ε would be the same by homogeneity). **Consequence (O's Remark 4.0′, re-derived):** the proposal's value is the same for *every* probability measure on B and uses neither the group law nor the invariance of Haar; and since it is compared at k ≠ 0 only, it is independent of the δ_0 convention (χ_Co δ_0, or any t = 0 renormalization of the [ALKL] Z_μ type). Hence one model with |L(h^k)| ≠ 1 refutes it for all measures; a model with L(h^k) = 0 refutes it for all normalizations as well.

**2.2 The three objects that "the distributional trace of the flow on leafwise forms" could mean.** Probe F distinguishes three, probe O two; the adjudicator keeps F's three because the answer splits along exactly this line.
- **(T-coh)** the cohomological/Hörmander trace: A_ϕ = ∫ϕ(t)φ^{t\*}dt on the Hilbert completion Ĥ^n of the reduced leafwise cohomology (or on L²-leafwise-harmonic forms), trace class, Tr(φ\*|H̄^n)(ϕ) := tr A_ϕ ([Den05] Thm 5.3 p. 22; equivalently Σ_{λ∈Sp_n(Θ)} e^{tλ}, Thm 7.8 p. 35; [x-22] Thm 2.1 p. 5, Thm 3.1 p. 12; [Lei07] Thm 2 p. 17 with TR; [ALKL] with the b-trace renormalization). **A Hilbert-space trace: no transverse measure weights the orbit sum; the transverse measure enters only through the L²-structure and χ_Co.** This is the object of every trace formula on disk, and the one DQ-M's wording names ("on leafwise forms").
- **(T-II)** the transverse-measure (von Neumann) trace of a measured lamination — [Lei07] Prop. 2 pp. 15–16 builds it for the surface lamination (S, F) from Λ = μ_L dx, and uses it only to identify the type III_{1/q} factor; his trace formula (Thm 2) is (T-coh). Probe F applies the type II trace instead to the *orbit* lamination G (leaves = flow orbits), where φ^f = ∫f(t)φ^{t\*}dt is leafwise (convolution along orbits) — Definition 4.3 / Theorem M.
- **(T-mix)** a measured family of (T-coh) slices, τ = ∫_Z Tr_b dμ(b) (F's §2.4, Theorem P, Proposition F).

**2.3 Hypothesis H4 and how it is violated.** "Simple closed orbit": id − φ_\*^{kℓ(c)} on T_pF an isomorphism ([ALKL] p. 3; [Den05] p. 20: eigenvalue 1 of T_xφ^{kℓ} with algebraic multiplicity one — the flow direction); consequences on a closed manifold: C_I(φ) finite for compact I, P(φ) discrete ([ALKL] p. 99); [ALKM] (A1) p. 4: "which implies that P is a countable set". A continuum of period-ℓ orbits accumulating at c forces det(id − φ_\*^{ℓ}|T_pF) = 0 (the accumulation direction is killed), so ε_c(k) is **undefined**; the Guillemin–Sternberg local expression quoted at [Lei07] p. 3 — numerator Σ_j(−1)^j Tr(Dφ^{±kℓ})^\*|Λ^jT^\*F = det(id − Dφ^{±kℓ}|T_yF), denominator its absolute value — is 0/0 on the whole continuum. DQ-M is therefore, before anything else, the question of what replaces ε_c(k); the model-world answer (§3–§5) is: an index.

**2.4 The model world and the two positions of B.** Foliated spaces and smooth solenoids as in [Den05] §7.1 (pp. 28–29); codimension-one F in a solenoid, F-compatible flows (§7.2–7.3, pp. 29–30); the §7.7 suspension X = M̄ ×_Λ R, Λ = lZ acting by (m,t)·λ = (f̄^{−λ/l}(m), t+λ), F-leaves the images of M̃ × {γ} × {t}, flow φ^t[m,t′] = [m, t+t′], transverse to F, no fixed points, closed orbits ↔ finite f-orbits on M with l(γ) = |γ_M| l (pp. 34–35, verbatim). A packet configuration: a continuum P ≅ B × R/ℓZ of closed orbits of common least period ℓ, B a Cantor group. B can lie **inside the F-leaves** (a compact subset of Fix(f^k) ⊂ M, fixed pointwise by the leafwise return map — the only position available in the §7.7 class, §5.2) or be a **transverse factor** (products B × X, realizable only by an external product — §5.3). Both are decided below.

## 3. Re-derivation of the shared core: the leafwise trace of a mapping torus without non-degeneracy (F's Theorem L = O's Theorem A)

Both probes rest their verdicts on one theorem, proved by each independently. The adjudicator re-derived it from the definitions; the derivation below is the adjudicator's own and is complete.

**Setting.** M a closed smooth manifold, h ∈ Diff(M), ℓ > 0. Let Z act on M × R by the generator g(x, s) = (h^{−1}(x), s + ℓ) and put Y := (M × R)/Z, F := the foliation by the images of M × {s}, φ^t[x, s] := [x, s + t]. This is [Den05] §7.7 with f = h (an unramified covering of degree 1, so Γ_i = Γ, Γ̄ = pt, M̄ = M), Λ = ℓZ, (m, t)·λ = (f̄^{−λ/ℓ}(m), t + λ) — read verbatim p. 35. Y is a closed (dim M + 1)-manifold fibering over R/ℓZ, F is the fibration (Riemannian), φ is transverse to F with no fixed points and no preserved leaves; it is Hector's case (c) of [ALKL] p. 101.

**Step 1 — leafwise forms, flow action, return map.** A leafwise n-form on Y is a g-invariant form ω̃ on M × R along the M-direction; writing ω(s) := ω̃(·, s) ∈ Ω^n(M), invariance g^\*ω̃ = ω̃ reads (h^{−1})^\*ω(s + ℓ) = ω(s), i.e.
  **(tw)  ω(s + ℓ) = h^\*ω(s)**,  d_F = d_M pointwise in s,  (φ^{t\*}ω)(s) = ω(s + t).
Regularity in s: smooth (Y is a manifold; [Den05] Warning p. 30) or merely continuous (the foliated-space convention, [Den05] 7.2 p. 29) — every step below works verbatim in both conventions. The return map: [x, s + ℓ] = [hx, s], so φ^ℓ preserves each leaf L_s ≅ M and equals h on it; hence the closed orbits of period kℓ correspond bijectively to Fix(h^k), an orbit of least period nℓ to an h-orbit of n points (n | k) — [Den05] p. 35's bijection made explicit. ✓ (both probes)

**Step 2 — the reduced leafwise cohomology.** Fix a Riemannian metric on M; let H be the harmonic projection and G the Green operator of the Hodge decomposition Ω^n(M) = ℋ^n ⊕ dΩ^{n−1} ⊕ d^\*Ω^{n+1} [classical; both continuous for the C^∞ topology]; a closed form ω is exact iff Hω = 0, and then ω = d(d^\*Gω). Put C_tw(R; H^n(M)) := {c : R → H^n(M) smooth (resp. continuous), c(s + ℓ) = h^\*c(s)}.
*Claim:* [ω] ↦ (s ↦ [ω(s)]) is an isomorphism H^n_F(Y) ≅ C_tw(R; H^n(M)), and Im d_F is closed, so H̄^n_F(Y) = H^n_F(Y).
*Proof.* Well defined and twisted because h^\* on forms induces h^\* on cohomology; regular in s because s ↦ [ω(s)] = Hω(s) under ℋ^n ≅ H^n(M). Surjective: given c, let η(s) be the harmonic representative of c(s); choose ψ ∈ C_c^∞(R) with Σ_{j∈Z} ψ(s − jℓ) ≡ 1, and set ω(s) := Σ_j ψ(s − jℓ)(h^j)^\*η(s − jℓ) (locally finite, closed). Then ω(s + ℓ) = Σ_j ψ(s + ℓ − jℓ)(h^j)^\*η(s + ℓ − jℓ) = Σ_i ψ(s − iℓ)(h^{i+1})^\*η(s − iℓ) = h^\*ω(s), and [ω(s)] = Σ_j ψ(s − jℓ)(h^\*)^j c(s − jℓ) = Σ_j ψ(s − jℓ) c(s) = c(s), using c(s − jℓ) = (h^\*)^{−j}c(s). Injective on cohomology: if ω is closed, twisted, with [ω(s)] = 0 for all s, put β(s) := d^\*Gω(s) (regular in s) and β̃(s) := Σ_j ψ(s − jℓ)(h^j)^\*β(s − jℓ); β̃ is twisted by the same computation, and dβ̃(s) = Σ_j ψ(s − jℓ)(h^j)^\*ω(s − jℓ) = Σ_j ψ(s − jℓ) ω(s) = ω(s) because ω is twisted. Conversely d_F of a twisted form has zero classes. Closedness: Im d_F is the kernel of the continuous map ω ↦ (s ↦ Hω(s)) on the Fréchet space of closed twisted forms. ∎
(Probe O reaches the same space through [ALKL] (1.2.2)–(1.2.3) p. 2 — leafwise Hodge for the Riemannian fibration — and a fiberwise identification with h^\*-twisted sections; probe F's direct proof, reproduced here, needs no citation. Both are correct.)

**Step 3 — Hilbert completion and the trace.** Let V := H^n(M; C), A := h^\* ∈ GL(V), and Ĥ^n := {c ∈ L²_loc(R; V) : c(s + ℓ) = A c(s)} with ‖c‖² := ∫_0^ℓ |c(s)|² ds — the completion of the twisted sections; any inner product on V gives an equivalent norm, and [Den05]'s pre-Hilbert structure (15) (p. 21: the L²-product of harmonic representatives against the transverse measure ds) is one of them. The flow acts by (φ^{t\*}c)(s) = c(s + t), strongly continuous; A_ϕ := ∫ϕ(t)φ^{t\*}dt for ϕ ∈ C_c^∞(R).
*Claim:* A_ϕ is of trace class on Ĥ^n and **Tr(A_ϕ) = ℓ Σ_{k∈Z} ϕ(kℓ) tr(A^k)**.
*Proof.* Choose Ξ ∈ End(V) with e^{ℓΞ} = A [classical: an invertible matrix has a logarithm]. J: c ↦ v, v(s) := e^{−sΞ}c(s), is a bounded isomorphism Ĥ^n → L²(R/ℓZ; V) (v(s + ℓ) = e^{−sΞ}e^{−ℓΞ}A c(s) = v(s); ‖e^{±sΞ}‖ bounded on [0, ℓ]). It conjugates A_ϕ to (Ã_ϕ v)(s) = ∫ϕ(t) e^{tΞ} v(s + t) dt. In the orthogonal decomposition L²(R/ℓZ; V) = ⊕_{ν∈Z} e^{2πiνs/ℓ} ⊗ V, Ã_ϕ is block-diagonal with blocks Φ_ν := ∫ϕ(t) e^{2πiνt/ℓ} e^{tΞ} dt ∈ End(V), the Fourier coefficients of the compactly supported smooth End(V)-valued function t ↦ ϕ(t)e^{tΞ}, hence ‖Φ_ν‖ = O(|ν|^{−N}) for every N.
