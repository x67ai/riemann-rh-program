## 0. VERDICT (stated first)

**THEOREM (NO) on Q-a — unconditional, and far stronger than asked.** For X₀ = Spec Z and **every** admissible class E (Def. 4.1 of [x-03]; not only E ⊇ E_f, but also E_max, E_tors and probe A's one-orbit cut classes E(a₀)):

1. **(Theorem 1, §4)** Every periodic orbit γ of the system X₀ = X̌₀(C)^E ×_{Q>0} R>0 (quotient topology) is an **indiscrete** subspace: every open subset of X₀ that meets γ contains γ. More: every open subset meeting a packet Γ^E_p contains the whole packet. Consequently **no T₀ subspace of X₀ — a fortiori no Hausdorff one — contains two points of one periodic orbit**, and the answer to Q-a is NO with no use of compactness, of the dimension 3, of flow-invariance, or of "one orbit per packet": no Hausdorff subspace of X₀ contains even one full periodic orbit.
2. **(Theorem 2, §6 — backward escape)** Every point of X₀ over the generic point of Spec Z has **empty α-limit set**: its backward orbit has no cluster point in X₀. Hence **every compact flow-invariant subspace of X₀ lies inside the periodic locus ∐_p Γ^E_p**, and (with Lemma 1, §5, the p → ∞ accumulation lemma) **meets only finitely many packets** (Theorem 3). This is a second, independent proof that Q-a is NO — one that needs no Hausdorff hypothesis at all.
3. **TRANSFER TO FACE (b) — also NO, proved in full (Theorem 3, Corollary 3.2).** Theorems 2–3 are stated for continuous flow-equivariant maps f: Y → X₀ from an arbitrary compact space Y with a continuous R-action: f(Y) lies in finitely many packets. So **no compact lamination (metrizable or not, of any dimension, with any orbit count) admits a continuous flow-equivariant map to X₀ — or to the unitary system Y₀ = X̌₀(S¹) ×_{Q>0} R>0 — meeting infinitely many packets**, let alone one orbit per prime. The statement is robust under equivariance-up-to-time-change (§6.4) and under replacing the quotient topology by the finer coproduct topology of [x-03] Thm 7.10 (§7.2).

## 3. Three lemmas on the quotient (all proved in full; nothing beyond A1–A9)

**Lemma 3.1 (lifting convergent nets through an open map).** Let q: X̃ → X be a continuous open surjection, (z_α)_{α∈A} a net in X with z_α → z, and z̃ ∈ q⁻¹(z). Then there are a subnet (z_{α_β})_{β∈B} and lifts z̃_β ∈ q⁻¹(z_{α_β}) with z̃_β → z̃.

*Proof.* For each open Ṽ ∋ z̃, q(Ṽ) is an open neighborhood of z, so there is α(Ṽ) ∈ A with z_α ∈ q(Ṽ) for all α ≥ α(Ṽ). Let B := {(α, Ṽ) : Ṽ an open neighborhood of z̃, z_α ∈ q(Ṽ)}, ordered by (α, Ṽ) ≤ (α′, Ṽ′) iff α ≤ α′ and Ṽ ⊇ Ṽ′. B is directed: given (α, Ṽ), (α′, Ṽ′), the set Ṽ″ := Ṽ ∩ Ṽ′ is an open neighborhood of z̃ and any α″ ≥ α, α′, α(Ṽ″) gives (α″, Ṽ″) ≥ both. Put α_β := α for β = (α, Ṽ) — cofinal and monotone, so (z_{α_β}) is a subnet — and choose z̃_β ∈ Ṽ ∩ q⁻¹(z_α) (nonempty because z_α ∈ q(Ṽ)). For any open Ṽ₀ ∋ z̃ and any β₀ = (α₀, Ṽ₀) ∈ B, every β ≥ β₀ has z̃_β ∈ Ṽ ⊆ Ṽ₀; so z̃_β → z̃. ∎

**Lemma 3.2 (lifting through the Galois quotient).** Let π: X̊(C) → X̊₀(C) be the quotient by the compact group G (A4). If (P_α) is a net in X̊₀(C) with P_α → P, and P̃_α ∈ π⁻¹(P_α) are arbitrary lifts, then some subnet of (P̃_α) converges in X̊(C) to a point of π⁻¹(P).

*Proof.* π is a closed map with compact fibres: the fibre π⁻¹(P) = P̃·G is the continuous image of the compact G (Prop. 7.5), hence compact; and for A ⊂ X̊(C) closed, the saturation A·G = pr₁(Φ(A × G)) where Φ(P̃, σ) := (P̃σ, σ) is a homeomorphism of X̊(C) × G (continuous by Prop. 7.5, with continuous inverse (Q̃, σ) ↦ (Q̃σ⁻¹, σ)) and pr₁: X̊(C) × G → X̊(C) is a closed map because G is compact (tube lemma); so A·G is closed and π(A) = X̊₀(C) ∖ π(X̊(C) ∖ A·G)… more directly: π(A) is closed iff its preimage A·G is closed, which it is. Now suppose no subnet of (P̃_α) converges to a point of the compact fibre F := π⁻¹(P). Then each z ∈ F has an open neighborhood V_z and an index α_z with P̃_α ∉ V_z for all α ≥ α_z (otherwise z would be a cluster point, and a net has a subnet converging to each of its cluster points). Finitely many V_{z_1}, …, V_{z_k} cover F; let V := ⋃ V_{z_i} and α* ≥ all α_{z_i}. For α ≥ α*, P̃_α ∈ X̊(C) ∖ V, a closed set whose image π(X̊(C) ∖ V) is closed and does not contain P (F ⊂ V). So X̊₀(C) ∖ π(X̊(C) ∖ V) is an open neighborhood of P containing no P_α with α ≥ α*, contradicting P_α → P. ∎

**Lemma 3.3 (the chart criterion for generic points).** Let P′ = π(η, Ψ) ∈ X̊₀(C)^E be a point over the generic point η of X (so Ψ: Q̄^× → C^× is a character), and let μ_e := ker(Ψ|_{μ(Q̄)}), a finite subgroup of μ(Q̄) by (Tors), hence cyclic of some order e ≥ 1. For m, n ∈ N coprime, the point F_{m/n}P′ of the colimit X̌₀(C)^E lies in the first chart X̊₀(C)^E **if and only if n | me**.

*Proof.* F_{m/n}P′ = F_n⁻¹(F_mP′), and F_mP′ = π(η, Ψ^m) ∈ X̊₀(C)^E. In the colimit along the injections F_ν (Prop. 4.2/Lemma 7.3), the element F_n⁻¹(F_mP′) equals an element Φ of the first chart iff F_nΦ = F_mP′ in X̊₀(C)^E (if F_NΦ′ = F_{N/n}F_mP′ for a common index N with n | N, apply injectivity of F_{N/n} to F_{N/n}(F_nΦ) = F_{N/n}(F_mP′)). Write Φ = π(η, Φ̃); F_nΦ = F_mP′ means π(η, Φ̃ⁿ) = π(η, Ψ^m), i.e. Φ̃ⁿ = Ψ^m∘σ for some σ ∈ G (G acts on points over the G-fixed point η by precomposition of characters). So F_{m/n}P′ ∈ X̊₀(C)^E iff the multiplicative map Ξ := Ψ^m∘σ: Q̄ → C (extended by Ξ(0) = 0) has an n-th root Φ̃ for some σ ∈ G. By Lemma 4.6(1) (A3) with R = K = Q̄, o = C, N = n: an n-th root exists iff Ξ|_{μ_n(Q̄)} = 1, and is then unique. Now Ξ|_{μ_n} = 1 iff Ψ^m|_{σ(μ_n)} = 1 iff Ψ^m|_{μ_n} = 1 (σ permutes μ_n) iff μ_n ⊆ ker(Ψ^m|_μ) = {ζ ∈ μ : ζ^m ∈ μ_e} = μ_{me} iff n | me — independent of σ. When the root Φ̃ exists it is a character of Q̄^× with values in C^× (Φ̃(r)ⁿ = Ξ(r) ≠ 0 for r ≠ 0), with ker(Φ̃|_μ) ⊆ {ζ : ζⁿ ∈ μ_{me}} finite, so (η, Φ̃) ∈ X̊(C) satisfies (Tors); it is in E because Φ̃ⁿ = Ψ^m∘σ ∈ E and E is closed under σ and under ν-th powers biconditionally (Def. 4.1). So Φ := π(η, Φ̃) ∈ X̊₀(C)^E and F_nΦ = F_mP′. ∎

*Remark.* The criterion is the exact generic-point counterpart of the packet computation of the adjudication (§4 item 5b: an injective packet character has an n-th root iff n is a power of p). For packets the denominators are unbounded (p^j); for generic points they are bounded by e. That asymmetry is the whole of §6.

## 4. THEOREM 1 — periodic orbits and packets are indiscrete subspaces of X₀ `[novelty: single-check]`

**Lemma 4.1 (packet neighborhoods contain Frobenius return classes).** Let P₀ = π(x, χ^a) with a ∈ Ẑ×_{(p)} and χ^a ∈ E, viewed in the first chart X̊₀(C)^E ⊂ X̌₀(C)^E, and let W be an open neighborhood of P₀ in X̌₀(C)^E. Then there is an integer M ≥ 1 prime to p with the following property: for every c ∈ Ẑ×_{(p)} with χ^c ∈ E and every m ∈ N with mc ≡ a (mod M) (i.e. mc − a ∈ MẐ_{(p)}), F_m π(x, χ^c) ∈ W.

*Proof.* The first chart is open in X̌₀(C)^E and carries the subspace topology of X̊₀(C) (A5, A6), so W ∩ X̊₀(C)^E = W″ ∩ X̊₀(C)^E for some open W″ ⊂ X̊₀(C) containing P₀. Since π is continuous, π⁻¹(W″) is an open subset of X̊(C) containing (x, χ^a); by A1 it contains a basic pointwise neighborhood N = {P : |P(r_i) − χ^a(r̄_i)| < ε, i = 1, …, s} for finitely many r_1, …, r_s ∈ Z̄ (writing χ^a(r̄) := χ(r̄)^a for r ∉ 𝔭_x and := 0 for r ∈ 𝔭_x, r̄ the image in κ(x)). Let d_i be the order of r̄_i in the torsion group κ(x)^× for those r_i ∉ 𝔭_x (each d_i is prime to p, as κ(x)^× = μ^{(p)}), and M := lcm of the d_i (M := 1 if all r_i ∈ 𝔭_x). For m, c as in the statement, the point F_m(x, χ^c) = (x, χ^{cm}) takes the value χ(r̄_i)^{cm} = χ(r̄_i^{cm}) at r_i ∉ 𝔭_x; since cm ≡ a mod d_i and r̄_i has order d_i, r̄_i^{cm} = r̄_i^a, so the value equals χ^a(r̄_i) exactly; at r_i ∈ 𝔭_x both values are 0. Hence (x, χ^{cm}) ∈ N ⊂ π⁻¹(W″). It lies in the E-locus: χ^c ∈ E ⟹ χ^{cm} = (χ^c)∘( )^m ∈ E (Def. 4.1), and (Tors) holds (kernel of χ^{cm} on μ^{(p)} is μ_{m′}, m′ the prime-to-p part of m — finite). Therefore F_mπ(x, χ^c) = π(x, χ^{cm}) ∈ W″ ∩ X̊₀(C)^E ⊆ W. ∎

**Lemma 4.2 (logarithms of an arithmetic progression are dense modulo any period).** Let M ≥ 1, b ∈ {1, …, M} and L > 0. The set {log m mod L : m ∈ N, m ≡ b (mod M)} is dense in R/LZ.

*Proof.* Put m_t := b + tM (t ≥ 0). Then log m_t → ∞ and 0 < log m_{t+1} − log m_t = log(1 + M/(b + tM)) ≤ M/(b + tM) → 0. Given y ∈ R and δ > 0, choose T with all gaps < δ for t ≥ T, and k ∈ Z with y + kL ≥ log m_T; the sequence (log m_t)_{t ≥ T} increases to ∞ in steps < δ, so some log m_t lies within δ of y + kL. ∎

**Theorem 1.** Let X₀ = Spec Z, E any admissible class, and X₀ = X̌₀(C)^E ×_{Q>0} R>0 with the quotient topology (A7).

**(a)** Let γ ⊂ X₀ be any periodic orbit. Every open subset of X₀ that meets γ contains γ. Equivalently: any two points of γ have the same open neighborhoods in X₀ (they are topologically indistinguishable), and the subspace topology on γ is the indiscrete topology {∅, γ}.

**(b)** Let p be a prime and Γ^E_p the packet. Every open subset of X₀ that meets Γ^E_p contains Γ^E_p. So Γ^E_p is a **closed** (Prop. A.1′) and **indiscrete** subspace of X₀, and all its points are topologically indistinguishable.

*Proof.* (b) implies (a) (a periodic orbit lies in a unique packet, Thm 6.1; but note (a) also follows from the proof below with c = a, without Thm 5.2 or any second base class). Let V ⊂ X₀ be open with V ∩ Γ^E_p ≠ ∅, and pick a point of the intersection in normal form, [P₀, v] with P₀ = π(x, χ^a), a ∈ Ẑ×_{(p)}, χ^a ∈ E (A9). The set q⁻¹(V) is open in X̃ = X̌₀(C)^E × R>0 and contains (P₀, v), so it contains a basic open box W × I with W an open neighborhood of P₀ in X̌₀(C)^E and I ∋ v an open interval in R>0; as q⁻¹(V) is Q>0-saturated, q(W × I) ⊆ V. Let M be as in Lemma 4.1 for (P₀, W). Now let [π(x, χ^c), w] be an arbitrary point of Γ^E_p in normal form (c ∈ Ẑ×_{(p)}, χ^c ∈ E, w ∈ R>0). Since c is a unit of Ẑ_{(p)} and M is prime to p, c is invertible mod M; fix the residue class b := c⁻¹a mod M, represented in {1, …, M}. By Lemma 4.2 with L = log p there is m ∈ N, m ≡ b (mod M), with log m ∈ (log w − log I) + (log p)Z, i.e. there is j ∈ Z with s := w p^j / m ∈ I. Then:

  [π(x, χ^c), w] = [π(x, χ^c), w p^j]   (isotropy p^Z, Thm 6.1 — A9)
                 = [π(x, χ^c), m s]
                 = [F_m π(x, χ^c), s]   (return identity, A9)
                 ∈ q(W × I) ⊆ V,

because F_mπ(x, χ^c) ∈ W by Lemma 4.1 (mc ≡ a mod M) and s ∈ I. As the point was arbitrary, V ⊇ Γ^E_p. Taking c = a gives V ⊇ γ for the orbit γ of [P₀, v], which is (a). The equivalent formulations: if every open set meeting γ contains γ, then for y, y′ ∈ γ every open neighborhood of y contains y′ and vice versa; the open sets of the subspace γ are the traces V ∩ γ ∈ {∅, γ}. ∎

**Corollary 4.3 (Q-a is NO — strong form).** Let Y ⊆ X₀ be any subspace containing two points y ≠ y′ of one periodic orbit (in particular any subspace containing a full periodic orbit). Then Y, with the subspace topology, is not T₀, hence not T₁ and not Hausdorff. Consequently there is no Hausdorff-in-its-subspace-topology subspace Y ⊂ X₀ — compact or not, flow-invariant or not, of any dimension — meeting even one packet in a full orbit; a fortiori none meeting every packet in exactly one orbit. **This holds for every admissible E**, including E ⊇ E_f (the charter's case), E_max, E_tors, and the one-orbit cut classes E(a₀) of probe A's Theorem C(b).

*Proof.* Subspace open sets are traces of open sets of X₀; every open set of X₀ containing y contains y′ (Theorem 1(a)); so every open set of Y containing y contains y′, and symmetrically. ∎

**Corollary 4.4 (Theorem A, re-proved; Cor. A.2 sharpened).** For every periodic orbit γ ⊂ Γ^E_p: cl_{X₀}(γ) ⊇ Γ^E_p (Theorem A). Indeed z ∈ Γ^E_p lies in cl(γ) iff every open V ∋ z meets γ, and every such V contains Γ^E_p ⊇ γ. Moreover X₀ is **not T₀** (Theorem 1(a)), which strictly sharpens the Session-8 verdict "non-Hausdorff along packets" (W12) and closes the residual Hausdorff question left open by the adjudication (§3, scope note): the cut suspensions X₀^{E(a₀)} are non-T₀ along their single orbit per prime, hence non-Hausdorff.

**Corollary 4.5 (the Kolmogorov quotient collapses packets to fixed points).** Let k: X₀ → X₀^{T₀} be the Kolmogorov (T₀) quotient, identifying topologically indistinguishable points. The flow descends (each φ^t is a homeomorphism of X₀, so it preserves indistinguishability), and by Theorem 1(b) k(Γ^E_p) is a single point, which is fixed by the descended flow. Since k induces a bijection between the open sets of X₀ and of X₀^{T₀}, a map f: Y → X₀ from any space is continuous iff k∘f is; so **the periods log p are invisible to the topology of X₀**: continuity of a map into X₀ near a packet is exactly continuity into a space in which that packet is one fixed point. (Not load-bearing; recorded because it recasts face (b), §6.5.)

**Proposition 4.6 (every orbit is non-Hausdorff in its subspace topology — generic unitary orbits included; not load-bearing).** Let z = [P, u] be a point of X₀ over the generic point with unitary character: P ∈ 𝒞_ν^E, F_νP = π(η, Ψ), Ψ(Q̄^×) ⊂ S¹. Then for any two points z_1 = [P, u_1], z_2 = [P, u_2] of the orbit of z and any open V_1 ∋ z_1, V_2 ∋ z_2, V_1 ∩ V_2 ∩ (orbit) ≠ ∅.

*Proof.* Replacing P by F_νP and u_i by ν⁻¹u_i we may assume ν = 1, P = π(η, Ψ). As in Theorem 1, q⁻¹(V_i) ⊇ W_i × I_i, and (as in Lemma 4.1) W_i ⊇ π{(η, Ψ^m) : m ∈ B_i} where B_i := {m ∈ N : |Ψ(r)^{m} − Ψ(r)| < ε_i for r in a finite set T_i} = 1 + {n ∈ Z : |Ψ(r)ⁿ − 1| < ε_i, r ∈ T_i} ∩ (N − 1) — here (η, Ψ^m) ∈ E by Def. 4.1. Put T := T_1 ∪ T_2, ε := min ε_i, and B := 1 + B′ with B′ := {n ∈ Z : |Ψ(r)ⁿ − 1| < ε ∀r ∈ T}; then B ∩ N ⊆ B_1 ∩ B_2. *B′ is syndetic:* let θ ∈ (S¹)^T be the tuple (Ψ(r))_{r∈T} and H := closure of {θⁿ : n ∈ Z} in the compact group (S¹)^T; U := {h ∈ H : |h_r − 1| < ε ∀r} is an open neighborhood of 1 in H; the translates θ^kU (k ∈ Z) cover H (for h ∈ H, hU⁻¹ is an open neighborhood of h in H, so it contains some θ^k, i.e. h ∈ θ^kU); by compactness finitely many θ^{k_1}U, …, θ^{k_K}U cover H; with g := max|k_i|, every n ∈ Z has θⁿ ∈ θ^{k_i}U for some i, i.e. n − k_i ∈ B′, so every integer is within g of an element of B′. Hence B ∩ N contains elements in every interval of length 2g + 1 beyond g + 1. Now V_i ⊇ q(W_i × I_i) ∋ [F_mP, s] = [P, ms] for m ∈ B ∩ N, s ∈ I_i. We need m_1 s_1 = m_2 s_2 with m_i ∈ B ∩ N, s_i ∈ I_i, i.e. m_1/m_2 ∈ I_2/I_1 (an open interval around u_2/u_1 in R>0): choose m_2 ∈ B ∩ N so large that (2g+1)/m_2 is smaller than the width of I_2/I_1 around λ := (any point of I_2/I_1), and then m_1 ∈ B ∩ N within 2g+1 of λm_2; then m_1/m_2 ∈ I_2/I_1. The common point [P, m_1s_1] = [P, m_2s_2] lies in V_1 ∩ V_2 ∩ (orbit). ∎

So the non-Hausdorffness of X₀ is not a packet peculiarity: **every unitary orbit — every orbit of the accumulation locus of §5 — is non-Hausdorff in the subspace topology**; the packets are merely the extreme case (indiscrete). This is the subspace-topology face of Deninger's Remark 1 on p. 65 ("the subspace topology induced by the ambient space on a dense leaf").

## 5. LEMMA 1 — the p → ∞ accumulation lemma, and the cross-prime closure law (charter line (1))

**Lemma 5.1 (Lemma 1).** Let (z_β)_{β∈B} be a net in X₀ with z_β ∈ Γ^E_{p_β} and p_β → ∞ (for every N, p_β > N eventually). If z_β → z* in X₀, then z* lies over the generic point of Spec Z, and its character is unitary: z* = [P*, u*] with F_{ν′}P* = π(η, Ψ*) for some chart ν′ and some Ψ*: Q̄^× → S¹. In particular z* is not a packet point and (Thm 6.1) is aperiodic.

*Proof.* Write z* = [P*, u*] and let ν′ be a chart index with P* ∈ 𝒞_{ν′}^E. By Lemma 3.1 (q is open, A7) there is a subnet — still written (z_β) — with lifts (F_{q_β}P_β, q_β⁻¹u_β) → (P*, u*) in X̃, where z_β = [P_β, u_β] and q_β ∈ Q>0. Since 𝒞_{ν′}^E is open in X̌₀(C)^E (A6), eventually F_{q_β}P_β ∈ 𝒞_{ν′}^E, i.e. P′_β := F_{ν′q_β}P_β ∈ X̊₀(C)^E, and F_{ν′} being a homeomorphism 𝒞_{ν′}^E → X̊₀(C)^E, P′_β → P′* := F_{ν′}P* in X̊₀(C)^E ⊂ X̊₀(C). Each P′_β is a point of X̊₀(C) over the closed point x_β of X (over p_β) — F_r does not move the underlying point of X — and is therefore a (Tors)-character of κ(x_β)^× = μ^{(p_β)}, all of whose values are roots of unity (A9). By Lemma 3.2 a subnet of arbitrary lifts P̃′_β ∈ X̊(C) of P′_β converges pointwise to a lift P̃′* of P′*. Fix r ∈ Z̄ ∖ {0}. The set of primes p for which r lies in some prime of Z̄ over p is finite (r ∈ 𝔭 ⟹ N_{Q(r)/Q}(r) ∈ 𝔭 ∩ Z = pZ, and the norm is a nonzero integer). As p_β → ∞, eventually r ∉ 𝔭_{x′_β} (x′_β the point of the lift), so |P̃′_β(r)| = 1 eventually, whence |P̃′*(r)| = 1. Thus P̃′*⁻¹(0) = {0}: P̃′* lies over the generic point η, and its character Ψ* := P̃′*|_{Q̄^×} (extended multiplicatively) takes values in S¹. Since F_{ν′} preserves the underlying point of X, P* = F_{ν′}⁻¹P′* lies over η as well. ∎

**Proposition 5.2 (the cross-prime closure law; charter line (1) answered).** Let E ⊇ E_f and let (γ_p)_p be any family with γ_p ⊂ Γ_p one periodic orbit per prime. Then

  cl_{X₀}(⋃_p γ_p) = cl_{X₀}(⋃_p Γ_p) = Y₀ := q(X̌₀(S¹) × R>0) = X̌₀(S¹) ×_{Q>0} R>0,

the full closure-of-periodic-orbits system of [x-03] §8, "still infinite-dimensional" (A10). In particular the closure of a one-orbit-per-prime family meets every packet Γ_q in the whole of Γ_q (not just in γ_q), and contains the entire unitary generic stratum.

*Proof.* ⋃_p Γ_p ⊆ ⋃_p cl(γ_p) ⊆ cl(⋃_p γ_p) by Theorem A (Cor. 4.4); hence cl(⋃_pΓ_p) ⊆ cl(⋃_pγ_p) ⊆ cl(⋃_pΓ_p). For the identification of cl(⋃_p Γ_p): q⁻¹(⋃_pΓ_p) = X̌₀(C)_per × R>0 (Thm 6.1: periodic points are exactly the packet points, and ⋃_p C_{x₀} = X̌₀(C)_per for E ⊇ E_f by Thm 5.2), and for the open surjection q one has cl(q(A)) = q(cl(A)) for saturated A [q(cl A) ⊆ cl(qA) by continuity; conversely q⁻¹(cl(qA)) is closed and contains A, and for any open q, q⁻¹(cl B) = cl(q⁻¹B) — if z̃ ∉ cl(q⁻¹B) pick an open Ũ ∋ z̃ missing q⁻¹B, then the open q(Ũ) ∋ q(z̃) misses B — so q⁻¹(cl(qA)) = cl(q⁻¹qA) = cl(A) and cl(qA) = q(cl A)]. Now cl_{X̃}(X̌₀(C)_per × R>0) = cl(X̌₀(C)_per) × R>0 = X̌₀(S¹) × R>0 by Thm 8.2 (unconditional for Spec Z, A10). ∎

**What line (1) yields for the kill, and what it does not.** The closure law answers the charter's question — the closure of the chosen family meets every other packet off the chosen orbit and swallows the whole unitary stratum — but, exactly as the adjudication foresaw, closure is the wrong instrument for a compact non-closed Y: compactness of Y forces only that every net in ⋃γ_p has a cluster point **in Y**, not that Y contains all cluster points. Lemma 1 tells us where those cluster points must lie (the generic unitary stratum), and that is the input for §6, where the compactness of Y is turned against itself: the forced cluster points are points whose backward orbits leave every compact set.

## 6. THEOREMS 2 and 3 — backward escape; compact flows cannot map into X₀ meeting infinitely many packets `[novelty: single-check]`

**Theorem 2 (backward escape of generic points).** Let z = [P, u] ∈ X₀ with P over the generic point η of Spec Z (any admissible E; any character, unitary or not). Then the backward half-orbit {φ^{−t}z : t ≥ 0} has **no cluster point in X₀**: for every net (t_β) with t_β → +∞, the net (φ^{−t_β}z) has no convergent subnet. Equivalently, the α-limit set of every generic point is empty.

*Proof.* Let P ∈ 𝒞_ν^E and P′ := F_νP = π(η, Ψ) ∈ X̊₀(C)^E, with e := |ker(Ψ|_μ)| as in Lemma 3.3. Suppose, for contradiction, that [P, w_β] → z* = [P*, u*] along a net with w_β := e^{−t_β}u → 0. Let ν′ be a chart index with P* ∈ 𝒞_{ν′}^E. By Lemma 3.1 there are a subnet and q_β ∈ Q>0 with (F_{q_β}P, q_β⁻¹w_β) → (P*, u*) in X̃. Then q_β⁻¹w_β → u* ∈ R>0 and w_β → 0 force q_β = w_β/(q_β⁻¹w_β) → 0. On the other hand F_{q_β}P → P* and 𝒞_{ν′}^E is open, so eventually F_{q_β}P ∈ 𝒞_{ν′}^E, i.e. F_{ν′q_β}P = F_{ν′q_β/ν}P′ ∈ X̊₀(C)^E. Write ν′q_β/ν = m_β/n_β in lowest terms. By Lemma 3.3, n_β | m_βe, so n_β ≤ m_βe and ν′q_β/ν = m_β/n_β ≥ 1/e. Hence q_β ≥ ν/(ν′e) > 0 eventually, contradicting q_β → 0. ∎

*Remarks.* (i) The bound is uniform along the orbit: the whole backward orbit of z leaves every compact subset of X₀ and never returns. Forward orbits are unconstrained by this argument (q_β → ∞ is compatible with bounded denominators; indeed by Prop. 4.6's Bohr-set mechanism generic unitary points are forward-recurrent). (ii) This is the precise content of Deninger's expectation (A11) that generic orbits should "tend to the fixed point [z, 0] … for u → 0+" of a compactification that X₀ lacks. (iii) Packet points behave oppositely: for P₀ over p, F_{p^{−j}}P₀ = P₀ lies in the first chart for all j, the denominators are unbounded, and the backward orbit is the periodic orbit itself.

**Corollary 6.1 (compact invariant subspaces avoid the generic stratum).** Let Y ⊆ X₀ be a compact (in the subspace topology) flow-invariant subspace. Then Y ⊆ ∐_p Γ^E_p, the periodic locus.

*Proof.* If z ∈ Y is generic, the net (φ^{−t}z)_{t ≥ 0} lies in the compact space Y and so has a cluster point in Y, hence in X₀ — contradicting Theorem 2. (Compactness of Y suffices; no separation axiom on Y is used.) ∎

**Theorem 3 (compact flows into X₀ meet finitely many packets).** Let Y be a compact topological space with a continuous R-action (φ_Y^t), and f: Y → X₀ a continuous flow-equivariant map. Then:

**(a)** f(Y) ⊆ ∐_p Γ^E_p.
**(b)** f(Y) meets only finitely many packets: there is a finite set S of primes with f(Y) ⊆ ⋃_{p∈S} Γ^E_p.

*Proof.* (a) Let y ∈ Y with f(y) generic. The net (φ_Y^{−t}y)_{t≥0} has a cluster point b ∈ Y (Y compact): some subnet φ_Y^{−t_β}y → b with t_β → ∞ (subnets are cofinal). By continuity and equivariance, f(φ_Y^{−t_β}y) = φ^{−t_β}f(y) → f(b), a cluster point of the backward orbit of the generic point f(y) — contradicting Theorem 2. (b) Suppose f(Y) meets Γ^E_{p_k} for infinitely many distinct primes p_1 < p_2 < ⋯; choose y_k with f(y_k) ∈ Γ^E_{p_k}. The sequence (y_k) has a cluster point y* ∈ Y, so a subnet y_{k_β} → y* with k_β → ∞ (cofinality), hence p_{k_β} → ∞. Then f(y_{k_β}) → f(y*), and Lemma 5.1 says f(y*) is generic — contradicting (a). ∎

**Corollary 6.2 (Q-a is NO — second proof, no separation axiom).** No compact flow-invariant subspace Y ⊆ X₀ meets infinitely many packets. In particular none meets every packet, with any multiplicity. (Apply Theorem 3 to the inclusion Y ↪ X₀, which is continuous and equivariant.)

**Corollary 6.3 (TRANSFER TO FACE (b): Q-b is NO).** Let (Y, φ_Y) be any compact space with a continuous R-action — in particular any compact metrizable lamination of any dimension with any family of closed orbits, with or without "exactly one closed orbit of length log p per prime". Then there is **no** continuous flow-equivariant map f: Y → X₀ with f(Y) ∩ Γ_p ≠ ∅ for infinitely many p. The same holds with the target replaced by the unitary system Y₀ = X̌₀(S¹) ×_{Q>0} R>0 (a closed invariant subspace of X₀ (Prop. 5.2), so a continuous equivariant map into Y₀ is one into X₀). Both alternatives of Deninger's question ([x-03] p. 40) — "a sub-dynamical system Y₀ ⊂ X₀ … or at least one which maps to X₀ … such that Y₀ contains at least one periodic orbit in Γ_{x₀} for every closed point x₀" — have answer **NO for compact Y₀**, for X₀ = Spec Z and every admissible E.

**6.4 Robustness of the transfer.** (i) *Time changes.* If f is only equivariant up to a continuous reparametrization — f(φ_Y^t y) = φ^{τ_y(t)}f(y) with τ_y(t) → −∞ as t → −∞ — the proof of Theorem 3(a) is unchanged (the backward orbit of f(y) still has a cluster point), and (b) uses only points. (ii) *Weaker continuity.* Only the continuity of f at the cluster points b (in (a)) and y* (in (b)) is used. (iii) *No orbit count, no dimension, no metrizability, no Hausdorffness of Y.* (iv) *Every admissible E.* Nothing in §§3–6 uses E ⊇ E_f except the identification of the closure in Prop. 5.2.

**6.5 Face (b) recast through Corollary 4.5 (for the record).** By Cor. 4.5, f: Y → X₀ is continuous iff k∘f: Y → X₀^{T₀} is, and k∘f sends each closed orbit γ_p to the fixed point k(Γ_p); so the mapping face never saw the periods log p through the topology at all — the only topological content was the accumulation of the fixed points k(Γ_p) (p → ∞) at generic points and the backward dynamics there. Theorem 3 shows that content is fatal.
