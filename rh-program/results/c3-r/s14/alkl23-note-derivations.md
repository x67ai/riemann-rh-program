## 1. Conventions

U ⊂ ℝ^n open (n ≥ 0), l ≥ 1. S^m = S^m(U × ℝ^l) with the seminorms (3.1). Every (3.4)- and (3.5)-seminorm is continuous on S^m: ‖a‖_{Q,C^k} ≤ max_{|α|+|β|≤k} sup_Q (1+|ξ|)^{m−|β|} ‖a‖_{K,α,β,m} for Q ⊂ K × B̄(0,ρ), and ‖a‖′_{K,α,β,m} ≤ ‖a‖_{K,α,β,m} because |ξ|^{|β|−m}/(1+|ξ|)^{|β|−m} → 1. So the topology S′^m of (3.4)+(3.5) is coarser than or equal to that of S^m, and the C^∞-topology (given by (3.4)) is coarser than or equal to S′^m. θ ∈ C_c^∞(ℝ^l) is a fixed bump with θ(0) = 1 and supp θ ⊂ B(0, r). All witnesses are independent of x (or have a fixed compactly supported factor in the base variable).

## 2. Witness (a): g_N. Prop. 3.2 and the first assertion of Cor. 3.4 are false

g_N(x, ξ) := θ(ξ − N e_1), N ∈ ℕ.

1. g_N ∈ C^∞(U × ℝ^l), independent of x, compactly supported in ξ, so ‖g_N‖_{K,α,β,m} < ∞ for all α, β, m (α ≠ 0 gives 0; α = 0: a bounded function times a bounded weight on a compact set). Hence g_N ∈ S^{−∞} ⊂ S^m for every m ∈ ℝ.
2. (3.4): if Q ⊂ K × B̄(0, ρ) then supp g_N ⊂ U × {|ξ| ≥ N − r}, so ‖g_N‖_{Q,C^k} = 0 for N > ρ + r. Hence g_N → 0 in C^∞(U × ℝ^l).
3. (3.5): for each x, ∂^α_x∂^β_ξ g_N(x, ξ) = 0 for |ξ| > N + r, so the limsup is 0: every (3.5)-seminorm of g_N is 0.
4. (3.1) with α = 0: ‖g_N‖_{K,0,β,m} = sup_ξ |∂^βθ(ξ − Ne_1)|(1+|ξ|)^{|β|−m} = sup_{|η|≤r} |∂^βθ(η)|(1+|η+Ne_1|)^{|β|−m}. For |η| ≤ r, N − r ≤ |η + Ne_1| ≤ N + r. If |β| − m > 0, then ‖g_N‖_{K,0,β,m} ≥ (1 + N − r)^{|β|−m} sup|∂^βθ| → ∞ provided ∂^βθ ≢ 0.
5. Existence of β with |β| > m and ∂^βθ ≢ 0: if all derivatives of some order k vanished identically, θ would be a polynomial of degree < k, and a compactly supported polynomial is 0, contradicting θ(0) = 1. So for every k there is β with |β| = k and ∂^βθ ≢ 0.

Conclusions. (i) g_N → 0 in S′^m but (g_N) is unbounded in S^m, so the identity S^m → S′^m is continuous and not a homeomorphism: **Prop. 3.2 is false** for every m ∈ ℝ, every U, every l ≥ 1. (ii) For m < m′ choose |β| > m′: g_N ∈ S^m, g_N → 0 in C^∞, g_N ↛ 0 in S^{m′} (unbounded). **The first assertion of Cor. 3.4 is false** for every m < m′. (In fact for all m, m′.)

## 3. Witness (b): a Cauchy sequence of S′^m with no limit; the failing sentence

Fix R > 2r, so the translates θ(ξ − iRe_1), i ∈ ℕ, have pairwise disjoint supports. c_i := (1 + iR)^{m+1}, b_j := Σ_{i=1}^{j} c_i θ(ξ − iRe_1) (independent of x). Each b_j ∈ S^{−∞} ⊂ S^m.

1. For i < j, b_j − b_i = Σ_{k=i+1}^{j} c_k θ(ξ − kRe_1) is supported in {|ξ| ≥ (i+1)R − r} and has compact ξ-support. So all its (3.5)-seminorms vanish, and for Q ⊂ K × B̄(0, ρ) its (3.4)-seminorms vanish as soon as (i+1)R − r > ρ. Hence for every continuous seminorm p of S′^m there is i_0 with p(b_j − b_i) = 0 for all j > i ≥ i_0: **(b_j) is Cauchy in S′^m.**
2. b := Σ_{i≥1} c_i θ(ξ − iRe_1) is a locally finite sum, hence smooth, and b_j → b in C^∞(U × ℝ^l) (on a compact ξ-set, b_j = b for j large).
3. b ∉ S^m: at ξ = iRe_1 only the i-th term is nonzero, so |b(x, iRe_1)| = c_i, and |b(x, iRe_1)|(1 + |iRe_1|)^{−m} = (1+iR)^{m+1}(1+iR)^{−m} = 1 + iR → ∞; thus ‖b‖_{K,0,0,m} = ∞ (any real m).
4. If b_j → b̃ in S′^m with b̃ ∈ S^m, then b_j → b̃ in C^∞ (S′^m → C^∞ continuous), so b̃ = b by Hausdorffness of C^∞, contradicting 3. **So (b_j) has no limit in S′^m; S′^m is not complete**, contrary to "Hence S′^m(U × ℝ^l) is complete" (p. 17).
5. The failing sentence. Let a ∈ Ŝ′^m be the class of (b_j) in the completion. The continuous extension of ‖·‖′_{K,0,0,m} satisfies ‖a‖′_{K,0,0,m} = lim_j ‖b_j‖′_{K,0,0,m} = 0. The extended map φ: Ŝ′^m → C^∞ sends a to lim b_j = b, and ‖φ(a)‖′_{K,0,0,m} = ‖b‖′_{K,0,0,m} = sup_x limsup_{|ξ|→∞} |b(x,ξ)|/|ξ|^m ≥ limsup_i (1+iR)^{m+1}/(iR)^m = ∞. So the printed "‖φ(a)‖′_{K,α,β,m} = ‖a‖′_{K,α,β,m} < ∞" (p. 17, line 2) is false: the (3.5)-seminorms of the completion are not computed by φ.

(A second gap in the same sentence, noted in the adjudication: finiteness of a sup over x ∈ K of limsups gives no threshold R uniform in x ∈ K. Not used in the note.)

## 4. Witness (c): the second assertion of Cor. 3.4 is false

c_j := e^j θ(ξ − je_1), j ∈ ℕ. As in §2, c_j ∈ S^{−∞} ⊂ S^m for all m, and c_j → 0 in C^∞ (supports leave every compact set).

Fix x_0 ∈ U, K := {x_0}. For k ∈ ℕ: ε_k := ½ inf_{j≥1} e^j (1+j)^{−k}. The sequence e^j(1+j)^{−k} is positive and tends to ∞, so ε_k > 0. W_k := {a ∈ S^k : ‖a‖_{K,0,0,k} < ε_k}, an open 0-neighborhood of S^k. W := absolutely convex hull of ⋃_{k≥1} W_k (finite sums Σλ_i w_i with Σ|λ_i| ≤ 1, w_i ∈ ⋃W_k).

1. W is a 0-neighborhood of S^∞ = ind_k S^k: it is absolutely convex and W ∩ S^k ⊇ W_k is a 0-neighborhood of S^k for every k ∈ ℕ (definition of the locally convex inductive limit topology: the absolutely convex sets meeting every step in a 0-neighborhood form a 0-basis). Since S^∞ = ⋃_{m∈ℝ} S^m = ⋃_{k∈ℕ} S^k with continuous inclusions S^m ⊂ S^k (k ≥ m), the inductive limit over ℝ and over ℕ carry the same topology (the paper's own real-index license, p. 5).
2. If c_j ∈ W, write c_j = Σ_i λ_i w_i as above. For w ∈ W_k, |w(x_0, ξ)| ≤ ‖w‖_{K,0,0,k}(1+|ξ|)^k < ε_k(1+|ξ|)^k. At ξ = je_1: |w_i(x_0, je_1)| < ε_{k_i}(1+j)^{k_i} ≤ ½ e^j (by definition of ε_{k_i}, ε_{k_i}(1+j)^{k_i} ≤ ½ e^j(1+j)^{−k_i}(1+j)^{k_i}). Hence e^j = |c_j(x_0, je_1)| ≤ Σ|λ_i| |w_i(x_0,je_1)| < ½ e^j, contradiction.
3. So c_j ∉ W for every j: c_j ↛ 0 in S^∞, while c_j → 0 in C^∞ and c_j ∈ S^m for every m. **The topologies of S^∞ and C^∞ differ on every S^m.** No regularity of S^∞ is used.

## 5. Witness (d): Cor. 4.5 is false for every compact (M, L), codim ≥ 1; Cor. 6.21

Setting: M compact, L closed regular submanifold of codimension n′ ≥ 1, n = dim M, n″ = n − n′. Chart (U_1, x = (x′, x″)) adapted to L (p. 19: L ∩ U_1 = {x′ = 0}, x′ ∈ ℝ^{n′}), chosen around p_0 ∈ L with x(p_0) = 0 and x(U_1) ⊃ {|x′| < 1} × U″. m < m′ < m″, m̄ = m + n/4 − n′/2 etc. (4.9); m̄′ < m̄″.

Partition of unity: given any {h, f_j} subordinate to {M∖L, U_j} and ρ ∈ C_c^∞(U_1) with ρ = 1 on a neighborhood N of p_0, the family h̃ := (1−ρ)h, f̃_1 := ρ + (1−ρ)f_1, f̃_j := (1−ρ)f_j (j ≥ 2) is smooth, nonnegative, sums to ρ + (1−ρ) = 1, has supp h̃ ⊂ supp h ⊂ M∖L, supp f̃_j ⊂ supp f_j ⊂ U_j (j ≥ 2), supp f̃_1 ⊂ supp ρ ∪ supp f_1 ⊂ U_1: a partition of unity subordinate to the same cover with f̃_1 = 1, h̃ = f̃_j = 0 on N. The paper allows any such partition (p. 22). Independence of the topology of I^m from this choice: any two Fréchet topologies on I^m(M, L) both finer than the (Hausdorff) topology induced from C^{−∞}(M) coincide by the closed graph theorem (I^m ⊂ I^{(s)} ⊂ H^s(M) ⊂ C^{−∞}(M) continuously by (4.12) and the definitions). ### 9.1 The models

### 9.2 Lemma A (one variable; Vandermonde)
N ≥ 1, f ∈ C^N([t, t+Nh]), h > 0. Then for 0 ≤ j ≤ N: |f^{(j)}(t)| ≤ C_N(h^{−j}‖f‖_∞ + h^{N−j}‖f^{(N)}‖_∞) (sup norms on [t, t+Nh]).
Proof. Taylor at t: f(t+kh) = Σ_{i=0}^{N−1} y_i k^i + r_k with y_i := f^{(i)}(t)h^i/i! and |r_k| ≤ (kh)^N‖f^{(N)}‖/N! ≤ (Nh)^N‖f^{(N)}‖/N!, k = 0, …, N−1. The matrix (k^i)_{0≤k,i≤N−1} is Vandermonde with distinct nodes 0, …, N−1, hence invertible with an inverse depending only on N. So |y_j| ≤ C_N max_k|f(t+kh) − r_k| ≤ C_N(‖f‖ + (Nh)^N‖f^{(N)}‖/N!), and |f^{(j)}(t)| = j!h^{−j}|y_j| ≤ C′_N(h^{−j}‖f‖ + h^{N−j}‖f^{(N)}‖). (For j = N use f^{(N)} directly.) ∎

### 9.3 Lemma B (box, per-variable scales)
F ∈ C^∞ on a neighborhood of the box Q = ∏_{i=1}^{D}[t_i, t_i + Nh_i], γ ∈ ℕ^D with γ_i ≤ N. Then
|∂^γF(t)| ≤ C_{N,D} Σ_{S⊂{1,…,D}} (∏_{i∈S} h_i^{N−γ_i})(∏_{i∉S} h_i^{−γ_i}) sup_Q |∂^{Nχ_S}F|,

### 9.4 Proposition (interpolation inequality)

Proof. Fix (x, ξ), w := 1+|ξ|.
(i) w ≤ R: |∂^γa|w^{|β|−m′} = |∂^γa|w^{|β|−m″}·w^{m″−m′} ≤ R^{m″−m′}N_{m″}(a; γ). (For γ = 0 the same.)

Lemma B (D = d + l, γ_i ≤ |γ| < N) and multiplication by w^{|β|−m′}:
 - S = ∅: (∏h_i^{−γ_i}) = w^{c|α|}w^{−(1−c)|β|}; total exponent c|α| − (1−c)|β| + m + |β| − m′ = c|γ| + m − m′ = −(m′−m)/2. Contribution ≤ C w^{−(m′−m)/2}N_m(a;0) ≤ C R^{−(m′−m)/2}N_m(a; 0).
 - S ≠ ∅: (∏_{i∈S}h_i^{N−γ_i})(∏_{i∉S}h_i^{−γ_i}) = w^{−c(N|S_x|−|α_S|)} w^{(1−c)(N|S_ξ|−|β_S|)} w^{c|α_{S^c}|} w^{−(1−c)|β_{S^c}|}; with the sup factor w^{m″−N|S_ξ|} and the weight w^{|β|−m′} the total exponent is −cN|S_x| + c|α| + N|S_ξ| − cN|S_ξ| − |β| + c|β| + m″ − N|S_ξ| + |β| − m′ = −c(N|S| − |γ|) + m″ − m′ ≤ −c(N−|γ|) + m″ − m′ < 0 by the choice of N. Contribution ≤ C N_{m″}(a; Nχ_S) ≤ C R^{m″−m′}N_{m″}(a; Nχ_S) (R ≥ 1).
Taking the sup over (x, ξ) gives the inequality. For γ = 0 and w > R: |a|w^{−m′} = |a|w^{−m}w^{m−m′} ≤ R^{m−m′}N_m(a; 0). ∎
Where compact base support enters: N_m(a; 0) and N_{m″}(a; ·) are global suprema, so the box may be placed anywhere; for S^m(U × ℝ^l) with non-compact U the seminorms over a fixed compact K control nothing outside K, which is exactly witness (f)5.

### 9.5 From the inequality to the criterion of p. 4
(M*) at (m, m′, m″): V := {a ∈ S^m_K : N_m(a; 0) < 1}. Given a basic 0-neighborhood O = {N_{m′}(·; γ_i) < ε, i ≤ p} of S^{m′}_K, choose R ≥ R_0 with C_iR^{−(m′−m)/2} < ε/2 for all i, then δ > 0 with C_iR^{m″−m′}δ < ε/2, and W := {N_{m″}(·; γ′) < δ, γ′ ∈ ⋃_iΓ_i}. By 9.4, V ∩ W ⊂ O.
(M*) ⟹ coincidence on V (the form quoted on p. 4): the S^{m″}-topology is coarser than the S^{m′}-topology, so it suffices that for a ∈ V and O there is W_0 with (a + W_0) ∩ V ⊂ a + O. Take W_0 with V ∩ W_0 ⊂ ½O (½O is also a 0-neighborhood). If b ∈ V, b − a ∈ W_0, then (b−a)/2 ∈ V (absolutely convex) and (b−a)/2 ∈ W_0, so (b−a)/2 ∈ ½O, b − a ∈ O. ∎ So for every m the topologies of S^{m′}_K and S^{m″}_K coincide on the 0-neighborhood V of S^m_K, for all m′ > m and m″ > m′: this is "[39, Theorem 6.1]" as quoted, with k′ = k+1; hence **⋃_m S^m_K is acyclic** (indices in ℕ by cofinality, p. 5).
Transfer rules (each immediate): (M*) holds for a constant spectrum X_k = X (V = X, W = O); for finite products (product neighborhoods); for a spectrum (Y_k) with Y_k carrying the initial topology of a fixed linear map j with j(Y_k) ⊂ X_k (V_Y := j^{−1}(V) ∩ Y_k; for O_Y ⊇ j^{−1}(O) ∩ Y_{k′} take W_Y := j^{−1}(W) ∩ Y_{k″}; then V_Y ∩ W_Y ⊂ Y_k ⊂ Y_{k′} and j(V_Y ∩ W_Y) ⊂ V ∩ W ⊂ O); in particular for closed subspaces with the induced topologies.

### 9.7 Assembly (M compact, or compact with boundary)
- I(M, L): I^m carries the initial topology of (4.10) into C^∞(M∖L) × ∏_j S^{m̄}_{K_j} (9.1), the same map for all m; the target spectrum is a finite product of a constant spectrum and models satisfying (M*) at every triple; so (I^m)_m satisfies (M*), and ⋃_m I^m is acyclic. Steps are Fréchet (p. 22).
- A(M): take a partition of unity {λ_j} ∪ {μ} with λ_j supported in collar charts and μ in M̊, and Λ: u ↦ ((λ_ju)(e^{−ϱ}, y))_j ⊕ μu ∈ ∏_j B^m_{K_j} × C^∞_{K′}(M̊) (K_j the y-projection of supp λ_j, K′ = supp μ). Continuity: sup e^{mϱ}|∂^a_ϱ∂^b_y(λ_ju)| = sup x^{−m}|(x∂_x)^a∂_y^b(λ_ju)| is the seminorm of P := (x∂_x)^a∂_y^b∘λ_j ∈ Diff_b(M); μu by (6.33). - Hence all six LF-spaces are acyclic, and, the steps being Fréchet, boundedly/compactly/sequentially retractive, complete, regular ("[39, Proposition 6.4], [39, Corollary 6.5]", p. 5). This proves the acyclicity clauses of Cor. 3.6 (compact base), 4.7, 6.16, 6.22, 6.28, 7.15, 7.23.
- Montel clauses: bounded subsets of S^m_K are relatively compact in S^{m′}_K for m′ > m (Arzelà–Ascoli gives a C^∞-convergent subsequence on compacta; the limit a satisfies the S^m bounds; N_{m′}(a_ν − a; γ) ≤ 2C(1+R)^{m−m′} on |ξ| > R plus a uniform-convergence term on K × B̄(0,R)).
