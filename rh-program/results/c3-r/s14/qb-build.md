## 0. VERDICT (stated first): **THEOREM (NO)** — Q-b is dead, and it is dead for a reason that touches none of the structure the question asked about

The construction cannot be carried out, and the obstruction is a theorem about X₀ alone. What is proved here, in full:

> **Theorem 1 (the escape function).** Let Spec Z be the base, C = ℂ, N₀ = N, E any admissible class of characters ([x-03] Def. 4.1, p. 27), and X₀ = X̌₀(ℂ)_E ×_{ℚ^{>0}} ℝ^{>0} Deninger's dynamical system with the quotient topology and the flow φ^t[P₀,u] = [P₀,ue^t] ([x-03] §6 p. 38). Put w([P₀,u]) := ρ(P₀)·u ∈ (0,∞) if P₀ lies over the generic point of Spec Z, where ρ is the torsion-kernel order of [x-03] (41)–(45) (pp. 33–34), and w := +∞ on the points over closed points. Then w : X₀ → (0, +∞] is well defined, **lower semicontinuous**, satisfies **w(φ^t z) = e^t · w(z)** for all t ∈ ℝ, and **w^{-1}(+∞) = ⨆_p Γ^E_p** is exactly the union of the packets.

> **Theorem 2 (no quasi-compact invariant set meets the generic locus).** Every quasi-compact flow-invariant subset K ⊆ X₀ (subspace topology; Hausdorffness not assumed) is contained in ⨆_p Γ^E_p.

> **Theorem 3 (finiteness).** Every quasi-compact flow-invariant subset K ⊆ X₀ is contained in ⨆_{p∈S} Γ^E_p for a **finite** set S of primes, and K = ⨆_{p∈S} (K ∩ Γ^E_p) is a partition of K into pairwise disjoint sets that are open and closed in K and flow-invariant.

> **Corollary 4 (Q-b: NO).** Let Y be any quasi-compact topological space with an ℝ-action (continuity of the action on Y is not needed) and f : Y → X₀ any continuous map with f(φ_Y^t y) = φ^t f(y). Then f(Y) meets only finitely many packets. In particular **no compact Y whatsoever — metrizable or not, of any dimension, laminated or not, with any orbit structure — admits a continuous flow-equivariant map into X₀ (or into the unitary subsystem X̌₀(S¹)_E ×_{ℚ^{>0}} ℝ^{>0}) sending a closed orbit γ_p into Γ_p for infinitely many primes p.** Q-b, which asks for this for every prime, has answer NO for every admissible E.

**Consequences proved alongside (§5):** Q-a is NO by a second, independent route (Cor. 5); **X₀ itself is not quasi-compact**, nor is Deninger's Y₀ = X̌₀(S¹) ×_{ℚ^{>0}} ℝ^{>0}, nor any cut suspension, nor any flow-invariant subset meeting infinitely many packets (Cor. 6 — this settles the compactness questions the sibling Q-a probe left open and the ledger's W1 "not established anywhere"); every compact subset of X₀, invariant or not, is bounded away from the archimedean end w → 0 (Cor. 7); and **compactness is the exact obstruction**: the non-compact one-dimensional flow Y_∞ = ⨆_p ℝ^{>0}/p^ℤ (one circle of length log p per prime, no other orbits) does map continuously and equivariantly into X₀ with γ_p → Γ_p for every p (Prop. 8).

**Candidate by candidate (§6):** (1) [Den05] §7.7 tower suspensions M̄ ×_{lℤ} ℝ fail already at the orbit spectrum (all lengths lie in l·N; {log p} does not — Prop. 6.1); every other compact solenoidal suspension, and every compact Y of any kind, fails at the map f (Cor. 4) — the property that fails is "f continuous, equivariant, with f(γ_p) ⊂ Γ_p for all p", and it fails for every possible Y, so the existence of the lamination itself is moot. (2) Any recipe for f from Frobenius/Witt-vector data is subject to Cor. 4 as soon as its domain is compact; no recipe can evade a theorem about the target. (3) The cut suspensions X₀^{E(a)} are not compact (Prop. 6.3(a), independently of the sibling's Prop. 7.1), not T₀ (sibling Theorem 1, re-derived here in outline), of dimension 0, and hence not laminations; the enlarged cuts X₀^{E(a)′} are not quasi-compact either (Cor. 6).

**What is NOT claimed.** The theorem is about Deninger's X₀ as constructed in [x-03], and about its E-subsystems, including the unitary one. It says nothing about the conjectural compactification X̄₀ of [x-03] p. 39, whose added fixed points are precisely where the escape function goes to zero (§7). That is the single residual escape, and it is named exactly in §7.3 as a re-posed question Q-b′ — with the structural constraint Theorem 1 imposes on any such object (every backward orbit must accumulate only at the fixed points). It is not a gap in the proof; it is a different target, and building it is the substrate problem itself.

**Program effect (§10).** Together with the sibling `qa-build.md` (Q-a: NO by packet indiscreteness) and with Cor. 5 here, **both faces of Q\* are NO for X₀ as constructed**. Per the adjudication's own clause ("a NO on both kills S4 and with it Route 2, firing the kill-criterion input") the kill-criterion input FIRES for S4-in-X₀; whether the program re-poses S4 against a compactification X̄₀ (Q-b′) is a program decision this note does not make.

## 2. The objects, written out (transcription of the anchors; nothing new here)

Throughout: the base scheme is **Spec Z**, K₀ = ℚ, K = ℚ̄, X = Spec ℤ̄ (the normalization of Spec Z in ℚ̄), G = Aut(ℚ̄/ℚ), **C = ℂ**, **N₀ = N**, ℚ₀^{>0} = ℚ^{>0} ([x-03] §8 p. 49: "we take N₀ = N"). "The system X₀" always means the dynamical system; the scheme is always written Spec Z.

**2.1 Points.** X̊(ℂ) is the set of pairs (x, P^×), x ∈ X, P^× : κ(x)^× → ℂ^× a homomorphism; for the affine X = Spec ℤ̄ these are the multiplicative maps P : ℤ̄ → ℂ with P(0) = 0, P(1) = 1, p̄_x := P^{-1}(0) a prime ideal, P factoring through ℤ̄/p̄_x, and P^× the induced character of κ(p̄_x)^× (Remark 3.4, p. 23). Since Spec ℤ̄ has dimension 1, x is either the **generic point** η (p̄_η = 0, κ(η) = ℚ̄) or a **closed point over a rational prime p** (κ(x) = F̄_p). G acts by (x, P^×)σ = (x^σ, P^×∘σ); F_ν(x, P^×) = (x, P^×∘( )^ν) for ν ∈ N (p. 22); both preserve the point x's image in Spec Z (F_ν does not change x; G permutes the points over a fixed closed point).

**2.2 Admissible classes.** E is admissible iff for all σ ∈ Aut κ and ν ∈ N: χ ∈ E ⟺ χ∘σ ∈ E ⟺ χ∘( )^ν ∈ E, **and every χ ∈ E satisfies (Tors)**: ker(χ|_{μ(κ)}) is finite of order in N (Def. 4.1, p. 27). Hence E ⊆ E_tors; over Spec Z the condition (Image) is vacuous (it constrains only characters of positive-characteristic fields κ with κ^× not torsion, and κ(x)^× = F̄_p^× is torsion), so E ⊆ E_max and Thms. 5.2/6.1 apply to every admissible E. The generic E-locus is nonempty for E ⊇ E_f (Lemma 4.3 + Cor. 4.4, p. 28: injective characters ℚ̄^× ↪ ℂ^× exist and lie in E_f).

**2.3 Topologies.** X̊(ℂ): pointwise convergence on ℤ̄ (Tychonov subspace; metrizable) (p. 40). X̊₀(ℂ) = X̊(ℂ)/G: quotient topology (p. 42); π : X̊(ℂ) → X̊₀(ℂ) continuous and open (p. 43). X̌(ℂ) = colim_N X̊(ℂ): inductive-limit topology (53) (p. 43); by Prop. 7.4, X̊(ℂ) is clopen in X̌(ℂ), F_q is a homeomorphism for every q ∈ ℚ^{>0}, G acts by homeomorphisms; hence every **chart** F_ν^{-1}X̊(ℂ) is open in X̌(ℂ) and F_ν : F_ν^{-1}X̊(ℂ) → X̊(ℂ) is a homeomorphism; the charts cover X̌(ℂ). X̌₀(ℂ) = X̌(ℂ)/G: quotient topology; π̌ continuous and open (p. 43). The E-versions carry the subspace topologies, which agree with the inductive-limit topologies, and "all preceding results in this section remain true" for them (p. 47); in particular the E-charts F_ν^{-1}X̊(ℂ)_E = F_ν^{-1}X̊(ℂ) ∩ X̌(ℂ)_E (Prop. 4.2's forward-and-backward invariance) are open in X̌(ℂ)_E and F_ν maps them homeomorphically onto X̊(ℂ)_E; π̌ : X̌(ℂ)_E → X̌₀(ℂ)_E is continuous, open and surjective (restriction of an open map to a G-saturated subspace: for U = X̌(ℂ)_E ∩ V with V open, π̌(U) = π̌(X̌(ℂ)_E) ∩ π̌(V) because X̌(ℂ)_E is G-saturated).

**2.4 The suspension.** X₀ := X̌₀(ℂ)_E ×_{ℚ^{>0}} ℝ^{>0} is the set of orbits of the right action (P₀, u)·q = (F_q(P₀), q^{-1}u) (p. 38), with the flow φ^t[P₀, u] = [P₀, ue^t]. It carries the **quotient topology** of X̌₀(ℂ)_E × ℝ^{>0}; the warrant is the same as in every previous program note (adjudication §2(ii); probe B §3 referee block; `qa-build.md` §2.3): [x-03] §10 p. 63 defines the leafwise sheaf on X = M ×_Q ℝ^{>0} as R_X = (π_*R_X̃)^Q ⊂ (π_*C⁰_X̃)^Q = C⁰_X and states that π|_{M×{u}} is a continuous bijection onto its image which "will not be a homeomorphism if π(M × {u}) is equipped with the subspace topology of X"; and [x-06] p. 11 writes X₀ = (X̌₀(C) × R^{>0})/Q^{>0} outright. Write **Q : X̌₀(ℂ)_E × ℝ^{>0} → X₀** for the quotient map. Q is continuous (definition) and **open** (Q^{-1}(Q(U)) = ⋃_{q} U·q, and each (P₀,u) ↦ (F_q P₀, q^{-1}u) is a homeomorphism: F_q is a homeomorphism of X̌₀(ℂ) since π̌ is an open quotient and F_q∘π̌ = π̌∘F_q, and it preserves X̌₀(ℂ)_E in both directions by Prop. 4.2). If a strictly coarser topology on X₀ were intended, every theorem below would only get stronger (fewer open sets, fewer quasi-compact-set covers to test — see Remark 3.5).

**2.5 The map to Spec Z; packets; the two loci.** By Prop. A.1′ of the Session-14 referee block (probe B §5, referee grade; re-derived in one line here): pr_{X₀} : X̌₀(ℂ)_E → Spec Z is continuous (p. 43 with p. 47) and ℚ^{>0}-invariant with trivial action on Spec Z (p. 27), so (P₀,u) ↦ pr_{X₀}(P₀) descends through Q to a continuous flow-invariant **Π : X₀ → Spec Z**. Since C_{x₀} = pr_{X₀}^{-1}(x₀) (p. 31) and Γ^E_{x₀} = C^E_{x₀} ×_{ℚ^{>0}} ℝ^{>0} (p. 38), **Γ^E_p = Π^{-1}((p))** for every prime p, and these are closed in X₀ (the closed points of Spec Z are closed). Define

  **X₀^{gen} := Π^{-1}((0))** (the *generic locus*) and **X₀^{per} := Π^{-1}({closed points}) = ⨆_p Γ^E_p** (the *periodic locus*).

By Thm. 6.1 (p. 39) X₀^{per} is exactly the set of points with nontrivial isotropy, each on a circle of length log p; every point of X₀^{gen} lies on an injectively parametrized ℝ-orbit. Both loci are flow-invariant and X₀ = X₀^{gen} ⨆ X₀^{per}.

**2.6 The unitary subsystem.** X̊(S¹) ⊂ X̊(ℂ) is the closed subspace of points with unitary P^× (p. 50); Y₀^{Den} := X̌₀(S¹)_E ×_{ℚ^{>0}} ℝ^{>0} is the closure of the union of the periodic orbits ([x-03] Thm. 8.2, unconditional for Spec Z via [Per11]; [x-06] p. 12 states verbatim that the closure is the E-subsystem built from unitary characters). It is a flow-invariant subspace of X₀, and every packet lies in it (characters of the torsion group F̄_p^× are unitary). Its subspace topology is the suspension topology of X̌₀(S¹)_E × ℝ^{>0} (sibling `qa-build.md` Lemma 3.2, which is the saturated-subspace argument of §2.3 applied to Q). Everything proved for X₀ below applies verbatim to Y₀^{Den} and to every E-subsystem, since a quasi-compact invariant subset of a subspace is one of X₀.

**2.7 Q-b, as posed** (adjudication §5): does a compact metrizable 3-dimensional lamination (Y, F, φ) with exactly one closed orbit γ_p of length log p per prime admit a continuous flow-equivariant f : Y → X₀ (or → Y₀^{Den}) with f(γ_p) ⊂ Γ_p for every p?

## 3. The escape function w and its lower semicontinuity (Theorem 1)

**3.1 Two elementary facts about torsion kernels.**

**Lemma 1.** Let P = (x, P^×) ∈ X̊(ℂ)_E, viewed as a multiplicative map P : ℤ̄ → ℂ.
(a) Every root of unity ζ ∈ μ(ℚ̄) lies in ℤ̄, and P(ζ) ∈ μ_{ord ζ}(ℂ) — a root of unity of order dividing ord ζ — for every point P (of either characteristic).
(b) If x = η (characteristic 0), then ker(P^×|_{μ(ℚ̄)}) is the finite cyclic group μ_{ρ(P)}(ℚ̄), where ρ(P) = |(ker P^×)_tors| ∈ N is the number of [x-03] (41); every subgroup of μ(ℚ̄) of order n is μ_n.
(c) If x lies over the prime p, then: P(r) = 0 for r ∈ p̄_x; |P(r)| = 1 for r ∈ ℤ̄ ∖ p̄_x (indeed P(r) = P^×(r̄) with r̄ ∈ F̄_p^× of finite order, so P(r) is a root of unity); and **P(ζ) = 1 for every ζ ∈ μ_{p^∞}(ℚ̄)**.

*Proof.* (a) ζ is integral over ℤ, so ζ ∈ ℤ̄; P(ζ)^{ord ζ} = P(ζ^{ord ζ}) = P(1) = 1 by multiplicativity and P(1) = 1. (b) μ(ℚ̄) ≅ ℚ/ℤ, whose finite subgroups are exactly the cyclic groups μ_n, one for each n; (Tors) says ker(P^×|_{μ(κ(η))}) is finite of order ρ(P), so it is μ_{ρ(P)}. (c) The first two clauses are Remark 3.4 and the torsion of F̄_p^× ([x-03] p. 50: "Any character of the torsion group F̄_p^× takes values in the roots of unity"). For ζ with ζ^{p^k} = 1, the reduction ζ̄ ∈ F̄_p satisfies ζ̄^{p^k} = 1, and in characteristic p one has T^{p^k} − 1 = (T − 1)^{p^k}, so ζ̄ = 1 and P(ζ) = P^×(ζ̄) = 1. ∎

*(This is the content of [x-03] (62)–(63), p. 48: the characters in H̊_{fin,p} are those with χ|_{μ_{p^∞}(K)} = 1 and finite kernel on μ^{(p)}(K). The lemma is written out because Theorem 1 rests on it.)*

**3.2 The definition of w.** For Q̌ ∈ X̌(ℂ)_E lying over η write Q̌ = F_ν^{-1}(P) with P ∈ X̊(ℂ)_E over η and ν ∈ N, and set

  ρ̌(Q̌) := ρ(P)/ν ∈ ℚ^{>0}.

*Well defined:* if F_ν^{-1}(P) = F_{ν′}^{-1}(P′) in the colimit then, at a common level ν″ ∈ νN ∩ ν′N, F_{ν″/ν}(P) = F_{ν″/ν′}(P′) in X̊(ℂ) (the colimit is along injections, Prop. 4.2), and by (43) with ν_x = ν in characteristic 0, (ν″/ν)ρ(P) = (ν″/ν′)ρ(P′), i.e. ρ(P)/ν = ρ(P′)/ν′. This is [x-03]'s map (45) restricted to the E-locus, and Prop. 5.1's displayed formula "ρ(Q̌) = ν_x^{-1}|(ker P^×)_tors|" (p. 34). *ℚ^{>0}-equivariance:* ρ̌(F_q Q̌) = q·ρ̌(Q̌) for q ∈ ℚ^{>0} (for q = m/n: F_{m/n}F_ν^{-1}P = F_{nν}^{-1}F_m P, and ρ(F_m P) = mρ(P)). *G-invariance:* ρ(P∘σ) = ρ(P) because ker((P∘σ)|_μ) = σ^{-1}(ker(P|_μ)) has the same order; so ρ̌ descends to **ρ̌₀ : X̌₀(ℂ)_E ∩ pr_{X₀}^{-1}(η₀) → ℚ^{>0}** ("ρ factors over a map ρ₀", p. 33), still ℚ^{>0}-equivariant.

Define w̃ : X̌₀(ℂ)_E × ℝ^{>0} → (0, +∞] by

  w̃(P₀, u) := ρ̌₀(P₀)·u if pr_{X₀}(P₀) = (0),  w̃(P₀, u) := +∞ if pr_{X₀}(P₀) is a closed point.

Then w̃((P₀,u)·q) = w̃(F_q P₀, q^{-1}u) = q ρ̌₀(P₀)·q^{-1}u = w̃(P₀,u) on the generic part, and the closed-point part is ℚ^{>0}-saturated (pr_{X₀} is ℚ^{>0}-invariant, p. 27). Hence w̃ descends to

  **w : X₀ → (0, +∞], w([P₀,u]) = ρ̌₀(P₀)·u on X₀^{gen}, w ≡ +∞ on X₀^{per} = ⨆_p Γ^E_p,**

and w(φ^t[P₀,u]) = w([P₀, ue^t]) = e^t w([P₀,u]) (with e^t·∞ = ∞). The equality w^{-1}(∞) = ⨆_p Γ^E_p is §2.5. What remains for Theorem 1 is lower semicontinuity.

**3.3 The chart-level semicontinuity lemma.**

**Lemma 2.** Let (P_α, u_α)_α be a net in X̊(ℂ)_E × ℝ^{>0} with every P_α over η, converging in X̊(ℂ) × ℝ^{>0} to (P, u) with P ∈ X̊(ℂ)_E and u ∈ ℝ^{>0}. Then:
(a) if P lies over η: ρ(P) divides ρ(P_α) for all α beyond some index, and hence lim inf_α ρ(P_α)u_α ≥ ρ(P)·u;
(b) if P lies over a closed point x over p: ρ(P_α)u_α → +∞.

*Proof.* Convergence in the pointwise topology means P_α(r) → P(r) in ℂ for every r ∈ ℤ̄; apply this to r = ζ ∈ μ(ℚ̄) ⊂ ℤ̄. By Lemma 1(a) the values P_α(ζ) and P(ζ) all lie in the **finite** set μ_{ord ζ}(ℂ). A finite subset of the Hausdorff space ℂ is discrete, so a net with values in it converges to P(ζ) only if it is eventually equal to P(ζ): **for every ζ ∈ μ(ℚ̄), P_α(ζ) = P(ζ) for all α beyond some index α_ζ.** Since the index set is directed, for every finite set Z ⊂ μ(ℚ̄) there is an index beyond which P_α(ζ) = P(ζ) for all ζ ∈ Z simultaneously.

(a) Take Z = μ_{ρ(P)}(ℚ̄) = ker(P^×|_μ) (Lemma 1(b); finite). Beyond the corresponding index, P_α(ζ) = P(ζ) = 1 for all ζ ∈ Z, i.e. μ_{ρ(P)} ⊆ ker(P_α^×|_μ) = μ_{ρ(P_α)}, i.e. ρ(P) | ρ(P_α), so ρ(P_α) ≥ ρ(P). Since u_α → u, lim inf ρ(P_α)u_α ≥ ρ(P)u.

(b) Fix k ≥ 1 and take Z = μ_{p^k}(ℚ̄) (finite, of order p^k). By Lemma 1(c), P(ζ) = 1 for all ζ ∈ Z, so beyond some index P_α(ζ) = 1 for all ζ ∈ Z, i.e. μ_{p^k} ⊆ μ_{ρ(P_α)}, i.e. p^k | ρ(P_α), so ρ(P_α) ≥ p^k. As k is arbitrary, ρ(P_α) → +∞; since u_α → u > 0, ρ(P_α)u_α → +∞. ∎

**3.4 Theorem 1 (the escape function).** *w : X₀ → (0, +∞] is lower semicontinuous: for every c ∈ ℝ the set {z ∈ X₀ : w(z) > c} is open in X₀. Moreover w(φ^t z) = e^t w(z) for all t ∈ ℝ and z ∈ X₀, and w^{-1}(+∞) = X₀^{per} = ⨆_p Γ^E_p.*

*Proof.* The last two assertions are §3.2 and §2.5. For c ≤ 0 the set is all of X₀. Fix c > 0 and put U_c := {w > c}. By definition of the quotient topology, U_c is open in X₀ iff Q^{-1}(U_c) = {(P₀,u) : w̃(P₀,u) > c} is open in X̌₀(ℂ)_E × ℝ^{>0}. The map π̌ × id : X̌(ℂ)_E × ℝ^{>0} → X̌₀(ℂ)_E × ℝ^{>0} is a continuous open surjection (§2.3; the product of an open map with the identity is open), hence a quotient map, so Q^{-1}(U_c) is open iff its preimage

  Ũ_c := {(Q̌, u) ∈ X̌(ℂ)_E × ℝ^{>0} : Q̌ over η and ρ̌(Q̌)·u > c} ∪ {(Q̌, u) : Q̌ over a closed point}

is open in X̌(ℂ)_E × ℝ^{>0}. The charts F_ν^{-1}X̊(ℂ)_E × ℝ^{>0} (ν ∈ N) are open and cover this space, so Ũ_c is open iff Ũ_c ∩ (F_ν^{-1}X̊(ℂ)_E × ℝ^{>0}) is open for each ν; transporting by the homeomorphism F_ν × id onto X̊(ℂ)_E × ℝ^{>0}, and using ρ̌(F_ν^{-1}P) = ρ(P)/ν and the fact that F_ν does not move the point x, this is the set

  A_ν := {(P, u) ∈ X̊(ℂ)_E × ℝ^{>0} : P over η and ρ(P)·u > cν} ∪ {(P, u) : P over a closed point}.

Its complement in X̊(ℂ)_E × ℝ^{>0} is B_ν := {(P,u) : P over η and ρ(P)u ≤ cν}. We show B_ν is closed in X̊(ℂ)_E × ℝ^{>0}. Let (P_α, u_α) be a net in B_ν converging to (P, u) ∈ X̊(ℂ)_E × ℝ^{>0} (the subspace topology of X̊(ℂ) × ℝ^{>0}, so the net converges there as well and Lemma 2 applies). If P lies over η, Lemma 2(a) gives ρ(P)u ≤ lim inf ρ(P_α)u_α ≤ cν, so (P,u) ∈ B_ν. If P lies over a closed point, Lemma 2(b) gives ρ(P_α)u_α → ∞, contradicting ρ(P_α)u_α ≤ cν. Hence B_ν is closed, A_ν is open, Ũ_c is open, Q^{-1}(U_c) is open, and U_c is open in X₀. ∎

**Remark 3.5 (what the proof used, and what it did not).** Used: the pointwise-convergence topology on X̊(ℂ) (p. 40); the colimit criterion and the openness of the charts (pp. 43, 47); the openness of π̌ (p. 43) — only to know that π̌ × id is a quotient map; the definition of the quotient topology on X₀. Not used: Hausdorffness of anything, metrizability, first countability, the openness of Q, Deninger's continuity statement for ρ on p. 49, and no property of E beyond (Tors). If X₀ were given any topology coarser than the quotient topology, "U_c open in X₀" would be a stronger demand and the theorem might fail; if X₀ is given the quotient topology — the only reading consistent with §10 p. 63 — the theorem holds as stated. (Conversely Theorems 2 and 3 below use only that the sets {w > c} are open and that the sets V_p of Lemma 3 are open, both of which are *inherited* by any finer topology; and quasi-compactness is a demand that gets *harder* to meet in a finer topology. So for the negative conclusions — no quasi-compact invariant set, no map from a compact space — the quotient topology is the *weakest* case, and the conclusions persist for every finer topology on X₀.)

**Remark 3.6 (the adelic reading of w — consistency check, not used).** On the generic locus, w is the inverse idelic norm transported by Deninger's Bost–Connes map. Indeed r(P) = P|_{μ(ℚ̄)} ∈ Ẑ via (56) (p. 47–48) has, for P over η in the E-locus, the form r(P) = ρ(P)·ε with ε ∈ Ẑ^× ((63): H̊_fin = N Ẑ^×, since ker(ζ ↦ ι(ζ)^a) has order ∏_ℓ ℓ^{v_ℓ(a)}); so |r(P)|_{A_f} = ∏_ℓ |ρ(P)|_ℓ = 1/ρ(P) and w([P,u]) = u/|r(P)|_{A_f}, the inverse of the adelic norm of the image of (P,u) under (68). The product formula |q|_{A_f}·q = 1 for q ∈ ℚ^{>0} is the ℚ^{>0}-invariance of w. On the packets the image under (68) lies in A^{>0}_{(p)} = ℚ^{>0}(Ẑ×_{(p)} × 0) × ℝ^{>0}, adeles with **p-component 0**, whose finite norm is 0 — which is w = ∞. Lower semicontinuity of w is the well-known upper semicontinuity of |·|_{A_f} in the adele topology ("The topology induced on Q^{>0}Ẑ^× = A_f^× by A_f is not the idèle topology, as is well known", p. 48). This note derives everything directly from the pointwise topology and does not rely on the continuity of (66)/(68); the reading is recorded because it explains *why* the escape function exists: **w is the norm character of the idele class group, and the packets are the norm-zero locus.**

## 4. Quasi-compact invariant sets: Theorems 2 and 3

**4.1 Theorem 2.** *Let K ⊆ X₀ be quasi-compact in the subspace topology (every open cover of K has a finite subcover; no separation axiom is assumed) and flow-invariant in the weak sense φ^{-t}(K) ⊆ K for all t > 0. Then K ⊆ X₀^{per} = ⨆_p Γ^E_p, i.e. K contains no point of the generic locus.*

*Proof.* Let m := inf_K w ∈ (0, +∞]. **Step 1: the infimum is attained.** If m = +∞ then w ≡ ∞ on K and we are done. Suppose m < ∞ and, for contradiction, that w(z) > m for all z ∈ K. For each z ∈ K choose k(z) ∈ N with w(z) > m + 1/k(z); the sets U_k := K ∩ {w > m + 1/k} (k ∈ N) are open in K by Theorem 1, increase with k, and cover K. By quasi-compactness finitely many cover K, hence K = U_{k₀} for some k₀, i.e. w > m + 1/k₀ on K, contradicting inf_K w = m. So there is z₀ ∈ K with w(z₀) = m. **Step 2: the flow pushes below the infimum.** If m < ∞, then φ^{-1}(z₀) ∈ K by invariance and w(φ^{-1}z₀) = e^{-1}m < m = inf_K w, a contradiction. Hence m = +∞, i.e. w ≡ +∞ on K, i.e. K ⊆ w^{-1}(∞) = X₀^{per}. ∎

**4.2 Lemma 3 (each packet is open and closed in the periodic locus).** *For every prime p there is an open set V_p ⊆ X₀ with Γ^E_p ⊆ V_p and V_p ∩ Γ^E_q = ∅ for every prime q ≠ p. Consequently Γ^E_p = V_p ∩ X₀^{per} is open in X₀^{per}, and it is closed in X₀^{per} (indeed in X₀) as the fiber Π^{-1}((p)) of §2.5.*

*(This is Lemma 3.3 of the sibling `qa-build.md`; the proof is re-derived here from the anchors since Theorem 3 rests on it.)*

*Proof.* Let D := {P ∈ X̊(ℂ) : |P(p)| < 1/2}, where P(p) is the value of the multiplicative map at the element p ∈ ℤ ⊂ ℤ̄. D is open in X̊(ℂ) (pointwise topology). D is open in X̌(ℂ): by (53) (p. 43) it suffices that F_ν(D) ∩ X̊(ℂ) = F_ν(D) be open in X̊(ℂ) for every ν ∈ N; since F_ν(P)(p) = P(p^ν) = P(p)^ν and F_ν is injective, F_ν(D) = F_ν(X̊(ℂ)) ∩ {R ∈ X̊(ℂ) : |R(p)| < 2^{-ν}}, which is open because F_ν(X̊(ℂ)) is open (Lemma 7.3, p. 42). Let S := ⋃_{r∈ℚ^{>0}} F_r(D), open (each F_r is a homeomorphism of X̌(ℂ), Prop. 7.4 b) and ℚ^{>0}-invariant, and put S_E := S ∩ X̌(ℂ)_E (open in X̌(ℂ)_E, ℚ^{>0}-invariant, G-invariant since G fixes p ∈ ℚ and commutes with the F_r). Define V_p := Q(π̌(S_E) × ℝ^{>0}); this is open in X₀ because π̌ is open (§2.3) and Q is open (§2.4).

*Γ^E_p ⊆ V_p.* A point of Γ^E_p is [P₀, u] with P₀ ∈ C^E_{(p)}, so P₀ = π̌(Q̌) with Q̌ ∈ X̌(ℂ)_E over some x over p; write Q̌ = F_ν^{-1}(P) with P ∈ X̊(ℂ)_E over x. Then P(p) = 0 since p ∈ p̄_x, so P ∈ D and Q̌ = F_{1/ν}(P) ∈ S_E; hence [P₀,u] ∈ V_p.

*V_p ∩ Γ^E_q = ∅ for q ≠ p.* Let z ∈ V_p ∩ Γ^E_q. By definition of V_p, z = [π̌(Q̌′), u′] for some Q̌′ ∈ S_E, i.e. Q̌′ = F_r(P″) with r ∈ ℚ^{>0} and P″ ∈ D ∩ X̊(ℂ) (any point of S lies in some F_r(D)). Since z ∈ Γ^E_q = Π^{-1}((q)), the point π̌(Q̌′) lies over (q), hence so does Q̌′ (pr_{X₀}∘π̌ = π∘pr_X), hence so does P″ (pr_X is ℚ^{>0}-invariant, p. 27): P″ ∈ X̊(ℂ) lies over a point y over q. Then p ∉ p̄_y (because p ≠ q and p̄_y ∩ ℤ = qℤ), so by Lemma 1(c) |P″(p)| = 1, contradicting P″ ∈ D. ∎

**4.3 Theorem 3 (finiteness).** *Let K ⊆ X₀ be quasi-compact and flow-invariant (φ^{-t}K ⊆ K for t > 0 suffices). Then there is a finite set S of primes with K ⊆ ⨆_{p∈S} Γ^E_p, and K = ⨆_{p∈S}(K ∩ Γ^E_p) is a partition of K into finitely many pairwise disjoint subsets, each open and closed in K, each flow-invariant (under all φ^t, since the packets are).*

*Proof.* By Theorem 2, K ⊆ X₀^{per} = ⨆_p Γ^E_p. By Lemma 3 each K ∩ Γ^E_p = K ∩ V_p is open in K, and they are pairwise disjoint (distinct packets are disjoint: distinct fibers of Π) and cover K. Quasi-compactness gives a finite subcover; disjointness then forces all the other pieces to be empty. Each piece is closed in K as the complement of the (finite) union of the others, and as K ∩ Π^{-1}((p)). Flow-invariance of the pieces: the packets are flow-invariant and K ∩ Γ^E_p ⊇ φ^{-t}(K ∩ Γ^E_p) for t > 0; for the full statement note that a point of K ∩ Γ^E_p lies on a circle of length log p contained in K by the weak invariance (φ^{-t} for t ranging over one period covers the circle). ∎

**Remark 4.4 (the two mechanisms, and what each needs).** Theorem 2 is *dissipation*: a flow-conformal weight of exponent one, lower semicontinuous, has no positive minimum on a backward-invariant quasi-compact set unless it is identically infinite. Theorem 3 adds *separation*: in the norm-zero locus the primes are topologically separated from one another (Lemma 3 — the evaluation at the integer p distinguishes "p-component zero" from "p-component a root of unity"). Neither mechanism sees the packet's internal structure: Theorem A, the profinite base B_p, non-Hausdorffness, indiscreteness — none of it enters. That is why the theorems are insensitive to the choice of E and to every hypothesis on Y.

## 5. Consequences

**Corollary 4 (Q-b: NO).** *Let Y be a quasi-compact topological space with an ℝ-action (φ_Y^t)_{t∈ℝ} — continuity of the action is not required — and let f : Y → X₀ be continuous with f∘φ_Y^t = φ^t∘f for all t. Then f(Y) ⊆ ⨆_{p∈S} Γ^E_p for a finite set S of primes, and Y = ⨆_{p∈S} f^{-1}(Γ^E_p) is a partition of Y into finitely many clopen invariant pieces. In particular, no such (Y, f) satisfies f(γ_p) ⊂ Γ_p for infinitely many primes p, for any family of subsets γ_p ⊆ Y; a fortiori no compact metrizable 3-dimensional lamination with one closed orbit γ_p of length log p per prime admits a continuous flow-equivariant map into X₀, or into Y₀^{Den}, with f(γ_p) ⊂ Γ_p for all p. This holds for every admissible E.*

*Proof.* K := f(Y) is quasi-compact (continuous image) and flow-invariant (f(φ_Y^t y) = φ^t f(y)). Theorem 3 gives S. The pieces f^{-1}(Γ^E_p) = f^{-1}(K ∩ Γ^E_p) are clopen in Y as preimages of clopen subsets of K, and invariant. For the unitary target, K is a quasi-compact invariant subset of Y₀^{Den} ⊆ X₀, hence of X₀ (§2.6). ∎

*The hypotheses of Q-b that the proof does not use:* Y metrizable; Y Hausdorff; dim Y = 3; Y a lamination or foliated space; the flow continuous on Y; "exactly one" closed orbit per prime; the lengths log p; the absence of other closed orbits. Only quasi-compactness of Y and equivariance of f are consumed. (Probe B §6.3 had already noted that for a **finite** set S of primes the disjoint union of circles Y_S maps in; Corollary 4 says that finite is all there is.)

**Corollary 5 (Q-a: NO, second route).** *Every quasi-compact flow-invariant subspace Y ⊆ X₀ lies in finitely many packets. Hence there is no compact flow-invariant subspace of X₀ — Hausdorff-in-itself or not, of any dimension — meeting infinitely many packets, let alone each packet in exactly one orbit.* (Theorem 3 with K = Y. The sibling `qa-build.md` kills Q-a by a different mechanism — packets are indiscrete, so no invariant subspace meeting a packet is T₀; the two proofs are independent and consistent.)

**Corollary 6 (compactness of Deninger's spaces, settled).** *Let E be admissible. A flow-invariant subset of X₀ that meets infinitely many packets, or that contains a point of the generic locus, is not quasi-compact. In particular:*
*(a) X₀ = X₀^E is not quasi-compact for every E with E ⊇ E_f (all packets are nonempty, Thm. 5.2 "If e.g. E ⊃ E_f then C^E_{x₀} = C_{x₀}" and the point π(x, χ_x) ∈ C_{x₀} of p. 32), and more generally for every E whose generic locus is nonempty or whose packets are nonempty for infinitely many p;*
*(b) the unitary system Y₀^{Den} = X̌₀(S¹)_E ×_{ℚ^{>0}} ℝ^{>0} is not quasi-compact for E ⊇ E_f (it contains every packet);*
*(c) every cut suspension X₀^{E(a)} and every enlarged cut X₀^{E(a)′} (§6.3) is not quasi-compact;*
*(d) any flow-invariant subset of X₀ containing, for infinitely many p, at least one point of Γ^E_p is not quasi-compact — in particular the closure of any family of periodic orbits over infinitely many primes.*
*Conversely, if the generic E-locus is empty and only finitely many packets are nonempty, then X₀^E is a finite union of packets, each of which is compact (the sibling's indiscreteness theorem, or for E ⊇ E_f the continuous-image argument of the referee block at probe B Cor. A.1), so X₀^E is quasi-compact. Thus: **X₀^E is quasi-compact if and only if X₀^E is a finite union of packets.***

*Proof.* The first sentence is Theorem 3 (finitely many packets) and Theorem 2 (no generic point); (a)–(d) are instances. The converse uses only that a finite union of compact sets is compact; the compactness of a packet is not needed anywhere else in this note and is cited, not re-proved. ∎

This settles the item the sibling `qa-build.md` §7.3 left open ("Compactness of X₀^{E(a)′}, of X₀ = X₀^{E_max}, and of Y₀^{Den} … is **left open** … [x-03] asserts none of them") and the ledger's row W1 ("Compactness of X₀: not established anywhere in the paper"): **X₀ is not compact, and neither is the closure of its periodic orbits.** Deninger's own compactness statements — "compact packets" (p. 2), "The compact subsets Γ_{x₀}" ([x-06] p. 12) — are the finite-S case of Theorem 3 and are consistent with it.

**Corollary 7 (compact sets are bounded away from the archimedean end).** *For every quasi-compact subset K ⊆ X₀ (invariant or not), inf_K w > 0; i.e. there is m > 0 with w ≥ m on K. Equivalently: no net in a fixed quasi-compact subset of X₀ has w → 0. For a continuous f : Y → X₀ with Y quasi-compact, w∘f is bounded below by a positive constant.* (Step 1 of the proof of Theorem 2 used only quasi-compactness and lower semicontinuity: the infimum is attained, and w > 0 everywhere.)

Since w([P,u]) = u on the classical orbit of [x-03] p. 39 (for the embedding i : ℚ̄ ↪ ℂ, ρ(i) = 1) and Deninger's fixed point [z, 0] of X̄₀ is the limit u → 0+ along that orbit (p. 40), Corollary 7 says that **the archimedean end of X₀ is at infinite distance from every compact subset**: the predicted fixed points of X̄₀ cannot be limits of anything that lives in a compact part of X₀.

**Proposition 8 (compactness is the exact obstruction: the non-compact witness).** *Assume the packets Γ^E_p are nonempty for all p (e.g. E ⊇ E_f). Let Y_∞ := ⨆_p ℝ^{>0}/p^ℤ (topological sum of circles, one of length log p per prime; a non-compact, locally compact, metrizable 1-manifold) with the flow y ↦ ye^t. Then there is a continuous flow-equivariant map f_∞ : Y_∞ → X₀ with f_∞(ℝ^{>0}/p^ℤ) ⊂ Γ^E_p for every p, indeed f_∞ maps the p-th circle bijectively onto one periodic orbit of Γ^E_p. Y_∞ has exactly one closed orbit of length log p per prime and no other orbits at all.*

*Proof.* For each p choose P₀^{(p)} ∈ C^E_{(p)}. The map ℝ^{>0} → X₀, u ↦ Q(P₀^{(p)}, u) = [P₀^{(p)}, u], is continuous (Q is continuous) and equivariant. Since the isotropy of P₀^{(p)} is p^ℤ (Thm. 5.2), F_p P₀^{(p)} = P₀^{(p)} and [P₀^{(p)}, pu] = [F_p P₀^{(p)}, u] = [P₀^{(p)}, u] (the relation (P₀,u)·p = (F_p P₀, p^{-1}u)); so the map factors through the quotient ℝ^{>0} → ℝ^{>0}/p^ℤ, and the induced map is continuous (quotient topology), equivariant, and bijective onto the orbit ℝ^{>0}/p^ℤ ≅ orbit through [P₀^{(p)},1] (Thm. 6.1: the isotropy is exactly p^ℤ). Take the sum over p. ∎

Proposition 8 and Corollary 4 together locate the obstruction exactly: *everything Q-b asks of Y except compactness is compatible with X₀* (one orbit per prime, lengths log p, no other orbits, continuity, equivariance, γ_p → Γ_p), and *compactness alone is incompatible* — with every Y, not merely with laminations. This also shows that no "p → ∞ accumulation" analysis is needed for the kill: in Y_∞ the orbits do not accumulate at all, and the moment one forces them to (by compactifying Y_∞ in any way whatsoever, equivariantly or not), the map f_∞ can no longer be continuous on the compactification (Cor. 4).

## 6. The three candidate constructions, each with the property that fails

### 6.1 Candidate (1): solenoidal suspensions

**(1a) The tower suspensions of [Den05] §7.7.** There X = M̄ ×_Λ ℝ with Λ = lℤ for a single real l > 0, and ([Den05] p. 35, verbatim) "l(γ) = |γ_M| l": every closed orbit has length in l·N.

**Proposition 6.1.** *No suspension of the form M̄ ×_{lℤ} ℝ has a closed orbit of length log p for every prime p (for any l > 0, any tower M̄, any f).* *Proof.* If log 2 = a·l and log 3 = b·l with a, b ∈ N then 2^b = e^{abl} = 3^a, contradicting unique factorization in ℤ. ∎

So candidate (1a) fails already at the **orbit-length spectrum**, before any map is considered. (This is not the ledger's rejected "rank obstruction for solenoids": the ledger correctly notes that solenoids over a rank-∞ group Λ, e.g. adelic ones, can carry closed orbits of all lengths log p; Prop. 6.1 concerns only the rank-1 suspensions of [Den05] §7.7, which are the ones the trace formula Thm. 7.8 is proved for.)

**(1b) General compact solenoidal suspensions.** Let K be a compact space with an action of a group Λ ⊆ ℝ^{>0} (e.g. Λ = ℚ^{>0}, or the group generated by a monoid of "Frobenii") and Y := (K × ℝ^{>0})/Λ with (k,u)·λ = (λk, λ^{-1}u), flow by multiplication on u. *Closed orbits.* The flow orbit through [k,u] closes at time log λ iff λ ∈ Λ_k (the stabilizer of k), so primitive closed orbits correspond to Λ-orbits in K of points with nontrivial stabilizer, and an orbit is a circle of length log λ₀ iff Λ_k = λ₀^ℤ is infinite cyclic (a stabilizer of rank ≥ 2 makes the orbit a non-Hausdorff quotient ℝ^{>0}/Λ_k with Λ_k dense — impossible in a metrizable Y). *Composite lengths.* A composite length log n with n = ab appears iff some stabilizer is n^ℤ; whether "composite-length orbits" exist is therefore a property of the stabilizer structure of the Λ-space K, not of solenoids as such — and Deninger's own X₀ already has **only prime stabilizers** (Thm. 5.2: the isotropy of every packet point is exactly p^ℤ; Thm. 6.1), so there is nothing to remove in X₀, and the task's worry that "solenoids typically have closed orbits for every element of the acting group" is not what happens in the arithmetic suspension. *Compactness.* If Λ is dense in ℝ^{>0} (e.g. Λ = ℚ^{>0}) then every class has a representative with u ∈ [1,2] and Y = Q(K × [1,2]) is quasi-compact; if Λ = λ₀^ℤ, Y = Q(K × [1, λ₀]) is quasi-compact. Either way:

**Verdict on (1b).** Whatever K and Λ are chosen, and whether or not the orbit spectrum is arranged to be exactly {log p} with multiplicity one (this note does not decide whether such a compact Λ-space K exists — the question is moot), **the property that fails is the map f**: by Corollary 4, a quasi-compact Y admits no continuous equivariant map into X₀ hitting infinitely many packets. This is a theorem about the target, uniform in the domain. There is no version of candidate (1) that survives.

### 6.2 Candidate (2): a map from the universal property of X₀

Points of X₀ are classes [(x, P^×)G, u] (§2), so any "recipe" f(y) = [(x(y), P_y^×), u(y)] assigns to y ∈ Y a point of Spec ℤ̄, a character of its residue field's unit group satisfying (Tors), and a phase. Two things are established about every such recipe on a compact Y:

*(i) It cannot exist with infinitely many packets in its image* (Corollary 4). No property of the recipe — how the character is built from "Frobenius-type data" of Y, whether it is unitary, whether it is injective on roots of unity — can change this, because the obstruction is a property of the target's topology and the domain's compactness, and equivariance is the only property of f that is used.

*(ii) Quantitatively, for any continuous f on compact Y (equivariant or not), the function y ↦ ρ(P_y)·u(y) is bounded below by a positive constant on f^{-1}(X₀^{gen})* (Corollary 7) — the "torsion-kernel order times phase" of the image of a compact set cannot approach zero. Equivariance then forces it to be infinite (Theorem 2), i.e. forces every image point to lie over a closed point: a Frobenius-type recipe from a compact source can only ever produce packet points, and then only over finitely many primes.

*What the sources offer.* [x-06] §4 (pp. 11–13) describes X₀'s points and states the problem ("too many periodic orbits … In the local p-adic situation below, we know the right modification to make. However in the global case presently we can only impose an 'admissible' condition E") without any map into X₀ from a smaller object. [D25] (pp. 1–3, read in full) argues why rational Witt vectors are *functions on* the spaces X_A (its (1)–(3)) and proposes enlarging W_rat by stronger descent "to remedy the defects in the constructions of [KS16] and [Den24]" — i.e. to change the space, not to map a compact object into it; its body is sheaf-theoretic foundations (the probe-9.4 note's scan, §1 there, is confirmed by this reading of the introduction). **Neither source contains a recipe for f, and by (i) none can.** Candidate (2) fails at f, as a theorem, with no sub-case surviving.

### 6.3 Candidate (3): the cut suspensions X₀^{E(a)} with f = inclusion

Fix, for each prime p, a base class a₀(p) ∈ Ẑ×_{(p)} and let E(a) be the smallest admissible class containing the injective characters χ_{x_p}^{a₀(p)} (empty in characteristic 0), and E(a)′ := E(a) ∪ {all (Tors)-characters in characteristic 0} (probe A Thm. C(b); sibling `qa-build.md` §4 with the reachability computation re-derived there and in the adjudication §4 item 5). By Thm. 6.1, X₀^{E(a)} has exactly one periodic orbit γ_p per prime, a circle of length log p, and no other periodic orbits; X₀^{E(a)′} has the same periodic orbits plus a generic locus. The inclusions X₀^{E(a)} ⊆ X₀^{E(a)′} ⊆ X₀^{E_max} are continuous, equivariant, and homeomorphisms onto their images with the subspace topology (saturated-subspace argument, §2.3–2.4; sibling Lemma 3.2). So f = inclusion is a legitimate continuous equivariant map with f(γ_p) ⊂ Γ_p for every p. **What fails is Y itself**, in every way Q-b requires:

**Proposition 6.3.** *(a) X₀^{E(a)} = ⨆_p γ_p, each γ_p is open and closed in X₀^{E(a)}, and X₀^{E(a)} is not quasi-compact. (b) X₀^{E(a)′} is not quasi-compact. (c) Neither is a lamination, a foliated space, a Moore–Schochet foliated space, or even a T₀ space; both have topological dimension 0 along the periodic locus.*

*Proof.* (a) Every point of X̌₀(ℂ)_{E(a)} lies over a closed point (E(a) has no characteristic-0 members), so X₀^{E(a)} = X₀^{per,E(a)} = ⨆_p γ_p; each γ_p = Γ^{E(a)}_p is clopen in this periodic locus by Lemma 3 (applied to the system X₀^{E(a)}, or to X₀^{E_max} and intersected); an infinite disjoint union of nonempty clopen sets is not quasi-compact. (This is the sibling's Prop. 7.1/Cor. 7.2 by the same argument; here it is a special case of Theorem 3 as well: an invariant set meeting all packets.) (b) Theorem 3: X₀^{E(a)′} is invariant and meets every packet. (c) The failure of T₀ is the sibling's Theorem 1 (every packet, hence every periodic orbit, is an indiscrete subspace of X₀ — verified in outline this session: its Lemma 5.2 chooses m_k ∈ N with m_k → c in Ẑ_{(p)} and m_k p^{-j_k} → t, which is the Chinese remainder theorem inside a real window of width M_k, and then (P^a, w)·(m_k p^{-j_k}) → (P^b, u) upstairs because F_{p^{-j}} fixes packet points; the writer of this note re-derived that argument before reading the sibling's text and records it as sibling-owned, referee-pending, and correct). An indiscrete space with two points is not T₀; a lamination, a foliated space in the sense of [Den05] §7.1 ("a separable metrizable topological space"), and a Moore–Schochet foliated space are all Hausdorff. Dimension 0 is the sibling's Cor. 7.4. ∎

**Does the cut help with [x-03] §10?** No. The §10 remark (p. 63) says the leaf partition "will not be locally trivial" unless the ℚ^{>0}-action is properly discontinuous; on the cut character space the action still has the isotropy p^ℤ at every packet point and non-closed orbits (the sibling's §6 explains the mechanism: the character coordinate sees ℚ^{>0} only modulo the isotropy, the ℝ-coordinate sees all of it), so it is not properly discontinuous, and the pathology is *per orbit*: cutting the packets down to one orbit leaves each surviving orbit indiscrete. The cut removes the uncountable multiplicity (probe A Thm. C(b), sibling Prop. 4.1) and nothing else.

**Verdict on (3).** With f = inclusion the map is fine and *Y fails*: not compact (Prop. 6.3(a)/(b), a theorem here), not Hausdorff (sibling Theorem 1), dimension 0 (sibling Cor. 7.4), not a lamination. And any attempt to repair Y by passing to a compact invariant subspace of X₀^{E(a)} or X₀^{E(a)′} lands in finitely many γ_p (Theorem 3). Candidate (3) reduces Q-b to Q-a exactly as the charter anticipated, and Q-a is NO twice over.

## 7. What the theorem means, and the one thing it leaves open

### 7.1 The mechanism in Deninger's own terms

The escape function restricted to the classical orbit is the coordinate u: for the field embedding i : ℚ̄ ↪ ℂ, ρ(i) = 1 and w([i,u]) = u. [x-03] p. 39–40 predicts that this orbit "tend[s] to the fixed point [z,0] … for u → 0+" in a compactification X̄₀ whose fixed points are X₀(C)/F_∞, and adds "it is unclear, why X̄₀ would not contain many more fixed points obtained in the same way from the points in X̌₀(C) ∖ X₀(C)". Theorem 1 extends u from the classical orbit to a lower semicontinuous function on all of X₀ that is conformal of exponent one under the flow and infinite exactly on the periodic locus; Theorem 2 says that along the backward flow **every generic point of X₀ heads to the archimedean end** (w → 0) and no compact invariant set can contain such a point; Theorem 3 says the periodic locus, where nothing heads anywhere, is a topological sum of its packets. The flow on X₀ is *dissipative* in the literal sense that it admits a strict Lyapunov-type function, and it has *no compact invariant set that sees more than finitely many primes*, because such a set would have to contain the archimedean end. That end is not in X₀ — this is row W5 of the ledger, "There are no fixed points of the flow" ([x-03] p. 2), made quantitative.

### 7.2 [Den05]'s "dream" and the compact global attractor

[Den05] p. 36: "To an algebraic scheme X/Z one should first attach an infinite dimensional dissipative dynamical system, possibly using GL_∞ in some way. The desired dynamical system should then be obtained by passing to the finite dimensional compact global attractor." X₀ is infinite-dimensional ([x-03] p. 49) and dissipative (Theorem 1). Theorem 3 says: **inside X₀, no compact invariant set — attractor or otherwise — carries periodic orbits over infinitely many primes.** So if this dream is to be realized on Deninger's X₀, the compact attractor must live in a space strictly larger than X₀ — one containing the archimedean end where w → 0 — exactly the compactification X̄₀ that [x-03] p. 39 predicts and does not construct. [Judgment-grade reading of a remark in a survey, flagged; nothing rests on it.]

### 7.3 The residual escape, stated as a question: Q-b′

**Q-b′.** *Is there a compact metrizable space Ȳ with a continuous flow whose fixed-point set Fix ≠ ∅ realizes the S3 archimedean data, such that Y := Ȳ ∖ Fix is a 3-dimensional lamination with exactly one closed orbit γ_p of length log p per prime and no other closed orbits, and a continuous flow-equivariant f : Y → X₀ with f(γ_p) ⊂ Γ_p for all p?*

This is Deninger's own picture (an ℝ^{>0}-immersion X₀(C) ×_{F_∞} ℝ ↪ X̄₀ with the fixed points added at u = 0, p. 40) turned into a question about maps into X₀ rather than into the nonexistent X̄₀. **This note does not decide Q-b′.** It does prove the shape any answer must have:

**Proposition 7.4 (constraints on Q-b′).** *Let Ȳ be a compact metrizable space with a continuous flow, Fix its (closed) fixed-point set, Y := Ȳ ∖ Fix, and f : Y → X₀ continuous and flow-equivariant. Then:*
*(i) for every y ∈ Y with f(y) ∈ X₀^{gen}, the α-limit set α(y) := ⋂_{T>0} cl{φ^{-t}y : t ≥ T} (nonempty, by compactness of Ȳ) is contained in Fix; in particular Fix ≠ ∅ as soon as f meets the generic locus;*
*(ii) every closed invariant subset A ⊆ Ȳ with A ∩ Fix = ∅ has f(A) contained in finitely many packets;*
*(iii) if f(γ_p) ⊂ Γ^E_p for closed orbits γ_p ⊆ Y and infinitely many primes p, then cl_Ȳ(⋃_p γ_p) ∩ Fix ≠ ∅: **the closed orbits accumulate at the archimedean fixed points**;*
*(iv) on Y^{per} := f^{-1}(X₀^{per}) the pieces Y_p := f^{-1}(Γ^E_p) form a partition into subsets that are open and closed in Y^{per} and flow-invariant.*

*Proof.* (i) Let y′ ∈ α(y) ∩ Y, so φ^{-t_k}y → y′ for some t_k → ∞ (Ȳ is metrizable). By continuity of f on Y, f(φ^{-t_k}y) → f(y′), and by lower semicontinuity of w at f(y′) (Theorem 1), lim inf_k w(f(φ^{-t_k}y)) ≥ w(f(y′)) > 0. But w(f(φ^{-t_k}y)) = w(φ^{-t_k}f(y)) = e^{-t_k}w(f(y)) → 0 since w(f(y)) < ∞. Contradiction; so α(y) ⊆ Fix. (ii) A is compact (closed in compact Ȳ), contained in Y, and invariant, so f(A) is a quasi-compact invariant subset of X₀; apply Theorem 3. (iii) A := cl_Ȳ(⋃_p γ_p) is closed and invariant (the closure of an invariant set under a continuous flow is invariant); if A ∩ Fix = ∅, then (ii) puts f(A) ⊇ ⋃_p f(γ_p) into finitely many packets, contradicting f(γ_p) ⊂ Γ^E_p for infinitely many p (packets are disjoint). (iv) Lemma 3 pulled back along f. ∎

Constraint (iii) is the honest replacement for the adjudication's "the orbits γ_p accumulate on a nonempty closed invariant set A of non-periodic points with f(A) in the unitary locus": in any compact substrate mapping to X₀, the accumulation set of the closed orbits must reach the archimedean fixed points; no such set can stay inside Y. Whether a 3-dimensional lamination with the S3 data and this accumulation pattern exists, and whether X₀'s own closure of periodic orbits (Y₀^{Den}, which contains no classical point: by Thm. 8.2 it is the unitary locus, and the embedding i is not unitary) can be reconciled with it, is the substrate problem; nothing in this note bears on it beyond Prop. 7.4.
