### 2.2 Line (2) — the pointwise-convergence claim F_{m_k}(P^a) → P^b in the colimit topology

For r ∈ 𝔭_x both values are 0. So (x, χ^{am_k}) → (x, χ^{ac}) in the pointwise topology (§1.3), coordinatewise eventually constant — no metric, no C = ℂ, no continuity of χ used. Push through π (continuous, p. 43): π(x, χ^{am_k}) → π(x, χ^{ac}) in X̊₀(C). ac = b a⁻¹ a = b, so the limit is P^b. All terms and the limit lie in X̊₀(C)_E (χ^{am_k} = (χ^a)∘( )^{m_k} ∈ E by Def. 4.1; χ^b ∈ E by hypothesis), and X̊₀(C)_E has the subspace topology (p. 47), so the convergence holds in X̊₀(C)_E; X̊₀(C)_E is open in X̌₀(C)_E with agreeing topology (§1.4, §1.5), so it holds in X̌₀(C)_E.

No separating open set exists. (Note p. 47 Remark 2's disconnectedness of the model generic fibre lives in the *generic* stratum's model, not here.) **Outcome: STANDS; no gap.**

### 2.3 Line (3) — the isotropy step F_r(P^a) = F_m(P^a) for r = m p^{−j}, on the nose

*Thm 5.2's hypotheses (p. 34):* E admissible with E ⊂ E_max; X₀ integral normal; C satisfying the conditions before Cor. 4.4 (C^× ≠ μ(C), card K₀ ≤ card C, char) — all met for Spec Z and C = ℂ; the conclusion applies to every P₀ ∈ C^E_{x₀}, and P^a = π(x, χ^a) ∈ pr₀⁻¹(x₀) ∩ X̌₀(C)_E ⊂ C^E_{x₀}. For Spec Z, E ⊂ E_max is automatic (§1.2). But the step does not need Thm 5.2 at all: §1.6 re-derives F_p(P^a) = P^a in X̊₀(C) from the Frobenius element σ ∈ G_x ((23), p. 22) and the G-action formula (p. 22), and the colimit bookkeeping (§1.4) gives F_{p^{−j}}(P^a) = P^a in X̌₀(C) for every j ∈ Z. Then F_r := F_m∘F_{p^{−j}} (the F_q commute, Prop. 7.4b extends the monoid action to Q>0), so F_r(P^a) = F_m(P^a) = π(x, χ^{am}) exactly, in the first chart. The direction is correct: r = m p^{−j}, F_r = F_m ∘ (F_{p^j})⁻¹. **Outcome: STANDS, on the nose, with a proof independent of Thm 5.2.**

### 2.4 Line (4) — simultaneous approximation m_k → c in Ẑ_(p) and m_k p^{−j_k} → t in R

*Re-derivation (adjudication Step 1 / qa-build Lemma 5.2).* M_k := ∏_{ℓ ≤ k, ℓ ≠ p} ℓ^k; c_k ∈ Z with c_k ≡ c (mod M_k) (Ẑ_(p) = lim Z/M over M prime to p, so the residue of c mod M_k is defined; CRT is not even needed — it is the projection); j_k with t p^{j_k} > M_k and M_k p^{−j_k} < 1/k; m_k := the unique integer in the half-open interval [t p^{j_k}, t p^{j_k} + M_k) with m_k ≡ c_k (mod M_k) (an interval of length M_k contains exactly M_k consecutive integers, one per residue class). Then m_k > M_k > 0; for any M prime to p, M | M_k once every prime ℓ | M has ℓ ≤ k and v_ℓ(M) ≤ k, so m_k ≡ c (mod M) eventually, i.e. m_k → c in Ẑ_(p); and |m_k p^{−j_k} − t| < M_k p^{−j_k} < 1/k. This is strong approximation for Q with the single place p removed (Z[1/p] is dense in ∏_{ℓ≠p} Z_ℓ × R), made explicit; Deninger's own proof of Thm 9.6 (p. 62) uses the same CRT step with the infinite place *excluded* — the packets restore it because p is a unit in Ẑ_(p) and the p-part of r is invisible to the character coordinate (§2.3).

### 2.5 Line (5) — the quotient step and the action conventions

Convention (p. 38): (P₀, u)q = (F_q(P₀), q⁻¹u); orbit [P₀, u]. So (P^a, w)·r_k = (F_{r_k}P^a, r_k⁻¹w) = (F_{m_k}P^a, w p^{j_k}/m_k) by §2.3, and [F_q P, s] = [P, qs] (check: (P, qs)·q = (F_qP, q⁻¹qs) = (F_qP, s)). Every (P^a, w)·r_k lies in the Q>0-orbit of (P^a, w), so q((P^a,w)·r_k) = z for all k. Convergence upstairs: first coordinate → P^b (§2.2), second → w/t = w·u/w = u; so (P^a, w)·r_k → (P^b, u) in the product topology. Let U ∋ z′ = q(P^b, u) be open in X₀. By definition of the quotient topology q⁻¹(U) is open in X̌₀(C)_E × R>0 and contains (P^b, u), hence contains (P^a, w)·r_k for all large k, hence z = q((P^a,w)·r_k) ∈ U. So z ∈ every neighborhood of z′: z′ ∈ cl{z}. Symmetrically (c′ = ab⁻¹, t′ = u/w) z ∈ cl{z′}. Two points with the same neighborhood filter; an open set meeting Γ^E_p contains it; the packet, and (b = a) each orbit, is indiscrete. Equivalent formulation, checked: in a quotient by a group action, [y] ∈ cl{[x]} iff y ∈ cl(orbit of x) upstairs — what was shown is exactly (P^b, u) ∈ cl_{X̌₀(C)_E × R>0}(orbit of (P^a, w)).

*Direction sanity check I ran because it once looked wrong:* one might try to place (P^a, w) inside a translate (W × I)·r⁻¹ of a box around (P^b, u), which requires F_{1/r}P^a = F_{p^j/m}P^a = F_m⁻¹(P^a) ∈ W — a point of the m-th chart, not the first, and P^a has an m-th root only for m ∈ p^N by (51). That is not what the argument does; it uses (P^a, w)·r ∈ W × I, i.e. F_r P^a = F_m P^a in the first chart. Both formulations are the same statement (saturation of q⁻¹(U)); the first-chart one is the one that is checked. **Outcome: STANDS.**

*The alternative N-description (Remark p. 39), checked for consistency:* X₀ ≅ (X̊₀(C)_E × R>0)/∼ with (P₀,u)·ν = (F_νP₀, ν⁻¹u), y ~ y′ iff y·ν = y′·ν′. For j ≥ 0: (P^a, w)·(m ν″) = (F_{mν″}P^a, w/(mν″)) and (F_mP^a, wp^j/m)·(p^jν″) = (F_{mν″}F_{p^j}P^a, w/(mν″)) = (F_{mν″}P^a, w/(mν″)) — equal, so (P^a, w) ~ (F_mP^a, wp^j/m), as required; and X̊₀(C)_E × R>0 → X₀ is the restriction of the open quotient q (openness: Q>0 acts by homeomorphisms, Prop. 7.4b + p. 47) to an open saturating subset, hence itself a quotient map, so the topology is the same. No escape there.

### 2.7 The simplest analog computed by hand

*One-prime, one-ℓ toy.* Let ℓ ≠ p be a prime and M := Z_ℓ with the Q>0-action in which q = m p^{−j} (m prime to p) acts by multiplication by the integer m and p acts trivially — the packet's character coordinate with all but one ℓ dropped and the isotropy built in. X_toy := M ×_{Q>0} R>0, quotient topology. The orbit of (1, w) is {(m, w p^j/m) : m ∈ N prime to p, j ∈ Z}. Given (c, u) ∈ Z_ℓ × R>0: choose m_k ≡ c (mod ℓ^k) with m_k ≈ w p^{j_k}/u (interval of length ℓ^k placed at w p^{j_k}/u, j_k large); then (m_k, w p^{j_k}/m_k) → (c, u). So (c, u) ∈ cl(orbit of (1, w)) and [c, u] ∈ cl{[1, w]} for all c, u: X_toy is indiscrete on the image of the unit orbit — the phenomenon in its smallest form. *Adelic contrast.* Replace M by Ẑ ⊗ Q (all components, p included) with Q>0 acting by multiplication and no isotropy — Deninger's Prop. 10.3 model. The orbit of (1, w) is {(r, w/r) : r ∈ Q>0} ⊂ A_f × R = A; Q is discrete and closed in A, so the orbit is closed and the quotient is T₁ (p. 64 Remark). The only difference is whether the p-component is retained: retained ⟹ closed orbits, T₁; killed by the isotropy p^Z ⟹ indiscrete. The theorem is this dichotomy applied to [x-03]'s actual character space, where §2.2 supplies the identification of the packet's first chart with (Ẑ_(p) ∖ 0)/p^Ẑ ⊃ B_p in the pointwise topology.

## 3. The strongest attack, and why it fails

**The attack.** "The theorem is an artifact of the wrong topology. Deninger's packets are compact packets fibred over the compact group B_p with periodic-orbit fibres (pp. 2–3; [x-06] Thm 4.2), reminiscent of invariant tori; in the topology that realizes that description — the left-hand side of Thm 7.10 — each packet is the compact Hausdorff space B_p × (R>0/p^Z) (§2.1), on which the whole indiscreteness claim is false. The program's 'quotient topology' was flagged by its own adjudicator as a judgment-grade reading; if the reading is wrong, face (a) collapses, and with it the kill."

**Why it fails.** (i) The topology is not a reading. [x-03] p. 59 defines it: "We give … X₀ = X̌₀(C)_E ×_{Q>0₀} R>0 the quotient topologies." That is the only topology [x-03] ever puts on X₀, and §8 (closure of the periodic orbits), §9 (connectedness), §10 (leafwise cohomology) are all proved for it. (ii) Deninger himself says the model bijection is not a homeomorphism (Thm 7.10 Remark 2, p. 47; [x-06] Thm 4.4) and that the non-proper-discontinuity of the Q>0-action "works to our advantage" (p. 49), i.e. the collapse of the model's separation is intended, not accidental. (iii) The technical text states the packet fibration only as a bijection (p. 38), before any topology exists; the "compact packets / fibred over" phrases are in the introduction and the survey. (iv) Q-a as posed asks about a *subspace* of X₀ "Hausdorff in its subspace topology", and a subspace topology is induced by the ambient one, which is p. 59's. So the attack, if it succeeds at all, succeeds only by changing the target from X₀ to the model space of Thm 7.10 — a different topological space on the same set, in which the packets are compact Hausdorff, the generic fibre has uncountably many components (p. 47), and X₀'s connectedness (Cor. 9.7), H⁰_F = R (Thm 10.2), and the closure statement of §8 all fail to apply. Within the program's own terms that is the "change of target" escape, the same shape as the X̄₀ residue, and not a refutation of face (a).

**What the attack does establish, and the program should bank it:** the face-(a) theorem is exactly the statement that on each packet the Thm-7.10 bijection goes from a compact Hausdorff solenoidal torus B_p × S¹ onto an indiscrete set. Read together with Deninger's own pp. 2–3 language, this says that whatever "compact packets reminiscent of invariant tori" is meant to describe, it is the model topology and not the subspace topology of X₀. The subspace face of S4 was therefore never a question about the geometry Deninger has in mind; it was a question about a topology in which packets carry no geometry at all. (That is a remark about how Q-a was posed, not a defect of the theorem.)

**Second-strongest attack (fails outright).** "The colimit topology on X̌₀(C) might separate P^{am_k} from P^b, e.g. through the root-of-unity conditions (51) that make the charts clopen." Fails because all the points involved are in the first chart, which is open in the colimit with its original pointwise topology (§1.4, re-derived from the definition on p. 43 and Lemma 7.3), and the pointwise convergence is coordinatewise eventually exact (§2.2). The clopen conditions (51) distinguish charts from each other, never points within the first chart.

**Third attack (fails outright).** "F_{p^{−j}}P^a = P^a might fail in the colimit even though F_pP^a = P^a in X̊₀(C), if the colimit identified fewer elements than the naive bookkeeping." Fails: the colimit is along the *injective* maps F_ν (Cor. 3.2, p. 23; Prop. 4.2), and by definition F_p⁻¹(P) = F_1⁻¹(P) iff F_p(P) = P (§1.4); and F_p(P^a) = P^a is forced by the existence of a Frobenius element in G_x ((23), p. 22).

## 4. The theorem's honest scope

* **Which space.** X₀ = X(Spec Z), i.e. the suspension X₀ = X̌₀(C)_E ×_{Q>0} R>0 of p. 38 with the **quotient topology of p. 59**. Not the model space of Thm 7.10 (where the statement is false). Not the conjectural compactification X̄₀ (pp. 39–40; not constructed). The same proof applies verbatim to the unitary subsystem Y₀ = X̌₀(S¹) ×_{Q>0} R>0 (every point and approximant used has root-of-unity values) and to every cut suspension X₀^{E′}, E′ ⊆ E admissible (each is itself a p. 59 suspension; whether it is also a subspace of X₀^E is qa-build's Lemma 3.2, which is not needed for the indiscreteness of X₀^{E′} in its own topology).
* **Which E.** Every admissible class (Def. 4.1). Over Spec Z, E ⊂ E_max automatically (§1.2), and the argument uses admissibility only through the ν-biconditional (to keep χ^{am_k} in E) and (Tors) (vacuous for the points used: kernels are μ_{m′}, finite). In particular E_f, E_max = E_tors, and the one-orbit cut classes.
* **Which C.** §9 assumes C = ℂ (p. 53); the argument uses only that C carries a topology in which a net that is eventually constant at every coordinate converges — any valued algebraically closed field satisfying the conditions before Cor. 4.4 (p. 28) will do. Metrizability (p. 40) is never used.
* **Which points.** Any two points of one packet; a fortiori any two points of one periodic orbit (b = a). The generic (characteristic-zero) stratum is *not* covered: Deninger's Prop. 10.3 model of it is T₁ (p. 64), and qa-kill's Prop. 4.6 (unitary orbits non-Hausdorff) is a different, weaker statement that I did not re-derive and that face (a) does not need.
* **Compact vs. quasi-compact vs. non-compact.** A packet is a closed (q⁻¹(Γ^E_p) closed and saturated) indiscrete subset of X₀; as a subspace it is quasi-compact (every open cover of an indiscrete space has a one-element subcover) and, if one uses "compact" without Hausdorff, compact — but it is not compact Hausdorff, not T₀, and has covering dimension 0. "Compact" in [x-03] p. 2 and [x-06] Thm 4.2 is therefore true-but-vacuous in the subspace topology and substantive only in the model topology, where Γ_p ≅ B_p × S¹ is compact Hausdorff (§2.1). The closure of a single packet point is the whole packet.
* **What follows for Q-a.** Any subspace Y ⊆ X₀ containing two points of one packet inherits an indiscrete two-point subspace and is not T₀; a compact 3-dimensional lamination is metrizable, hence Hausdorff, hence T₀; so no such Y meets a packet in an orbit. Q-a is NO for every admissible E — with no use of compactness, dimension, flow-invariance, or one-orbit-per-packet. This is the adjudication's corollary and it is correct.
* **What does not follow.** Nothing about maps *into* X₀ (face (b)); nothing about the model topology; nothing about X̄₀; nothing about general arithmetic schemes (the extension sketched in the probes needs m_k ∈ N₀ in a prescribed residue class and real window — plausible by the prime-number theorem in progressions, not verified here); nothing about the generic stratum's separation axioms.
