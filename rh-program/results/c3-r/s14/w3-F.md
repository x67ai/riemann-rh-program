## 2. The objects, written out

### 2.1 The acyclicity criterion, exactly as available on disk

Verbatim from [ÁLKL23] §2.1, p. 4 (and identically [ALKL] §2.1.1, p. 9, with "[39]" = "[Wen03]"):

> "For instance, for an inductive spectrum of LCSs of the form (X_k) = (X₀ ⊂ X₁ ⊂ ···), the condition of being acyclic can be described as follows [39, Theorem 6.1]: for all k, there is some k′ ≥ k such that, for all k″ ≥ k′, the topologies of X_{k′} and X_{k″} coincide on some 0-neighborhood of X_k. In this case, X := ⋃_k X_k is Hausdorff if and only if all X_k are Hausdorff [39, Proposition 6.3]. It is said that (X_k) is regular if any bounded B ⊂ X is contained and bounded in some step X_k. If moreover the topologies of X and X_k coincide on B, then (X_k) is said to be boundedly retractive. The conditions of being compactly retractive or sequentially retractive are similarly defined, using compact sets or convergent sequences.
> If the steps X_k are Fréchet spaces, the above properties of (X_k) depend only on the LF-space X [39, Chapter 6, p. 111]; thus it may be said that they are properties of X. In this case, X is acyclic if and only if it is boundedly/compactly/sequentially retractive [39, Proposition 6.4]. As a consequence, acyclic LF-spaces are complete and regular [39, Corollary 6.5]."

and, same page: "The above concepts and properties also apply to an inductive/projective spectrum consisting of continuous inclusions X_r ⊂ X_{r′} for r < r′ in ℝ because ⋃_r X_r = ⋃_k X_{r_k} and ⋂_r X_r = ⋂_k X_{s_k} for sequences r_k ↓ −∞ and s_k ↑ ∞."

**Terminology used below.** For a spectrum (X_k) of LCSs and indices k ≤ k′ ≤ k″ we say the triple satisfies **(M*)** if there is an absolutely convex 0-neighborhood V of X_k such that for every 0-neighborhood O of X_{k′} there is a 0-neighborhood W of X_{k″} with V ∩ W ⊂ O. We say (X_k) satisfies **(M)** if for all k there is k′ ≥ k such that for all k″ ≥ k′ the topologies of X_{k′} and X_{k″} coincide on some 0-neighborhood of X_k — the quoted criterion. (Below, "acyclic", "regular", "boundedly retractive" are used only in the quoted senses, and every use of [Wen03] is one of the three quoted statements.)

**Lemma 2.1 ((M*) ⟹ coincidence on V).** If (X_k ⊂ X_{k′} ⊂ X_{k″}) satisfies (M*) with the 0-neighborhood V, then the topologies of X_{k′} and X_{k″} coincide on V.
*Proof.* The topology of X_{k′} is finer than that of X_{k″} on X_{k′} (the inclusion is continuous), so it suffices to show every τ_{k′}-neighborhood of a point a ∈ V in V contains a τ_{k″}-neighborhood of a in V. First, (M*) for V implies (M*) for 2V: given O, apply (M*) to O/2 to get W with V ∩ W ⊂ O/2; then 2V ∩ 2W ⊂ O/2 + O/2 = O (O absolutely convex without loss of generality) and 2W is a 0-neighborhood of X_{k″}. Now let O be a 0-neighborhood of X_{k′}, W as in (M*) for 2V. If b ∈ (a + W) ∩ V then b − a ∈ W ∩ (V − V) ⊂ W ∩ 2V ⊂ O (V − V ⊂ 2V by convexity), so b ∈ a + O. Hence (a + W) ∩ V ⊂ (a + O) ∩ V. ∎

**Lemma 2.2 ((M*) passes to finite products, subspaces and compatible embeddings).** (a) If (X^{(i)}_k)_k, i = 1, …, r, each satisfy (M*) at (k, k′, k″) with neighborhoods V_i, then (∏_i X^{(i)}_k)_k satisfies (M*) at (k, k′, k″) with V = ∏V_i. (b) If Y_k ⊂ X_k are linear subspaces with the induced topologies, (M*) for (X_k) at (k, k′, k″) implies (M*) for (Y_k) with V ∩ Y_k. (c) More generally, if j: Y → X is a linear map with j(Y_k) ⊂ X_k such that each Y_k carries the initial topology of j: Y_k → X_k, then (M*) for (X_k) with V implies (M*) for (Y_k) with j^{−1}(V) ∩ Y_k.
*Proof.* (a) A 0-neighborhood of ∏X^{(i)}_{k′} contains a product ∏O_i of 0-neighborhoods; take W = ∏W_i. (b) is the case j = inclusion of (c). (c) Given a 0-neighborhood O of Y_{k′}, by the initial topology O ⊃ j^{−1}(O′) ∩ Y_{k′} for a 0-neighborhood O′ of X_{k′}; take W′ for (V, O′) and W := j^{−1}(W′) ∩ Y_{k″}, a 0-neighborhood of Y_{k″}; then j^{−1}(V) ∩ W ⊂ j^{−1}(V ∩ W′) ⊂ j^{−1}(O′), and this set lies in Y_{k′} since it lies in Y_{k″} ∩ Y — hmm, to be precise: an element y ∈ j^{−1}(V) ∩ Y_k ∩ W lies in Y_k ⊂ Y_{k′}, so y ∈ j^{−1}(O′) ∩ Y_{k′} ⊂ O. ∎

(In (c) the point is only that the neighborhood V is taken in the lowest step Y_k, where elements are automatically in Y_{k′}.)

### 2.2 Symbols (from [ÁLKL23] §3, pp. 13–14, read verbatim)

For U ⊂ ℝⁿ open, l ∈ ℕ and m ∈ ℝ, S^m(U × ℝ^l) is the set of a ∈ C^∞(U × ℝ^l) with, for every compact K ⊂ U and all α ∈ ℕ₀ⁿ, β ∈ ℕ₀^l,

(3.1)  ‖a‖_{K,α,β,m} := sup_{x∈K, ξ∈ℝ^l} |∂_x^α ∂_ξ^β a(x, ξ)| / (1 + |ξ|)^{m−|β|} < ∞,

(3.4)  ‖a‖_{Q,C^k} := sup_{(x,ξ)∈Q, |α|+|β|≤k} |∂_x^α ∂_ξ^β a(x, ξ)|,

and

(3.5)  ‖a‖′_{K,α,β,m} := sup_{x∈K} limsup_{|ξ|→∞} |∂_x^α ∂_ξ^β a(x, ξ)| / |ξ|^{m−|β|}.

### 2.5 The transplanted (parametrized) spaces

Let T be a compact Hausdorff space (in the S2 setting: compact and totally disconnected — the transversal factor of the preserved solenoidal leaf X⁰; compact because X is compact and X⁰ closed, per the S2 note's (A-i)/(A-ii)).

**Lemma 2.3 (C(T; F) is Fréchet).** Let F be a Fréchet space whose topology is generated by an increasing sequence of seminorms p₁ ≤ p₂ ≤ ···. Then C(T; F), the space of continuous maps a: T → F, with the seminorms P_k(a) := sup_{t∈T} p_k(a(t)), is a Fréchet space. If the topology of F is the initial topology of a countable family of continuous linear maps Π_i: F → E_i into Fréchet spaces E_i, then a map a: T → F is continuous iff every Π_i ∘ a: T → E_i is continuous, and the topology of C(T; F) is the initial topology of the maps C(T; F) → C(T; E_i), a ↦ Π_i ∘ a.
*Proof.* P_k(a) < ∞: t ↦ p_k(a(t)) is continuous on the compact T. Countably many seminorms, Hausdorff (P_k(a) = 0 ∀k ⟹ a(t) = 0 ∀t). Completeness: if (a_j) is Cauchy for every P_k then (a_j(t)) is Cauchy in F for every t, so a_j(t) → a(t) for some a(t) ∈ F; for fixed j and t, p_k(a_j(t) − a(t)) = lim_i p_k(a_j(t) − a_i(t)) ≤ sup_{i≥j} P_k(a_j − a_i) → 0 as j → ∞, uniformly in t; so P_k(a_j − a) → 0 once we know a ∈ C(T; F): for t_ν → t, p_k(a(t_ν) − a(t)) ≤ 2P_k(a − a_j) + p_k(a_j(t_ν) − a_j(t)), and both terms are small (first choose j, then ν), for every k, which is continuity of a since F's topology is generated by the p_k. The second statement: a is continuous iff Π_i ∘ a is continuous for all i (definition of the initial topology, applied to the map a), and the seminorms of C(T; F) can be taken to be sup_t q_{i,r}(Π_i a(t)) with q_{i,r} the seminorms of E_i, which are exactly the seminorms pulled back from C(T; E_i). ∎

**Definition 2.4 (parametrized model spectra).** For T compact:
- S^m_{K,T} := C(T; S^m_K), with seminorms ‖a‖^T_{α,β,m} := sup_{t∈T} ‖a(t)‖_{α,β,m}. The steps are Fréchet (Lemma 2.3), the inclusions S^m_{K,T} ⊂ S^{m′}_{K,T} (m < m′) are continuous, and S^∞_{K,T} := ⋃_m S^m_{K,T} is an LF-space. (Equivalently: functions a(t, y, ξ), smooth in (y, ξ), y-supported in K, such that t ↦ a(t, ·, ·) is continuous into every step-seminorm; note that this is *stronger* than joint continuity of all (y, ξ)-derivatives on T × ℝ^d × ℝ^l, because the seminorms are global in ξ — the family a(t) = φ(ξ − k(t)) with k(t) → ∞ as t → t₀ has jointly continuous derivatives but is not continuous into S^m; the S2 note's design (H^s_τ = C(T, H^s), "estimates sup over t", §3.3) selects the C(T; ·) version, which is the one used here.)
