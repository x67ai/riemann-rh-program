## 2. The objects, written out

### 2.1 The acyclicity criterion, exactly as available on disk

Verbatim from [ÁLKL23] §2.1, p. 4 (identically in substance at [ALKL] §2.1.1, p. 9, with "[39]" = "[Wen03]"):

> "For instance, for an inductive spectrum of LCSs of the form (X_k) = (X₀ ⊂ X₁ ⊂ ···), the condition of being acyclic can be described as follows [39, Theorem 6.1]: for all k, there is some k′ ≥ k such that, for all k″ ≥ k′, the topologies of X_{k′} and X_{k″} coincide on some 0-neighborhood of X_k. In this case, X := ⋃_k X_k is Hausdorff if and only if all X_k are Hausdorff [39, Proposition 6.3]. It is said that (X_k) is regular if any bounded B ⊂ X is contained and bounded in some step X_k. If moreover the topologies of X and X_k coincide on B, then (X_k) is said to be boundedly retractive. The conditions of being compactly retractive or sequentially retractive are similarly defined, using compact sets or convergent sequences.
> If the steps X_k are Fréchet spaces, the above properties of (X_k) depend only on the LF-space X [39, Chapter 6, p. 111]; thus it may be said that they are properties of X. In this case, X is acyclic if and only if it is boundedly/compactly/sequentially retractive [39, Proposition 6.4]. As a consequence, acyclic LF-spaces are complete and regular [39, Corollary 6.5]."

Same page, on real indices: "The above concepts and properties also apply to an inductive/projective spectrum consisting of continuous inclusions X_r ⊂ X_{r′} for r < r′ in ℝ because ⋃_r X_r = ⋃_k X_{r_k} and ⋂_r X_r = ⋂_k X_{s_k} for sequences r_k ↓ −∞ and s_k ↑ ∞." And, same page, the compact-spectrum shortcut: "Assume the steps X_k are LCHSs. It is said that (X_k) is compact if the inclusion maps are compact operators. In this case, (X_k) is clearly acyclic, and so X is Hausdorff. Moreover X is a complete bornological DF Montel space [18, Theorem 6′]." (The shortcut is not available for any spectrum in this note — the inclusions are not compact operators in the LCS sense, see Prop. 5.7 — and is not used.)

**Terminology used below.** For an inductive spectrum (X_k) of LCSs and indices k ≤ k′ ≤ k″, the triple satisfies **(M*)** if there is an absolutely convex 0-neighborhood V of X_k such that for every 0-neighborhood O of X_{k′} there is a 0-neighborhood W of X_{k″} with V ∩ W ⊂ O. The spectrum satisfies **(M)** if for all k there is k′ ≥ k such that for all k″ ≥ k′ the topologies of X_{k′} and X_{k″} coincide on some 0-neighborhood of X_k — the quoted criterion. "Acyclic", "regular", "boundedly/compactly/sequentially retractive" are used only in the quoted senses, and every use of [Wen03] below is one of the quoted statements.

**Lemma 2.1 ((M*) ⟹ coincidence on V).** If (k, k′, k″) satisfies (M*) with the neighborhood V, then the topologies of X_{k′} and X_{k″} coincide on V. Hence a spectrum satisfying (M*) at every triple k ≤ k′ ≤ k″ (or merely: for every k at some k′ and all k″ ≥ k′) satisfies (M).
*Proof.* The inclusion X_{k′} ⊂ X_{k″} is continuous, so the k′-topology is finer than the k″-topology on V; it remains to show that every k′-neighborhood of a point a ∈ V within V contains a k″-neighborhood of a within V. Let O be an absolutely convex 0-neighborhood of X_{k′} (these form a base). Apply (M*) to O/2: there is a 0-neighborhood W₀ of X_{k″}, which we may take absolutely convex, with V ∩ W₀ ⊂ O/2. Let b ∈ (a + W₀) ∩ V. Then z := b − a ∈ W₀ ∩ (V − V) ⊂ W₀ ∩ 2V (V is absolutely convex), so z/2 ∈ (W₀/2) ∩ V ⊂ W₀ ∩ V ⊂ O/2, i.e. z ∈ O, i.e. b ∈ a + O. Thus (a + W₀) ∩ V ⊂ (a + O) ∩ V. ∎

**Lemma 2.2 ((M*) passes to finite products, to subspaces, and along compatible initial-topology maps).** (a) If spectra (X^{(i)}_k)_k, i = 1, …, r, satisfy (M*) at (k, k′, k″) with neighborhoods V_i, then (∏_i X^{(i)}_k)_k satisfies (M*) at (k, k′, k″) with V = ∏_i V_i. (b) A constant spectrum (X_k = X for all k, same topology) satisfies (M*) at every triple with V = X. (c) Let (X_k), (Y_k) be spectra and j: ⋃Y_k → ⋃X_k a linear map with j(Y_k) ⊂ X_k such that each Y_k carries the initial topology of j|_{Y_k}: Y_k → X_k. If (X_k) satisfies (M*) at (k, k′, k″) with V, then (Y_k) satisfies (M*) at (k, k′, k″) with j^{−1}(V) ∩ Y_k. In particular (M*) passes to linear subspaces with the induced topologies (j = inclusion), closed or not.
*Proof.* (a) A 0-neighborhood of ∏_i X^{(i)}_{k′} contains a product ∏_i O_i of 0-neighborhoods; take W := ∏_i W_i with W_i from (M*) for (V_i, O_i). (b) Given O take W := O. (c) Let O be a 0-neighborhood of Y_{k′}. By the initial topology, O ⊃ j^{−1}(O′) ∩ Y_{k′} for some 0-neighborhood O′ of X_{k′}. Take W′ from (M*) for (V, O′) and put W := j^{−1}(W′) ∩ Y_{k″}, a 0-neighborhood of Y_{k″}. If y ∈ j^{−1}(V) ∩ Y_k ∩ W then j(y) ∈ V ∩ W′ ⊂ O′ and y ∈ Y_k ⊂ Y_{k′}, so y ∈ j^{−1}(O′) ∩ Y_{k′} ⊂ O. ∎

### 2.2 Symbols ([ÁLKL23] §3, pp. 13–14, read verbatim)

For U ⊂ ℝⁿ open, l ∈ ℕ and m ∈ ℝ, S^m(U × ℝ^l) is the set of a ∈ C^∞(U × ℝ^l) such that for every compact K ⊂ U and all α ∈ ℕ₀ⁿ, β ∈ ℕ₀^l

(3.1)  ‖a‖_{K,α,β,m} := sup_{x∈K, ξ∈ℝ^l} |∂_x^α ∂_ξ^β a(x, ξ)| / (1 + |ξ|)^{m−|β|} < ∞,

a Fréchet space with these seminorms; S^m ⊂ S^{m′} continuously for m < m′ (3.2); S^∞ = ⋃_m S^m (LF-space), S^{−∞} = ⋂_m S^m. The auxiliary seminorms: for compact Q ⊂ U × ℝ^l and k ∈ ℕ₀,

(3.4)  ‖a‖_{Q,C^k} := sup_{(x,ξ)∈Q, |α|+|β|≤k} |∂_x^α ∂_ξ^β a(x, ξ)|,   (3.5)  ‖a‖′_{K,α,β,m} := sup_{x∈K} limsup_{|ξ|→∞} |∂_x^α ∂_ξ^β a(x, ξ)| / |ξ|^{m−|β|}.

The statements at issue, verbatim: **Prop. 3.2** (p. 14): "The semi-norms (3.4) and (3.5) together describe the topology of S^m(U × ℝ^l)." **Prop. 3.3**: for m < m′ (the statement says m, m′ ∈ ℕ₀), ‖·‖′_{K,α,β,m′} = 0 on S^m. **Cor. 3.4**: "For m < m′, the topologies of S^{m′}(U × ℝ^l) and C^∞(U × ℝ^l) coincide on S^m(U × ℝ^l). Therefore the topologies of S^∞(U × ℝ^l) and C^∞(U × ℝ^l) coincide on S^m(U × ℝ^l)." Proof: "The first assertion is a consequence of Propositions 3.2 and 3.3." **Cor. 3.5**: C_c^∞ dense in S^m with the S^{m′}-topology; hence dense in S^∞. **Cor. 3.6** (p. 15): "S^∞(U × ℝ^l) is an acyclic Montel space, and therefore complete, boundedly retractive and reflexive." Proof begins: "Corollary 3.4 gives the property of being acyclic, and therefore complete and boundedly retractive (Section 2.1)." p. 15 extends everything to symbol spaces S^m(E) on a vector bundle "via charts of M and local trivializations of E, with K contained in the domains of charts where E is trivial", and asserts "Propositions 3.2 and 3.3 and Corollaries 3.4 to 3.6 can be directly extended to this setting."

The proof of Prop. 3.2 on p. 14 (read in full) defines S′^m as the vector space S^m with the topology of (3.4)+(3.5), notes it is metrizable, lets Ŝ′^m be its completion "where the stated semi-norms have continuous extensions", extends the continuous inclusion S′^m ⊂ C^∞ to φ: Ŝ′^m → C^∞, and then argues: "For any a ∈ Ŝ′^m(U × ℝ^l), and K, α and β like in (3.5), since ‖φ(a)‖′_{K,α,β,m} = ‖a‖′_{K,α,β,m} < ∞, there are C, R > 0 so that, if x ∈ K and |ξ| ≥ R, then |∂^α_x∂^β_ξ φ(a)(x,ξ)|/(1+|ξ|)^{m−|β|} ≤ C" — concluding a ≡ φ(a) ∈ S^m, "Hence S′^m is complete, and therefore it is a Fréchet space", and finishing with the open mapping theorem. The equality ‖φ(a)‖′ = ‖a‖′ is the step refuted in Theorem 3.1.

**The compactly based model.** For d ≥ 0, l ≥ 1, a compact K ⊂ ℝ^d and m ∈ ℝ put

S^m_K := { a ∈ C^∞(ℝ^d × ℝ^l) : a(y, ξ) = 0 for y ∉ K, and N_m(a; γ) < ∞ for all γ ∈ ℕ₀^d × ℕ₀^l }.

With the countable family {N_m(·; γ)}_γ this is a Fréchet space (a closed subspace of S^m(ℝ^d × ℝ^l): its seminorms with K′ ⊃ K coincide with N_m(·; γ), and every ‖·‖_{K′,α,β,m} is dominated by ‖·‖_{K∪K′,α,β,m} = N_m(·; γ)). For m < m′, S^m_K ⊂ S^{m′}_K continuously (N_{m′} ≤ N_m); the spectrum (S^m_K)_{m∈ℝ} has union S^∞_K. If U″ ⊂ ℝ^d is open and K ⊂ U″, the subspace of S^m(U″ × ℝ^l) of symbols vanishing for x ∉ K is, with the induced topology, exactly S^m_K (extension by zero is smooth since K is a compact subset of the open U″; the seminorms with K′ ⊂ U″ are dominated by the one with K), and this is where the symbols of (4.10) live (§2.3).

### 2.3 Conormal distributions ([ÁLKL23] §4, pp. 15–19, read verbatim)

M a compact manifold, L ⊂ M a closed regular submanifold of codimension n′ and dimension n″; Diff(M, L) the filtered algebra generated by C^∞(M) and vector fields tangent to L; in an adapted chart (U, x = (x′, x″)), L₀ = L ∩ U = {x′ = 0} and (p. 16) "Diff^m(U, L₀) is C^∞(U)-spanned by the operators x′^α ∂_{x′}^β ∂_{x″}^γ with |β| + |γ| ≤ m and |α| = |β|".

(4.3)  I^{(s)}(M, L) = { u ∈ C^{−∞}(M) | Diff(M, L)u ⊂ H^s(M) }, projective topology of the maps P: I^{(s)} → H^s(M). ### 2.5 The transplanted (parametrized) spaces

Let T be a compact Hausdorff space (in the S2 setting: the compact totally disconnected transversal factor of a collar chart of the preserved leaf X⁰; compact because X is compact and X⁰ is closed — S2 note §3.2 (A-i)/(A-ii), §3.3).

**Lemma 2.3 (C(T; F) is Fréchet; initial topologies).** Let F be a Fréchet space with topology generated by an increasing sequence of seminorms p₁ ≤ p₂ ≤ ···. Then C(T; F), the space of continuous maps a: T → F, with the seminorms P_k(a) := sup_{t∈T} p_k(a(t)), is a Fréchet space; so is ℓ^∞(T; F), the space of all maps a: T → F with P_k(a) < ∞ for all k, with the same seminorms; and C(T; F) is a closed subspace of ℓ^∞(T; F). If the topology of F is the initial topology of countably many continuous linear maps Π_i: F → E_i into Fréchet spaces, then a map a: T → F is continuous iff every Π_i ∘ a is continuous, and C(T; F) carries the initial topology of the maps a ↦ Π_i ∘ a into C(T; E_i).
*Proof.* P_k(a) < ∞ for a ∈ C(T; F): t ↦ p_k(a(t)) is continuous on the compact T. The families are countable and Hausdorff. Completeness of ℓ^∞(T; F): if (a_j) is Cauchy for every P_k then (a_j(t)) is Cauchy in F for every t, so a_j(t) → a(t) for some a(t) ∈ F; for fixed j, p_k(a_j(t) − a(t)) = lim_i p_k(a_j(t) − a_i(t)) ≤ sup_{i≥j} P_k(a_j − a_i), uniformly in t, so P_k(a_j − a) → 0 and P_k(a) < ∞. Closedness of C(T; F): if a_j ∈ C(T; F) and P_k(a_j − a) → 0 for all k then for t_ν → t, p_k(a(t_ν) − a(t)) ≤ 2P_k(a − a_j) + p_k(a_j(t_ν) − a_j(t)), small for j then ν large, for every k; since the p_k generate the topology of F, a is continuous. The last statement: continuity of a into the initial topology means continuity of every Π_i ∘ a (definition of the initial topology), and the seminorms of F can be taken to be the q_{i,r} ∘ Π_i (q_{i,r} the seminorms of E_i), whose sup-over-T versions are exactly the seminorms pulled back from C(T; E_i). ∎

**Definition 2.4 (parametrized model spectra; sup-seminorm designs).** For T compact Hausdorff:
- S^m_{K,T} := C(T; S^m_K), seminorms N^T_m(a; γ) := sup_{t∈T} N_m(a(t); γ). Fréchet (Lemma 2.3); S^m_{K,T} ⊂ S^{m′}_{K,T} continuously (m < m′); S^∞_{K,T} := ⋃_m S^m_{K,T}, an LF-space.
- B^m_{K,T} := C(T; B^m_K), seminorms sup_t N^b_m(u(t); γ); steps ordered by decreasing m; B_{K,T} := ⋃_m B^m_{K,T}.
- More generally, a **sup-seminorm design** is any choice, for each m, of a linear subspace E^m ⊂ ℓ^∞(T; S^m_K) (resp. ⊂ ℓ^∞(T; B^m_K)) with E^m ⊂ E^{m′} for m < m′ (resp. m′ < m) and E^m ∩ ℓ^∞(T; S^{m}_K)-topology given by the seminorms N^T_m(·; γ). C(T; ·) and ℓ^∞(T; ·) are the two extreme designs; the S2 note's H^s_τ = C(T, H^s), "estimates sup over t" (§3.3, row I-7) selects the C(T; ·) design, which is the one used in Theorem 6.5; Theorem 5.3(b) shows the choice is immaterial for (M*). (Note: the C(T; ·) design is STRONGER than joint continuity of all (y, ξ)-derivatives on T × ℝ^d × ℝ^l, because the seminorms are global in ξ — the family a(t) = φ(ξ − k(t)e₁) with k(t) → ∞ as t → t₀ has jointly continuous derivatives but is not continuous into S^m_K; Lemma 6.4 shows that, for the spaces the S2 note actually defines, the C(T; ·) design is forced.)

**Lemma 2.5 (tangential smoothness in a product chart).** Let U ⊂ ℝⁿ be open and T compact Hausdorff. A function f: U × T → ℂ is tangentially smooth in the uniform model (all ∂_x^α f exist and are jointly continuous on U × T — [Den05] §7.1 condition (b) as quoted in the S2 note §3.1) if and only if t ↦ f(·, t) is a continuous map T → C^∞(U) (compact-convergence topology of all derivatives). Hence C^∞_τ(U × T) = C(T; C^∞(U)) as vector spaces, and the natural Fréchet topologies (sup over compact K × T of |∂^α f|) coincide.
*Proof.* If ∂^α f is jointly continuous, it is uniformly continuous on the compact K × T, so sup_{x∈K}|∂^αf(x, t) − ∂^αf(x, t₀)| → 0 as t → t₀: continuity into C^∞(U). Conversely |∂^αf(x,t) − ∂^αf(x₀,t₀)| ≤ sup_{K}|∂^αf(·,t) − ∂^αf(·,t₀)| + |∂^αf(x,t₀) − ∂^αf(x₀,t₀)| → 0 as (x, t) → (x₀, t₀). The seminorms are the same expressions. ∎

The countability of all inductive systems below is by the last sentence quoted in §2.1 (real indices are cofinal with integer sequences).

## 3. The source statements are false as written (manifold case, one-point transversal)

Throughout, φ ∈ C_c^∞(ℝ^l) is a fixed function with φ(0) = 1 and supp φ ⊂ B(0, r_φ), and e₁ is the first coordinate vector of ℝ^l. All counterexamples are independent of the base variable x (or constant in any parameter), so they lift verbatim to every parametrized version.

**Theorem 3.1 ([ÁLKL23] Prop. 3.2 is false; the invalid step of its proof).** `[novelty: single-check]` Let U ⊂ ℝⁿ be open and nonempty (n ≥ 0), l ≥ 1, m ∈ ℝ. Let S′^m denote S^m(U × ℝ^l) with the topology generated by the seminorms (3.4) and (3.5). Then the identity S^m(U × ℝ^l) → S′^m is continuous but not a homeomorphism; S′^m is not complete; and the step "‖φ(a)‖′_{K,α,β,m} = ‖a‖′_{K,α,β,m}" in the printed proof (p. 14) is false.
*Proof.* Continuity: (3.4) is dominated by (3.1) on compact Q (the weight is bounded there) and (3.5) by (3.1) (limsup of a quotient bounded by a multiple of the (3.1)-quotient). Put a_j(x, ξ) := (1 + j)^m φ(ξ − je₁), j ∈ ℕ. (i) a_j ∈ S^{−∞}(U × ℝ^l) ⊂ S^m: x-independent and compactly supported in ξ, so every seminorm (3.1) of every order is finite. (ii) For every compact Q ⊂ U × ℝ^l and every k, ‖a_j‖_{Q,C^k} = 0 as soon as j > r_φ + max_{(x,ξ)∈Q}|ξ|, because supp_ξ a_j ⊂ B(je₁, r_φ) is then disjoint from the ξ-projection of Q. (iii) ‖a_j‖′_{K,α,β,m} = 0 for all j, K, α, β: a compactly supported function of ξ has limsup_{|ξ|→∞} = 0. So a_j → 0 in S′^m. (iv) For any compact K ≠ ∅ and x ∈ K, ‖a_j‖_{K,0,0,m} ≥ |a_j(x, je₁)|(1 + j)^{−m} = φ(0) = 1. So a_j ↛ 0 in S^m. Hence the two topologies differ, and Prop. 3.2 is false.
The failing step. Fix R > 2r_φ and put b_j := Σ_{i=1}^{j} (1 + iR)^{m+1} φ(ξ − iRe₁) (x-independent; the balls B(iRe₁, r_φ) are pairwise disjoint). For i < j, b_j − b_i is compactly supported in ξ, supported in |ξ| ≥ (i+1)R − r_φ, so its (3.4)-seminorms vanish for i large and its (3.5)-seminorms vanish identically: (b_j) is Cauchy in S′^m. Its image under the continuous map φ: Ŝ′^m → C^∞(U × ℝ^l) of the printed proof is the C^∞-limit b := Σ_{i≥1}(1 + iR)^{m+1}φ(ξ − iRe₁) (a locally finite sum of smooth functions, hence smooth). For the class b̂ ∈ Ŝ′^m of (b_j), the continuously extended seminorm is ‖b̂‖′_{K,0,0,m} = lim_j ‖b_j‖′_{K,0,0,m} = 0, whereas the formula (3.5) applied to φ(b̂) = b gives ‖b‖′_{K,0,0,m} = limsup_{|ξ|→∞}|b(ξ)|/|ξ|^m ≥ limsup_i (1 + iR)^{m+1}/(iR)^m = ∞ (evaluate at ξ = iRe₁; (1 + iR)^m/(iR)^m → 1). So ‖φ(b̂)‖′ ≠ ‖b̂‖′, and b ∉ S^m: the printed conclusion "a ≡ φ(a) ∈ S^m … Hence S′^m is complete" fails for a = b̂. Directly: (b_j) is a Cauchy sequence of S′^m with no limit in S′^m, since a limit would have to be b by the continuity of S′^m ⊂ C^∞(U × ℝ^l) and the Hausdorffness of C^∞, and b ∉ S^m. ∎

Lower bound: pick η₀ ≠ 0 with ψ̂_∗(η₀) ≠ 0; then Φ_{0,m̄′}(j) ≥ |ψ̂_∗(η₀)|(1/j + |η₀|)^{−m̄′} → |ψ̂_∗(η₀)||η₀|^{−m̄′} > 0, so Φ_{0,m̄′}(j) ≥ c₀ > 0 for j ≥ j₀. Now put c_j := j^{n′+m̄′}. Then ‖a_j‖_{K,α,β,m̄″} ≤ C_{α,β} j^{m̄′−m̄″} → 0 for every K, α, β (m̄′ < m̄″), so (hu_j, (a_j)_j) → 0 in C^∞(M∖L) ⊕ ∏_j S^{m̄″}(N*L_j; ΩN*L_j) and, (4.10) being a TVS-embedding by definition, u_j → 0 in I^{m″}(M, L). But with K ⊃ supp g, ‖a_j‖_{K,0,0,m̄′} = ‖g‖_∞ Φ_{0,m̄′}(j) ≥ ‖g‖_∞ c₀ > 0 for j ≥ j₀, so u_j ↛ 0 in I^{m′}(M, L). ∎

*Remark 3.9 (independence of choices).* The space I^m(M, L) is independent of the cover and partition of unity ([ÁLKL23] p. 18, citing [27, Prop. 9.33] — not on disk, taken as quoted), and any two Fréchet topologies on it that are both finer than the Hausdorff topology induced from C^{−∞}(M) (as all (4.10)-topologies are, by (4.5)/(4.12)) coincide, by the closed graph theorem for Fréchet spaces (standard textbook theorem, not re-derived; the source itself invokes the open mapping theorem at p. 14 with references [21, §15.12], [31, Thm. ## 4. The interpolation inequality (the mechanism)

The whole positive content of W3 rests on one elementary inequality: a derivative of intermediate order at a point is controlled by the sup of the function and the sup of a high derivative on a box around the point, with a free scale h; choosing h as a power of the weight converts the weight mismatch m vs. m″ into smallness. Everything is proved from Taylor's theorem; no constant is optimized.

**Lemma 4.1 (one variable, free scale).** Let n ≥ 1, h > 0, x ∈ ℝ, and let g be C^n on the interval J := [x, x + nh] (or on J := [x − nh, x]). Then for every 0 ≤ j ≤ n
 |g^{(j)}(x)| ≤ C_n ( h^{−j} sup_J |g| + h^{n−j} sup_J |g^{(n)}| ),
with C_n depending only on n (one may take C_n = (2^n n^n)^n).
*Proof.* The backward case follows from the forward case applied to s ↦ g(−s), so let J = [x, x + nh]. For j = n and j = 0 the claim is trivial (C_n ≥ 1). For 0 ≤ k ≤ n − 1 Taylor's theorem with Lagrange remainder gives g(x + kh) = Σ_{i=0}^{n−1} g^{(i)}(x)(kh)^i/i! + R_k with |R_k| ≤ (kh)^n/n! · sup_J|g^{(n)}| ≤ (nh)^n/n! · sup_J|g^{(n)}|. For 1 ≤ j ≤ n − 1 form the forward difference Δ_h^j g(x) := Σ_{k=0}^{j}(−1)^{j−k}\binom{j}{k} g(x + kh); then
 Δ_h^j g(x) = Σ_{i=0}^{n−1} (h^i/i!) S(j, i) g^{(i)}(x) + Σ_{k=0}^{j}(−1)^{j−k}\binom{j}{k}R_k,  S(j, i) := Σ_{k=0}^{j}(−1)^{j−k}\binom{j}{k}k^i.
S(j, i) is the j-th forward difference (step 1) of the polynomial k ↦ k^i at 0; each difference lowers the degree by one and multiplies the leading coefficient by the degree, so S(j, i) = 0 for i < j and S(j, j) = j!. Hence
 h^j g^{(j)}(x) = Δ_h^j g(x) − Σ_{i=j+1}^{n−1}(h^i/i!)S(j, i)g^{(i)}(x) − Σ_k(−1)^{j−k}\binom{j}{k}R_k,
and, with |Δ_h^j g(x)| ≤ 2^j sup_J|g| and |S(j, i)| ≤ 2^j j^i ≤ 2^n n^n,
 |g^{(j)}(x)| ≤ 2^n h^{−j} sup_J|g| + 2^n n^n Σ_{i=j+1}^{n−1} h^{i−j}|g^{(i)}(x)| + 2^n n^n h^{n−j} sup_J|g^{(n)}|.
Downward induction on j: for j = n − 1 the middle sum is empty and the claim holds with constant 2^n n^n. If the claim holds for all i with j < i ≤ n − 1 with constants C_{n,i}, substitute |g^{(i)}(x)| ≤ C_{n,i}(h^{−i}sup|g| + h^{n−i}sup|g^{(n)}|) and note h^{i−j}h^{−i} = h^{−j}, h^{i−j}h^{n−i} = h^{n−j}; the claim follows for j with a constant depending only on n. ∎

**Lemma 4.2 (corner inequality on a box, per-variable scales).** Let N ≥ 1, x ∈ ℝ^N, n ≥ 1, h₁, …, h_N > 0, orientations σ_i ∈ {+1, −1}, and let F be C^∞ on a neighborhood of the box Q := ∏_{i=1}^{N} J_i, J_i := the closed interval between x_i and x_i + σ_i n h_i. Then for every multi-index γ ∈ ℕ₀^N with γ_i ≤ n for all i,
 |∂^γ F(x)| ≤ C_n^N Σ_{S ⊂ {1,…,N}} ( ∏_{i∈S} h_i^{n−γ_i} )( ∏_{i∉S} h_i^{−γ_i} ) sup_Q |∂^{nχ_S} F|.
*Proof.* Induction on N. For N = 1 this is Lemma 4.1. For N > 1 write γ = (γ₁, γ′) and G := ∂^{γ′}_{x′}F, so ∂^γF(x) = ∂_1^{γ₁}G(x). Lemma 4.1 in the first variable, with the other variables frozen at x′, gives |∂_1^{γ₁}G(x)| ≤ C_n(h₁^{−γ₁} sup_{s∈J₁}|G(s, x′)| + h₁^{n−γ₁} sup_{s∈J₁}|∂_1^nG(s, x′)|). For each s ∈ J₁ apply the inductive hypothesis (N − 1 variables, point (s, x′), box ∏_{i≥2}J_i) to ∂^{γ′}_{x′}F(s, ·) and to ∂^{γ′}_{x′}∂_1^nF(s, ·), and take the sup over s ∈ J₁; the corner sets S′ ⊂ {2, …, N} of the two applications become S = S′ and S = S′ ∪ {1}, with the stated powers of the h_i. ∎

Notation for the models. For a ∈ C^∞(ℝ^d × ℝ^l) recall N_m(a; γ) := sup |∂^γ a|(1 + |ξ|)^{|β|−m} (γ = (α, β)); for u ∈ C^∞((−∞, r₀] × ℝ^d), N^b_m(u; γ) := sup e^{−mr}|∂^γ u| (γ = (γ_r, γ_y)). For γ ≠ 0 and n ∈ ℕ put Γ_n(γ) := {γ} ∪ {nχ_S : ∅ ≠ S ⊂ {1, …, d + l}} (resp. S ⊂ {1, …, 1 + d}), a finite set of multi-indices.

**Proposition 4.3 (weighted interpolation, symbol model).** `[novelty: single-check]` Let d ≥ 0, l ≥ 1, K ⊂ ℝ^d compact, m < m′ < m″ real, and γ = (α, β) ≠ 0. Put c := (m′ − m)/(2|γ|) and let n be the least integer with n > |γ| + (m″ − m′)/c. There are C = C(γ, m, m′, m″, d, l) and R₀ ≥ 1 such that for every R ≥ R₀ and every a ∈ S^{m″}_K,
 N_{m′}(a; γ) ≤ C [ R^{−(m′−m)/2} N_m(a; 0) + R^{m″−m′} max_{γ′∈Γ_n(γ)} N_{m″}(a; γ′) ]   (with the convention ∞ · 0 = ∞ on the right if N_m(a; 0) = ∞).
*Proof.* Put R₀ := (2n√l)^{1/c} and fix R ≥ R₀ and a ∈ S^{m″}_K. Since a vanishes on the open set (ℝ^d∖K) × ℝ^l, so do all its derivatives, and every sup below may be taken over all of ℝ^d × ℝ^l. Fix (y, ξ) and write w := 1 + |ξ| ≥ 1.
Case w ≤ R. |∂^γa(y, ξ)|w^{|β|−m′} = |∂^γa|w^{|β|−m″} · w^{m″−m′} ≤ R^{m″−m′} N_{m″}(a; γ).
Case w > R. Put h_y := w^{−c} for the d base variables and h_ξ := w^{1−c} for the l fiber variables, all orientations +1, and Q := ∏_{i≤d}[y_i, y_i + nh_y] × ∏_{k≤l}[ξ_k, ξ_k + nh_ξ]. For (y′, ξ′) ∈ Q, |ξ′ − ξ| ≤ √l·n·w^{1−c} ≤ w/2 because w^{−c} ≤ R₀^{−c} = 1/(2n√l); hence 1 + |ξ′| ∈ [w/2, 3w/2] on Q, and for every real e, sup_Q(1 + |ξ′|)^e ≤ 2^{|e|}w^e. Lemma 4.2 (all γ_i ≤ |γ| < n) gives
 |∂^γa(y, ξ)| ≤ C_n^{d+l} Σ_S h_y^{n|S_y|−|α|} h_ξ^{n|S_ξ|−|β|} sup_Q|∂^{nχ_S}a|,
where S = S_y ⊔ S_ξ splits a corner set into base and fiber positions. Bound the sups: for S = ∅, sup_Q|a| ≤ N_m(a; 0)·2^{|m|}w^m; for S ≠ ∅, sup_Q|∂^{nχ_S}a| ≤ N_{m″}(a; nχ_S)·2^{|m″|+n l}w^{m″−n|S_ξ|}. Multiply by the target weight w^{|β|−m′}:
 S = ∅: h_y^{−|α|}h_ξ^{−|β|}w^{m+|β|−m′} = w^{c|α|}·w^{−(1−c)|β|}·w^{m+|β|−m′} = w^{c|γ|+m−m′} = w^{−(m′−m)/2} ≤ R^{−(m′−m)/2};
 S ≠ ∅: h_y^{n|S_y|−|α|}h_ξ^{n|S_ξ|−|β|}w^{m″−n|S_ξ|+|β|−m′} = w^{−c(n|S_y|−|α|)}·w^{(1−c)(n|S_ξ|−|β|)}·w^{m″−m′−(n|S_ξ|−|β|)} = w^{−c(n|S|−|γ|)+m″−m′} ≤ 1,
the last because |S| ≥ 1, n − |γ| > (m″ − m′)/c, hence c(n|S| − |γ|) ≥ c(n − |γ|) > m″ − m′, and w ≥ 1. Collecting, with C := C_n^{d+l}2^{|m|+|m″|+nl}2^{d+l},
 |∂^γa(y, ξ)|w^{|β|−m′} ≤ C [ R^{−(m′−m)/2}N_m(a; 0) + max_{S≠∅}N_{m″}(a; nχ_S) ]  for w > R.
Taking the sup over (y, ξ) in both cases and using R ≥ 1, m″ − m′ > 0 gives the statement. ∎

**Proposition 4.4 (weighted interpolation, b-model).** `[novelty: single-check]` Let d ≥ 0, r₀ ∈ ℝ, K ⊂ ℝ^d compact, m″ < m′ < m real, and γ = (γ_r, γ_y) ≠ 0. Put c := (m − m′)/(2|γ|) and let n be the least integer with n > |γ| + (m′ − m″)/c. There are C = C(γ, m, m′, m″, d, r₀) and R₀ ≥ max(1, −r₀) such that for every R ≥ R₀ and every u ∈ B^{m″}_K,
 N^b_{m′}(u; γ) ≤ C [ e^{−(m−m′)R/2} N^b_m(u; 0) + e^{(m′−m″)R} max_{γ′∈Γ_n(γ)} N^b_{m″}(u; γ′) ].
*Proof.* Fix (r, y) with r ≤ r₀.
Case r ≥ −R. e^{−m′r}|∂^γu| = e^{(m″−m′)r}e^{−m″r}|∂^γu| ≤ e^{(m′−m″)R}N^b_{m″}(u; γ), because m″ − m′ < 0 and −R ≤ r ≤ r₀ ≤ R give (m″ − m′)r ≤ (m′ − m″)R.
Case r < −R. Put h := e^{cr} (≤ e^{−cR} ≤ 1), orientation −1 in the r-variable and +1 in the y-variables, Q := [r − nh, r] × ∏_{i≤d}[y_i, y_i + nh] ⊂ (−∞, r₀] × ℝ^d. On Q, e^{−m₁r′} ≤ e^{|m₁|n}e^{−m₁r} for every real m₁ (since |r′ − r| ≤ nh ≤ n). Lemma 4.2 gives |∂^γu(r, y)| ≤ C_n^{1+d}Σ_S h^{n|S|−|γ|}sup_Q|∂^{nχ_S}u|, with sup_Q|u| ≤ e^{|m|n}e^{mr}N^b_m(u; 0) and sup_Q|∂^{nχ_S}u| ≤ e^{|m″|n}e^{m″r}N^b_{m″}(u; nχ_S) for S ≠ ∅. Multiply by e^{−m′r}: for S = ∅ the exponent of e^r is −c|γ| + m − m′ = (m − m′)/2 > 0, so the factor is e^{(m−m′)r/2} ≤ e^{−(m−m′)R/2} (r < −R); for S ≠ ∅ the exponent is c(n|S| − |γ|) + m″ − m′ ≥ c(n − |γ|) − (m′ − m″) > 0, so the factor e^{(…)r} ≤ 1 (r < 0). Collect constants and take sups as before. ∎

*Remark 4.5 (what the inequality does, and why the whole-step versions had to fail).* On the 0-neighborhood V = {N_m(·; 0) < 1} the first term is O(R^{−(m′−m)/2}), uniformly over V; the second is small when finitely many m″-seminorms of order ≤ n(d + l) are small. Nothing bounds N_{m′}(a; γ) by m″-seminorms alone, and nothing bounds it by the single seminorm N_m(a; 0) alone: both inputs are needed, which is exactly the shape of (M*) and exactly what the whole-step statements of §3 lack (their counterexamples have N_m(a_j; 0) → ∞).

## 5. Acyclicity of the model spectra and of their parametrized versions

**Theorem 5.1 ((M*) for the compactly based symbol spectrum).** `[novelty: single-check]` Let d ≥ 0, l ≥ 1, K ⊂ ℝ^d compact. For every m < m′ < m″ the triple (S^m_K ⊂ S^{m′}_K ⊂ S^{m″}_K) satisfies (M*) with V := {a ∈ S^m_K : N_m(a; 0) < 1}. Consequently the spectrum (S^m_K)_{m∈ℝ} satisfies (M) (at every k′ > k and every k″ ≥ k′), and the LF-space S^∞_K is acyclic.
*Proof.* V is absolutely convex and is a 0-neighborhood of S^m_K. Let O be a 0-neighborhood of S^{m′}_K; it contains {a : N_{m′}(a; γ_i) < ε, i = 1, …, r} for some finite set of multi-indices γ_i and some ε > 0 (for γ_i = 0 no interpolation is needed: splitting at w = R exactly as in the two cases of Prop. 4.3 gives N_{m′}(a; 0) ≤ R^{m″−m′}N_{m″}(a; 0) + R^{m−m′}N_m(a; 0), a bound of the same form). For each i apply Prop. 4.3 with constants C_i, n_i, R_{0,i}. Choose R ≥ max_i R_{0,i} with C_iR^{−(m′−m)/2} < ε/2 for all i, then δ > 0 with C_iR^{m″−m′}δ < ε/2 for all i, and put W := {a ∈ S^{m″}_K : N_{m″}(a; γ′) < δ for all γ′ ∈ ⋃_iΓ_{n_i}(γ_i)}, a 0-neighborhood of S^{m″}_K. For a ∈ V ∩ W, Prop. 4.3 gives N_{m′}(a; γ_i) < ε/2 + ε/2 = ε for each i, so a ∈ O. Thus (M*) holds; Lemma 2.1 gives (M); the quoted [Wen03, Thm. 6.1] gives acyclicity. ∎

**Theorem 5.2 ((M*) for the compactly based b-spectrum).** `[novelty: single-check]` Let d ≥ 0, r₀ ∈ ℝ, K ⊂ ℝ^d compact. For every m″ < m′ < m the triple (B^m_K ⊂ B^{m′}_K ⊂ B^{m″}_K) satisfies (M*) with V := {u ∈ B^m_K : N^b_m(u; 0) < 1}. Consequently (B^m_K)_{m∈ℝ, decreasing} satisfies (M) and B_K := ⋃_m B^m_K is acyclic.
*Proof.* Identical to Theorem 5.1 with Prop. 4.4 in place of Prop. 4.3 (and, for γ_i = 0, the split at r = −R: N^b_{m′}(u; 0) ≤ e^{(m′−m″)R}N^b_{m″}(u; 0) + e^{−(m−m′)R}N^b_m(u; 0)). ∎

**Theorem 5.3 ((M*) transplants over any compact parameter space, for any sup-seminorm design).** `[novelty: single-check]` Let T be a compact Hausdorff space (or, for (b), any set).
(a) For every m < m′ < m″, the triple (C(T; S^m_K) ⊂ C(T; S^{m′}_K) ⊂ C(T; S^{m″}_K)) satisfies (M*) with V^T := {a : sup_{t∈T}N_m(a(t); 0) < 1}; the spectrum (C(T; S^m_K))_m satisfies (M) and S^∞_{K,T} = ⋃_m C(T; S^m_K) is acyclic. The same holds for (C(T; B^m_K))_m (decreasing m) and B_{K,T}.
(b) The same holds for every sup-seminorm design (Definition 2.4): for any linear subspaces E^m ⊂ ℓ^∞(T; S^m_K) with E^m ⊂ E^{m′} (m < m′), topologized by the seminorms N^T_m(·; γ) = sup_t N_m(·(t); γ), the triples (E^m ⊂ E^{m′} ⊂ E^{m″}) satisfy (M*) with V^T ∩ E^m. In particular for ℓ^∞(T; S^m_K).
*Proof.* (b) contains (a). Let O be a 0-neighborhood of E^{m′}, O ⊃ {a : N^T_{m′}(a; γ_i) < ε, i ≤ r}. Choose R, δ, n_i as in the proof of Theorem 5.1 for these γ_i and this ε, and put W := {a ∈ E^{m″} : N^T_{m″}(a; γ′) < δ, γ′ ∈ ⋃_iΓ_{n_i}(γ_i)}, a 0-neighborhood of E^{m″}. If a ∈ V^T ∩ W then for EVERY t ∈ T the function a(t) ∈ S^{m″}_K satisfies N_m(a(t); 0) < 1 and N_{m″}(a(t); γ′) < δ for all γ′ in the finite set, so Prop. 4.3 gives N_{m′}(a(t); γ_i) < ε for each i and each t; taking the sup over t, N^T_{m′}(a; γ_i) ≤ ε (strict after shrinking ε by half at the start), i.e. a ∈ O. The (M) and acyclicity conclusions for (a) use that the steps are Fréchet (Lemma 2.3) and the quoted [Wen03, Thm. 6.1]; for a general design (b) they need the steps to be at least LCSs, which they are, and for the Fréchet-only consequences of Cor. 5.4 the steps must be complete — true for C(T; ·) and ℓ^∞(T; ·) by Lemma 2.3 and to be checked for any intermediate design. The b-case is identical with Prop. 4.4. ∎

**Corollary 5.4 (the package W3 was to deliver).** For T compact Hausdorff and the C(T; ·) or ℓ^∞(T; ·) design, the LF-spaces S^∞_{K,T} and B_{K,T} — and, by Lemma 2.2, every LF-space whose steps are Fréchet spaces carrying the initial topology of a compatible family of maps into finite products of such steps and of constant Fréchet spaces — are acyclic; therefore (all by the statements quoted in §2.1, the steps being Fréchet and Hausdorff) boundedly, compactly and sequentially retractive ([Wen03, Prop. 6.4]), complete and regular ([Wen03, Cor. 6.5]), and Hausdorff ([Wen03, Prop. 6.3]).

*Remark 5.5 (what is and is not used about T).* Compactness of T enters in exactly two places: the finiteness of the sup-seminorms (Lemma 2.3) and nowhere else; total disconnectedness enters nowhere. If T is merely locally compact and non-compact and the parametrized spaces are given the topology of uniform convergence on compact subsets of T (the "C^∞_τ-style" design with compact-convergence transversally), then the spectrum is NOT regular: replacing the escaping base points x_j of Theorem 3.5 by points t_j escaping every compact subset of T and χ_j by continuous cut-offs on T with pairwise disjoint supports, the proof of Theorem 3.5 goes through verbatim (the 0-neighborhoods of the steps now control only a compact subset of T). In the S2 setting T is compact ((A-i)/(A-ii)), so this does not bite; it does show that Theorem 5.3 cannot be extended to non-compact transversals without changing the design, and that the S2 note's choice "estimates sup over t" over a compact T is the right one.

**Proposition 5.6 (bounded sets of the model steps are relatively compact in higher steps — for a one-point parameter space).** Let m < m′. Every bounded subset of S^m_K is relatively compact in S^{m′}_K. Likewise every bounded subset of B^m_K is relatively compact in B^{m′}_K for m′ < m.
*Proof.* Let B ⊂ S^m_K be bounded: C_γ := sup_{a∈B}N_m(a; γ) < ∞ for all γ. All derivatives of all a ∈ B are uniformly bounded on every compact subset of ℝ^d × ℝ^l, so by the Arzelà–Ascoli theorem and a diagonal argument over derivative orders and an exhaustion by compacts (standard; not re-derived) every sequence in B has a subsequence (a_k) converging with all derivatives uniformly on compacts to some a ∈ C^∞; a has y-support in K and N_m(a; γ) ≤ C_γ (pointwise limits), so a ∈ S^m_K. Convergence in S^{m′}_K: given γ and ε, choose R with 2C_γ(1 + R)^{m−m′} < ε; for |ξ| ≥ R, |∂^γ(a_k − a)|(1 + |ξ|)^{|β|−m′} ≤ 2C_γ(1 + |ξ|)^{m−m′} < ε, and on the compact {|ξ| ≤ R} × K the uniform convergence of ∂^γa_k gives < ε for k large. S^{m′}_K is metrizable, so sequential relative compactness is relative compactness. The b-case is the same with the split at r = −R. ∎

**Proposition 5.7 (the compact-spectrum shortcut is unavailable, in both settings).** (a) For no m < m′ is the inclusion S^m_K → S^{m′}_K a compact operator in the LCS sense (some 0-neighborhood mapped onto a relatively compact set): a 0-neighborhood {N_m(·; γ) < 1, γ ∈ F} (F finite) contains a_k := ψ(y)φ(ξ)sin(kξ₁)/k^{p} with p := max_{γ∈F}|γ| + 1, a sequence which is unbounded in S^{m′}_K (its seminorm of any order |γ| > p grows like k^{|γ|−p}). So (S^m_K)_m is not a "compact spectrum" in the sense of [ÁLKL23] p. 4, and the acyclicity of S^∞_K genuinely requires Theorem 5.1. (b) For T infinite, the inclusion C(T; S^m_K) → C(T; S^{m′}_K) does not even map bounded sets to relatively compact sets: with a fixed a₀ ∈ S^m_K, a₀ ≠ 0, the set {f ⊗ a₀ : f ∈ C(T), ‖f‖_∞ ≤ 1} is bounded, and if it were relatively compact in C(T; S^{m′}_K), then composing with a ↦ (t ↦ ℓ(a(t))) for a continuous linear functional ℓ on S^{m′}_K with ℓ(a₀) = 1 (e.g. a normalized point evaluation) would make the closed unit ball of C(T) compact in sup norm, which is false for infinite compact Hausdorff T (Urysohn functions with pairwise disjoint supports are at mutual distance 1). This is the proof-level form of the S2 note's row I-10 (loss of Montel/reflexivity): the Montel property of the manifold-level spaces (Theorem 6.3) comes from Prop. 5.6, which fails over an infinite transversal; acyclicity (Theorem 5.3) does not come from it and survives.

## 6. Consequences: the manifold case re-secured, and the transplant theorem

### 6.1 Localization of A^m(M̄) and the manifold-level acyclicity

**Lemma 6.1 (localization of A^m(M̄) into finitely many b-models).** Let M̄ be compact with boundary, with a collar [0, ε) × ∂M̄ and boundary defining function x. Choose finitely many collar charts U_j ≅ [0, ε) × V_j (V_j ⊂ ℝ^d open, coordinates (x, y)) covering a neighborhood of ∂M̄, an open U₀ with Ū₀ ⊂ M̊ such that {U_j}_{j≥0} covers M̄, and a partition of unity {μ, λ_j} subordinate to {U₀, U_j}. Let K_j ⊂ V_j be the compact y-projection of supp λ_j, K′ := supp μ ⊂ M̊, r₀ := log ε. Define
 Λ: A^m(M̄) → ∏_j B^m_{K_j} × C^∞_{K′}(M̊),  u ↦ ((x, y) ↦ (λ_j u)(e^r, y))_j ⊕ μu,
where C^∞_{K′}(M̊) is the Fréchet space of smooth functions supported in K′ with the sup-seminorms of all derivatives (the same space, with the same topology, for every m). Then Λ is well defined, linear, injective, continuous, and A^m(M̄) carries the initial topology of Λ; and Λ is the same map for every m.
*Proof.* Well defined and continuous. On U_j, Diff_b(U_j) is C^∞(U_j)-spanned by the monomials (x∂_x)^a∂_y^b (§2.4), and x∂_x = ∂_r; so N^b_m(Λ_ju; (a, b)) = sup x^{−m}|(x∂_x)^a∂_y^b(λ_ju)|. The operator λ̃_j(x∂_x)^a∂_y^b, with λ̃_j ∈ C_c^∞(U_j), λ̃_j = 1 on supp λ_j, extended by zero, lies in Diff_b(M̄), hence is a finite C^∞(M̄)-combination Σ_k c_kP_k of the spanning set of (6.40); and P_k∘λ_j ∈ Diff_b(M̄) is again such a combination Σ_{k′}c_{kk′}P_{k′}. Hence sup x^{−m}|(x∂_x)^a∂_y^b(λ_ju)| ≤ C Σ_{k′} ‖u‖_{k′,m}: the j-th component is finite and continuous. The component μu is smooth with support in K′; on K′ the weight x^{−m} is bounded above and below and Diff_b restricted to a compact subset of M̊ contains every ∂^δ in local coordinates (x∂_x is a nonvanishing multiple of ∂_x there), so the C^∞_{K′}-seminorms of μu are bounded by finitely many ‖u‖_{k,m} (using P∘μ ∈ Diff_b) — continuity of the last component; conversely all ‖μu‖_{k,m} are bounded by C^∞_{K′}-seminorms of μu. Initial topology. u = Σ_jλ_ju + μu, so for each k, ‖u‖_{k,m} ≤ Σ_j sup x^{−m}|P_k(λ_ju)| + sup x^{−m}|P_k(μu)|; on U_j, P_k = Σ_{(a,b)}c_{k,a,b}(x∂_x)^a∂_y^b with c_{k,a,b} bounded on supp λ_j, so sup x^{−m}|P_k(λ_ju)| ≤ C max_{(a,b)}N^b_m(Λ_ju; (a, b)); and sup x^{−m}|P_k(μu)| is bounded by C^∞_{K′}-seminorms of μu. Hence every seminorm of A^m is dominated by seminorms of the target pulled back by Λ: A^m carries the initial topology. Injectivity: Λu = 0 forces λ_ju = 0 for all j and μu = 0, so u = 0. Independence of m is by construction. ∎

**Theorem 6.2 (acyclicity of the manifold-level conormal LF-spaces, with a valid proof).** `[novelty: single-check]` (a) For M compact and L ⊂ M a closed regular submanifold, the spectrum (I^m(M, L))_{m∈ℝ} satisfies (M*) at every m < m′ < m″; the LF-space ⋃_mI^m(M, L) is acyclic, and, by (4.12) as quoted (Prop. 4.3, [Hör85, Thm. 18.2.8] class), it is the LF-space I(M, L) = ⋃_sI^{(s)}(M, L), whose acyclicity is then a property of I(M, L) itself ([Wen03, Ch. 6 p. 111] as quoted). (b) For M̄ compact with boundary, (A^m(M̄))_m satisfies (M*) at every m″ < m′ < m and A(M̄) = ⋃_mA^m = ⋃_sA^{(s)} ((6.38)–(6.39)) is acyclic. (c) (Ȧ^m(M̄))_m satisfies (M*) and Ȧ(M̄) is acyclic. (d) (K^m(M̄))_m satisfies (M*) and K(M̄) is acyclic. (e) Under the identifications of [ÁLKL23] §7.7 and §7.10 (J^m(M, L) with A^m(M̄) via π_*, K^m(M, L) with K^m(M̄); asserted there, not re-derived here), J(M, L) and K(M, L) are acyclic. In all cases the LF-space is therefore boundedly, compactly and sequentially retractive, complete, regular and Hausdorff ([Wen03] Props. 6.3–6.4, Cor. 6.5 as quoted). This gives valid proofs of the acyclicity clauses of [ÁLKL23] Cors. 4.7, 6.14, 6.20, 6.25, 7.13, 7.21 and of the sentence at [ALKL] §2.2.2 p. 21 quoted in §1.
*Proof.* (a) The map (4.10) is, by definition, a TVS-embedding I^m(M, L) → C^∞(M∖L) ⊕ ∏_jS^{m̄}(N*L_j; ΩN*L_j), it is the same map for every m, and its image lies in C^∞(M∖L) ⊕ ∏_jS^{m̄}_{K_j} with the induced topology (§2.3). The target spectrum (indexed by m, with m̄ = m + n/4 − n′/2 an increasing affine function of m) is a finite product of the constant spectrum C^∞(M∖L) and the spectra (S^{m̄}_{K_j})_{m̄}; by Theorem 5.1 and Lemma 2.2(a),(b) it satisfies (M*) at every triple; by Lemma 2.2(c) (initial topology of a compatible map) so does (I^m(M, L))_m. Lemma 2.1 gives (M) and the quoted [Wen03, Thm. 6.1] gives acyclicity of ⋃_mI^m with its LF-topology. Since the spectra (I^m)_m and (I^{(s)})_s are mutually cofinal with continuous inclusions both ways ((4.12)), the identity of ⋃_mI^m = ⋃_sI^{(s)} is continuous in both directions between the two LF-topologies (each step of either spectrum maps continuously into a step of the other), so the two LF-spaces coincide, and acyclicity is a property of I(M, L). (b) Lemma 6.1 gives, for every m, the initial topology of one and the same map Λ into ∏_jB^m_{K_j} × C^∞_{K′}(M̊); Theorem 5.2 and Lemma 2.2(a),(b),(c) give (M*) for (A^m)_m; (6.38)–(6.39) make (A^m)_m and (A^{(s)})_s mutually cofinal as in (a). (c) Ȧ^m(M̄) = I^m_{M̄}(M̆, ∂M̄) is a closed subspace of I^m(M̆, ∂M̄) with the induced topology (6.46), and M̆ is compact without boundary with ∂M̄ ⊂ M̆ a closed regular hypersurface; apply (a) to (M̆, ∂M̄) and Lemma 2.2(c) with j = inclusion. The identification of ⋃_mȦ^m with Ȧ(M̄) = ⋃_sȦ^{(s)} uses the (4.11)–(4.12)-analogs asserted at (6.46) and Cors. 6.18 (whose proof uses Prop. 6.17, whose proof uses Cor. 6.14, now valid by (b)). (d) K^m(M̄) = Ȧ^m_{∂M̄}(M̄) is a closed subspace of Ȧ^m(M̄) with the induced topology (§6.13); Lemma 2.2(c). (e) As stated. ∎

**Theorem 6.3 (the Montel clauses of the manifold case, with a valid proof).** `[novelty: single-check]` S^∞_K, I(M, L) (M compact), A(M̄), Ȧ(M̄), K(M̄) are Montel spaces.
*Proof.* Each space is barreled: S^∞_K and I(M, L) as LF-spaces of Fréchet steps (the argument of [ÁLKL23] Prop. 3.1 / Cor. 4.2), A, Ȧ by Cor. 6.6, K by Cor. 6.23 (all read on disk; barreledness of countable inductive limits of Fréchet spaces is standard). It remains to see that bounded sets are relatively compact. Let B be bounded in I(M, L). By Theorem 6.2(a) I(M, L) is regular and boundedly retractive: B is contained and bounded in some I^m(M, L), and the topologies of I(M, L) and I^m coincide on B. Fix m′ > m. The image of B under the embedding (4.10)_{m′} is bounded in C^∞(M∖L) ⊕ ∏_jS^{m̄′}_{K_j}; its C^∞(M∖L)-component is relatively compact because C^∞(M∖L) is a Montel space (the fact "C^∞(U × ℝ^l) is a Montel space" is used at [ÁLKL23] p. 15 with references; for a manifold it is the standard Fréchet–Montel property of C^∞, not re-derived), and its S^{m̄′}_{K_j}-components are relatively compact by Prop. 5.6 (bounded in S^{m̄}_{K_j}, m̄ < m̄′). So the image of B is relatively compact in the product. The image of I^{m′}(M, L) is closed in the product (a TVS-embedding of a Fréchet space has complete, hence closed, image), and (4.10)_{m′} is a homeomorphism onto its image; so B is relatively compact in I^{m′}(M, L), hence in I(M, L) (continuous image; I(M, L) is Hausdorff, so the compact closure of B in I^{m′} is closed in I(M, L) and contains the closure of B in I(M, L)). For S^∞_K argue the same with Prop. 5.6 directly; for A(M̄) with Lemma 6.1 (the factor C^∞_{K′}(M̊) is a closed subspace of a Montel space) and the b-version of Prop. 5.6; Ȧ(M̄) and K(M̄) are closed subspaces of I(M̆, ∂M̄) and of Ȧ(M̄) respectively, in which bounded sets are bounded, hence relatively compact, hence relatively compact in the closed subspace. ∎

### 6.2 The transplant: interface hypotheses and the W3 theorem

Notation (S2 note §3.3, uniform model): X compact solenoid with preserved leaf X⁰, collar charts U_j × T_j (U_j ⊂ ℝⁿ adapted, T_j compact totally disconnected), C^∞_τ, H^s_τ (= C(T_j; H^s) in charts), Diff_τ(X, X⁰), I^{(s)}_τ, I_τ = ⋃_sI^{(s)}_τ, the cut space X̄, J_τ, Ȧ_τ, K_τ. The following are the interface hypotheses — the outputs of the S2 note's items W1/W2 that Theorem 6.5 consumes; they are definitional (they say how the symbol-order steps are to be defined), not analytic claims.

- **(H1) (parametrized symbol-order steps).** For each m ∈ ℝ a Fréchet space I^m_τ ⊂ I_τ is defined, together with a fixed finite family of collar charts and a fixed tangentially smooth partition of unity {h, f_j} (clopen in the transversal — S2 note §3.3), such that u ↦ (hu, (a_j)_j) is a TVS-embedding of I^m_τ into C^∞_τ(X∖X⁰) ⊕ ∏_jE^{m̄}_j, where E^{m̄}_j is a sup-seminorm design (Definition 2.4) of maps T_j → S^{m̄}_{K_j} with complete steps (C(T_j; S^{m̄}_{K_j}) in the S2 note's design), a_j the partial Fourier transform in x′ of f_ju (t-wise), and C^∞_τ(X∖X⁰) is a Fréchet space independent of m; the map is the same for all m.
- **(H2) (interleaving).** I^{(−m−n/4+ε)}_τ ⊂ I^m_τ ⊂ I^{(−m−n/4−ε)}_τ continuously for all ε > 0 — the transplant of (4.12), i.e. of Prop. 4.3 with parameters; equivalently ⋃_mI^m_τ = I_τ as LF-spaces.
- **(H1′)/(H2′).** The same for J_τ^m = A^m_τ(X̄): a localization map Λ_τ as in Lemma 6.1 into ∏_jE^m_{b,j} × (an m-independent Fréchet space), with E^m_{b,j} a sup-seminorm design of maps T_j → B^m_{K_j}; and the (6.38)–(6.39)-type interleaving with the Sobolev steps. For Ȧ_τ, K_τ: Ȧ^m_τ a closed subspace of I^m_τ of the double, K^m_τ a closed subspace of Ȧ^m_τ, with the induced topologies, and the corresponding interleavings.

**Lemma 6.4 (what is already true at chart level).** Let U ⊂ ℝⁿ be an adapted chart domain, L₀ = {x′ = 0}, T compact Hausdorff.
(i) C^∞_τ(U × T) = C(T; C^∞(U)) (Lemma 2.5).
(ii) Let Diff_τ(U × T, L₀ × T) be the algebra generated by C^∞_τ(U × T) and the tangential vector fields Σ_ia_i(x, t)∂_{x_i} (a_i ∈ C^∞_τ) tangent to L₀ × T. Then it is the C^∞_τ(U × T)-span of the t-independent monomials x′^α∂_{x′}^β∂_{x″}^γ with |α| = |β|, and its order-k part is the span of those with |β| + |γ| ≤ k. (The same argument with C^∞(U) in place of C^∞_τ re-derives the assertion at [ÁLKL23] p. 16.)
(iii) Let I^{(s)}(U, L₀) := {u ∈ C^{−∞}(U) : Diff(U, L₀)u ⊂ H^s_loc(U)} with the projective topology (the non-compact version of (4.3), [ÁLKL23] §4.2.2), and define the chart-level transplanted space I^{(s)}_τ(U × T) as the set of maps u: T → C^{−∞}(U) such that for every P ∈ Diff_τ(U × T, L₀ × T) the map t ↦ P_t(u(t)) lies in C(T; H^s_loc(U)), topologized by the seminorms sup_t‖χP_t u(t)‖_{H^s} (χ over a countable family of cut-offs). (iv) If u ∈ C(T; I^{(s)}_c(U, L₀)) has x-support in a fixed compact set and m̄ > −s − n′/2, then t ↦ a(t) := ∫e^{−i⟨x′,ξ⟩}u(t)(x′, ·)dx′ lies in C(T; S^{m̄}_K) with K the x″-projection of the support, and u ↦ a is continuous C(T; I^{(s)}_c) → C(T; S^{m̄}_K). (This uses the continuity in Prop. 4.3 as quoted; it shows that, given the C(T; H^s) design of H^s_τ, the symbol side of (H1) is forced to be the C(T; ·) design.)
*Proof.* (i) is Lemma 2.5. (ii) A tangential field Σa_i∂_i is tangent to L₀ × T iff a_i(0, x″, t) = 0 for i ≤ n′; then a_i(x′, x″, t) = Σ_jx′_j∫₀¹(∂_{x′_j}a_i)(sx′, x″, t)ds with the integrals in C^∞_τ (differentiation under the integral in x, continuity in t by uniform continuity on compacts). So the generators are C^∞_τ-combinations of the monomials x′_j∂_{x′_i}, ∂_{x″_k} and 1. So 𝔐 is a subalgebra containing the generators, hence equals Diff_τ; the order statement is read off the same computation. (iii) Let {P_j} be the countable set of monomials. ## 7. The exactness engine without the false statement

**Proposition 7.1 (Claim 6.43 of [ÁLKL23] — "topological lifting of bounded sets" for R: Ȧ(M̄) → A(M̄) — holds, with a proof using only bounded retractivity of A(M̄) and the partial extension maps).** `[novelty: single-check]` For every bounded A ⊂ A(M̄) there is a bounded B ⊂ Ȧ(M̄) such that for every 0-neighborhood U of Ȧ(M̄) there is a 0-neighborhood V of A(M̄) with A ∩ V ⊂ R(B ∩ U). Consequently the printed proofs of [ÁLKL23] Prop. 6.42 (exactness of the dual-conormal sequence at the boundary), of its "formally the same" transplant Prop. 8.8 (exactness of [ALKL]'s (1.3.2), with Cor. 7.11 replaced by the bounded retractivity of J(M, L) from Theorem 6.2(e)), and of the S2 note's row I-9 no longer consume Cor. 6.12 / Cor. 7.11.
*Proof.* Reduction to absolutely convex A: if A ⊂ A₁ with A₁ bounded and the statement holds for A₁ with B₁, then A ∩ V ⊂ A₁ ∩ V ⊂ R(B₁ ∩ U); the absolutely convex hull of a bounded set is bounded; so assume A absolutely convex, in particular 0 ∈ A. By Theorem 6.2(b) A(M̄) is boundedly retractive: A is contained and bounded in some step A^m(M̄), and the topology of A(M̄) coincides with that of A^m on A. Fix m′ < m (so A^m ⊂ A^{m′} continuously, (6.37)) and let E_{m′}: A^{m′}(M̄) → Ȧ^{(s)}(M̄) be the continuous linear partial extension map of Prop. 6.26 ([ALKL] Prop. 2.5.1), with RE_{m′} = 1 on A^{m′} ([ALKL] §2.5.15, definition). B := E_{m′}(A) is bounded in Ȧ^{(s)} (continuous image of a set bounded in A^m, hence in A^{m′}), hence bounded in Ȧ(M̄). Given a 0-neighborhood U of Ȧ(M̄), U ∩ Ȧ^{(s)} is a 0-neighborhood of Ȧ^{(s)} (continuous inclusion), so W := E_{m′}^{−1}(U ∩ Ȧ^{(s)}) is a 0-neighborhood of A^{m′}. The three topologies of A(M̄) ⊂ A^{m′} ⊂ A^m (as topologies on A^m: τ_A ≤ τ_{m′} ≤ τ_m) coincide on A, the outer two doing so by bounded retractivity. Since 0 ∈ A and W ∩ A is a τ_{m′}-neighborhood of 0 in A, it is a τ_A-neighborhood of 0 in A: there is a 0-neighborhood V of A(M̄) with V ∩ A ⊂ W ∩ A. For a ∈ A ∩ V we then have a ∈ W, so E_{m′}a ∈ U ∩ Ȧ^{(s)}, and E_{m′}a ∈ E_{m′}(A) = B; thus E_{m′}a ∈ B ∩ U and a = R(E_{m′}a) ∈ R(B ∩ U). ∎

*Remark 7.2.* The printed proof (p. 38) obtains a 0-neighborhood V of A(M̄) with V ∩ A^m = W ∩ A^m from Cor. 6.12; by Theorem 3.4 no such V exists in general (the whole-step coincidence fails), but the proof only ever uses V ∩ A, and on the bounded set A bounded retractivity supplies exactly that.
