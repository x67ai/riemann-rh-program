## Verdict on the external assessment

The assessment is correct in every endorsement it makes and in every factual claim it makes about the paper, and its one criticism is correct as a description of the three-page PDF and incorrect as a description of the mathematics. Concretely: all six statements it lists as refuted (Prop. 3.2, Cor. 3.4, Cor. 4.5, Cor. 6.14, Cor. 6.21, Cor. 7.13) are refuted by the note's witnesses exactly as printed, and two hostile referees per witness plus this adjudication re-derived each one; its account of the completeness objection and its three "explicitly says" dependency quotations are verbatim-accurate (one of the three is incomplete, not wrong); its public-record statement is true today. Its criticism that the §4 estimate (1) "needs a full proof" and that "exactly how (1) implies the topological coincidence criterion" is not shown is right about what the reader had in hand (§4 gives a 32-word indication of the proof and §5 says the derivations are "available on request") and wrong about the state of the mathematics: the companion's §9.2–9.5 prove (1) and its passage to the p. 4 criterion, §9.6–9.7 assemble the six LF-spaces, and four independent hostile passes (two per half) found no error and confirmed the constants numerically. The reader did not spot the one assertion in §4 that is genuinely unsupported as printed ("whose steps are Fréchet spaces", true but not argued for A^m(M) and J^m(M,L)); its proposed softening of the §1 sentence is therefore a defensible editorial choice for a note shipped without its appendix, not a mathematical necessity, and its wording ("appear recoverable") understates what is proved. The assessment is also incomplete: it never examines witnesses (c), (d) or (f), the second assertions of Cors. 3.4 and 6.14, the p. 18 bundle-extension sentence, the memoir, or the four §4 repairs that do not depend on (1), and it misdescribes the note's proved Cors. 6.27/7.22 as statements that "may remain true". Bottom line: yes, the assessment is correct on everything it checked, and the sponsor should not read it as covering the whole note.

## What the cross-check found about the note itself

Nothing mathematical in the note falls. Every witness (a)–(f) refutes exactly the printed statement it names, every quoted page, statement number and sentence is verbatim against the published text (including the three quotations that turn on glyphs pdftotext destroys: the two primes on p. 17, the unprimed A^m on pp. 48–49, the bold 𝒙 and ordinary M in (7.27)), and every dependency chain the note asserts is printed in the paper in those words. What follows is the complete list of what was found, item by item, with my ruling after re-derivation.

### A. Upheld: errors or gaps in the NOTE (repair before sending)

**N1 (substantive, the only mathematical gap). §4, "whose steps are Fréchet spaces".** Asserted for all six LF-spaces; the paper supports it only for I^m(M,L) (p. 22, "which becomes a Fréchet space"); p. 38 calls A^m(M) only an "LCS", and the Fréchet property would have come out of the broken Prop. 6.12 route. It is needed twice on p. 5 ([39, Prop. 6.4]: acyclic ⇔ boundedly/compactly/sequentially retractive; and "the above properties ... depend only on the LF-space X"), i.e. exactly where the note passes from acyclicity of ⋃_m A^m to the properties of A(M) and J(M,L). Both assembly referees found it; I confirm it. It is true, and two fills exist, both checked here: (i) cite Cor. 6.38 (p. 46: "A^m(M) ≡ x^m H_b^∞(M) ≡ x^{m+1/2} H^∞(M̊) (m ∈ R)") with Remark 6.41 (p. 47: the first identity "is independent of g" and holds "without the assumptions (A) and (B)"); §§6.19–6.21 do not cite any of the broken statements, so this is not circular; (ii) directly: the topology of A^m(M) is given by the countable family (6.41), and for a Cauchy sequence (u_ν) each Pu_ν is Cauchy in the Banach space x^m L^∞(M), which sits continuously in C^{−∞}(M) (p. 37); with P = 1 this gives u_ν → u in C^{−∞}(M), continuity of P on C^{−∞}(M) gives Pu = lim Pu_ν ∈ x^m L^∞(M) for every P, so u ∈ A^m(M) and u_ν → u there. J^m(M,L) ≅ A^m(𝑴) by (7.26) inherits it; Ȧ^m, 𝒦^m, K^m(M,L) are closed subspaces of Fréchet spaces. The collar models B^m_K and the symbol models S^m_K need no fill: the transfer rule uses only (M*) of the target spectrum (verify-assembly's narrowing, which I uphold against refute-assembly's broader demand). Repair: one parenthesis in §4 (wording in §3 below) and the same two lines in companion §9.7.

**N2 (wording that contradicts the note's own thesis). §4, Claim 6.46 clause:** "the absolutely convex hull of A is bounded in some A^m(M), on which the topologies of A(M), A^{m′}(M) and A^m(M) coincide by bounded retractivity". The nearest antecedent of "on which" is A^m(M), and read that way the sentence asserts coincidence of the three topologies on the whole step, which is the very statement witness (e) refutes. The intended antecedent is the hull (companion §10.1 says so unambiguously). Found by verify-assembly; upheld. Repair: "and on that hull the topologies ... coincide".

**N3 (loose paraphrase). §3(f), parenthetical** "(Sect. 4.3.3, p. 23, excepts acyclicity for non-compact M; the symbol statements do not.)" The printed sentence (p. 23, re-read here) is "Corollary 4.7 has extensions for ⋃_m I^m(M,L) and I_{·/c}(M,L), except acyclicity in the case of I(M,L)": acyclicity is excepted for one space, I(M,L), and is still claimed for ⋃_m I^m(M,L) and I_c(M,L). Refute-df and verify-df call the note's version imprecise; refute-record and verify-record call it exact. I side with df: the contrast the note draws (the paper saw a non-compactness issue in §4 and none in §3) is real and worth keeping, but the paraphrase invites the reply "we excepted it only for I(M,L)". Repair in §3 below. (Side observation from refute-df, outside the note's claims and not to be added to it: for non-compact L the paper's remaining claim that ⋃_m I^m(M,L) is acyclic looks exposed to the same escaping-support mechanism as (f); the sponsor may want to record it.)
