## 3. The seminorms (6.41) as printed on p. 38 — quote

Published p. 38–39 (verbatim): "Let { P_j | j ∈ N_0 } be a countable C^∞(M)-spanning set of Diff_b(M). The topology of A^m(M) can be described by the semi-norms ‖·‖_{k,m} (k ∈ N_0) given by ‖u‖_{k,m} = ‖P_k u‖_{x^m L^∞} = ess sup_M x^{−m}|P_k u| = sup_{M̊} x^{−m}|P_k u|, (6.41) using (6.33) in the last expression."

Hostile check: the note writes "the seminorm (6.41) with P = 1". Strictly, (6.41) is indexed by the chosen spanning set {P_j}, and the paper does not say P_0 = 1. This does not matter: (i) the topology is defined (p. 38) as the projective topology over ALL P ∈ Diff_b(M), which contains 1 (item 2), so sup_{M̊} x^{−m}|u| is a continuous seminorm on A^m(M) regardless of the choice of the P_j; (ii) if one insists on (6.41) as printed, a C^∞(M)-spanning set gives 1 = Σ_{j≤N} f_j P_j with f_j ∈ C^∞(M), whence sup_{M̊} x^{−m}|u| ≤ Σ_j ‖f_j‖_∞ ‖u‖_{j,m}, so the P = 1 seminorm is dominated by finitely many printed (6.41) seminorms. The note's parenthetical "(the topology of A^m(M) is the projective one over all P ∈ Diff_b(M), p. 38)" is exactly the justification (i). Ruling: HOLDS.

## 4. (6.42) as printed on p. 39; K compact in M̊, not in M; vanishing on u_j

Published p. 39 (verbatim): "From (2.1) and (6.33), we also get the continuous semi-norms ‖·‖_{K,k,m} (for any compact K ⊂ M̊ and k ∈ N_0) on A^m(M) given by ‖u‖_{K,k,m} = sup_K |P_k u|. (6.42)"

So K is compact in the INTERIOR M̊ as printed. Computation: x is continuous and > 0 on M̊, so x_K := min_K x > 0 for compact K ⊂ M̊. supp u_j ⊂ {1/(2j) ≤ x ≤ 2/j}; for j > 2/x_K the open set {x > 2/j} contains K and u_j ≡ 0 on it; differential operators are local, so P_k u_j ≡ 0 on K and ‖u_j‖_{K,k,m} = 0 for all k, m and all j > 2/x_K. Same argument gives u_j → 0 in C^∞(M̊) (topology (2.1) of uniform convergence of all derivatives on compacts of M̊).

Alternative reading attempted (to break the witness): if K were allowed to be compact in M (e.g. K = M), sup_M |P_k u| would not be a continuous seminorm on A^m(M) for m < 0 (x^m ∈ A^m(M) is unbounded), contradicting the paper's own "continuous semi-norms … on A^m(M)"; and for m > 0 the witness still works under that reading (all b-derivatives of u_j are O(j^{−m}) uniformly, since (x∂_x)^a ∂_y^b u_j = j^{−m} ((jx)^a χ^{(a)}(jx)) ∂_y^b g). The printed reading K ⊂ M̊ is the only consistent one and is the one the note uses. Ruling: HOLDS.

## 6. (6.41) with P = 1: sup_{M̊} x^{−m'}|u_j| ≥ 1, computed at x = 1/j

At the interior point (x, y) = (1/j, y_0) (in M̊ for j > 1/ε_0): x^{−m'}|u_j| = (1/j)^{−m'} · j^{−m'} · |χ(j · 1/j)| · |g(y_0)| = j^{m'} j^{−m'} · χ(1) · g(y_0) = 1 · 1 · 1 = 1. Hence sup_{M̊} x^{−m'}|u_j| ≥ 1 for every j (the exact value is sup_{t} t^{−m'} χ(t) · sup|g| ≥ 1, finite since supp χ ⊂ (1/2, 2)). Sign of m' is irrelevant. Ruling: HOLDS.

## 8. Direction of (6.38) and which index each assertion needs

Published p. 38 (verbatim): "Note that (6.37) yields a continuous inclusion A^m(M) ⊂ A^{m'}(M) (m' < m). (6.38)" — consistent with (6.37) x^m L^∞ ⊂ x^{m'} L^∞ for m' < m (p. 37) and with Example 6.11 (A^{−m}(S^l_+) ↔ S^m(R^l)): A^m(M) DEcreases as m increases.

Prop. 6.12 (p. 39, verbatim): "The semi-norms (6.42) and (6.43) together describe the topology of A^m(M)." — one space, one m. The note's "(with m' = m) u_j → 0 in the topology of (6.42) and (6.43) on A^m(M) but not in A^m(M)": u_j = j^{−m} χ(jx) g(y) ∈ A^m(M); all (6.42) seminorms → 0 (item 4), all (6.43) seminorms = 0 (item 5), so u_j → 0 in the locally convex topology generated by (6.42)+(6.43); but sup_{M̊} x^{−m}|u_j| ≥ 1 (item 6) and that seminorm is continuous on A^m(M) (items 2–3), so u_j ↛ 0 in A^m(M). If (6.42)+(6.43) described (= generated) the topology of A^m(M), every continuous seminorm of A^m(M) — in particular sup x^{−m}|·| — would be dominated by finitely many of them and would tend to 0 along u_j. Contradiction. Prop. 6.12 is refuted for every m ∈ R (including m ∈ N_0, in case the "m, m', k ∈ N_0" of Prop. 6.13 was meant to restrict it).

Cor. 6.14, first assertion (p. 39, verbatim, primes restored): "If m' < m, then the topologies of A^{m'}(M) and C^∞(M̊) coincide on A^m(M)." Here m' < m gives A^m(M) ⊂ A^{m'}(M) by (6.38): the claim is that the subspace topology induced on the SMALLER space A^m(M) by the LARGER space A^{m'}(M) equals the one induced by C^∞(M̊) via (6.33). The note's "(with m' < m) u_j → 0 in C^∞(M̊) but not in A^{m'}(M)": u_j = j^{−m'} χ(jx) g(y) ∈ A^m(M) (item 7), u_j → 0 in C^∞(M̊) (item 4), sup x^{−m'}|u_j| ≥ 1 with sup x^{−m'}|·| continuous on A^{m'}(M) (items 2, 6), so u_j ↛ 0 in A^{m'}(M). This refutes the first assertion exactly as printed, for every pair m' < m (in fact for every pair m', m). The exponent in u_j is −m' (the index of the space whose topology is tested), which is the right one: the weight x^{−m'} of the tested space is what must be beaten, and it is beaten exactly (value 1), while the C^∞(M̊) side does not see the weight at all.

Why a separate witness is needed for the second assertion: A^m ⊂ A^{m'} ⊂ A(M) continuously, so the A(M)-topology on A^m is coarser than the A^{m'}-topology on A^m, and u_j ↛ 0 in A^{m'} does NOT imply u_j ↛ 0 in A(M). Indeed u_j → 0 in A^{−k}(M) for every k > −m' (sup x^k |(x∂_x)^a ∂_y^b u_j| ≤ C j^{−m'−k} → 0), hence u_j → 0 in A(M). The note correctly switches to v_j. Ruling: HOLDS.

## 9. Second assertion of Cor. 6.14: v_j = e^j χ(jx) g(y), the W_k construction, and A(M) = ⋃_k A^{−k}(M) as an inductive limit

(a) The LF topology as the paper defines it. Published p. 4 (verbatim): "For any inductive/projective system (or spectrum) of continuous linear maps between LCSs, we have its (locally convex) inductive/projective limit; in particular, when the inductive/projective spectrum consists of a sequence of continuous inclusions, their union/intersection is endowed with the inductive/projective limit topology." And p. 5 (verbatim, re-read on the rendered page): "The above concepts and properties also apply to an inductive/projective spectrum consisting of continuous inclusions X_r ⊂ X_{r'} for r < r' in R because ⋂_r X_r = ⋂_k X_{r_k} and ⋃_r X_r = ⋃_k X_{s_k} for sequences r_k ↓ −∞ and s_k ↑ ∞." (The pdftotext extraction drops the ⋂/⋃ symbols on this line; the rendered page pairs ⋂ with r_k ↓ −∞ and ⋃ with s_k ↑ ∞, which is the correct pairing for an increasing spectrum — no slip in the paper.)
A(M) is defined in (6.29) (p. 36) as the LF-space ⋃_s A^{(s)}(M); p. 38 (verbatim): "Hence A(M) = ⋃_m A^m(M). (6.40) Despite of defining the same LF-space, the filtrations of A(M) defined by the spaces A^{(s)}(M) and A^m(M) are not equivalent". So the paper asserts that ind_m A^m(M) carries the topology of A(M); independently this follows from (6.39) A^{(s)} ⊂ A^m ⊂ A^{(min{m,0})} (m < s − n/2 − 1) with continuous inclusions: the two families are mutually cofinal with continuous inclusions, hence define the same inductive-limit topology. Bookkeeping for the sequence: the A^m increase as m decreases ((6.38): A^m ⊂ A^{m'} for m' < m), so put X_r := A^{−r}(M); then r < r' gives −r' < −r and X_r = A^{−r} ⊂ A^{−r'} = X_{r'}, an increasing spectrum as on p. 5, and ⋃_r X_r = ⋃_k X_{s_k} with s_k = k ↑ ∞, i.e. A(M) = ⋃_{k∈N} A^{−k}(M) = ind_k A^{−k}(M) topologically ({A^{−k}}_k is cofinal in {A^m}_m since A^m ⊂ A^{−k} whenever −k < m). The note's "A(M) = ⋃_k A^{−k}(M)" is exactly this, by the paper's own p. 5 remark. HOLDS.

(b) W_k is a 0-neighborhood of A^{−k}(M). W_k = { u ∈ A^{−k}(M) : sup_{M̊} x^{k}|u| < ε_k } with ε_k = ½ inf_{i≥1} e^i i^{−k}. sup_{M̊} x^{k}|u| = sup_{M̊} x^{−(−k)}|u| is the P = 1 seminorm of the projective topology of A^{−k}(M) (item 2), i.e. a (6.41)-type seminorm with m = −k; W_k is its open ε_k-ball, a 0-neighborhood. ε_k > 0: i ↦ e^i i^{−k} is positive on N and → ∞, so its infimum over i ≥ 1 is a positive minimum (for k = 0 it is e). HOLDS.

(c) W := absolutely convex hull of ⋃_k W_k is a 0-neighborhood of ind_k A^{−k}(M): standard (Schaefer II.6.4 / Meise–Vogt 24.7): the absolutely convex hulls of ⋃_k U_k, U_k ranging over 0-neighborhoods of the steps, form a 0-neighborhood base of the locally convex inductive limit; here W ∩ A^{−k} ⊇ W_k. HOLDS.

(d) v_j ∉ W. Elements of A^{−k}(M) are smooth on M̊ by (6.33) ("Ȧ(M)|_{M̊}, A(M) ⊂ C^∞(M̊)", p. 36), so pointwise evaluation at (1/j, y_0) ∈ M̊ is legitimate. Suppose v_j = Σ_i λ_i w_i (finite), Σ|λ_i| ≤ 1, w_i ∈ W_{k_i}. Then |w_i(1/j, y_0)| = (1/j)^{−k_i} · (x^{k_i}|w_i|)(1/j, y_0) ≤ j^{k_i} sup x^{k_i}|w_i| < j^{k_i} ε_{k_i} ≤ j^{k_i} · ½ e^j j^{−k_i} = ½ e^j (using inf_{i≥1} e^i i^{−k_i} ≤ e^j j^{−k_i}). Hence |v_j(1/j, y_0)| ≤ Σ|λ_i| |w_i(1/j, y_0)| < ½ e^j (if the sum is empty, v_j = 0, also impossible). But v_j(1/j, y_0) = e^j χ(1) g(y_0) = e^j. Contradiction. So v_j ∉ W for every j, hence v_j ↛ 0 in A(M). Meanwhile v_j ∈ C_c^∞(M̊) ⊂ ⋂_m A^m(M) (item 7) and v_j → 0 in C^∞(M̊) (item 4; the factor e^j is irrelevant since v_j vanishes identically on any given compact for j large). So on every A^m(M) the topologies of A(M) and C^∞(M̊) differ: the second assertion of Cor. 6.14 ("Therefore the topologies of A(M) and C^∞(M̊) coincide on A^m(M)") is false for every m. HOLDS.

(e) Does the second assertion's refutation depend on which cofinal sequence is used or on the A^{(s)} filtration? No: the topology of A(M) is a single topology (the paper's own "the same LF-space", and the cofinality argument), and the argument only uses that W is a 0-neighborhood of that topology. HOLDS.
