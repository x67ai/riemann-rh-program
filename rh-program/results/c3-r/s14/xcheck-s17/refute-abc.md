## Item 0. The printed definitions, verbatim, with sign conventions

All quotations from the published text (JPDOA 15 (2024) 47), page numbers as printed.

(3.1), p. 15 (text lines 815–817; confirmed on the rendered page image — the weight is a denominator):

    ‖a‖_{K,α,β,m} := sup_{x∈K, ξ∈R^l} |∂_x^α ∂_ξ^β a(x,ξ)| / (1+|ξ|)^{m−|β|} < ∞ .        (3.1)

So the (3.1) seminorm is sup |∂^α_x∂^β_ξ a| · (1+|ξ|)^{|β|−m}: a ξ-derivative of order |β| > m is weighted by a *growing* power (1+|ξ|)^{|β|−m}. This is the standard Hörmander convention and it is the one the note uses (the note writes the factor (1+|η+Ne_1|)^{|β|−m}, which is exactly 1/(1+|ξ|)^{m−|β|}).

"The set of symbols of order at most m, S^m(U × R^l), becomes a Fréchet space with the semi-norms ‖·‖_{K,α,β,m} given by (3.1)." (p. 15)

(3.4), p. 16: "According to (2.1) and (3.3), we get continuous semi-norms ‖·‖_{Q,C^k} on S^∞(U × R^l), for any compact Q ⊂ U × R^l and k ∈ N_0, given by ‖a‖_{Q,C^k} = sup_{(x,ξ)∈Q, |α|+|β|≤k} |∂_x^α ∂_ξ^β a(x,ξ)|." These are exactly the seminorms (2.1) of C^∞(U × R^l) (p. 5), so the topology generated by (3.4) alone on S^m is the C^∞(U × R^l)-topology restricted to S^m.

(3.5), p. 16: "With the notation of (3.1), consider also the continuous semi-norms ‖·‖′_{K,α,β,m} on S^m(U × R^l) given by ‖a‖′_{K,α,β,m} = sup_{x∈K} limsup_{|ξ|→∞} |∂_x^α ∂_ξ^β a(x,ξ)| / |ξ|^{m−|β|}."

Prop. 3.2, p. 16: "The semi-norms (3.4) and (3.5) together describe the topology of S^m(U × R^l)." Its proof (pp. 16–17) defines S′^m(U × R^l) as S^m "with the topology induced by the semi-norms (3.4) and (3.5) together", notes it is metrizable, takes the completion Ŝ′^m "where the stated semi-norms have continuous extensions", extends the inclusion S′^m ⊂ C^∞ to φ: Ŝ′^m → C^∞, and then (p. 17, lines 1–2): "For any a ∈ Ŝ′^m(U × R^l), and K, α and β like in (3.5), since ‖φ(a)‖′_{K,α,β,m} = ‖a‖′_{K,α,β,m} < ∞, there are C, R > 0 so that, if x ∈ K and |ξ| ≥ R, then |∂_x^α∂_ξ^β φ(a)(x,ξ)|/(1+|ξ|)^{m−|β|} ≤ C." … "This shows that ‖φ(a)‖_{K,α,β,m} < ∞, obtaining that a ≡ φ(a) ∈ S^m(U × R^l). Hence S′^m(U × R^l) is complete, and therefore it is a Fréchet space. Thus the identity map S^m(U × R^l) → S′^m(U × R^l) is a continuous linear isomorphism between Fréchet spaces, obtaining that it is indeed a homeomorphism by a version of the open mapping theorem". So the intended meaning of "describe the topology" is unambiguous: the identity S^m → S′^m is claimed to be a homeomorphism.

Cor. 3.4, p. 17: "For m < m′, the topologies of S^{m′}(U × R^l) and C^∞(U × R^l) coincide on S^m(U × R^l). Therefore the topologies of S^∞(U × R^l) and C^∞(U × R^l) coincide on S^m(U × R^l)." Proof: "The first assertion is a consequence of Propositions 3.2 and 3.3." Second assertion: "by (3.3), it is enough to show that the topology of S^∞(U × R^l) is finer or equal than the topology of C^∞(U × R^l) on S^m(U × R^l)", then uses the first assertion.

S^∞, p. 16: "giving rise to the LCSs S^∞(U × R^l) = ⋃_m S^m(U × R^l), S^{−∞}(U × R^l) = ⋂_m S^m(U × R^l). The LF-space S^∞(U × R^l) is a filtered algebra …". The topology is fixed by p. 4: "when the inductive/projective spectrum consists of a sequence of continuous inclusions, their union/intersection is endowed with the inductive/projective limit topology", and p. 5: "The above concepts and properties also apply to an inductive/projective spectrum consisting of continuous inclusions X_r ⊂ X_{r′} for r < r′ in R because ⋃_r X_r = ⋃_k X_{r_k} and ⋂_r X_r = ⋂_k X_{s_k} for sequences …". So S^∞ is the locally convex inductive limit of the S^m, m ∈ R, equivalently of the cofinal sequence S^k, k ∈ N (a locally convex topology on ⋃ S^m makes every S^m → S^∞ continuous iff it makes every S^k → S^∞, k ∈ N, continuous, because S^m → S^k is continuous for k ≥ m by (3.2); hence the finest such topologies coincide).

Continuity of (3.4) and (3.5) on S^m (needed so that S^m → S′^m is continuous and the refutation is a failure of the *reverse* direction): for Q ⊂ K × B̄(0,ρ), |∂^α_x∂^β_ξ a| ≤ ‖a‖_{K,α,β,m} (1+|ξ|)^{m−|β|} ≤ ‖a‖_{K,α,β,m} max{1,(1+ρ)^{m−|β|}} on Q; and |∂a|/|ξ|^{m−|β|} = [|∂a|/(1+|ξ|)^{m−|β|}]·((1+|ξ|)/|ξ|)^{m−|β|} with the last factor → 1, so ‖a‖′_{K,α,β,m} ≤ ‖a‖_{K,α,β,m}. The paper itself calls both families continuous (p. 16). HOLDS.

## Item 1. Witness (a): g_N(x,ξ) = θ(ξ − Ne_1). Prop. 3.2 and the first assertion of Cor. 3.4

Setting: U ⊂ R^n open, nonempty, n ≥ 0; l ≥ 1 (needed for e_1 to exist; for l = 0 the statements are vacuous); θ ∈ C_c^∞(R^l), θ(0) = 1, supp θ ⊂ B(0,r); N ∈ N; K ⊂ U compact, nonempty.

(1a) g_N ∈ S^{−∞}. For every m ∈ R, α, β, K: if α ≠ 0 then ∂_x^α g_N ≡ 0 and the (3.1) seminorm is 0. If α = 0, |∂_ξ^β g_N(x,ξ)|(1+|ξ|)^{|β|−m} = |∂^βθ(ξ−Ne_1)|(1+|ξ|)^{|β|−m}, which vanishes for |ξ − Ne_1| ≥ r and is a continuous function on the compact set {|ξ−Ne_1| ≤ r}, hence bounded. So ‖g_N‖_{K,α,β,m} < ∞ for all (K,α,β,m): g_N ∈ ⋂_m S^m = S^{−∞} ⊂ S^m for every m. HOLDS.

(1b) Every (3.4) seminorm of g_N vanishes for N large. A compact Q ⊂ U × R^l lies in K × B̄(0,ρ) with K = pr_1(Q), ρ = max_Q |ξ|. Every ξ ∈ supp(g_N(x,·)) ⊂ B(Ne_1, r) has |ξ| > N − r. If N > ρ + r, then N − r > ρ, so g_N and all its derivatives vanish on Q, i.e. ‖g_N‖_{Q,C^k} = 0 for every k. Since the (3.4) seminorms are the seminorms (2.1) of C^∞(U × R^l), this is also the statement g_N → 0 in C^∞(U × R^l). HOLDS.

(1c) Every (3.5) seminorm of g_N vanishes (for every N, not only large N). For fixed x and any α, β, ∂_x^α∂_ξ^β g_N(x,ξ) = 0 for |ξ| ≥ N + r, so limsup_{|ξ|→∞} |∂_x^α∂_ξ^β g_N(x,ξ)|/|ξ|^{m−|β|} = 0 for each x, and the sup over x ∈ K is 0. HOLDS.

Hence g_N → 0 in S′^m (every defining seminorm of S′^m is eventually 0 on g_N).

(1d) The (3.1) seminorm with |β| > m blows up — re-derived from the printed definition. With α = 0, by (3.1) as printed (division by (1+|ξ|)^{m−|β|}):

    ‖g_N‖_{K,0,β,m} = sup_{x∈K} sup_{ξ∈R^l} |∂^βθ(ξ−Ne_1)| (1+|ξ|)^{|β|−m}
                    = sup_{η∈R^l} |∂^βθ(η)| (1+|η+Ne_1|)^{|β|−m}            (η = ξ − Ne_1; g_N is x-independent)
                    = sup_{|η|≤r} |∂^βθ(η)| (1+|η+Ne_1|)^{|β|−m}            (the integrand is 0 for |η| ≥ r).

For |η| ≤ r and N ≥ r: |η + Ne_1| ≥ N − |η| ≥ N − r ≥ 0. If |β| − m > 0 the map t ↦ (1+t)^{|β|−m} is increasing on [0,∞), so (1+|η+Ne_1|)^{|β|−m} ≥ (1+N−r)^{|β|−m} on the whole support, and therefore

    ‖g_N‖_{K,0,β,m} ≥ (1+N−r)^{|β|−m} · sup_{|η|≤r} |∂^βθ(η)| = (1+N−r)^{|β|−m} sup|∂^βθ|,

which is the note's displayed bound, verbatim. It tends to ∞ as N → ∞ exactly when |β| > m and ∂^βθ ≢ 0. (Numerical sanity check with l = 1, θ(t) = e·exp(−1/(1−t²)), β = 1, m ∈ {0, 0.5, −1}: the printed-convention seminorm exceeds the bound at N = 2, 5, …, 80 and grows like N^{1−m}; with the exponent sign reversed it would decay like N^{m−1}, so the witness is sensitive to the convention and the convention was checked on the rendered page image.) HOLDS.

(1e) Existence, in every order, of β with ∂^βθ ≢ 0. Fix k ∈ N_0 and suppose ∂^βθ ≡ 0 on R^l for every |β| = k. Then (R^l being connected; induction on k via Taylor's formula with integral remainder, or directly: all k-th partials vanish ⟹ all (k−1)-th partials are constant ⟹ … ) θ is a polynomial of degree ≤ k − 1. A polynomial vanishing on the nonempty open set R^l ∖ supp θ vanishes identically, contradicting θ(0) = 1. So for every k there is β with |β| = k and ∂^βθ ≢ 0; in particular for every m ∈ R there is β with |β| > m and ∂^βθ ≢ 0. HOLDS.

(1f) The refutations. (i) Prop. 3.2: if (3.4)+(3.5) described the topology of S^m, the identity S′^m → S^m would be continuous, so g_N → 0 in S′^m would force g_N → 0 in S^m, hence (g_N) bounded in S^m; but by (1d) sup_N ‖g_N‖_{K,0,β,m} = ∞ for any |β| > m with ∂^βθ ≢ 0. Contradiction. Prop. 3.2 is FALSE for every m ∈ R, every nonempty open U ⊂ R^n (n ≥ 0, including U = {0}, i.e. S^m(R^l)), every l ≥ 1. (ii) First assertion of Cor. 3.4: for m < m′, g_N ∈ S^{−∞} ⊂ S^m, g_N → 0 in C^∞(U × R^l) by (1b), while for |β| > m′ with ∂^βθ ≢ 0, ‖g_N‖_{K,0,β,m′} → ∞ by (1d), so g_N ↛ 0 in S^{m′}. The topologies of S^{m′} and C^∞ do not coincide on S^m. FALSE for every m < m′ (indeed for all m, m′). (iii) "Strictly coarser": S^m → S′^m is continuous (Item 0) and not open by (i). HOLDS.

Attempted rescues that fail. (α) "Prop. 3.2 means (3.4)+(3.5) together with (3.1)": excluded by the printed proof, which defines S′^m by (3.4) and (3.5) alone and asserts the identity S^m → S′^m is a homeomorphism (p. 17). (β) "The order of sup_{x∈K} and limsup in (3.5) could be different": irrelevant, g_N is independent of x. (γ) "K = ∅ or U = ∅": K is a nonempty compact subset of a nonempty U; the seminorm with K = ∅ is the zero seminorm and refutes nothing either way. (δ) "Restrict to m, m′ ∈ N_0 as in Prop. 3.3": the witness works for every real m, m′, and the displayed statements of Prop. 3.2 and Cor. 3.4 carry no such restriction.

Ruling on witness (a): HOLDS. (Prop. 3.2 FALLS; first assertion of Cor. 3.4 FALLS.)
