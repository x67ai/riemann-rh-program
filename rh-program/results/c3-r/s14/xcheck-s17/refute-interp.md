## Item 1 — Lemma A (companion §9.2, one variable, Vandermonde)

**As written.** N ≥ 1, f ∈ C^N([t, t+Nh]), h > 0. For 0 ≤ j ≤ N: |f^{(j)}(t)| ≤ C_N (h^{−j} ‖f‖_∞ + h^{N−j} ‖f^{(N)}‖_∞), sup norms over [t, t+Nh].

**Re-derivation (independent of the companion's wording).** For k = 0, …, N−1 the nodes t+kh lie in [t, t+(N−1)h] ⊂ [t, t+Nh]. Lagrange-remainder Taylor at t (needs exactly f ∈ C^N on [t, t+kh]): f(t+kh) = Σ_{i=0}^{N−1} f^{(i)}(t)(kh)^i/i! + r_k, |r_k| ≤ (kh)^N sup|f^{(N)}|/N! ≤ (Nh)^N ‖f^{(N)}‖/N!. With y_i := f^{(i)}(t) h^i/i! this is the N×N linear system V y = (f(t+kh) − r_k)_k, V = (k^i)_{0≤k,i≤N−1}, the Vandermonde matrix in the distinct nodes 0, 1, …, N−1, so det V = ∏_{0≤p<q≤N−1}(q−p) ≠ 0 and V^{−1} depends only on N. Hence |y_j| ≤ ‖V^{−1}‖_{∞→∞} max_k |f(t+kh) − r_k| ≤ ‖V^{−1}‖(‖f‖ + (Nh)^N‖f^{(N)}‖/N!), and f^{(j)}(t) = j! h^{−j} y_j gives |f^{(j)}(t)| ≤ j!‖V^{−1}‖ (h^{−j}‖f‖ + N^N h^{N−j}‖f^{(N)}‖/N!). The constant C_N := max_{j<N} j!‖V^{−1}‖·max(1, N^N/N!) depends on N only — not on h, t, or f. For j = N the claim is |f^{(N)}(t)| ≤ ‖f^{(N)}‖, trivial. So Lemma A is correct exactly as stated, including the one-sidedness of the interval (which is what lets the box in 9.4 and the ϱ-box in 9.6 be placed to the right of the base point).

## Item 5 — §9.5: from (interp) to the criterion of p. 4, and the transfer rules

**The published criterion, verbatim (p. 4; pdftotext drops the primes, restored in brackets):** "for an inductive spectrum of LCSs of the form (X_k) = (X_0 ⊂ X_1 ⊂ ⋯), the condition of being acyclic can be described as follows [39, Theorem 6.1]: for all k, there is some k[′] ≥ k such that, for all k[″] ≥ k[′], the topologies of X_k[′] and X_k[″] coincide on some 0-neighborhood of X_k." And p. 5: "If the steps X_k are Fréchet spaces, the above properties of (X_k) depend only on the LF-space X [39, Chapter 6, p. 111] … X is acyclic if and only if it is boundedly/compactly/sequentially retractive [39, Proposition 6.4]. As a consequence, acyclic LF-spaces are complete and regular [39, Corollary 6.5]." Real indices (p. 5): "The above concepts and properties also apply to an inductive/projective spectrum consisting of continuous inclusions X_r ⊂ X_r[′] for r < r[′] in ℝ because ⋃_r X_r = ⋃_k X_{r_k} and ⋂_r X_r = ⋂_k X_{s_k} for sequences r_k ↓ −∞ and s_k ↑ ∞."

**(M*) at (m, m′, m″), checked.** V := {a ∈ S^m_K : N_m(a;0) < 1} is a basic 0-neighborhood of S^m_K. Let O ⊇ {N_{m′}(·;γ_i) < ε, i ≤ p} (every 0-neighborhood of S^{m′}_K contains such a set; a common ε is no loss). For each i take C_i, R_{0,i}, Γ_i from 9.4; choose R ≥ max_i R_{0,i} with C_i R^{−(m′−m)/2} < ε/2 (possible as m′ > m), then δ > 0 with C_i R^{m″−m′} δ < ε/2, and W := {b ∈ S^{m″}_K : N_{m″}(b;γ′) < δ, γ′ ∈ ⋃_i Γ_i} — a 0-neighborhood of S^{m″}_K since ⋃_iΓ_i is finite. For a ∈ V ∩ W: a ∈ S^m_K ⊂ S^{m″}_K, so 9.4 applies and N_{m′}(a;γ_i) < C_i[R^{−(m′−m)/2}·1 + R^{m″−m′}δ] < ε for each i, i.e. a ∈ O. ✓ (The companion's "R ≥ R_0" should read R ≥ max_i R_{0,i}; trivial.)

**(M*) ⟹ the topologies of S^{m′}_K and S^{m″}_K coincide on V, checked.** The inclusion S^{m′}_K → S^{m″}_K is continuous ((3.2); all N-seminorms non-increasing in the order), so on V the S^{m″}-topology is coarser than or equal to the S^{m′}-topology. Converse: fix a ∈ V and a 0-neighborhood O of S^{m′}_K; ½O is a 0-neighborhood, so (M*) gives a balanced 0-neighborhood W_0 of S^{m″}_K with V ∩ W_0 ⊂ ½O. If b ∈ V and b − a ∈ W_0, then (b−a)/2 = ½b + ½(−a) ∈ V because V is absolutely convex (the open unit ball of a seminorm), and (b−a)/2 ∈ W_0 because W_0 is balanced; hence (b−a)/2 ∈ ½O, b ∈ a + O. So (a + W_0) ∩ V ⊂ (a + O) ∩ V: every S^{m′}-neighborhood of a in V contains an S^{m″}-neighborhood of a in V. ✓

**Match with the quoted criterion.** Reindex by the p. 5 sentence: (S^m_K)_{m∈ℝ} and (S^k_K)_{k∈ℕ} (or any m_k ↑ ∞) have the same union and the same inductive topology (cofinal subsequence). For every k take k′ = k+1; for every k″ ≥ k′ the topologies of S^{k′}_K and S^{k″}_K coincide on V = {N_k(·;0) < 1}, a 0-neighborhood of S^k_K; k″ = k′ is trivial. The quantifier order of the quoted condition is ∀k ∃k′ ∀k″ ∃V, so V is allowed to depend on k″; here V is independent of k″ (it involves only m = k) — the companion therefore establishes the stronger Retakh-type condition ∀k ∃k′ ∃V ∀k″, and so satisfies the criterion whichever of these forms Theorem 6.1 of [39] carries (I could not consult [39] itself; the repo holds no copy). With Fréchet steps (S^m_K is closed in the Fréchet space S^m(U×ℝ^l), p. 15; directly: a Cauchy sequence converges locally uniformly with all derivatives and the weighted bounds pass to the limit), p. 5 gives boundedly/compactly/sequentially retractive, complete, regular. ✓

**Transfer rules, each written out.**
(a) Constant spectrum X_k = X: V := X, and for O take W := O; V ∩ W = O ⊂ O. ✓
(b) Finite products (X_k × Y_k)_k with both factors satisfying (M*) at (k,k′,k″): V := V_X × V_Y is a 0-neighborhood of X_k × Y_k; a 0-neighborhood O of X_{k′} × Y_{k′} contains some O_X × O_Y; with W := W_X × W_Y, V ∩ W = (V_X ∩ W_X) × (V_Y ∩ W_Y) ⊂ O_X × O_Y ⊂ O. ✓ Absolute convexity of V is inherited. (c) Initial topology of a fixed linear map j: Y_k → X_k, j(Y_k) ⊂ X_k, Y_k ⊂ Y_{k′} ⊂ Y_{k″}, each Y_k carrying the initial topology of j into X_k (basic 0-neighborhoods j^{−1}(O_X) ∩ Y_k).
