## §0. CLASSIFICATION AND VERDICT

**CLASSIFICATION: INSTRUMENT** — with two named DEAD-ENDs for specializations of S4′, and one clause rewritten.

**Verdict.** S4′ is not a program invention and not unstudied: it is **Leichtnam's printed conjecture**
(Contemp. Math. 387 (2005) §5, "a conjecture stating the existence of a Riemannian laminated foliated space
(S̄_Q, F) endowed with a flow φ^t and satisfying certain geometric properties" — zbMATH review, fetched
verbatim; and Bull. Sci. Math. 131 (2007) p. 2, "the existence of such a quadruple (S_Q, F, g, φ^t) is still
unknown"), whose printed shape S_Q = (L × R^{>0})/Q^{>0} ∪ L/Q^{>0} matches all four clauses, with clause (ii)
Deninger's own prescription ([Den05] pp. 32–33) and clause (iii) the printed χ(L) weight in the only proved
trace formula (ALKL Thm. 1.3.10). **Nobody has constructed it, in any class, and no printed theorem kills it
as posed.** But print constrains it very sharply, and in two directions the constraint is fatal. (1) **The
closed-3-manifold class is dead twice over.** Derivation D-I (from Kim–Morishita–Noda–Terashima's decomposition
theorem and period group, and independently from the ALKL memoir's cutting construction) shows the group
generated by the closed-orbit lengths of *any* foliated flow on a closed 3-manifold is **finitely generated**,
while ⟨log p : p⟩ has infinite rank — this kills **clause (i)** there, strengthening [Den05] p. 23 Remark 3,
which proved it only for fixed-point-free flows and left fixed points as the hoped-for escape. Derivation D-II
shows the archimedean term (1 − e^{−2t})^{−1} demands Σ_{κ_L = −2} χ(L) = 1, while ALKL prove the preserved
leaves on a closed manifold are **compact** and holomorphy makes their χ **even** — this kills **clause (iii)**
there, on parity. (2) **Clause (iv) as posed is wrong and must be rewritten.** Derivation D-III shows the
transverse measure of an S4′ object cannot be flow-invariant: it must be **scaled**, φ^t_*µ = e^{−t}µ, with
the return map at γ_p scaling it by exactly 1/p — which is precisely Leichtnam's printed axiom
(Jac M_q = 1/q against g̃(q_*u) = q g̃(u)), and precisely why a manifold cannot work (there the transverse
measure |ω_φ| is flow-invariant, forcing α = 0). The forced transversal is a totally disconnected group with a
Q^{>0}-action of modulus 1/q — canonically the finite adeles. (3) **The one clause nobody has ever satisfied is
clause (i)**, and derivation D-IV reduces it, for the entire printed suspension shape, to a **purely
group-theoretic** condition with no analysis in it: the Q^{>0}-space L must have isotropy groups exactly {1}
and the p^Z, with **one orbit per prime**. That is what Deninger means by "too many periodic orbits" ([x-06]
p. 11), it is what defeats Connes–Consani's X_Q as well (D-IV), and it is decidable on its own. **Net for the
program: the substrate must be a compact non-manifold foliated space with a scaling transverse measure, a
non-compact archimedean leaf conformal to C, and an infinite-rank period group — and in that class there is at
present no trace formula in print at all (ALKL's is for closed manifolds, and they say so). Standing order 6
therefore does not yet license a construction attempt: the cheap decider, Q-S4″(a,b) of §5, has not been run.**

**One-line summary of what changed.** Before this sweep the program held S4′ as "Deninger's/Leichtnam's open
problem, sharpened by four clauses, with nothing in print constraining it beyond the rank-1 dichotomy". After
it: three of the four clauses have printed sources (ii) or printed weights (iii) or printed corrections (iv);
the manifold class is provably dead on two independent grounds; the archimedean leaf is pinned to a euclidean
plane; and clause (i) is reduced to a question about isotropy in a Q^{>0}-space.

### A. DIRECT — has anyone built a foliated dynamical system whose primitive closed orbits are the primes with lengths log p?

**A1. Deninger, *Arithmetic geometry and analysis on foliated spaces*, Arizona Winter School / [Den05]
= on disk `fetched/x-20-…pdf`. READ pp. 19–35.** This is the source of the S4′ desideratum and of
three of its four clauses.

  terms it would be very desirable to find a system (X, φ^t, F) which actually realizes this
  correspondence. For this the class of compact 3-manifolds as phase spaces has to be generalized as
  will become clear from the following discussion."
- p. 26, the dictionary the system must realize: "finite place p ≙ closed orbit γ = γ_p not contained
  in a leaf and hence transversal to F such that l(γ_p) = log N p and ε_{γ_p}(k) = 1 for all k ≥ 1";

- p. 32–33 (clause (ii), verbatim, the "Fact"): "In the situation of the preceeding remark, ε_k(γ) = +1
  for all k ∈ Z∖0 if and only if on T_xF we have: T_xφ^{k l(γ)} = e^{(k/2) l(γ)}·O_k for O_k ∈ SO(T_xF).
  For fixed points, ε_x = 1 is automatic and we have: T_xφ^t = e^{t/2} O_t for O_t ∈ SO(T_xF)." And:
  "In the number theoretical case the eigenvalues of T_xφ^{log N p} on T_xF for x ∈ γ_p would therefore

  dynamical system corresponding to number theory we must have α = 1. … However as mentioned before,
  this is not possible in the manifold setting of corollary 5.5 which actually implies α = 0."
- p. 23, **Corollary 5.5 Remark 3 (verbatim) — the printed length-group obstruction**: "One can show
  that the group generated by the lengths of closed orbits is a finitely generated subgroup of R under
  the assumptions of the corollary. In order to achieve an infinitely generated group the flow must
  have fixed points."  Cor. 5.5's assumptions, read at p. 23: *X a compact 3-manifold, F a foliation

  be isometric with respect to g. We have chosen to leave the α in the fomulation since there are good

  α ≠ 0 becomes possible i.e. to Sullivan's generalized solenoids."
- p. 33, Remark 7) — **a printed constraint on the leaves for K = Q**: "If there does exist a foliated
  dynamical system attached to spec o_K … we would expect in particular that for a preferred transverse
  measure µ we have: χ_Co(F, µ) = − log |d_{K/Q}|. … In the unramified case, |d_{K/Q}| = 1 we must have

  either a plane, a torus or a cylinder c.f. [9]." ([9] = Candel.)  For K = Q, d = 1, so **the S4′
  object must have χ_Co(F, ν) = 0 for every transverse measure and must contain a leaf that is a plane,
  a torus or a cylinder.** Of those three, only the plane has χ ≠ 0 — so clause (iii)'s archimedean
  leaf, if it is one of Candel's three, is a **plane** (χ = 1). *(The identification of the χ ≠ 0 leaf
  with the plane is my inference from Deninger's printed sentence, not printed.)*
- p. 26, Fact 6.1 (Connes, printed here): "Let F be a foliation of a compact 3-manifold by surfaces such
  that the union of the compact leaves has µ-measure zero, then χ_Co(F, µ) ≤ 0", with the printed proof
  χ_Co = −β_1(F, µ).
- pp. 34–36, §7.7 and **Theorem 7.8** — the one solved case: X = M̄ ×_Λ R for an unramified covering
  f : M → M, Λ = lZ; closed orbits ↔ finite f-orbits, l(γ) = |γ_M| l; χ_Co(F, µ) = χ(M)·l. Example
  p. 36: E/F_p ordinary elliptic curve, l = log p, X ≅ (C ×_Γ T_πΓ) ×_{p^Z} R^*_+, and "the metric g on
  TF given by g_{[z,y,t]}(ξ, η) = e^t Re(ξη̄) … satisfies the conformality condition (20) for α = 1."
  **What it gives:** clause (ii) is realized (α = 1) on a genuine non-manifold solenoid; clause (iv) is
  realized (canonical transverse measure µ, and χ_Co(F,µ) = χ(M)·l). **What it does not give:** the
  length group is (log p)Z — rank 1, one prime — so clause (i) fails by an infinite margin, and there
  are no fixed points, so clause (iii) fails. Deninger's own verdict, p. 36: "The construction of
  (X, φ^t) that we made for ordinary elliptic curves is misleading however, since it almost never
  happens that a variety in characteristic p can be lifted to characteristic zero together with its
  Frobenius endomorphism."

### B. INVERSE PROBLEMS — which sets can be the primitive-period set of a flow?

**B1. Huub de Jong, *On sets of periodic orbit lengths in finitely presented dynamical systems*,
Ergodic Theory and Dynamical Systems, First View, pp. 1–33, published online 15 June 2026,
DOI 10.1017/etds.2026.10313 (page fetched from cambridge.org this session).** Abstract, verbatim:
"We classify the sets of natural numbers n for which certain dynamical systems (X,f) on a compact
metric space X have a periodic point of (least) period n. … Given a system for which the logarithmic
derivative of the Artin–Mazur zeta function is rational, we use the Skolem–Mahler–Lech theorem to
classify for which n the system has a periodic point of (not necessarily least) period n. Moreover,
we build on work on finitely presented (FP) systems and their relationship to symbolic dynamics to
classify the set of least periods, that is, periodic orbit lengths, for arbitrary FP systems, extending
a known classification for shifts of finite type. We also provide several constructions to realize any
such least period sets."  Result as stated on the Cambridge page: for finitely presented systems the
least-period set has the form F ∪ ⋃_{i=1}^n d_i S_i with F finite and each S_i cofinite; for mixing
systems it is {1} or cofinite.
**What it gives:** this is the closest printed *inverse period-set* classification the sweep found, and
it is a **positive realization theorem** — every set of the stated form is realized. **What it does not
give:** it is discrete time, integer periods, and the FP (Smale-space/symbolic) class. It says nothing
about which subsets of R_{>0} are primitive-period sets of *flows*; and S4′'s spectrum is not a period
set of an FP system in this sense — the FP dichotomy (finite ∪ cofinite) has no bearing on a continuous-
time length set. **It does not constrain S4′.** *(This last judgment is mine.)*

built to be *foliation-destroying*. **Verdict:** flexible in the ambient category, silent in the foliated
category — which is exactly where S4′ lives.

**B4. Riemann-surface laminations — É. Ghys, *Laminations par surfaces de Riemann*, Panoramas et
Synthèses 8 (1999) 49–95 (located this session at perso.ens-lyon.fr/ghys/articles/laminationssurfaces.pdf

Candel's printed theorems (verbatim):
 - p. 490: "THEOREM. — Let M be a compact oriented surface lamination with a riemannian metric g. Then
   χ(M, µ) < 0 for every positive invariant transverse measure if and only if g is conformal to a metric
   of curvature −1. In particular, this holds true if M has no invariant measure."
 - p. 490: "THEOREM. — Let M be a compact, connected, oriented surface lamination with a nontrivial
   invariant transverse measure and let g be a riemannian metric on M. Then χ(M, µ) > 0 for every positive
   invariant transverse measure if and only if the metric g is conformal to a metric of curvature 1.
   Furthermore, M is a two-sphere bundle over a metric space and the fibers are the leaves of M."
 - p. 497, **Theorem 4.3**: "If χ(M, µ) < 0 for every positive invariant transverse measure µ, then all
   leaves are hyperbolic Riemann surfaces. In fact, if L is a euclidean leaf, then there exists µ with

second theorem (it would make X a two-sphere bundle with all leaves spheres, incompatible with a dense
leaf and infinitely many transverse closed orbits); torus and cylinder have χ = 0. **So clause (iii)'s
archimedean leaf is conformal to the euclidean plane, χ = 1.** *(The exclusion of the sphere and the
identification of the χ ≠ 0 leaf as the plane are my inferences from the two printed theorems.)*

### C. RIGIDITY FROM PERIODIC DATA — does "every closed orbit has leafwise exponent 1/2" force anything?

**C1. B. Kalinin, V. Sadovskaya, *Linear cocycles over hyperbolic systems and criteria of conformality*,
arXiv:1008.2563, J. Modern Dynamics 4 (2010) no. 3, 419–441 (abstract fetched verbatim this session).**
Abstract, verbatim: "In this paper we study Holder continuous linear cocycles over transitive Anosov
diffeomorphisms. Under various conditions of relative pinching we establish properties including
existence and continuity of measurable invariant sub-bundles and conformal structures. We use these
results to obtain criteria for cocycles to be isometric or conformal in terms of their periodic data.
We show that if the return maps at the periodic points are, in a sense, conformal or isometric then so
is the cocycle itself with respect to a Holder continuous Riemannian metric."
**What it gives:** the exact shape of clause (ii) — "the return maps at the periodic points are conformal"
— is a *known sufficient condition* for the cocycle to be globally conformal with respect to a continuous
metric. Deninger's global conformality (31)/(20) with α = 1 is therefore **not an extra axiom beyond
clause (ii)** in the hyperbolic category: it is what clause (ii) buys. That is a genuinely useful design
fact for the program. **What it does not give:** the theorem's base is a *transitive Anosov
diffeomorphism*; the S4′ flow is not Anosov (see §2 row L6), its closed orbits are isolated with empty
stable bundle, and it has a preserved leaf. So this is an INSTRUMENT with an unverified hypothesis, not a
theorem about S4′. *(This scoping judgment is mine.)*

**C2. Periodic-data rigidity (de la Llave–Marco–Moriyón; Gogolev; Kalinin–Sadovskaya; Gogolev–Rodriguez
Hertz; Gogolev's CIRM lecture notes; arXiv:2606.29603 "Global periodic-data rigidity for irreducible toral
automorphisms"; arXiv:2210.16702; arXiv:1808.06249 — located this session, NOT OPENED).** All of these are
**local or global rigidity statements for perturbations of an algebraic model** (hyperbolic toral
automorphisms, Anosov diffeomorphisms): equal periodic data ⟹ smooth conjugacy to the model. **There is no

### D. COUNTING AND ZETA CONSTRAINTS

**D1. The Ruelle zeta identity.** [x-06] p. 7, verbatim (read this session): "if we had a bijection p ↔ γ

required orbit growth is precisely that of an entropy-1 hyperbolic flow. This is the well-known content of
the analogy, and the sweep found nothing that turns it into a constraint. **Verdict: no obstruction from
counting.** *(Mine; the Parry–Pollicott asymptotic is stated from the standard literature and is NOT read

### D-I. Derivation: the length group is finitely generated on a closed 3-manifold (mine; printed antecedent [Den05] p. 23 Rem. 3)

Let (M, F, φ) be a 3-dimensional foliated dynamical system in the sense of Kim–Morishita–Noda–Terashima,
*On 3-dimensional foliated dynamical systems and Hilbert type reciprocity law*, arXiv:1906.02424, Münster
J. Math. 14 (2) (2021) (PDF downloaded and read this session): M a closed 3-manifold, F a 2-dimensional
foliation by Riemann surfaces, φ^t mapping leaves to leaves. Write L_∞ for the union of the preserved
leaves and M_0 = M ∖ L_∞.

1. KMNT Definition 1.11 (read): "We call the smooth closed 1-form in Lemma 1.10 the canonical 1-form of S
   and denote it by ω_S. … the period homomorphism [ω_S] : H_1(M_0; Z) → R; [ℓ] ↦ ∫_ℓ ω_S, and the period
   group of S is defined by the image of [ω_S], which we denote by Λ_S."  Lemma 1.10's ω_S satisfies
   ω_S|TF = 0 and ω_S(φ̇) = 1 (KMNT Lemma 1.12, read).
2. Every closed orbit c of φ lies in M_0, and ∫_c ω_S = ∫_0^{ℓ(c)} ω_S(φ̇) dt = ℓ(c). Hence **ℓ(c) ∈ Λ_S**
   for every closed orbit c.
3. KMNT Corollary 2.2.4 (read, verbatim): "III. P_S^∞ is a non-empty (**finite**) set." So L_∞ is a finite
   union of compact leaves and M_0 has finitely many connected components X_1, …, X_d.
4. KMNT Theorem 2.2.2 (read, verbatim): "The foliated 3-manifold (X_a, F|X_a) is one of the followings.
   (1) X_a is a surface bundle over S^1 or an open interval, and F|X_a is the bundle foliation. (2) X_a is a
   surface-bundle over S^1 and any leaf in F|X_a is dense in X_a."  In every case X_a is homotopy equivalent
   to a finite CW complex (a surface bundle over S^1 is compact; a surface bundle over an open interval
   deformation retracts to a fibre), so **H_1(X_a; Z) is a finitely generated abelian group**.
5. Hence H_1(M_0; Z) = ⊕_{a=1}^d H_1(X_a; Z) is finitely generated, and **Λ_S = [ω_S](H_1(M_0; Z)) is a
   finitely generated subgroup of R**, so rank Λ_S < ∞.
6. {log p : p prime} is Z-linearly independent: if Σ_p n_p log p = 0 with n_p ∈ Z almost all zero, then
   Π_p p^{n_p} = 1, so all n_p = 0 by unique factorization. Hence ⟨log p : p⟩ ⊂ R has **infinite rank**.
7. By 2, 5, 6: **no 3-dimensional foliated dynamical system on a closed 3-manifold has closed orbits of
   length log p for infinitely many primes p.** ∎

### D-II. Derivation: the archimedean weight has the wrong parity on a closed manifold (mine)

Hypotheses: (M, F, φ) as in the ALKL memoir — M a closed manifold, F transversely oriented of codimension
one with 2-dimensional leaves carrying a leafwise holomorphic structure, φ a foliated flow whose closed
orbits are simple and whose preserved leaves are transversely simple.

1. ALKL memoir p. 7 (read, verbatim): the preserved-leaf part of the Lefschetz distribution is
   "L_dis,K,K′(φ) = Σ_L χ(L)/|e^{κ_L t} − 1|", extended over t = 0 to Σ_L χ(L) W_L (Theorem 1.3.10, read).
2. The target, for spec Z, is the archimedean term of the Weil explicit formula, [x-18] p. 4 formula (2)
   (read, verbatim): "1 − Σ_ρ e^{tρ} + e^t = Σ_p log p Σ_{k≥1} δ_{k log p} + **(1 − e^{−2t})^{−1}**", i.e.
   the function t ↦ (1 − e^{−2t})^{−1} on t > 0 with coefficient **1**.
3. On t > 0 the functions t ↦ |e^{κt} − 1|^{−1}, κ ∈ R^×, are R-linearly independent (their behaviour as
   t ↓ 0 is 1/(|κ| t) + O(1), separating |κ|; their behaviour as t ↑ ∞ is e^{−|κ|t}(1 + o(1)) for κ > 0 and
   1 + e^{κ t}(1+o(1)) for κ < 0, separating the sign). Grouping preserved leaves by the value of κ_L, the
   equality of 1 and 2 forces  Σ_{L : κ_L = −2} χ(L) = 1  and  Σ_{L : κ_L = κ} χ(L) = 0 for every κ ≠ −2.
4. ALKL Abstract (read, verbatim): under these hypotheses "there are finitely many preserved leaves, which
   are **compact**". A compact leaf carrying a holomorphic structure is a closed **orientable** surface, so
   χ(L) = 2 − 2g(L) is **even**.
5. A finite sum of even integers is even; 1 is odd. **Contradiction.** ∎

*Cross-check against Deninger's isolated-fixed-point form (also mine).* [Den05] p. 26 dictionary: one
infinite place of Q ≙ one fixed point x_∞ with ε_{x_∞} = 1 and κ_{x_∞} = κ_∞. [Den05] pp. 32–33: at such a
fixed point T_xφ^t = e^{t/2}O_t with O_t ∈ SO(T_xF), and "For fixed points, ε_x = 1 is automatic"; such a
fixed point is a source for the leafwise flow on its preserved leaf L, of Poincaré–Hopf index +1. If L is
compact, Poincaré–Hopf gives Σ_{x ∈ L} index(x) = χ(L), so the number of archimedean fixed points on L
equals χ(L), which is even — while Q has exactly **one**. Same contradiction, obtained without the ALKL
formula. **Conclusion: the archimedean leaf of an S4′ object must be non-compact**, hence (ALKL Abstract) X
is not a closed manifold — in agreement with L6's determination that the leaf is a euclidean plane.

### D-III. Derivation: why α = 0 on manifolds and what a foliated space must supply (mine; printed antecedents [Den05] p. 23 Rem. 2, [x-06] p. 8, Leichtnam 2007 axioms (i)–(ii))

Let X be a closed 3-manifold, F codimension one, φ transverse to F, g a leafwise metric with
g(Tφ^t v, Tφ^t w) = e^{αt} g(v,w) on TF ([Den05] (20)). Let ω_φ be Deninger's 1-form ([x-18] p. 3): it is
closed and **φ-invariant**, φ^{t*}ω_φ = ω_φ (printed there). Let dA_g be the leafwise area form of g; then
φ^{t*}dA_g = e^{αt} dA_g. So ν := ω_φ ∧ dA_g is a volume form on X with φ^{t*}ν = e^{αt}ν. Since φ^t is a
diffeomorphism of the closed manifold X, ∫_X φ^{t*}ν = ∫_X ν, whence e^{αt} = 1 for all t, i.e. **α = 0**.
This re-derives [Den05] p. 23 Remark 2 and [x-06] p. 8 ("the conformal factor … necessarily has to be 1,
i.e. α = 0").

The obstruction is precisely that on a manifold the transverse measure of F is |ω_φ| and the flow preserves
it. In a foliated space with totally disconnected transversals the local model is (leaf) × R (the flow
direction) × T (totally disconnected); the transverse measure is dt ⊗ ν_T and the holonomy can **scale**
ν_T. Leichtnam 2007's axioms do exactly this (read this session, p. 13): "M_q ∈ M_m(Z_p) ∩ GL_m(Q_p) is such
that **Jac M_q = 1/q**" and "for any l ∈ L one has: ∀u ∈ T_lL, **g̃(q_*(u)) = q g̃(u)**" — the p-adic transverse
Haar measure is divided by q exactly when the leafwise metric is multiplied by q, so the product is invariant
and α = 1 becomes possible. This is the content of [x-06] p. 9, "α = 1 can be achieved if for X we allow the
local structure (totally disconnected) × (3-dimensional ball), c.f. [Lei07]".

**Consequence for clause (iv), and it is a correction to S4′ as posed:** the transverse measure of an S4′
object is not invariant. It must satisfy φ^t_*µ = e^{−t}µ, and the return map along γ_p must scale it by
exactly 1/p. A locally compact totally disconnected group carrying a Q^{>0}-action of modulus 1/q is
therefore forced; the canonical one is the ring of finite adeles A_f, on which multiplication by q ∈ Q^{>0}
multiplies Haar measure by Π_p |q|_p = 1/q. *(The modulus computation is mine, from the product formula.)*

### D-IV. Derivation: clause (i) as an isotropy condition (mine)

Let L be a space with a Q^{>0}-action, and form Deninger's/Leichtnam's suspension X = (L × R^{>0})/Q^{>0}
with q·(l, u) = (q·l, qu) and φ^t[l, u] = [l, e^t u] (the convention of [x-03] p. 59, a printed definition,
and of Leichtnam 2007 p. 2). Then [l, e^T u] = [l, u] for T > 0 iff there is q ∈ Q^{>0} with q·l = l and
q = e^T. Hence the set of periods of the R-orbit through [l, u] is log Isot_{Q^{>0}}(l), and:

 - Isot(l) = {1}  ⟹ the orbit is not closed;
 - Isot(l) = q_0^Z with q_0 > 1  ⟹ the orbit is a circle of length log q_0;
 - rank Isot(l) ≥ 2  ⟹ the period set is a dense subgroup of R, so the orbit is not closed and the
   quotient is not T₁ there.

So **clause (i) is exactly:** for each prime p the set {l ∈ L : Isot(l) = p^Z} is a **single** Q^{>0}-orbit;
Isot(l) ∈ {{1}} ∪ {p^Z : p prime} for every l; and no l has isotropy of rank ≥ 2. This is a purely
group-theoretic requirement on the Q^{>0}-space L — no analysis, no foliation. It is also exactly what
Deninger's own systems fail: [x-06] p. 11 Theorem 4.2 gives **packets** Γ_{x_0} of orbits of length log N x_0,
and p. 11, verbatim: "In general, the dynamical system (X_0, φ^t) has too many periodic orbits, since the
N-space W_rat(X_0)(C) does not know enough about the addition in O_{X_0}."  The same calculus applied to
Connes–Consani's X_Q = Q^×\A/Ẑ^× (Morishita (1.1.2)–(1.1.5), read) shows that the adeles vanishing at exactly
one place p give closed orbits of primitive period log p but form a **family**, not one orbit, and that adeles
vanishing at ≥ 2 places have isotropy of rank ≥ 2 hence non-closed orbits. *(Both applications are my
derivations from the printed definitions.)*

**Bearing on the program's Theorem T.** Theorem T (Session 14, ledger §15) says no Q^{>0}-suspension of a
**compact** base is T₁, via points with relatively compact Q^{>0}-orbit. Leichtnam's printed shape takes L
only **σ-compact** (Leichtnam 2007 p. 2, read: "L is a σ-compact complex 1-dimensional laminated space"),
and on the invertible locus the Q^{>0}-orbits are closed (Laca–Raeburn, Math. Ann. 318 (2000) Lemma 3.1, as
already recorded in the program's Session-14 novelty adjudication and quoted there from [x-03] p. 64). So
**Theorem T does not apply to Leichtnam's S_Q**, and the σ-compactness of L is precisely the hypothesis that
escapes it. This is a live correction to the reach the program has been assigning to Theorem T.

## §5. THE NEXT DECIDABLE QUESTION, AND WHETHER STANDING ORDER 6 LICENSES A CONSTRUCTION ATTEMPT

**Q-S4″ (the next decidable question I would put to the program).** *Is there a σ-compact space L with a
Q^{>0}-action by homeomorphisms, a lamination of L by Riemann surfaces preserved by the action, and a
leafwise Kähler metric g̃ with g̃(q_*u) = q·g̃(u), such that*

  **(a)** for every prime p, {l ∈ L : Isot_{Q^{>0}}(l) = p^Z} is a **single** Q^{>0}-orbit;
  **(b)** every other l ∈ L has Isot_{Q^{>0}}(l) = {1};
  **(c)** the quotient (L × R^{>0})/Q^{>0} ∪ L/Q^{>0} is compact, Hausdorff and a foliated space;
  **(d)** L/Q^{>0} contains a single point fixed by the induced flow, lying on a leaf conformal to C;
  **(e)** the transverse measure is scaled by 1/q under q ∈ Q^{>0}?

This is S4′ with the analysis stripped out. **(a)+(b) alone are a purely group-theoretic question about
Q^{>0}-spaces** and can be settled — positively or negatively — without any foliation theory; D-IV proves
they are *equivalent* to clause (i) for any object of the printed Leichtnam shape. That makes Q-S4″(a,b) the
cheapest genuine decider the program has had on this leg: a Q^{>0}-space whose isotropy groups are exactly
{1} and the p^Z, one orbit each, either exists or does not, and the answer is a paragraph either way. My own
first move would be to test the candidate L = (A_f ∖ {0}) / Ẑ^× with the multiplicative Q^{>0}-action — the
one space forced by D-III's modulus requirement — and compute its isotropy: I expect (b) to fail badly there
(adeles vanishing at several places give rank ≥ 2 isotropy, and adeles vanishing at exactly one place give a
positive-dimensional family), which is the precise form "too many periodic orbits" takes. If it fails, the
question becomes: **is there any Q^{>0}-space at all with isotropy spectrum exactly {{1}} ∪ {p^Z}, one orbit
per prime?** — and that is where I would spend the next session.

**Two further decidable items the sweep produced, both cheap:**
- **Q-S4‴ (parity):** D-II shows the closed-manifold obstruction is a **parity** obstruction that is specific
  to the number of archimedean places. For a real quadratic field (r_1 = 2) the parity obstruction does **not**
  fire, while D-I's length-group obstruction still does. Is there a **third** obstruction that is uniform in the
  field? Deciding this tells the program whether the manifold class is dead for arithmetic reasons or only for
  homological ones.
- **Theorem T's reach:** D-IV shows Theorem T's hypothesis (a relatively compact Q^{>0}-orbit) is exactly what
  Leichtnam's σ-compact L avoids. The program should re-scope Theorem T's statement in the ledger before it is
  cited again as ruling out the suspension shape in general. **This is a correction, not a new question.**

**Does standing order 6 now license a construction attempt?** **My judgment: not yet, and the sweep says
precisely why.** Standing order 6 asks whether the object, an obstruction to it, or the inverse problem for it
already exists in print. The answer this sweep returns is: (α) the object is **Leichtnam's printed conjecture**
(Contemp. Math. 387 §5; Bull. Sci. Math. 131 (2007) p. 2), not a program invention; (β) **two independent
obstructions kill it in the only class where the trace formula it needs is proved** (D-I and D-II, from KMNT
and ALKL); (γ) the escape class — compact non-manifold foliated spaces with a scaling transverse measure — has
**no trace formula at all** in print, so a construction there would produce an object the program could not yet
use; and (δ) the one clause nobody has ever satisfied, in any class, is clause (i), and D-IV reduces it to a
question with no analysis in it. **Building the analytic object before answering Q-S4″(a,b) would be building
the expensive half first.** A construction attempt is licensed **only** after Q-S4″(a,b) returns YES for some
explicit L; if it returns NO, S4′ is dead for the whole Leichtnam/Deninger suspension shape and the program has
a genuine theorem rather than another unbuilt object.
