### 1.3 (c) Poincaré–Bendixson on S², and the transversality of γ_p

Both steps are **correct**; I re-derived them.

*A source is not in any other point's ω-limit set.* Let x be a hyperbolic source with generator B,
Re spec B = ½ > 0. Choose linear coordinates in which V(z) = |z|² satisfies d/dt V(φ^t z) ≥ c V > 0
on a punctured ball U = {V < r} (possible since Re spec B > 0). Then V is strictly increasing along
orbits inside U; an orbit that leaves U never returns, and an orbit that stays in U forever has
V(φ^t y) ≥ V(y) > 0, so it cannot converge to x. Either way x ∉ ω(y) for y ≠ x. ✔

*Poincaré–Bendixson.* Let y ∈ L = S² not be fixed. ω(y) ⊆ S² is nonempty, compact, connected,
invariant, and by the previous paragraph contains neither of the two sources p, q. Hence
cl(orbit^+(y)) = orbit^+(y) ∪ ω(y) misses p (p is fixed and y ≠ p, so p is not on the orbit either),
so the whole forward orbit lies in a **compact** subset of S² ∖ {p} ≅ R², and the planar
Poincaré–Bendixson theorem applies: an ω-limit set of a C¹ planar flow that is compact, nonempty and
free of equilibria is a **periodic orbit**. ✔

*Does clause (ii) force γ_p ⊄ leaf?* **Yes.** If a closed orbit c of period ℓ lies inside a leaf,
then Y_φ(x) ∈ T_xF at every x ∈ c, and since the flow commutes with itself, T_xφ^ℓ(Y_φ(x)) = Y_φ(x):
the restriction T_xφ^ℓ|T_xF has **eigenvalue 1**. Clause (ii) says T_xφ^{log p}|T_xF = p^{1/2}·O with
O orthogonal, whose eigenvalues have modulus p^{1/2} ≥ 2^{1/2} > 1. Contradiction. ✔ (This is also

## §2. T2 = A-III (Candel existence). **STANDS-NARROWED.**

### 2.1 The theorem, read at source

Fetched this session from NUMDAM (open access), `www.numdam.org/item/ASENS_1993_4_26_4_489_0.pdf`,
A. Candel, *Uniformization of surface laminations*, Ann. Sci. É.N.S. (4) **26** (1993) 489–516.
**p. 490, verbatim:**

> "THEOREM. — Let M be a compact oriented surface lamination with a riemannian metric g. Then
> χ(M, µ) < 0 for every positive invariant transverse measure if and only if g is conformal to a
> metric of curvature −1. **In particular, this holds true if M has no invariant measure.**"

Definitions, p. 489–490 (read): "A surface lamination is a metric space that carries a foliation with
leaves of dimension two … a topological space locally homeomorphic to the product of a disc in the
plane and a piece of metric space, with the overlap homeomorphisms preserving the disc factor";
invariant transverse measures are "Radon measures on transversals to the leaves, invariant by the
local transformations defining the lamination"; and χ(M, µ) = µ(e_TM) via Ruelle–Sullivan.

### 2.2 Does it apply to an S4′ object? **Yes.**

- *Compact*: an S4′ object is compact by hypothesis. ✔
- *Surface lamination*: Candel's transversal is "a piece of metric space", so a 3-dimensional
  foliated space with 2-dimensional leaves — transversal an interval, a Cantor set, or a product —
  is a surface lamination in exactly his sense. There is **no** requirement that the leaves be all of
  the space, and none that the transversal be totally disconnected or 1-dimensional. ✔
- *Oriented*: the leafwise holomorphic structure of S4′ orients the leaves. ✔
- *Riemannian metric along the leaves*: S4′ carries one (and clause (0) presupposes it). ✔

So the adjudicator's first hypothesis check passes and I confirm it. Note also that Deninger himself
routes through the same theorem at [Den05] p. 33 ("Hence by a result of Candel [9] there is a
Riemannian metric on TF, such that every F-leaf has constant curvature −1"), which is independent
corroboration that Candel's hypotheses are meant to cover this class.

### 2.3 Does the conclusion need a parabolic leaf, and is that supplied only through T1(e)?

supplied ONLY through T1(e), which falls.**

The contrapositive he uses is sound: if M has no positive invariant transverse measure then, by the

transverse measure. The logic is valid. What is not valid is the input: A-II(c)'s C-leaf rests on
[Den05] p. 33 Rem. 7, an expectation (§1.5). **Strip the expectation and A-III has no premise.**

**Rescue (mine).** Theorem O-1 supplies the missing premise from a theorem instead of an expectation,
and supplies *more* than is needed: only "some leaf is non-hyperbolic" is required, and O-1 gives

## §3. T3 = A-IV. **STANDS-NARROWED.**

### 3.1 (a) "A closed leaf of a compact foliated space is compact" — **TRUE; the adjudicator asserts it, I prove it**

The adjudicator's parenthesis — "for a closed leaf the leaf topology and the subspace topology agree
(a closed leaf is proper: each plaque chain meeting a small transversal meets it in a set with no
accumulation point in L′)" — *asserts* properness, which is the entire content. Closedness alone does
not give it: L closed makes L ∩ T closed in a transversal T, and a closed set may perfectly well
accumulate on itself. Here is the proof.

> **Lemma O-3.** Let X be a compact foliated space (separable metrizable, in the sense of [Den05] 7.1,
> read) and L a leaf that is closed as a subset of X. Then L is compact.
>
> *Proof.* (1) *L meets every transversal in a countable set.* X is separable metrizable, hence
> Lindelöf, so it has a countable foliated atlas {U_i ≅ B^d × T_i}. A plaque P ≅ B^d is second
> countable, so P ∩ U_j is a countable union of connected components, each inside a single plaque of
> U_j; hence each plaque meets each chart in at most countably many plaques. L is the union of the
> plaque chains issuing from one plaque, so by induction L is a countable union of plaques, and
> L ∩ T is countable for any transversal T.
> (2) *Properness is a leafwise-invariant property.* For x ∈ L and a small transversal T ∋ x, either
> x is isolated in L ∩ T or it is an accumulation point of L ∩ T. Holonomy transport along a path in
> L carries a neighborhood of x in T homeomorphically onto a neighborhood of x′ in T′ and carries
> L ∩ T onto L ∩ T′ near those points; so the alternative is the same at every point of L.
> (3) *Baire.* Suppose L is not proper, so **every** point of L ∩ T is an accumulation point of
> L ∩ T. Shrink to a chart with T′ ⊂⊂ T compact and put A = L ∩ T′ ∩ B̄(x, r) for small r. Since L is
> closed in X, A is closed in the compact T′, hence a **compact metric space**; A is **countable** by
> (1); and A is **perfect** by (2) (accumulation is local, so for r small the accumulating points lie
> in A). A nonempty compact metric space with no isolated point is uncountable (Baire). Contradiction.
> So L is proper: the leaf topology equals the subspace topology.
> (4) L is closed in the compact X, hence compact in the subspace topology, hence compact as a
> surface. ∎

Contrapositive: **a non-compact leaf of a compact foliated space is not closed**, so L̄ ∖ L ≠ ∅.
This is A-IV step (2), and it now has a proof. I found no counterexample and do not believe one
exists: the countability of L ∩ T is what does the work, and it is a consequence of separability of
X, which is part of the definition of a foliated space.

### 3.2 (b) Is N saturated, and closed, when the transverse structure is only topological?

**Yes for the right N; the adjudicator's N is the wrong one, and his step 1 fails for it.**

*The clean statement.* Define **N := {x ∈ X : φ^t(x) ∈ L_x for all t ∈ R}** = the union of the
preserved leaves.
- *Saturated*: immediate, since {t : φ^t(x) ∈ L_x} depends only on L_x (φ^t maps leaves to leaves).
- *A subgroup*: if φ^t(x), φ^s(x) ∈ L_x then φ^{t+s}(x) = φ^t(φ^s x) ∈ φ^t(L_x) = L_{φ^t x} = L_x.
  So the set of such t is a subgroup of R, and if it contains (−ε, ε) it is all of R.
- *Closed*: in a foliated chart U ≅ B^d × T the flow induces, for |t| < ε, a continuous local map
  h^t on the local leaf space T (well defined because φ^t maps plaques into plaques). Then
  N ∩ U = B^d × ⋂_{|t|<ε} Fix(h^t), and each Fix(h^t) = {y : h^t(y) = y} is closed in the Hausdorff
  metrizable T by continuity of h^t. An intersection of closed sets is closed. ∎
No differentiability, no transverse smooth structure, no line bundle is needed.

*Why the adjudicator's N is not this N.* He defines N = {x : Y_φ(x) ∈ T_xF} and then argues "If a leaf
L′ lies in N then Y_φ is tangent to L′ everywhere on L′, so the integral curves of Y_φ through points
of L′ stay in L′ (a leaf is a maximal integral manifold of TF)". That inference is **an appeal to
uniqueness of integral curves**, and it fails for a transverse flow that is only continuous.

> **Counterexample O-4.** On R define h^t(y) = (y^{1/3} + t)^3. This is a genuine continuous flow
> (h^0 = id, h^{t+s} = h^t ∘ h^s), with **no** fixed point, yet d/dt|_{t=0} h^t(0) = 0. Suspend it
> transversally: on X = T² × R/…, or simply on the foliated chart B² × R with the plaques B² × {y}
> and the flow φ^t(z, y) = (z, h^t(y)) composed with any leafwise motion, the transverse component of
> Y_φ vanishes on the plaque y = 0 while that plaque is **not** preserved. So
> {Y_φ ∈ TF} ⊋ {preserved leaves}, the adjudicator's N contains non-preserved leaves, and his step
> (1)'s conclusion "every leaf contained in N is preserved" is false as stated.

The repair is exactly the definition above; and if one insists on the tangency definition, the extra
hypothesis needed is that the induced transverse flow is C¹ (equivalently, that φ is smooth along the
3-dimensional path components — Leichtnam 2013 Assumption 1], read: "the path connected components of
S_K are three dimensional"; [Den05] 7.3, read: "the induced R-actions on the leaves of L are smooth").
Under that hypothesis Y_φ(φ^s x) = T φ^s(Y_φ(x)) holds, the tangency set is flow-invariant, and the
two definitions of N agree. **Either repair suffices; the adjudicator supplied neither.**

*The rest of A-IV then goes through.* L is preserved and non-compact (T1, under clause (0)(a)); by
Lemma O-3 it is not closed, so L̄ ∖ L ≠ ∅; the closure of a saturated set is saturated (propagate
plaques along a plaque chain), so L̄ ∖ L is a nonempty union of leaves; L ⊆ N and N closed give
L̄ ⊆ N, so each of those leaves is preserved. Hence the preserved set contains **at least two** leaves,
at least one non-compact, and **is never a finite union of compact leaves**. ✔ I re-derived every step.

> **A-I (my derivation).** X compact foliated space, 2-dimensional leaves, g a continuous leafwise
> metric with (20) for the constant α; µ a positive holonomy-invariant transverse measure, finite on
> compact transversals, with (φ^t)_\*µ = e^{−βt}µ. Let m = vol_g ⊗ µ be the induced measure on X.
> Compactness gives a finite foliated atlas; continuity of g gives finite leafwise area on each plaque;

### 5.2 The three defects in the restatement

> **Theorem O-6 (existence of the e^{−t}-eigenmeasure).** Let X be a compact foliated space satisfying
