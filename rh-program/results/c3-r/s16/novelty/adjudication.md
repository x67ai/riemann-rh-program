## §1. PER CLAIM — the sources I opened, and what remains new

### N-A — Theorem A (A) + (B). **PARTIAL.**

**Claim as bound** (IV.14; `refute-adjudication.md` §1.5; `f1-check-O.md` §1–§2). X a compact metrizable foliated space with 2-dimensional (Riemann-surface) leaves, φ a **jointly continuous** foliated R-action, N the union of the leaves preserved by φ. **(A)** every positive holonomy-invariant transverse measure µ concentrated on N is flow-invariant, φ^t_*µ = µ. **(B)** if φ^{t*}[λ_g] = e^t[λ_g] in H̄²_F(X) there is no nonzero **flow-invariant** holonomy-invariant transverse measure; with (A), none concentrated on N.

**Precursors opened by me, at the page.**

1. **ÁLKL, memoir arXiv:2402.06671, §4.1.2, printed p. 100** (PDF p. 104; the running head "100 4. FOLIATIONS WITH SIMPLE FOLIATED FLOWS" precedes the section) — read:
   > "Let φ̄ be the local flow on Σ generated by Z ∈ X(Σ, H). Via the homeomorphism M/F → Σ/H induced by the coordinates x_k : U_k → Σ_k, **the leaves preserved by φ correspond to the H-orbits preserved by φ̄, which indeed form Fix(φ̄) because the H-orbits are totally disconnected.** Z is H-invariant, and φ̄ is H-equivariant in an obvious sense."

   and **arXiv:1906.06753 (*Simple foliated flows*), printed p. 2** — read:
   > "Using the canonical identity between leaf and orbit spaces, M/F ≡ Σ/H, **the leaves preserved by a foliated flow φ** … **correspond to H-orbits consisting of fixed points of φ̄.**"

   **Decision on (A).** This is a printed *device* that covers part of (A) in an adjacent setting, and it is decisive for the grade. Wherever the transverse local flow φ̄ exists — which requires the infinitesimal generator Z ∈ X(M, F) of a **smooth** foliated flow on a **manifold** (memoir §3.1, printed p. 2: "its infinitesimal generator Z is an infinitesimal transformation of (M, F)") — (A) is immediate: a transverse measure concentrated on N is a measure on the H-saturation of Fix(φ̄), the transverse map induced by φ^t is φ̄^t, and φ̄^t fixes that set pointwise. Nobody prints the corollary (I found no sentence anywhere pairing "preserved leaves" with "transverse measure"), but the grade cannot be NOVEL. **What is new and load-bearing:** on a foliated space with a merely **jointly continuous** R-action there is no Z, no X(Σ, H) and no φ̄ — the transverse map induced by φ^t need not exist at all. The program builds it by plaque chains and proves it equals holonomy **on N only** (`f1-check-O.md` §1.2 (★)), from joint continuity plus countability of the plaque decomposition and Lindelöf disjointification; and the observation that holonomy invariance may be applied to the Borel set B ∩ N is what makes "concentrated on N" a sufficient hypothesis. Sweep F did not find this precursor; sweep O did, and I confirm it at the page.

2. **Deninger, arXiv:math/0204110 (2002), printed p. 13, Remark 2 to Corollary 3.5**, and verbatim identically **arXiv:math/0505354 = [x-20], printed p. 24, Remark 2 to Corollary 5.5** — read at both places:
   > "2) **Actually the conditions of the corollary force α = 0 i.e. the flow must be isometric with respect to g.** We have chosen to leave the α in the fomulation since there are good reasons to expect the corollary to generalize to more general phase spaces X than manifolds, where α ≠ 0 becomes possible i.e. to Sullivan's generalized solenoids."

   Corollary 5.5's hypotheses, read at [x-20] **printed p. 23**: "*Let X be a compact 3-manifold with a foliation F by surfaces having a dense leaf. Let φ^t be a non-degenerate F-compatible flow which is **everywhere transversal to F**. Assume that φ^t is conformal as in (20)…*" I read the whole proof of 5.5 at pp. 24–25: **no proof of Remark 2 is given, and no reference is offered for it** — the only citation in the neighbourhood is "[21] proof of 2.6" (= Deninger–Singhof, Contemp. Math. 290, §4 item 1) for the *isometric* relation −Θ² = ∆¹|ker, which is used **after** assuming α = 0, not to derive it. Restated at [x-20] p. 27 ("this is not possible in the manifold setting of corollary 5.5 which actually implies α = 0") and at [x-18] p. 6 ("The conditions in the theorem actually force α = 0 so that φ^t is isometric").

   **Answer to the charter's explicit question — is (B) "Remark 2 with the transversality hypothesis removed"?** **No, and the program must not say so.** Remark 2 says *transversality + conformality ⇒ α = 0*. (B) says *α = 1 ⇒ no flow-invariant holonomy-invariant transverse measure*. Neither implies the other. They are joined by the intermediate proposition *"a flow-invariant holonomy-invariant transverse measure ⇒ α = 0"* — the contrapositive of (B) — which Deninger nowhere states and nowhere proves. What removing transversality removes is the **supply** of the invariant measure: on a closed 3-manifold with φ everywhere transverse, the canonical 1-form ω_φ (KMNT Lemma 1.9, published p. 328) is closed with ω_φ|TF = 0 and ω_φ(φ̇) = 1, so |ω_φ| is a holonomy-invariant transverse measure and it is flow-invariant. So **(B) supplies the proof Deninger's Remark 2 lacks**, in a generality that contains his case. That is the correct and stronger claim, and it is O's analysis; I adopt it as binding.

   > "One can show that the conformal factor e^{αt} for a metric g_F as above necessarily has to be 1, i.e. α = 0 which implies that the eigenvalues ρ of θ on ker ∆¹_{F,(2)} have real part Re ρ = 0. … This is only one instance which shows that the class of smooth compact manifolds is too restrictive to actually rewrite the explicit formulas of analytic number theory as a transversal index theorem."

   **The reading is adjudicated in §2, item 2.** Binding: the sentence is **ambiguous as printed**, and the broad reading (α = 0 asserted for Deninger's whole smooth-compact-manifold class of p. 6, fixed points on compact leaves included) is **at least as natural** as the narrow one. Every ingredient of (B)'s proof is on these pages — the eigen-relation, the Ruelle–Sullivan current, its closedness, the non-vanishing of the pairing — and the conclusion Leichtnam draws is the **positive** one: in his α = 1 object the transverse measure is scaled by exactly e^{−t}. **Verdict N-A: PARTIAL.** *Printed:* the identification that makes (A) a one-liner in the smooth manifold category (ÁLKL, memoir p. 100; SFF p. 2); the **conclusion** of (B) in the everywhere-transverse manifold case, as an assertion with no proof (Deninger math/0204110 p. 13 = math/0505354 p. 24 Rem. 2; [x-21] p. 18; [x-18] p. 6; [x-06] p. 8 with the reading flagged); the whole toolkit and the phenomenon for one α = 1 object (Leichtnam Prop. 2 2]–4], Lemma 10.1). *New:* (A) for a compact foliated space with a merely jointly continuous flow, where φ̄ does not exist and the transverse map must be built by plaque chains and identified with holonomy on N; (B) as a theorem in lamination generality from φ^{t*}[λ_g] = e^t[λ_g] **alone** — no transversality, no non-degeneracy, no dense leaf, no one-dimensionality of H̄²_F; and the combination (A)+(B) that makes N measure-null.

### N-B — Theorem A (C). **PARTIAL.**

**Precursors opened by me** (Candel downloaded from NUMDAM this session, `ASENS_1993_4_26_4_489_0`).

1. **Candel, Ann. Sci. ÉNS (4) 26 (1993) 489–516, printed p. 497** — read:
   > "THEOREM 4.1. — Let M be a compact oriented surface lamination and let g be a riemannian metric on M. Then g is conformal to a metric for which all leaves have curvature −1 if and only if χ(M, µ) < 0 for every positive invariant transverse measure µ. **Remark that the theorem includes the case no invariant transverse measure exists.** Thus
   > COROLLARY 4.2. — **If M is a compact Riemann surface lamination with no invariant transverse measure, then M has a conformal metric with curvature −1 on each leaf.**"

   and **printed p. 498, §4.1** — read:
   > "First we will show that if M contains a leaf L which is not a hyperbolic Riemann surface, then there is a positive invariant transverse measure µ with χ(M, µ) ≥ 0. **This is elementary if L is compact, for then it would be either a sphere or a torus, and the corresponding Dirac measure works.**"
   > "THEOREM 4.3. — If χ(M, µ) < 0 for every positive invariant transverse measure µ, then all leaves are hyperbolic Riemann surfaces. In fact, if L is a euclidean leaf, then there exists µ with support in L̄ and χ(M, µ) = 0."

   So **(C1)'s mechanism is Candel's own printed sentence** (a compact leaf carries a Dirac invariant transverse measure) and (C2)–(C3) are Candel's Thm. 4.3 / Cor. 4.2 applied to a compact sub-lamination.

2. **Deninger, [x-20] printed pp. 33–34, Remark 7** — read: "**If there does exist** a foliated dynamical system attached to spec o_K … **we would expect** … χ_Co(F, µ) = −log|d_{K/Q}| … since H̄²(X, F) must be one-dimensional, it follows that **χ_Co(F, ν) < 0 for all non-trivial transverse measures ν. Hence by a result of Candel [9] there is a Riemannian metric on TF, such that every F-leaf has constant curvature −1.** … **In the unramified case** … **there is an F-leaf which is either a plane, a torus or a cylinder.**" — the same inference pattern, printed by Deninger himself, but (i) run on the **whole** of X, (ii) **conditional on arithmetic input** (K/Q ramified) and on dim H̄²_F = 1, and (iii) for K = Q pointing the **opposite way** (a euclidean leaf somewhere).

**Verdict N-B: PARTIAL.** *Printed:* the engine (Candel pp. 497–498), the compact-leaf Dirac argument (p. 498), and the inference pattern applied to Deninger's objects by Deninger ([x-20] pp. 33–34); the manifold shadow of (C1)'s contrapositive at [x-06] p. 8 on the broad reading. *New:* that N is itself a compact Riemann-surface lamination to which Cor. 4.2 applies, with the restriction/extension of transverse measures between N and X; that hyperbolicity of every preserved leaf follows from (0-coh) **alone**, with no arithmetic input, no one-dimensionality, and **uniformly in the number field** — where Deninger's Rem. 7 predicts a non-hyperbolic leaf for K = Q (no contradiction: that leaf must be non-preserved — an observation printed nowhere and worth printing); and **(C4)**, µ(T ∩ N) = 0, of which I found no part in print. ### N-D — B1′ (finite-rank length group on a finite-type tangency set). **PARTIAL** — sweep F's NOVEL is overturned.

**The new find, opened this session; neither sweep reached it.** KMNT cite, for the rank-one characterization of their period group, "[7, 9.3], [19, 2.1]" (= Calegari's book; Farber, *Topology of closed one-forms*) and, in their bibliography, **[9] J. Cantwell and L. Conlon, *Tischler fibrations of open, foliated sets*, Ann. Inst. Fourier (Grenoble) 31 (1981), no. 2, 113–135**. That paper is open access at NUMDAM (`AIF_1981__31_2_113_0`); I downloaded and read it.

> **Setting** (printed pp. 113–114, read): "Let M be a smooth, closed n-manifold, F a foliation of M of codimension one … More generally, suppose that **U ⊂ M is an open, connected, F-saturated subset**, each leaf of F|U being dense in U with trivial holonomy. … Let Û be the completion of U in the sense of G. Hector and P. Dippolito. This is a manifold with finitely many boundary components and, generally, **it is not compact**. … F|U is defined by a closed, nonsingular 1-form ω on Û."
> **The period group** (printed p. 114, §1, read): "Let P(µ) ⊂ R be the additive subgroup of periods of Φ. That is, **t ∈ P(µ) if and only if Φ^t carries some (hence every) leaf of F|U onto itself.**" With (1.1)(3): "F|U is defined by a closed, nonsingular form ω ∈ A¹(Û), and **P(µ) = P(ω)**."
> **The finiteness theorem** (printed p. 127, proof of (3.11), read): "If σ is an integral 1-cycle contained in an arm U_i, then ω(σ) = 0. Thus, **P(ω) is the finitely generated image of ω : H₁(K;Z) → R** and (3.8) applies."
> **The hypothesis under which it runs** (printed p. 114 and (3.10), p. 126–127): F|U is *almost trivial* — a compact **nucleus** K with product-foliated arms — which holds if "(a) Û is compact; (b) F is of class at least C² and each leaf of F|U has two dense ends; (c) F is transversely analytic".

This is decisive for the grade. Cantwell–Conlon's P(ω) is **the same object** as Deninger's Λ = {t ∈ R | φ^t(F) = F} ([x-06] p. 6, read) and KMNT's Λ_S (published Def. 1.10, p. 328, read); their U is exactly the complement of a set of non-transverse leaves in a closed manifold; and their conclusion — the period group of an **open** saturated set is the finitely generated image of H₁ of a **compact core** — is the printed, **proved** relative of B1′'s mechanism. It is not B1′: their hypotheses (all leaves of F|U dense, trivial holonomy, almost triviality) are different from and incomparable with "the tangency set is a finite union of leaves each with dim_Q H₁ < ∞", they have no rank bound by dim_Q H₁(M ∖ N;Q), and they say nothing about closed orbits of a foliated flow or about {log p}. But a printed device covers part of the claim in an adjacent setting, and by the S14 rule that is **PARTIAL**.
