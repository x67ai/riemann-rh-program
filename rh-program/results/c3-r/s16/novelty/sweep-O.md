#### Candidate precursors opened, with the printed wording

1. **ÁLKL, *A trace formula for foliated flows*, arXiv:2402.06671 (memoir), §4.1.2, printed p. 100** — read:
   > "Let φ̄ be the local flow on Σ generated by Z ∈ X(Σ, H). Via the homeomorphism M/F → Σ/H induced by the
   > coordinates x_k : U_k → Σ_k, **the leaves preserved by φ correspond to the H-orbits preserved by φ̄, which
   > indeed form Fix(φ̄) because the H-orbits are totally disconnected.** Z is H-invariant, and φ̄ is
   > H-equivariant in an obvious sense."

   and **ÁLKL, *Simple foliated flows*, arXiv:1906.06753, printed p. 2**:
   > "Using the canonical identity between leaf and orbit spaces, M/F ≡ Σ/H, **the leaves preserved by a foliated
   > flow φ**, which will be shortly called preserved leaves in the sequel, **correspond to H-orbits consisting
   > of fixed points of φ̄**."

   **This is the decisive find for (A).** In the *smooth manifold* category, where the infinitesimal generator
   Z ∈ X(M, F) descends to a genuine H-equivariant local flow φ̄ on the transversal Σ, (A) is an immediate
   corollary: a transverse measure concentrated on N is concentrated on Fix(φ̄), and a flow fixing a set
   pointwise pushes any measure on that set to itself. Nobody prints that corollary — I found no sentence
   anywhere pairing "preserved leaves" with "transverse measure" — but it is a one-liner from a printed
   identification, and the sweep must say so.

   *What is not covered.* ÁLKL's φ̄ exists because Z is a smooth infinitesimal transformation of (M, F)
   (memoir §3.1, "its infinitesimal generator Z is an infinitesimal transformation of (M, F), or the induced
   section Z of the normal bundle NF = TM/TF is parallel with respect to the Bott partial connection", read at
   printed p. 2). On a foliated space with a merely **jointly continuous** R-action there is no Z, no
   X(Σ, H) and no φ̄: the transverse map induced by φ^t need not exist at all. The program's (A) supplies exactly
   this — the plaque-chain identity pr_{T_{k−1}}(φ^t(y)) = h_x(y) valid **on N only** (`f1-check-O.md` §1.2 (★)),
   from joint continuity plus the countability of plaques, and then Lindelöf-disjointification. That statement,
   in that generality, is in print nowhere I reached.

#### Verdict N-B: **PARTIAL**

*Anticipated.* (i) The engine is Candel's Cor. 4.2 / Thm. 4.3, quoted above — cited by the program already.
(ii) The **inference pattern** "no invariant transverse measure on this lamination ⇒ Candel ⇒ every leaf
hyperbolic" is printed by **Deninger himself for the whole of X** ([x-20] p. 33 Rem. 7) and is routine in the
lamination literature (1906.10029 p. 18). (iii) The compact-leaf case of (C1) is Candel's own printed sentence
("This is elementary if L is compact … the corresponding Dirac measure works", p. 498).

*New, and the brief's question answered.* **The localization to N is new**, and it is not cosmetic:
* Deninger's Rem. 7 conclusion is *conditional on the arithmetic input* (K/Q ramified, so that
  χ_Co(F, µ) = −log|d_{K/Q}| < 0) **and on one-dimensionality of H̄²_F**, and it applies to **all of X**. Under
  (0-coh) the program gets hyperbolicity of every leaf of N with **no** arithmetic input and **without**
  one-dimensionality (f1-check-O §2.2 remark (i)) — but only on N.
* The two results point in *opposite* directions on the unramified case: for K = Q Deninger's own Rem. 7 predicts
  a euclidean leaf (plane, torus or cylinder) somewhere in X, while (C2) forbids euclidean leaves **inside N**.
  There is no contradiction — the euclidean leaf must be non-preserved — but no printed source contains either
  half of that observation, and it is a real sharpening of Rem. 7 that the program should print.
* Establishing that N is itself a compact Riemann surface lamination in Candel's sense (so that Cor. 4.2 may be
  applied to it at all), and the restriction/extension of transverse measures between N and X, is done in
  `f1-check-O.md` §3.3 and I found it nowhere in print for this N.
* **(C4)** — "µ_T(T ∩ N) = 0 for every holonomy-invariant transverse measure µ of X and every regular transversal
  T" — I found **no** printed statement of any part of it. The nearest printed material is the *opposite*
  construction: ÁLKL build their invariant transverse measure on M¹ = M ∖ M⁰, i.e. away from the preserved
  leaves, and it is not even finite there (they must use a b-density and a b-integral; memoir §1.3 printed p. 9,
  §3.1.4 printed p. 80, and §7.3: "The usual Connes |ω_{b,l}|-Euler characteristic is not defined because M_l¹ is
  not compact"). So under Theorem A the trace-formula δ₀ term receives nothing from N, while in ÁLKL's
  (transversely simple, α = 0) world N is exactly where the density blows up. That contrast is new.

### N-E — "On a compact foliated space, a leaf closed as a subset is a compact leaf." **ANTICIPATED.**

The brief expected this and asked for the printed proposition. It is printed, in the general foliated-space
setting, with **the program's own proof**, fifty years ago.

#### The source, opened and read in full this session

**D. B. A. Epstein, *Foliations with all leaves compact*, Annales de l'Institut Fourier 26 (1976), no. 1,
265–282.** Open access at NUMDAM, `AIF_1976__26_1_265_0`; downloaded and extracted this session. §2.3 and §2.4

h : V × W → U with W an **arbitrary** topological space (the transverse model B), and only §1.6 specializes:

**§2.2–2.3, printed pp. 267–268, verbatim:**
> "Applying this result to the identity map X_ℓ → X, we see that X_ℓ is paracompact. Therefore every leaf is
> paracompact in the leaf topology. Since a leaf is also Hausdorff, and has a basis each member of which
> satisfies the second axiom of countability and has compact closure, we see by a chain argument that each leaf
> also satisfies the second axiom of countability in the leaf topology. **It follows that a leaf meets a
> coordinate neighbourhood in at most a countable number of slices.**
> 2.3. It now follows that a leaf L, which is closed in X, meets a coordinate neighbourhood in at most a finite
> number of slices. To see this, consider a transversal T₁ contained in a larger transversal T. … **If L ∩ T₁ is
> infinite, then it has a limit point in L ∩ T. The holonomy construction (which we will describe later in
> detail) then shows that each point of L ∩ T is a limit point of L ∩ T. In other words L ∩ T is perfect. By the
> Baire category theorem, no such a space is countable.** Therefore L ∩ T is uncountable which contradicts the
> consequences of 2.2."

**§2.4, printed p. 268, verbatim:**
> "2.4. In particular we see that **the subspace topology is equal to the leaf topology on a closed leaf** and
> therefore the same is true for a compact leaf. (An irrational linear flow on a torus gives an example of a

#### Verdict N-E: **ANTICIPATED**

Epstein 2.4 gives leaf topology = subspace topology on a closed leaf; when X is compact a closed subset is
compact in the subspace topology, hence the leaf is compact in its own topology. The remaining step is one

decomposition** (Epstein 2.2), **holonomy propagation of the limit points** and **Baire on a compact
transversal** (Epstein 2.3).

**Required change.** Everywhere the program uses "closed leaf ⇒ compact leaf, proved for compact laminations by
Baire plus holonomy" (ledger §16-bis S16-5; `f1-check-O.md` §6.5 step (3) uses the same device inside the B1′
proof), the sentence must read: *"Epstein, Ann. Inst. Fourier 26 (1976) 265–282, §2.3–2.4, p. 268 (leaf topology

*Not opened, and no number is guessed for them* — Candel–Conlon, *Foliations I* (GSM 23, 2000); Moore–Schochet,
