#### Candidate precursors opened, with the printed wording

1. **ÁLKL, *A trace formula for foliated flows*, arXiv:2402.06671 (memoir), §4.1.2, printed p. 100** — read:
   > "Let φ̄ be the local flow on Σ generated by Z ∈ X(Σ, H). Via the homeomorphism M/F → Σ/H induced by the
   > coordinates x_k : U_k → Σ_k, **the leaves preserved by φ correspond to the H-orbits preserved by φ̄, which
   > indeed form Fix(φ̄) because the H-orbits are totally disconnected.** Z is H-invariant, and φ̄ is
   > H-equivariant in an obvious sense."

   and **ÁLKL, *Simple foliated flows*, arXiv:1906.06753, printed p. 2**:
   > "Using the canonical identity between leaf and orbit spaces, M/F ≡ Σ/H, **the leaves preserved by a foliated
   > flow φ**, which will be shortly called preserved leaves in the sequel, **correspond to H-orbits consisting
   > of fixed points of φ̄**."

   **This is the decisive find for (A).** In the *smooth manifold* category, where the infinitesimal generator
   Z ∈ X(M, F) descends to a genuine H-equivariant local flow φ̄ on the transversal Σ, (A) is an immediate
   corollary: a transverse measure concentrated on N is concentrated on Fix(φ̄), and a flow fixing a set
   pointwise pushes any measure on that set to itself. Nobody prints that corollary — I found no sentence
   anywhere pairing "preserved leaves" with "transverse measure" — but it is a one-liner from a printed
   identification, and the sweep must say so.

   *What is not covered.* ÁLKL's φ̄ exists because Z is a smooth infinitesimal transformation of (M, F)
   (memoir §3.1, "its infinitesimal generator Z is an infinitesimal transformation of (M, F), or the induced
   section Z of the normal bundle NF = TM/TF is parallel with respect to the Bott partial connection", read at
   printed p. 2). On a foliated space with a merely **jointly continuous** R-action there is no Z, no
   X(Σ, H) and no φ̄: the transverse map induced by φ^t need not exist at all. The program's (A) supplies exactly
   this — the plaque-chain identity pr_{T_{k−1}}(φ^t(y)) = h_x(y) valid **on N only** (`f1-check-O.md` §1.2 (★)),
   from joint continuity plus the countability of plaques, and then Lindelöf-disjointification. That statement,
   in that generality, is in print nowhere I reached.

   - **Prop. 2, item 2]** (PDF p. 15): "**For any t ∈ R, (φ^t)^*[λ_g] = e^t[λ_g]** where [λ_g] denotes the

#### Verdict N-B: **PARTIAL**

*Anticipated.* (i) The engine is Candel's Cor. 4.2 / Thm. 4.3, quoted above — cited by the program already.
(ii) The **inference pattern** "no invariant transverse measure on this lamination ⇒ Candel ⇒ every leaf
hyperbolic" is printed by **Deninger himself for the whole of X** ([x-20] p. 33 Rem. 7) and is routine in the
lamination literature (1906.10029 p. 18). (iii) The compact-leaf case of (C1) is Candel's own printed sentence
("This is elementary if L is compact … the corresponding Dirac measure works", p. 498).

*New, and the brief's question answered.* **The localization to N is new**, and it is not cosmetic:
* Deninger's Rem. 7 conclusion is *conditional on the arithmetic input* (K/Q ramified, so that
  χ_Co(F, µ) = −log|d_{K/Q}| < 0) **and on one-dimensionality of H̄²_F**, and it applies to **all of X**. Under
  (0-coh) the program gets hyperbolicity of every leaf of N with **no** arithmetic input and **without**
  one-dimensionality (f1-check-O §2.2 remark (i)) — but only on N.
* The two results point in *opposite* directions on the unramified case: for K = Q Deninger's own Rem. 7 predicts
  a euclidean leaf (plane, torus or cylinder) somewhere in X, while (C2) forbids euclidean leaves **inside N**.
  There is no contradiction — the euclidean leaf must be non-preserved — but no printed source contains either
  half of that observation, and it is a real sharpening of Rem. 7 that the program should print.
* Establishing that N is itself a compact Riemann surface lamination in Candel's sense (so that Cor. 4.2 may be
  applied to it at all), and the restriction/extension of transverse measures between N and X, is done in
  `f1-check-O.md` §3.3 and I found it nowhere in print for this N.
* **(C4)** — "µ_T(T ∩ N) = 0 for every holonomy-invariant transverse measure µ of X and every regular transversal
  T" — I found **no** printed statement of any part of it. The nearest printed material is the *opposite*
  construction: ÁLKL build their invariant transverse measure on M¹ = M ∖ M⁰, i.e. away from the preserved
  leaves, and it is not even finite there (they must use a b-density and a b-integral; memoir §1.3 printed p. 9,
  §3.1.4 printed p. 80, and §7.3: "The usual Connes |ω_{b,l}|-Euler characteristic is not defined because M_l¹ is
  not compact"). So under Theorem A the trace-formula δ₀ term receives nothing from N, while in ÁLKL's
  (transversely simple, α = 0) world N is exactly where the density blows up. That contrast is new.

*Regularity.* The narrowing "g in Candel's regularity class C_l^∞" is forced by Candel §1.1–1.2 pp. 491–493
against Deninger's Warning at [x-20] p. 30 ("In the first one demands smoothness also in the transversal
direction whereas in the second one only wants continuity"); both were read this session. This is a *correction*
of a citation, not a novelty claim, and it is properly recorded in ledger §16-ter (N-1).

#### Printed wording — the two cautions, and what they do and do not say

**Leichtnam, arXiv:math/0603576 (Bull. Sci. Math. 131 (2007)), printed p. 11**, read verbatim:
> "**Deninger pointed out to us that the condition (4) (φ^t)^* g = e^t g was probably too strong for being
> generalized.** That is why in Proposition 2.2] we shall replace it by (φ^t)^*[λ_g] = e^t[λ_g] where [λ_g]
> denotes the reduced leafwise cohomology class of the leafwise kaehler form associated to g."

**Leichtnam, arXiv:1307.3851 (2013), Comment 6**, read verbatim:
> "The stronger assumption ∀t ∈ R, (φ^t)^*(g) = e^t g implies (13) (because φ⁰ = Id), (15) and the fact that φ^t
> commutes with the Hodge star not only on H¹_{F,K} but also on the vector space of leafwise differential
> 1−forms. **Deninger told us privately that this assumption (φ^t)^*(g) = e^t g might be too strong.**"

Both are reports of a *private* heuristic caution, and both give the same reason: "too strong **for being
generalized**" — a remark about the difficulty of constructing examples, not about inconsistency. Neither says,
or hints, that (31) is **inconsistent** with anything. Neither mentions fixed points in this connection. And
neither gives an argument.

**Deninger himself sets up the configuration and does not notice.** [x-20] printed p. 31, Remark 7.6(4), read:
> "We will see below that in our new context **metrics g on TF can exist for which the flow has the conformal
> behaviour (31)**. Assuming we are in such a situation and that F is 2-dimensional, we have:
> |det(T_xφ^{kl(γ)} | T_xF)| = e^{kl(γ)} for x ∈ γ, k ∈ Z and **|det(T_xφ^t | T_xF)| = e^t for a fixed point x**.
> In the latter case, we even have by continuity: det(T_xφ^t | T_xF) = e^t, the determinant being positive for
> t = 0."

and printed p. 33, the **Fact**:
> "For fixed points, ε_x = 1 is automatic and we have: T_xφ^t = e^{t/2} O_t for O_t ∈ SO(T_xF)."

So the object Theorem A(D) declares impossible — a **compact** X, (31) holding, a **fixed point** on a leaf, with
α = 1 — is exactly the object Deninger contemplates on pp. 31–33 and (31) is what he uses to derive the fixed
point's normal form. The inconsistency is not remarked on by him, by Leichtnam, or by anyone in the enumerated
citing sets.

#### Verdict N-C: **NOVEL**

Nothing in print states or implies that (31) along the fixed point's leaf is inconsistent with α = 1 on a compact
space. What must be **credited as inputs, not as precursors**: Schwarz–Pick and the classification of Aut of the
four uniformization types (classical); Candel Cor. 4.2 (for the C case, via (C2)); and the two Deninger/Leichtnam
cautions above, which should be cited as *the only printed suspicion in this direction* — with the honest note
that they give a different reason ("too strong for being generalized") and no argument.

**Guard, and it must travel with the claim.** The [De02] example ([x-20] §7.7, printed pp. 35–36, read:
g = e^t Re(ξη̄) "satisfies the conformality condition (20) for α = 1"; §7.7 "is everywhere transverse to the
leaves of F and in particular has no fixed points"; Thm. 7.8's "certain canonical transverse measure µ") is a
**compact** object with (31) globally, α = 1, euclidean leaves and a nonzero transverse measure. Any paraphrase
of N-C as "(31) with α = 1 is impossible on a compact space" is refuted by Deninger's own printed example. The
claim is: *(31) along the leaf of a **fixed point**, with α = 1, on a compact space.* This is already the §16-ter
§8.3 guard; the novelty sweep confirms it is load-bearing against a printed counterexample.

### N-D — B1′ (the finite-rank length group on a finite-type tangency set). **PARTIAL.**

*M a closed 3-manifold, F a codimension-one foliation by surfaces, φ a foliated flow whose tangency set
N = {x : Y_φ(x) ∈ T_xF} is a finite union of leaves each with dim_Q H₁ < ∞. Then the group generated by the
lengths of the closed orbits **transverse to F** has finite rank, dim_Q(Λ ⊗ Q) ≤ dim_Q H₁(M ∖ N; Q) < ∞; hence
the S4′ length spectrum {log p} is unrealizable in that class.*

#### Printed wording

**Deninger, arXiv:math/0204110 (2002), printed p. 13, Remark 3** (the earliest occurrence; verbatim, and
repeated word-for-word at [x-20] = math/0505354 printed p. 24):
> "3) **One can show that the group generated by the lengths of closed orbits is a finitely generated subgroup of
> R under the assumptions of the corollary. In order to achieve an infinitely generated group the flow must
> have fixed points.**"

The "assumptions of the corollary" are, read at math/0505354 printed p. 23: X a compact 3-manifold, F by
surfaces with a dense leaf, φ non-degenerate, F-compatible and **everywhere transversal to F**. No proof and no
reference is given, in either paper.

**Deninger, [x-18] printed p. 3**, verbatim: "**If one wants an infinitely generated Λ, one must allow the flow

**KMNT, arXiv:1906.02424 = Münster J. Math. 14 (2021):** Definition 1.5(i), read: "there are finite number of
**compact** leaves L^∞_1, …, L^∞_r, which may be empty, such that for any i and t we have φ^t(L^∞_i) = L^∞_i and

by the image of [ω_S], which we denote by Λ_S." Proposition 2.2.5 (= published Prop. 2.11): "If S is of type I
or of type III-1, then the period group Λ_S = Z. Conversely, if the Λ_S has rank one … then S is of type I or of

#### Verdict N-D: **PARTIAL**

*Anticipated, in an adjacent setting and without proof.* The conclusion — the closed-orbit length group of a
foliated flow on a closed 3-manifold is small — is **printed by Deninger** for the everywhere-transverse
(hence fixed-point-free) case, in the stronger form "finitely generated", with no proof; and the escape route
he names is exactly the hypothesis B1′ relaxes ("one must allow the flow to have fixed points"). The device
(the canonical closed 1-form ω with ω|TF = 0, ω(φ̇) = 1, and its period group) is printed by KMNT (Lemma 1.10,
Def. 1.11), and the rank-1 ⟺ fibration dichotomy is KMNT Prop. 2.2.5 and [x-18] p. 3.

*New.* Four things, each load-bearing:
1. **A proof.** Deninger's Remark 3 is an unproved assertion; nobody in the enumerated citing sets proves it or
   cites a proof. B1 supplies one for his case (cut along the compact leaves; H₁ of the resulting compact
   manifold with boundary is finitely generated), and B1′ supplies the correct statement beyond it.
2. **The hypothesis is on the tangency set and the leaves need not be compact.** KMNT's Def. 1.5(i) and ÁLKL's
   transverse simplicity both *force* finitely many **compact** preserved leaves. B1′ assumes only that the
   tangency set is a finite union of leaves with dim_Q H₁ < ∞ — non-compact, infinite-genus-free, finite-type
   leaves are allowed. No printed source treats that class.
3. **The conclusion is finite RANK, not finite generation**, and the correction matters: a subgroup of R of
   finite Q-rank need not be finitely generated (Z[1/2]). Deninger's printed word is "finitely generated"; in
   the compact-leaf case that is right (H₁(M₀; Z) is f.g.), in the finite-type case it is not derivable. No
   printed source makes this distinction.
4. **The restriction to closed orbits transverse to F**, with the observation that orbits inside N are invisible
   to ω and are excluded by S4′ clause (ii) via the eigenvalue 1 (`f1-check-O.md` §6.3). Printed nowhere.
5. The **kill** — {log p} is Q-linearly independent, hence spans an infinite-dimensional Q-subspace of R, so it
   cannot lie in a finite-rank group — is a dimension count applied to Deninger's own desideratum. Deninger
   states the desideratum ([x-20] p. 26, [x-03] p. 40) and the obstruction (Rem. 3) on separate pages and never
   joins them; nobody else does either (O-22, O-24 both empty).

**Required citation.** Any statement of B1/B1′ must lead with "Deninger, arXiv:math/0204110 (2002) p. 13 Rem. 3
= math/0505354 p. 24 Rem. 3 (asserted, unproved, everywhere-transverse case)" and "KMNT Lemma 1.10 / Def. 1.11
for ω and Λ_S", and must say that the new content is the proof, the finite-type tangency set, the rank
correction, and the transverse-orbit restriction.
