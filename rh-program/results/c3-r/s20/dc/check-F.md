## §2. Steps (a)–(i)

**(a) The cover exists with the stated properties and 𝓕̃ is C², codimension one, on a closed oriented M̃ — CLOSES.** Printed (Candel–Conlon I §3.5 "Orientation Covers", pp. 94–95, PDF 101–102, vision): "**Proposition 3.5.1.** Let (M, 𝓕) be a foliated manifold, M connected. If M is nonorientable and/or 𝓕 is not transversely orientable, there is a connected covering space (either 2-fold or 4-fold) π : M̃ → M such that M̃ is orientable and the lifted foliation 𝓕̃ is transversely orientable. In this case, the leaves of 𝓕̃ are all orientable." (p. 94, last two lines, continued on p. 95, first four lines.) Applied to the component M₀ of M that contains L (a closed connected 3-manifold; L̄, K and every γ_p that clause (iii) makes L accumulate on lie in M₀, and (D1)–(D5) restrict to M₀ verbatim since N ∩ M₀, L, K are unchanged); if M₀ is orientable and 𝓕|M₀ transversely orientable, take π = id (1-fold). Orient M̃ and ν(𝓕̃) once and for all: "orientable" becomes "oriented" by a choice. Regularity and codimension (one-line, from p. 94: "The differentiable structure of M is lifted to a differentiable structure of M̃, relative to which the covering projection is locally a diffeomorphism"): the C² foliated atlas of 𝓕 restricted to evenly covered foliated charts lifts sheet by sheet; a change of lifted charts is π⁻¹∘(change of charts)∘π on overlapping sheets, i.e., the same C² map read through local diffeomorphisms, so 𝓕̃ is C² and of the same codimension, one. Closed: ∂M = ∅ and π is a local homeomorphism, so ∂M̃ = ∅; M̃ is compact because it is a finite union of compact sets — cover M by finitely many opens U_i with cl(U_i) inside an evenly covered V_i, then M̃ = ⋃_{i,j} (sheet j of π⁻¹(V_i)) ∩ π⁻¹(cl(U_i)), each a homeomorphic copy of cl(U_i). Connected: printed in the Proposition. So (M̃, 𝓕̃) is a compact connected oriented 3-manifold without boundary carrying a C² codimension-one transversely oriented foliation — hypotheses 2 and 3 both hold, from the page plus the two one-line arguments. (The page settles the "4-fold" question: see §3.)

**(b) Components of π⁻¹(L) are homeomorphic copies of L — CLOSES.** One-line arguments. (b1) π⁻¹(L) is a union of leaves of 𝓕̃ and π maps each leaf of 𝓕̃ into a leaf of 𝓕: π carries plaques of 𝓕̃ onto plaques of 𝓕 (Notation), so a plaque chain in M̃ projects to a plaque chain in M, and conversely every plaque of 𝓕 in an evenly covered chart lifts to one plaque on each sheet. (b2) For a leaf L̃ ⊂ π⁻¹(L), the restriction π|L̃ : L̃ → L (leaf topologies) is surjective and a covering map: a leaf path in L from π(x̃) lifts through the covering π to a path in M̃ from x̃ that stays in plaques over the plaques traversed, hence in L̃, so π(L̃) = L; and each plaque P of L in an evenly covered chart is evenly covered by π|L̃, its preimage in L̃ being the disjoint union of those lifted plaques over P that lie in L̃, each carried homeomorphically onto P. (b3) The covering-space fact used: a covering map from a connected space onto a simply connected, locally path-connected space is a homeomorphism (its fibers are in bijection with the cosets of π₁(L) = 1, so it is one-sheeted, and a one-sheeted covering is a homeomorphism). L ≅ D by (D4) (ledger §16-ter N-3: Reading (iii)′, χ(L) = +1 ⟹ L ≈ R²), so L is simply connected and L̃ ≅ L ≅ D; in particular L̃ is one-ended and π⁻¹(L) has at most deg π ≤ 4 components. Nothing about the hyperbolic metric is used upstairs.

**(c) A proper side lifts — CLOSES.** Printed definitions. Warsaw p. 225 (PDF 234, vision): "Here, semiproper is taken to mean that at least one side of F is proper," (continued p. 226, per adjudication §1.1: "so we include the case in which F is a proper leaf"). Candel–Conlon I p. 133 (PDF 137, vision), §5.3: "Basically, a proper leaf L does not cluster on itself, while a semiproper leaf is allowed to cluster on itself from one side only. This condition only makes sense for transversely-orientable foliations of codimension 1 and is not meant to exclude the possibility that the leaf is proper." — "**Lemma 5.3.2.** A leaf L is semiproper if and only if it is a border leaf of some W ∈ 𝒪(𝓕)." — "**Proof.** Clearly, a leaf L ⊆ δW cannot enter W, hence cannot cluster on itself on the side bordering W. Conversely, if L is semiproper, let J be a transverse arc out of a point x₀ ∈ L that meets L only in {x₀}. The 𝓕-saturation W of int J is an element of 𝒪(𝓕) and L ⊆ δW." Hector–Hirsch B, V;3.1.1 p. 86 (PDF 96, vision): "i) A leaf L of (M,F) is called right-proper if there exists a right-neighbourhood W⁺ of L such that W⁺ ∩ L̄ = L. In the same way we define left-proper leaves. A semi-proper leaf is by definition either right- or left-proper." and "iii) … A good criterion for L ∈ F to be right-proper then is: There exist x ∈ L ∩ Q and a right-neighbourhood [x, u) ⊂ Q of x such that [x, u) ∩ cl(P(x)) = {x}". Candel–Conlon I p. 115 (PDF 121, vision), after Definition 4.3.3: "Thus, L′ is proper if N can be chosen so that N ∩ L′ = {x}."
*The weakest downstairs reading, (S_arc):* there exist x₀ ∈ L and a half-open arc J = [x₀, u) ⊂ M, C¹-embedded and transverse to 𝓕, with J ∩ L = {x₀}. Each printed reading of "a side of L is proper" implies (S_arc) — Warsaw/Candel–Conlon through the transverse arc "out of x₀" in the proof of Lemma 5.3.2, Hector–Hirsch through [x, u) ∩ L̄ = {x} ⟹ [x, u) ∩ L = {x} — so refuting (S_arc) refutes every one of them, and the lemma's "L is not semiproper" is asserted in that strongest form.
*Lift (the arc argument as written, with the point the sketch leaves implicit):* let x̃₀ ∈ π⁻¹(x₀) and L̃ the leaf of 𝓕̃ through x̃₀ (a component of π⁻¹(L), (b1)). Lift J through the covering π to the path J̃ from x̃₀; π|J̃ is injective because J is embedded, and J̃ is a C¹ arc transverse to 𝓕̃ because π is a local diffeomorphism carrying 𝓕̃ to 𝓕. If ỹ ∈ J̃ ∩ L̃ then π(ỹ) ∈ J ∩ π(L̃) = J ∩ L = {x₀} (using π(L̃) = L, (b2)), so ỹ ∈ J̃ ∩ π⁻¹(x₀) = {x̃₀} by injectivity of π|J̃. Hence J̃ ∩ L̃ = {x̃₀}: (S_arc) holds upstairs. *From (S_arc) upstairs to Warsaw's hypothesis:* upstairs 𝓕̃ is transversely oriented on compact M̃ with ∂_⋔M̃ = ∅ (the standing hypotheses of Candel–Conlon I §5.2, p. 126: "with M compact … the foliation is transversely oriented and that ∂_⋔M = ∅"), so Lemma 5.3.2 is in force; its proof's converse direction is exactly (S_arc) ⟹ "L̃ ⊆ δW for W = the 𝓕̃-saturation of int J̃ ∈ 𝒪(𝓕̃)" (one line: W is open and saturated; L̃ ∩ W = ∅ since L̃ ∩ int J̃ = ∅; x̃₀ ∈ cl(W) and cl(W) is saturated — in a foliated chart cl(W) ∩ U is a union of plaques because W ∩ U is — so L̃ ⊆ cl(W) ∖ W = δW), and the direct direction of the Lemma then gives L̃ semiproper: "a leaf L ⊆ δW cannot enter W, hence cannot cluster on itself on the side bordering W" — that side of L̃ is proper, which is Warsaw's "at least one side of F is proper" (Warsaw's [1] is Candel–Conlon I — adjudication §1.3 quotes "[1, Lemma 8.3.23]", "[1, Corollary 8.3.12]" — and the s19 adjudication used §5.3 p. 133 as the definition behind Duminy's hypothesis, §1.6, §2 D4; I read it the same way). So: (S_arc) downstairs ⟹ L̃ semiproper upstairs in the sense Theorem 1.1 uses.

**(d) cl(L̃) ∩ π⁻¹(K) is nonempty, compact, 𝓕̃-saturated — CLOSES.** One-line arguments. Saturated: cl(L̃) is saturated (in an evenly covered foliated chart Ũ ≅ D² × T, L̃ ∩ Ũ = D² × T_L for some T_L ⊂ T, so cl(L̃) ∩ Ũ = D² × cl(T_L), a union of plaques; plaque chains then keep a leaf inside cl(L̃)); π⁻¹(K) is saturated because π maps leaves of 𝓕̃ into leaves of 𝓕 (b1) and K is saturated ((D5): K is a minimal set); an intersection of saturated sets is saturated. Nonempty: π(cl(L̃)) is compact, hence closed in M, and contains π(L̃) = L (b2), hence contains cl_M(L) = L̄ ⊇ K ((D5)); so each k ∈ K has a preimage z ∈ cl(L̃) with π(z) = k, i.e., z ∈ cl(L̃) ∩ π⁻¹(K). **(e) A minimal set K̃ exists in it, is not a compact leaf, and is not open — CLOSES.** Existence: as in Theorem O-5 (`refute-adjudication.md` §4, re-derived there by the binding adjudicator): a nonempty compact 𝓕̃-saturated set contains a minimal set by Zorn's lemma, because a chain of nonempty compact saturated subsets has a nonempty compact saturated intersection (finite intersection property; saturation is preserved by intersection). K̃ ⊆ cl(L̃) ∩ π⁻¹(K) is then a minimal set of 𝓕̃ on M̃ (a nonempty compact saturated set with no proper such subset — the ambient set is irrelevant). *Not a compact leaf:* if K̃ were a single compact leaf, then π(K̃) is compact and, by (b1)–(b2) applied to K̃ in place of L̃, π(K̃) is a whole leaf L′ of 𝓕 (π|K̃ is surjective onto the leaf L′ containing π(K̃): leaf paths lift into K̃); L′ ⊂ K ⊂ L̄ ⊆ N ((D5), (D2)) would be a compact leaf of N, against (D3) "N contains no compact leaf" (Theorem A(C1), dual-model). *Not open:* if K̃ were open in M̃, then π(K̃) is open in M (π is a local homeomorphism, hence an open map), nonempty, and contained in K ⊂ L̄. ## §3. Hypothesis 2 — supplied from the page (Candel–Conlon I §3.5, pp. 94–95)

**Supplied.** The page gives three things. (1) *The orientation double cover of M* (p. 94): "if a connected n-manifold M is not orientable, it has a canonical, connected, 2-fold cover π : M̃ → M such that M̃ is orientable. This is called the orientation cover … The differentiable structure of M is lifted to a differentiable structure of M̃, relative to which the covering projection is locally a diffeomorphism. … Connectedness of M̃ is exactly the condition that M be nonorientable. In a tautological manner, the local orientations of M lift to a canonical orientation of M̃. If there is a foliation 𝓕 on M (of arbitrary codimension), π lifts 𝓕 to a well-defined foliation 𝓕̃ = π*(𝓕)." (2) *The transverse-orientation double cover of 𝓕* (p. 94): "Given a connected, foliated manifold (M, 𝓕), there is a corresponding construction of a 2-fold cover π : M̃ → M such that π*(𝓕) is transversely orientable. One proceeds exactly as above, using local orientations of the normal bundle ν(𝓕) in place of local orientations of T(M). Again, M̃ will be connected if and only if ν(𝓕) is a nonorientable vector bundle." (3) *The composite, as a proposition* (pp. 94–95): "Combining these observations, we have the following. **Proposition 3.5.1.** Let (M, 𝓕) be a foliated manifold, M connected. If M is nonorientable and/or 𝓕 is not transversely orientable, there is a connected covering space (either 2-fold or 4-fold) π : M̃ → M such that M̃ is orientable and the lifted foliation 𝓕̃ is transversely orientable. In this case, the leaves of 𝓕̃ are all orientable." Followed by: "This proposition often allows us to reduce the proof of a theorem to the special case in which everything in sight is orientable. This is especially useful for foliated manifolds of codimension one." (p. 95.)
**Is "at most 4-fold" right?** Yes, as printed: 1-fold when neither orientation fails (no cover needed), otherwise "either 2-fold or 4-fold". The page does not write out the composite construction (it says "Combining these observations") and does not say which case gives 2 and which 4; the lemma needs only existence, connectedness, orientability of M̃ and transverse orientability of 𝓕̃, all of which the Proposition prints. Connectedness of the composite is printed in the Proposition's statement ("a connected covering space"). The sketch's "M̃ oriented, 𝓕̃ transversely oriented" is the page's "orientable / transversely orientable" plus a choice of orientations (a), and the sketch's "at most 4-fold" is the page's "either 2-fold or 4-fold" together with the trivial case.
**What the page does NOT say and the lemma does not need:** that the deck group is Z/2 × Z/2 in the 4-fold case (I inferred it in (i) from the two double covers; used only for the scope remark about periods, load-bearing for nothing); that 𝓕̃ is C² (one-line argument in (a) from "locally a diffeomorphism").
