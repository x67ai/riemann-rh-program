## §1. Definitions as printed (page anchors; vision-verified unless marked OCR)

**1.1 Proper leaf, lim L, asymptotic** — Candel–Conlon I §4.3 p. 115 (PDF 121, vision). Setting: "Let L be a leaf of a compact, foliated manifold (M, 𝓕). Recall that {K_α}_{α∈𝔄} denotes the family of all compact subsets of L. Let W_α = L ∖ K_α and let W̄_α denote the closure of W_α in M." **Definition 4.3.1.** "The *limit set* of L is the set lim L = ⋂_{α∈𝔄} W̄_α. This is also called the *asymptote* of L." **Lemma 4.3.2.** "For each noncompact leaf L of 𝓕, lim L is a compact, nonempty, 𝓕-saturated set." **Definition 4.3.3.** "The leaf L is asymptotic to a leaf F if F ⊆ lim L. The leaf L is *proper* if it is not asymptotic to itself." Thus, L′ is proper if N can be chosen so that N ∩ L′ = {x}." (Same page: Definition 4.3.4 lim_e L, the e-limit set of an end e ∈ ℰ(L); Lemma 4.3.5: lim_e L is compact, nonempty, 𝓕-saturated.)

## §2. The printed theorems bearing on (S), quoted at the page

**2.1 Duminy's theorem and its corollary** — Cantwell–Conlon, Warsaw 2002 (vision, PDF 234–235 = pp. 225–226). Standing hypotheses (p. 225): "Let (M, 𝓕) be a transversely oriented, C²-foliated manifold of codimension one, with M compact and oriented. We assume each component of ∂M, if any, is a leaf of 𝓕. Remark that each leaf of 𝓕 is oriented." **Theorem 1.1 (Duminy)** (p. 225): "If the leaf F of 𝓕 is semiproper and if it accumulates on the exceptional LMS X, then ℰ^X(F) is homeomorphic to the Cantor set." **Corollary 1.2** (p. 226): "If X is an exceptional minimal set of 𝓕 and if F ⊂ X is a semiproper leaf, then ℰ(F) is homeomorphic to the Cantor set." — "Indeed, since X is minimal, ℰ^X(F) = ℰ(F)." **Theorem 1.4 (Duminy)** (p. 226): "If e ∈ ℰ^X(F), then every neighbourhood of e in F contains a complete submanifold that spirals on L with juncture H. Consequently, e is a cluster point of ℰ^X(F) and this set is a Cantor set." (L is "any semiproper leaf of 𝓕 that is approached by a possibly different semiproper leaf F", p. 226.)

**2.2 The conjecture printed right after** (pp. 226–227, vision): "It is natural to conjecture that, if F ⊂ X, the requirement in Theorem 1.1 that F be semiproper can be dropped. This is true for *Markov LMS* [4] but is unknown in general. A weaker conjecture would be that the generic leaf of an exceptional minimal set X have a Cantor set of ends. Here generic can be taken either in the sense of [5] or [7]. It is not hard to show that the generic leaf has one end or a Cantor set of ends." — The last sentence is the one printed sentence on disk that admits **one end** for a (generic, hence non-semiproper) leaf of an exceptional minimal set as an undecided possibility.

**2.3 Lemma 2.1 and its proof** — Warsaw p. 228 (vision, PDF 237). Setting: "Let X ⊂ M be an exceptional LMS of 𝓕, let F be a semiproper leaf that accumulates on X, L a semiproper leaf lying in X." **Lemma 2.1**: "The leaf L lies at finite level and the closure F̄ of F in M accumulates on F from at most one side." **Proof** (verbatim): "No leaf at infinite level can have a proper side [1, Lemma 8.3.23], so F and L each lie at finite level. This means that F lies in a unique LMS Q and Q must either reduce to F itself (and F is a *proper* leaf) or it must be exceptional. In either case, F̄ is a compact, nowhere dense, 𝓕-saturated set containing X. Further, by [1, Corollary 8.3.12], F̄ is a finite union of LMS, each of which is either a proper leaf or an exceptional LMS and none of which, except for Q, can accumulate on F. If Q reduces to F itself, F̄ does not accumulate on F. Alternatively, Q is exceptional and accumulates on F only from the nonproper side of that leaf." Then: "The leaf F accumulates on the semiproper leaf L ⊂ X. Since L accumulates on itself, F accumulates on L from the nonproper side. If F ⊄ X, it is possible that F also accumulates on L from the proper side, but this is immaterial for our purposes." ([1] = Candel–Conlon, *Foliations I*.)

**2.6 The open question, printed a second time, with the analytic and Markov cases** — Candel–Conlon I §8.4 Remark, p. 199 (vision, PDF 201): "Let L be a semiproper leaf in an exceptional minimal set. **2.8 A one-ended, non-semiproper leaf is printed as realizable** — Candel–Conlon I Example 8.3.20, p. 194 (vision, PDF 196): "By contrast, every noncompact, orientable surface occurs as a leaf of a C^∞ foliation in every compact 3-manifold, orientable or not, and every noncompact, nonorientable surface occurs as such a leaf L in every compact, nonorientable 3-manifold [21]. For this, one constructs a sequence {𝓕_k}_{k=1}^∞ of foliations converging to a limit foliation 𝓕. The foliation 𝓕_k contains a leaf L_k at level k + 1 that agrees with L in a compact region B_k. The leaf L appears at infinite level in 𝓕; so it is basically impossible to 'see' this leaf. In this construction, it turns out that one can find a Riemannian metric on M relative to which L has constant curvature −1. In particular, the hyperbolic plane can be realized as a leaf at infinite level in a suitable C^∞ foliation in every compact 3-manifold." With Lemma 8.3.23, such a leaf has no proper side. Whether its closure contains an exceptional minimal set is not stated on the page (Lemma 8.3.21, p. 194: "If there is no finite upper bound to the levels of the totally proper leaves in X_*, then all leaves in X_* are totally proper" — the alternative that the page leaves open).

## §3. L ⊂ K, or L merely accumulates on K? — what the record supplies, and what the printed levels theory adds

**3.1 What Theorem O-5 supplies (verbatim, `refute-adjudication.md` §4).** "**Theorem O-5, corrected [O; re-derived with the hypothesis it needs].** Under (0-coh) [not under (0-fix) alone — there a compact torus leaf in L̄ ∖ L could be the minimal set], with X compact: let L be clause (iii)'s leaf. L̄ is a nonempty compact saturated set, so it contains a minimal set K (Zorn; chains of nonempty compact saturated sets have nonempty compact saturated intersections). K is not a compact leaf (Theorem A(C1)). K is not open in X: by clause (iii) L lies in the closure of ⋃_p γ_p, so L̄ ⊂ cl(⋃γ_p), and a nonempty open K inside that closure would meet some γ_p, but K ⊂ N and γ_p ∩ N = ∅ (§1.3). So K is an **exceptional minimal set** in Hurder's sense (`r3s-29` §5, read: "a minimal set K is exceptional if K is not a compact leaf, and not an open set"), consisting of hyperbolic preserved leaves and carrying no invariant transverse measure. ∎" — So the record supplies **K ⊂ L̄** and nothing about L ⊂ K. Digest §G: "Sub-question 1 is answered by Theorem O-5 (L̄ ⊇ K exceptional; whether L ⊂ K or L merely accumulates on K is what Theorem 1.1 versus Cor. 1.2 distinguishes — Thm 1.1 needs only 'accumulates on the exceptional LMS X')."

**3.2 Which hypothesis each printed statement needs.** Theorem 1.1 (p. 225): F semiproper **and** "accumulates on the exceptional LMS X" — accumulation is enough; K ⊂ L̄ with K a minimal set is accumulation. Corollary 1.2 (p. 226): F ⊂ X, X exceptional minimal. The conjecture on p. 226 ("the requirement in Theorem 1.1 that F be semiproper can be dropped") is stated **only for F ⊂ X**; Hurder's 5.4 ("every leaf of K") likewise. So Theorem O-5's output lands the program in Theorem 1.1's setting, and only the sub-case L ⊂ K lands it in the setting of the printed conjecture.

**3.3 What the levels theory says about the two sub-cases** (Candel–Conlon I §8.3, standing hypotheses C², transversely oriented, codimension one, M compact — p. 187 vision; Warsaw p. 228 for the pattern of use). K is a minimal set of 𝓕 on M, hence at level 0 (Definition 8.3.4). Three mutually exclusive printed positions for L:
  - **(P0) L ⊂ K.** Then L is at level 0 and L̄ = K (K minimal). This is the printed conjecture's setting.
  - **(P1) L ⊄ K and L at finite level k ≥ 1.** Then L is a leaf of a minimal set Q of 𝓕|(M ∖ M_{k−1}) (Definition 8.3.6), i.e. of a local minimal set Q, and by Corollary 8.3.12 L̄ is a finite union of local minimal sets, K among them. By the trichotomy on p. 176, Q is a single (proper) leaf, an open saturated set, or an exceptional LMS. The open case is excluded for the contract's L by a two-line inference that no source prints (flagged as mine): L ⊂ cl(⋃γ_p) (clause (iii)) with γ_p ∩ N = ∅ gives L ⊂ cl(M ∖ N), so L ∩ int N = ∅, whereas an open LMS Q ∋ L would satisfy Q ⊂ L̄ ⊂ N and so Q ⊂ int N. The single-leaf case (Q = L proper, Proposition 8.1.19) is killed by Duminy — see §3.4. What remains under (P1): **L a non-proper leaf of an exceptional LMS Q at level k ≥ 1, with K ⊂ L̄ ∖ Q at level 0.** Neither the Warsaw conjecture nor Hurder 5.4 is stated for this configuration (both require F ⊂ X); Theorem 1.1 covers it only when L is semiproper.
  - **(P∞) L at infinite level** (L in no local minimal set — Corollary 8.3.10 with Definition 8.3.6). Then by Lemma 8.3.23 L "does not have a proper side", so L is **not** semiproper; by Corollary 8.3.12 L̄ contains a local minimal set at every level; K is one at level 0. No printed statement on disk addresses the ends of an infinite-level leaf; the one printed example (Example 8.3.20, p. 194) realizes the hyperbolic plane as such a leaf in a C^∞ foliation of every compact 3-manifold, and says nothing about exceptional minimal sets in its closure.

**3.4 The one sub-case print does decide: L is never proper, and (S) cannot hold.** In every position (P0), (P1), (P∞): if L were semiproper — in particular if L were proper — then, since L accumulates on the exceptional LMS K, Theorem 1.1 (p. 225) gives ℰ^K(L) homeomorphic to the Cantor set; but (D4) makes L ≅ D, a leaf with exactly one end, so ℰ^K(L) ⊆ ℰ(L) has at most one point. Contradiction. Hence **for every object of the contract the archimedean leaf is non-proper and non-semiproper: (S) fails.** This uses Duminy's standing hypotheses — C², codimension one, transversely oriented 𝓕, compact oriented M, boundary components leaves (p. 225). The contract's (H0)-manifold prints "closed 3-manifold, C² codimension-one foliation" and does not print orientability or transverse orientability; the passage to a finite cover that would supply them is not printed by Cantwell–Conlon (a01-12 §3.1 flags this as the ingest agent's inference) and is not made here. **Rider:** the statement above holds as printed for transversely oriented 𝓕 on oriented M; for the general case the contract must add those two hypotheses or record the cover argument as a program lemma.

**3.5 Consequence for the gate's manifold face.** With (S) refuted for objects, an object of the contract exists on a closed 3-manifold only if a **non-semiproper one-ended leaf** accumulates on (P1, P∞) or lies in (P0) an exceptional minimal set of a C² transversely oriented codimension-one foliation.
