## §2. The disagreements and near-disagreements, re-derived from the pages

**D1. Naming of the branches where L ⊄ K.** Scout F (§0, §3.3, §5 statement 3): the case L ⊄ K — L a non-proper leaf of an exceptional LMS at level k ≥ 1, or L at infinite level — is "OPEN and UNNAMED", because "Thm 1.1 needs semiproperness and the conjecture needs F ⊂ X". Scout O (§3): the level-k ≥ 1 branch is "the same configuration, at the LMS generality in which Duminy's Theorem 1.1 and the Warsaw conjecture are stated"; the infinite-level branch is "not even conjectured", with Warsaw p. 258 ("For x not on a semi-proper leaf there are no results") as the printed acknowledgment.

*Ruling, from the pages.* (a) **The three branches are exhaustive and each is what the scouts say it is.** Definition 8.3.6 (p. 189) puts every leaf at level 0, at some finite level k ≥ 1, or at infinite level. Level 0 (Definition 8.3.4): L belongs to a minimal set Y of 𝓕; L̄ ⊆ Y and Y minimal give L̄ = Y; K ⊆ L̄ = Y with K minimal gives K = Y, so **L ⊂ K = L̄**. Level k ≥ 1 (Theorem 8.3.5(1)): L lies in a minimal set Q of 𝓕|(M ∖ M_{k−1}), a local minimal set (Definition 8.1.17 with U = M ∖ M_{k−1} ∈ 𝒪(𝓕)) with Q ⊆ L̄; by the trichotomy on p. 176, Q is a single leaf — then Q = L is proper (Proposition 8.1.19), hence semiproper, hence excluded by statement 1 — or an element of 𝒪(𝓕) — excluded because a nonempty open subset of L̄ ⊂ cl(⋃γ_p) meets some γ_p while Q ⊂ L̄ ⊂ N and γ_p ∩ N = ∅ (the two-line inference both scouts flag as theirs; it uses only clause (iii) and (D1), and I accept it) — or an **exceptional LMS containing L**, with K ⊂ L̄ ∖ Q at level 0. Infinite level: L in no LMS (Corollary 8.3.10 with Definition 8.3.6), no proper side (Lemma 8.3.23), an LMS in L̄ at every level (Corollary 8.3.12). (b) **The level-k ≥ 1 branch IS named in print.** The conjecture on p. 226 reads "if F ⊂ X, the requirement in Theorem 1.1 that F be semiproper can be dropped", and X there is Theorem 1.1's X — an exceptional **local** minimal set; the next words, "This is true for Markov LMS [4]", fix the reading. With X = Q and F = L the conjecture asserts that ℰ^Q(L) is a Cantor set; L has one end. So the level-k ≥ 1 configuration is the negation of the printed conjecture in exactly the generality it is printed in. Scout F's "unnamed" for this branch rests on reading X as the minimal set K and is **withdrawn**; Scout O's reading stands, with one precision: the branch is named by Warsaw p. 226 alone — Hurder's Problem 5.4 ("every leaf of K", K an exceptional *minimal* set) and Candel–Conlon I p. 199 ("every leaf of an exceptional minimal set") are stated for the minimal case and name only the level-0 branch. (c) **The infinite-level branch is unnamed** — both scouts agree in substance, and I confirm it: no sentence on disk conjectures anything about the ends of a leaf at infinite level, and Example 8.3.20 (p. 194) shows one end is realizable there. But Scout O's chosen citation is out of scope: the Remark on p. 258 sits under the definition "Fix x ∈ C and let L be the leaf of 𝓕 containing x. The holonomy group of L relative to X is the group H_x(L, X) …" and under Dippolito's question about leaves L ⊂ X; "For x not on a semi-proper leaf there are no results" is a statement about H_x(L, X) for leaves of the LMS X — not about ends, and not about leaves outside X. It may not be quoted as the printed acknowledgment that the infinite-level case is unconjectured. The honest citation for that branch is the absence itself, recorded in §3 statement 3.

**D2. Scout O's statement 2 — "a semiproper leaf cannot have trivial holonomy".** Same Remark, same scope (§1.8): L a leaf of the LMS X, the group H_x(L, X). For L ⊂ K (level 0) or L ⊂ Q (level k ≥ 1), L ≅ D is simply connected, every holonomy germ along L is the identity, H_x(L, X) is trivial, and the Remark (backed by the paper's Theorem 3.2, p. 231, "The holonomy of L is unbounded on any side that is approached by F", with F = L allowed — p. 226 "a possibly different semiproper leaf F") gives a second reason L is not semiproper. At infinite level L lies in no LMS and the Remark does not apply. *Ruling:* statement 2 is a valid corroboration for the two finite-level branches only; its "for every object of the contract" is narrowed to "for every object whose archimedean leaf lies in an exceptional LMS". - **STATEMENT.** Let (M, 𝓕, φ) satisfy the contract of digest §I in its manifold case — M a closed 3-manifold, 𝓕 a C² codimension-one foliation, transversely oriented, M oriented (the hypotheses of the printed instruments; S19-7), φ a C¹ foliated flow with (i)–(iv) and clause (0) — so that (D1)–(D6) hold: the archimedean leaf L ≅ D (Reading (iii)′, χ = +1) has one end, and L̄ contains an exceptional minimal set K (Theorem O-5). **Then L is not semiproper: L has no proper side; equivalently (Candel–Conlon I Lemma 5.3.2, p. 133) L is not a border leaf of any open saturated set.** Proof: Duminy's Theorem 1.1 as printed by Cantwell–Conlon (Warsaw 2002, p. 225 — "If the leaf F of 𝓕 is semiproper and if it accumulates on the exceptional LMS X, then ℰ^X(F) is homeomorphic to the Cantor set") with F = L, X = K would make the set ℰ^K(L) of ends of L asymptotic to K a Cantor set; ℰ(L) is one point, that point is asymptotic to K (K ⊆ lim L), so ℰ^K(L) is one point. **Consequences, all printed:** L is not proper (a proper leaf is semiproper, pp. 225–226); if L is at infinite level this is also Candel–Conlon I Lemma 8.3.23 (p. 195); the route "(S) ⟹ (b-NO)" of digest §I is dead for every object. **What survives is a non-semiproper one-ended leaf in an exceptional LMS (levels 0 or k ≥ 1) or at infinite level** — the first two are the negation of the Cantwell–Conlon conjecture (Warsaw p. 226; Hurder 5.4 in the minimal case), the third is unconjectured in print; the kill of the object would need K (or Q) Markov (Hurder 5.5, open) or real analyticity (Candel–Conlon I p. 199, outside the contract). In C¹ nothing here applies.
