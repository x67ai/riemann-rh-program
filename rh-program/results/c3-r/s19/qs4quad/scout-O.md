### §2.1 Duminy's theorem — the instrument that refutes (S)

**Theorem 1.1 (Duminy), Warsaw, printed p. 225** (vision, PDF 234) — **digest §J sentence 1, verbatim**:
> "If the leaf F of 𝓕 is semiproper and if it accumulates on the exceptional LMS X, then ℰ^X(F) is homeomorphic to the Cantor set."

**Corollary 1.2, printed p. 226** (vision): "If X is an exceptional minimal set of 𝓕 and if F ⊂ X is a semiproper leaf, then ℰ(F) is homeomorphic to the Cantor set." — with the printed one-line reason: "Indeed, since X is minimal, ℰ^X(F) = ℰ(F)."

**Theorem 1.4 (Duminy), printed p. 226** (vision): "If e ∈ ℰ^X(F), then every neighbourhood of e in F contains a complete submanifold that spirals on L with juncture H. Consequently, e is a cluster point of ℰ^X(F) and this set is a Cantor set."

**Abstract, printed p. 225** (vision): "In 1977 Gerard Duminy proved that, if F is a semiproper leaf of a C² codimension-one foliation 𝓕 of a compact n-manifold M and X is an exceptional local minimal set of 𝓕, then the set ℰ^X(F) of ends of F asymptotic to X, if nonempty, is homeomorphic to a Cantor set. No proof of this remarkable result has ever appeared, even in preprint form. Here, we offer a proof of our own."

**How it applies to the contract, using only (D4), (D5) and the printed definitions.** (D4) gives L ≅ D, so L has exactly one end: ℰ(L) is a single point (Prop. 4.2.4, p. 113, makes ℰ(L) a compact totally disconnected space; for the disk it is a point). ℰ^K(L) is by definition (Warsaw p. 225) a **subspace of ℰ(L)**, so it has at most one point. A Cantor set is perfect and uncountable. Hence the conclusion of Theorem 1.1 is unavailable, so its hypothesis fails: **L is not semiproper, or L does not accumulate on K.** (D5) supplies K ⊂ L̄ with K exceptional minimal, i.e. L accumulates on K (§3 below settles this in the printed vocabulary of Definitions 4.3.1/4.3.3). Therefore **L is not semiproper**. Nothing here needs C¹ vs C² beyond Duminy's own hypothesis, which the contract's manifold case supplies as (H0)-manifold with the C² reading.

### §2.2 Level theory — what is printed about the leaves that are *not* semiproper

**Lemma 8.3.23, Candel–Conlon I, printed p. 195** (vision, PDF 197), the statement the Warsaw paper cites:
> "*If L is at infinite level, then each leaf L′ ⊂ L̄ at infinite level is dense in L̄ and does not have a proper side.*"

Last line of its proof, printed p. 196 (vision, PDF 198): "Since L′ approaches every leaf of ⋃_{k=N}^∞ X_k ∩ δU_k, it follows that L′ is asymptotic to itself on both sides."

**Corollary 8.3.24, printed p. 196** (vision): "*Let L be a leaf at infinite level, x ∈ L, and let J be an ℒ–plaque through x. Then, the pseudogroup Γ_L on J, induced by the holonomy around loops on L based at x, has fixed points clustering at x from both sides.*"

**Remark following it, printed p. 196** (vision) — this is the sentence that kills the "L ≅ D is simply connected, hence has trivial holonomy, hence cannot sit at infinite level" route:
> "Although a leaf at infinite level must have trivial infinitesimal holonomy, one can construct examples to show that the *germinal* holonomy of such a leaf may be nontrivial."

i.e. the book records only that infinite level forces **trivial infinitesimal** holonomy — exactly what a simply connected leaf already has. **Corollary 8.3.25, printed p. 196**: "*If the foliation 𝓕 is of class C^r, 2 ≤ r ≤ ∞, then 𝓕 is C^r–flat along any leaf L at infinite level.*" **Lemma 8.3.26, printed p. 196**: "*If L is at infinite level, then L̄ contains uncountably many leaves at infinite level.*"

**Warsaw Lemma 2.1 and its proof, printed p. 228** (text layer, script letters restored; PDF 237). Setup on the same page: "Let X ⊂ M be an exceptional LMS of 𝓕, let F be a semiproper leaf that accumulates on X, L a semiproper leaf lying in X."
> "**Lemma 2.1** The leaf L lies at finite level and the closure F̄ of F in M accumulates on F from at most one side."
> "*Proof.* No leaf at infinite level can have a proper side [1, Lemma 8.3.23], so F and L each lie at finite level. This means that F lies in a unique LMS Q and Q must either reduce to F itself (and F is a proper leaf) or it must be exceptional. In either case, F̄ is a compact, nowhere dense, 𝓕-saturated set containing X. Further, by [1, Corollary 8.3.12], F̄ is a finite union of LMS, each of which is either a proper leaf or an exceptional LMS and none of which, except for Q, can accumulate on F. If Q reduces to F itself, F̄ does not accumulate on F. Alternatively, Q is exceptional and accumulates on F only from the nonproper side of that leaf. ∎"

Two things the program must carry from this proof. (α) The **only** printed implication between level and semiproperness runs **semiproper ⇒ finite level**; the converse is not printed and is false in the intended examples (every leaf of an exceptional minimal set is at level 0 by Definition 8.3.4, and Problem 5.4 exists precisely because most of them are not semiproper). (β) If a leaf is semiproper, its closure is a **finite** union of local minimal sets ([1, Cor. 8.3.12]).

**Candel–Conlon I, Theorem 8.3.2, printed p. 188** (vision, PDF 190): "*Let U ∈ 𝒪(𝓕) and let Y ⊆ U be the union of all minimal sets of 𝓕|U. Then Y is closed in U. Furthermore, if U is connected, at most finitely many minimal sets of 𝓕|U are exceptional.*"

### §2.3 The printed configuration that shows (S) is not forced the other way

**Example 8.3.20, Candel–Conlon I, printed p. 194** (vision, PDF 196). Verbatim, the second paragraph:
> "By contrast, every noncompact, orientable surface occurs as a leaf of a C^∞ foliation in every compact 3–manifold, orientable or not, and every noncompact, nonorientable surface occurs as such a leaf L in every compact, nonorientable 3–manifold [21]. **What the program actually proves.** `results/c3-r/s16/qs4prime/refute-adjudication.md` §4, verbatim:
> "**Theorem O-5, corrected [O; re-derived with the hypothesis it needs].** Under (0-coh) [not under (0-fix) alone — there a compact torus leaf in L̄ ∖ L could be the minimal set], with X compact: let L be clause (iii)'s leaf. L̄ is a nonempty compact saturated set, so it contains a minimal set K (Zorn; chains of nonempty compact saturated sets have nonempty compact saturated intersections). K is not a compact leaf (Theorem A(C1)). K is not open in X: by clause (iii) L lies in the closure of ⋃_p γ_p, so L̄ ⊂ cl(⋃γ_p), and a nonempty open K inside that closure would meet some γ_p, but K ⊂ N and γ_p ∩ N = ∅ (§1.3). So K is an **exceptional minimal set** in Hurder's sense (`r3s-29` §5, read: 'a minimal set K is exceptional if K is not a compact leaf, and not an open set'), consisting of hyperbolic preserved leaves and carrying no invariant transverse measure. ∎"

Digest §G states the same in one line: "L̄ contains an exceptional minimal set K in Hurder's sense (Theorem O-5 corrected, §4 — under (0-coh))", and digest §I records it as **(D5)**: "L̄ contains an exceptional minimal set K (Hurder's sense: not a compact leaf, not open) consisting of hyperbolic preserved leaves with no invariant transverse measure."

**So the program supplies K ⊆ L̄ and nothing more.** It does **not** supply L ⊂ K.

**Which of Duminy's two statements that matches.** Theorem 1.1's hypothesis is "*F is semiproper and it accumulates on the exceptional LMS X*"; Corollary 1.2's is "*X is an exceptional minimal set and F ⊂ X is a semiproper leaf*". K ⊆ L̄ means every point of K is in the closure of L, i.e. L accumulates on K in exactly the sense of Definition 4.3.3 (K ⊆ lim L, since K ∩ L is either empty — and then K ⊆ L̄ ∖ L = lim L ∖ L — or all of L). **The program supplies Theorem 1.1's hypothesis, not Corollary 1.2's.** Theorem 1.1 is also the strictly more general statement (X need only be a *local* minimal set, F need not lie in X), so nothing is lost. The digest's contract §I is right to name Theorem 1.1 as the instrument and Corollary 1.2 as the alternative "if L ⊂ K".

**The two cases are exhaustive and both are covered.** K is saturated, so if L ∩ K ≠ ∅ then L ⊆ K, and then L̄ ⊆ K (K closed) while K ⊆ L̄, so **L̄ = K**. Otherwise L ∩ K = ∅ and L accumulates on K from outside it. Theorem 1.1 applies verbatim in both. This is the assembly the brief permits: printed definitions (4.3.1, 4.3.3, Warsaw p. 225) with the program's (D5).

**Which case the residue falls into, in the printed vocabulary of levels.** Under ¬(S) — which §2.1 forces — split by Definition 8.3.6 (p. 189):

- **L at level 0.** By Definition 8.3.4 (p. 189), L belongs to a minimal set Y of 𝓕; then L̄ ⊆ Y and Y minimal gives L̄ = Y, and K ⊆ L̄ = Y with K minimal gives **K = L̄ and L ⊂ K**. The residue is then, word for word, a leaf of an exceptional minimal set that is not semiproper and has one end — i.e. **a counterexample to Hurder's Problem 5.4** and to Cantwell–Conlon's printed conjecture (p. 226).
- **L at level k ≥ 1.** By Theorem 8.3.5(1) (p. 189), L lies in a minimal set Q of 𝓕|(M ∖ M_{k−1}), i.e. in an **LMS** (Warsaw p. 225's definition), and Q ⊆ L̄. Q is not open, because L̄ has empty interior — Theorem O-5's own clause-(iii) argument, quoted above, applies to any nonempty open subset of L̄, not only to K. So Q is either the single proper leaf L (excluded: proper ⇒ semiproper, Warsaw p. 226, contradicting ¬(S)) or an **exceptional LMS containing L**. The residue is again a non-semiproper one-ended leaf of an exceptional local minimal set — the same configuration, at the LMS generality in which Duminy's Theorem 1.1 and the Warsaw conjecture are stated.
- **L at infinite level.** Lemma 8.3.23 (p. 195) then gives, for free, what ¬(S) asserts ("does not have a proper side") and adds that L is dense in L̄; Lemma 8.3.26 (p. 196) adds that L̄ contains uncountably many leaves at infinite level. Here L need not lie in K, so the residue is a **non-semiproper one-ended leaf accumulating on an exceptional minimal set from outside it** — the case for which Cantwell–Conlon print no conjecture at all and record "For x not on a semi-proper leaf there are no results" (p. 258).

**Consequence for the adjudicator.** The naming of the residue depends on this trichotomy, and the trichotomy is not decided by (D1)–(D6). In two of the three branches the residue *is* Hurder's Problem 5.4 (with "minimal" relaxed to "local minimal" in the second); in the third it is strictly weaker than 5.4 — a configuration on which the literature is not merely undecided but silent. The honest naming is therefore: **at best Hurder 5.4, at worst a case the literature has not even conjectured**, and in neither branch is there a printed theorem.

**The one printed lever that would collapse the residue.** If K were **Markov**, the semiproperness hypothesis of Theorem 1.1 could be dropped for leaves of K ("This is true for Markov LMS [4]", Warsaw p. 226; "Cantwell and Conlon showed that if K is Markov …, then all four of the above problems are true [80, 83]", Hurder p. 10), and the manifold face would close at (b-NO). Nothing in (D1)–(D6) bears on it: (D1)–(D6) are statements about closedness, saturation, flow-invariance, non-compactness, hyperbolicity, absence of a holonomy-invariant transverse measure, conformal type, and the rank of H₁(M ∖ N; ℚ) — none of which is, or implies, "the holonomy pseudogroup Γ|K is generated by a one-sided subshift of finite type" (Hurder p. 10). And whether *every* exceptional minimal set is Markov is itself printed as an open problem (**Hurder Problem 5.5**, p. 10). The two Markov papers ([3] Tôhoku 40 (1988) 165–187 and [4] Publ. Mat. 33 (1989) 461–484) are **not on disk**; they are P1 in `FETCH-LIST-ROUND5.md` because they are the only printed route to (b-NO) that survives §2.1.
