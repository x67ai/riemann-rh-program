**Plain-English reading (I infer).** In 2005 Leichtnam printed Deninger's full conformal condition (31) — his (5.2) — as an axiom of the Spec Z conjecture with **no caution whatever**, and then applied it *at the fixed point* (p. 229, "(5.2) (on L/Q^{+*})") to derive e^{−t/2}T_{l_∞}φ^t ∈ SO(2). By 2008 the same author had replaced (5.2) by the pair 3] (12) (the SO_2 conclusion at x_∞, now assumed) + 4] (13) (the cohomological-class version) and had attached the "might be too strong" report. So the 2005 article records the state BEFORE Deninger's caution: the closed-orbit derivative statement (2013's (14)) is present in 2005 only as a *consequence* of (4.3)/(5.2) plus Deninger's rotation observation (p. 224), not as an axiom; and there is no Euler-characteristic or accumulation clause. This answers the residual question left at `s16/qs4prime/adjudication.md` (lines 646–648): **neither was in the 2005 list.**

Two structural differences from the 2008/2013 lists a future session should notice: (i) in 2005 the archimedean fixed point sits on the boundary L/Q^{+*} of a *compactification* \overline{S_Q}, the flow being transverse everywhere on S_Q itself (Conj. 1 (0)); in 2008 x_∞ ∈ S_Q and the flow is transverse to all leaves except the one through x_∞. ### (b) THE KEY QUESTION — does Leichtnam print a REASON for the "too strong" caution?

**The source says nothing of the kind about the Spec Z conjecture.** The phrase "too strong" does not occur in the article. Conjecture 1 (2) states (5.2) (φ^t)^*(g) = e^t g without any caveat, hedge, or remark; the Remarks following Conjecture 1 (pp. 226–227) discuss the compactification, the transverse measure, the sign convention e^{−t}, and the redundancy of part of (3) — not (2). §5.5 (p. 229) *uses* (5.2) at the fixed point to get the SO(2) normal form and to match W_∞. The proof of Theorem 9 (p. 228) uses (5.2) twice (for (5.4) and for the Hodge-star commutation). **No reason for doubting (5.2) is printed anywhere in the 36 pages, and no fixed-point obstruction is mentioned in this connection.** The 2005 Contemp. Math. article predates the caution; the earliest printed report remains math/0603576 (2006/07).

**What the article DOES print — the only passage bearing on the strength of the condition — is the manifold obstruction, p. 214 Remark (2)** (quoted verbatim in the running notes above): "it is known that a flow acting on a smooth compact Riemannian foliated manifold cannot satisfy the condition (2.3), it is thus necessary to consider **laminated** foliated spaces", followed by the degree argument for the suspension case (φ^*(g_1) = C g_1 on a compact manifold forces C = 1 "since it is the absolute value of the degree of the diffeomorphism φ"). This is the same fact Deninger prints at [x-20] p. 27 ("not possible in the manifold setting of corollary 5.5 which actually implies α = 0") and it is the reason the conjecture is set on a *laminated* space. It is a reason (31) is impossible on compact **manifolds** (with no fixed points, in the transverse setting of (2.9)) — **not** a reason it might be too strong on laminated spaces, and it says nothing about fixed points. **I infer:** the s16 N-C finding stands as recorded — the only printed suspicion about (31) in the laminated/fixed-point setting is the private caution reported from 2006/07 on, with no argument; the 2005 article, if anything, shows Leichtnam then regarded (31) + a fixed point (on the boundary leaf-space L/Q^{+*}) as unproblematic, exactly as Deninger does at [x-20] pp. 31–33.

One caution for whoever cites this: Leichtnam's p. 214 sentence is asserted as "it is known" without a reference; the degree argument given is for the suspension N ×_Z R^{+*} only. For the general transverse-flow-on-a-compact-manifold case the printed authority is Deninger's Corollary 5.5 / Remark 2 ([x-20] p. 24; math/0204110 p. 13), which the program already records as unproved there — and Theorem A(B) supplies the proof. Leichtnam's "it is known" is therefore not a new printed proof; **it is one more citation-free assertion of Remark 2's content**, worth adding to the N-B credit line ("asserted also by Leichtnam, Contemp.
