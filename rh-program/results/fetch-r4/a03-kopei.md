## §3. The answer from the source

### §3(b). Kopei's class — verbatim (printed p. 143, Definition 2.1; checked by vision against the page render)

> **Definition 2.1** The category 𝓕𝓓 of foliated dynamical systems is defined as follows: The Objects are triples (M, 𝓕, φ), where
> • M is a (2n + 1)-dimensional compact, connected, smooth manifold with a 1-codimensional foliation 𝓕 by complex manifolds. If the boundary of M is not empty, we require that it consists of leaves.
> • φ : M × ℝ → M is a smooth flow which preserves leaves (i.e. φ^t is a foliated map for all t ∈ ℝ) and which is transverse up to a finite number of compact leaves. The latter means that there are compact leaves K₁, …, K_k, such that for all t and all i we have φ^t(K_i) = K_i and
>   φ^t : M ∖ (⋃_{i=1}^k K_i) → M ∖ (⋃_{i=1}^k K_i)
> is transverse.
> The morphisms are smooth maps f from M′ to M, such that
> • around each point p ∈ M′ and f(p) ∈ M we can choose foliated coordinates, such that locally (depending if p and f(p) are in the boundary or not)
>   f(z, x) = (f^x(z), x) for f : 𝔻^{n′} × ℝ → 𝔻^n × ℝ; (f^x(z), x) for f : 𝔻^{n′} × ℝ⁺₀ → 𝔻^n × ℝ⁺₀; (f^x(z), |x|) for f : 𝔻^{n′} × ℝ → 𝔻^n × ℝ⁺₀,
> where f^x is holomorphic for fixed x. Here ℝ⁺₀ = {x ∈ ℝ : x ≥ 0}.
> • f commutes with the flows, i.e. f ∘ φ^t_{M′} = φ^t_M ∘ f for all t ∈ ℝ.
> Let dim M = dim M′ = 3 and let l′ ⊂ M′ be a leaf. A point p ∈ l′ is called a ramification point of f if p is a ramification point of f|_{l′} : l′ → f(l′).
> • If dim M = dim M′ = 3 we require in addition, that the ramification points are contained in a finite union of closed orbits.

> **Remark 2.2** Note that the transversality implies that ∂M ⊂ ⋃_{i=1}^k K_i.

> **Notation 2.3** We will write often M ∈ 𝓕𝓓^{2n+1} to denote (M, 𝓕, φ) ∈ Ob(𝓕𝓓) with dim M = 2n + 1. We will just write compact leaf to indicate one of the compact leaves K₁, …, K_k where the flow is not transverse. Similarly we will write only closed orbit to indicate a transverse closed orbit. We denote the length of a closed orbit γ, i.e. the first return time, by l(γ).

**What the definition requires and does not require (the source says, p. 143):** compact connected smooth manifold, odd dimension; codimension-one foliation by complex manifolds (Riemann surfaces when dim M = 3); boundary, if any, a union of leaves; a **smooth** flow that is **foliated** (leaf-preserving in the sense "φ^t is a foliated map") and **transverse to 𝓕 off a finite set of compact leaves**, each of which is flow-invariant. **No fixed-point hypothesis. No non-degeneracy hypothesis. No metric, no conformality, no condition on the flow restricted to the compact leaves, no condition on the genus of the compact leaves, no density hypothesis on leaves, no transverse-measure hypothesis.** Metrics enter only later, as data on line bundles ("hermitian vector bundle", Definition 2.37, p. 154) and on the leafwise tangent bundle ("metrized 𝓕𝓓³-manifolds", Definition 2.45, p. 157), never as a hypothesis on the dynamics.

**The fixed-point sentence (printed p. 144, Remark 2.5; checked by vision):**

> **Remark 2.5** Note that Deninger uses rather fixed points in compact leaves than compact leaves itself as an analogue of infinite primes. So the analogues of finite and infinite primes here are defined quite similarly to each other, which corresponds nicely to the fundamental unification of primes in Arakelov theory. Nevertheless, in our context we do not need these fixed points for any proof and therefore do not require that the flow has fixed points at all. However, if the flow has fixed points, by transversality they have to be in the compact leaves. Please note that seeing compact leaves as analogues to infinite primes corresponds to an idea of Ramachandran which suggests that infinite primes correspond to ends of manifolds (see [10]). The transfer to the model of Ramachandran is done by rather looking at M ∖ {K₁, …, K_n} than looking at M itself. Note furthermore that in Deninger's analogy near fixed points corresponding to real primes one should have an orbifold structure and a flow-invariant (orbifold) embedding ι : ℝ/{±1} ≅ ℝ^{≥0} → M where ι(0) is the fixed point. For the details we refer to [10]. This is somewhat similar to our assumption that a compact leaf corresponding to a real prime is in the boundary. In the following the latter assumption will be necessary to guarantee that the geometric formulas agree with the algebraic ones.

**So the list's description of the paper is wrong in one word.** The list (and adjudication §4 item 2) calls it "the only extended treatment of Deninger's manifold class with compact non-transverse leaves *carrying fixed points*". The source says the opposite: Kopei **drops the fixed points** ("do not require that the flow has fixed points at all") and replaces Deninger's "fixed point in a compact leaf" by the compact leaf itself. The only fixed-point statement in the whole paper is the one-line triviality "if the flow has fixed points, by transversality they have to be in the compact leaves".

**The dictionary (printed p. 144, paragraph before Remark 2.5):**
> "Similar to the dictionary of Deninger [14] our analogue for the ring 𝔬 of integers of an algebraic number field is an element M ∈ 𝓕𝓓³. The closed orbits γ should correspond to the finite primes 𝔭, such that l(γ) = log 𝒩(𝔭). The compact leaves should correspond to the infinite primes. Here compact leaves in the boundary should correspond to infinite primes coming from real embeddings. Compact leaves which are not in the boundary should correspond to primes coming from complex embeddings. This suggests to define 𝒩(γ) := e^{l(γ)} and 𝒩(K_i) = e^{f_{K_i}}, where f_{K_i} = 1 if K_i is in the boundary, and f_{K_i} = 2 otherwise."

**The 1-form (printed p. 146, Definition 2.14; checked by vision):**
> **Definition 2.14** Let M ∈ 𝓕𝓓³. Let ω ∈ Ω¹(M ∖ ⋃_i K_i) be the smooth 1-form which is defined by ω|_𝓕 ≡ 0 and ω(φ̇⁰) = ω(∂/∂t|_{t=0} φ^t) ≡ 1. To a non-zero 𝓕𝓓³-meromorphic function f we assign the 𝓕𝓓-Weil divisor
>   div(f) := (f)_{𝓕𝓓} := Σ_γ ord_γ f · γ + Σ_K (1/f_K)(∫_{∂TK} d^c log|f|² ∧ ω) K,

Kopei **uses** "ω is closed" twice (p. 155, proof of Proposition 2.40: "Using that ω is closed we compute by an application of the theorem of Poincaré–Lelong"; p. 158, proof of Theorem 2.49: "Using that ω is closed, for fixed l and α we …") but **never states it as a lemma and never proves it**. This is the form later printed with proof as KMNT Lemma 1.9 (published p. 328) and already present in Kopei's 2006 note (math/0605184 p. 2, per sweep F).

### §3(d). Kopei on Deninger — his own words, and his bibliography entries

**Where he places himself relative to Deninger (p. 142, Introduction):** "So, extending the work of Deninger, in the first section we define objects in the foliated world having properties similar to objects in the world of number fields like ramification and interia indices, Arakelov divisors and the Euler characteristic. In fact we use foliations rather than foliated spaces due to reasons which we explain in Remark 2.7." … "In this article, extending results of Deninger, we present a nice and surprising analogy between two quite different areas of mathematics: foliation theory and arithmetic geometry. **Abstract (p. 141):** "We also study higher dimensional foliations which have properties analogous to arithmetic surfaces. This provides more evidence for a conjecture of Deninger."

### §3(e). Plain-English reading, claim by claim

**The headline (the source says):** Kopei 2011 is an Arakelov-theory paper — divisors, degrees, coverings, Hurwitz, Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch, intersection numbers, adjunction — on the manifold class of Definition 2.1. It contains **no dynamics beyond the definition of the class**: no trace formula, no period group, no length group, no rank or finite-generation statement, no transverse-measure theorem, no fixed-point analysis, no statement about which surfaces the compact leaves are, no conformality or scaling hypothesis. It is not "an extended treatment of the manifold class with compact non-transverse leaves carrying fixed points"; it is an extended treatment of Arakelov-style invariants on a class in which fixed points are **explicitly not required** (Remark 2.5).

**N-A (Thm A (A)+(B) — transverse measures on the preserved-leaf set; α = 1 ⇒ none).** The paper prints exactly one transverse-measure sentence: "In the case where we do not have compact leaves ω induces a transverse measure" (p. 156), i.e. in the everywhere-transverse case, where ω is the closed form with ω|_𝓕 = 0, ω(φ̇) = 1 — the same supply of an invariant transverse measure the adjudication already credits to KMNT Lemma 1.9 (adjudication §1 N-A item 2, "What removing transversality removes is the supply of the invariant measure"). Kopei does not say the measure is flow-invariant, says nothing about the case with compact leaves, nothing about α or the scaling of [λ_g], nothing negative. **Does not bear on N-A**; it is at most a second, earlier printed instance of the KMNT supply, and adds no statement the adjudication does not already have. I infer: N-A's PARTIAL grade and its stated printed relatives are unchanged.

**N-D (B1′ — finite rank of the transverse closed-orbit length group for a finite-type tangency set).** The adjudication credits "the form ω and the group Λ_S in KMNT (published Lemma 1.9, Def. 1.10) and, earlier, in Kopei math/0605184 p. 2". The 2011 paper confirms the first half and not the second: it defines ω (Definition 2.14, p. 146), uses its closedness without proof (pp. 155, 158), and prints the cut-along-compact-leaves / boundary-of-tubular-neighbourhood device (Definition 2.38, Proposition 2.40, pp. 154–155; Appendix pp. 187–188 showing the boundary terms do not even have a limit as the neighbourhood shrinks). It **never defines a period group or a length group**, never mentions rank or finite generation, and never confronts orbit lengths with {log p} beyond the dictionary line "l(γ) = log 𝒩(𝔭)" (p. 144). **Does not bear on N-D** beyond what the 2006 note already supplied. The word "period" does not occur in the paper.

**N-F (A-II narrowed — under S4′ + (0-fix) a compact preserved leaf is a fixed-point-free torus; the printed contrast that "in Deninger's, Kopei's, KMNT's and ÁLKL's manifold class the archimedean fixed points lie on compact preserved leaves").** Two corrections to the record, both from the source:
  (1) The contrast sentence, as it names Kopei, is **inaccurate**. Kopei's class does not put fixed points on compact leaves; it has no fixed points in its definition, and Remark 2.5 says "we … do not require that the flow has fixed points at all. However, if the flow has fixed points, by transversality they have to be in the compact leaves." The correct wording for Kopei is: *in Kopei's class the compact non-transverse leaves themselves are the infinite primes, fixed points are optional, and if present they lie in the compact leaves* (p. 144). Sweep F's line "Kopei p. 3" (math/0605184) recorded the same thing for the 2006 note; the 2011 paper is consistent with it. The record should cite Kopei only for the weak form "fixed points, if any, lie in compact leaves".
  (2) **Kopei's own Reeb example (Lemma 4.2, p. 180) is, I infer, an example of N-F's arrangement rather than of the printed contrast**: the compact leaf is a torus, and on it the flow is (ϕ, ψ) ↦ (ϕ + (α + β)t, φ^t_ψ(ψ)) — for α + β ≠ 0 a fixed-point-free flow on a torus, exactly "a fixed-point-free torus". When it does have fixed points (α + β = 0, φ_ψ with a fixed point) they are circles, so never leafwise sources — again consistent with N-F. Kopei never remarks on the genus of the compact leaf or on fixed points there; footnote 1, p. 151 mentions the torus only for the covering property. **Nothing in Kopei covers any part of N-F's statement**; it stays NOVEL. But the contrast to be printed with N-F must be re-worded for Kopei as above.

**N-G (N1 — tangency set a finite union of compact leaves ⇒ finite-rank length group ⇒ S4′ (i)+(ii) fail).** Sweep F's stated hope was "a printed finite-generation statement or a compact-leaf/fixed-point analysis would move N-G toward ANTICIPATED in the compact-leaf case". **There is no such statement.** The only rank statements are ranks of vector bundles (Definition 2.37, p. 154; Theorem 2.63, p. 163) and the "rk(O_D)" of the Reeb Riemann–Roch (Proposition 4.22, Theorem 4.23, pp. 186–187). Corollary 4.3 (p. 180) constructs "a countable infinite number of closed transverse orbits" on the Reeb foliation — and, I infer from Lemma 4.2, all of them have length 1/α, so the length group is (1/α)ℤ: an **instance** consistent with N-G's conclusion in the compact-leaf class, printed without the observation. Kopei defines his class exactly as N-G's hypothesis (finitely many compact non-transverse leaves, no further condition — broader than ÁLKL's transverse simplicity, since he has no non-degeneracy) but proves nothing about periods in it. **N-G stays PARTIAL on ÁLKL/KMNT/Deninger; Kopei adds no printed relative.** The one thing worth adding to N-G's credit line: Kopei 2011 Definition 2.1 is the earliest printed *definition* of the compact-leaf class in exactly N-G's generality (finitely many flow-invariant compact leaves, transverse elsewhere, no non-degeneracy) — as a class, not as a theorem about it.

**Summary verdict for the adjudication's HIGH rating:** the fetch answered the question in the negative. Kopei 2011 contains none of the feared printed relatives; none of N-A, N-D, N-F, N-G moves. What it does contain, and what the record gets wrong: (i) the class is defined **without** fixed points (Remark 2.5) — the list's and adjudication §4 item 2's description "compact non-transverse leaves carrying fixed points" must be corrected, and the N-F contrast sentence must not attribute "fixed points on compact leaves" to Kopei as a hypothesis; (ii) Kopei cites Deninger–Singhof Contemp. Math. 290 as [15] and says of foliated trace formulas "They are known only in the transverse case" (p. 156) — a 2011 printed statement that no trace formula with compact non-transverse leaves was known to him, consistent with the adjudication's finding that the α = 0 mechanism is printed nowhere in that class.

## §4. Anything else a future session should know exists

- p. 145, Remark 2.7: printed argument that on Deninger's Cantor-transversal foliated spaces "meromorphic functions … cannot have their zeros and poles only along finitely many closed orbits" — a stated obstruction to running Arakelov-style divisor theory on laminations.
- p. 148, Theorem 2.26 + p. 149 proof: for an 𝓕𝓓³-covering, the length ratio l(Γ)/l(γ) of a closed orbit over its image is an integer (the "inertia index"); summation formula Σ e_{Γ|γ} f_{Γ|γ} = [M′ : M]; credited to Sunada [35] for the unramified case.
- p. 154 Definition 2.38 / Remark 2.39 / Appendix pp. 187–188: the boundary term ∫_{∂TK} η ∧ ω over the boundary of a tubular neighbourhood of a compact non-transverse leaf depends on the neighbourhood and in general has no limit as it shrinks (Example 4.24, ω = dr/(r−1)) — the singularity of ω at a compact leaf is exhibited explicitly.
- p. 156: Connes' index theorem used to define χ(E) via von Neumann dimensions dim_ω in the no-compact-leaf case; "Therefore the Euler characteristic depends on a metric on M"; Remark 2.44 links χ(M) = ½ deg(𝓕M, h) to Deninger's χ_Co(𝓕, ω) = 2χ(M) and −log|d_K|.
- p. 157 Theorem 2.49, p. 159 Corollary 2.50: Riemann–Hurwitz for 𝓕𝓓³-coverings, with closed-orbit lengths weighting the ramification.
- p. 163 Theorem 2.63: a "1-dim, 0-codim Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch" in tangential currents D⁰_τ(M).
- pp. 166–179 (§3): 5-dimensional "𝓕𝓓-arithmetic" fibrations π : M → N over a 3-dimensional N, intersection numbers, adjunction formula (Proposition 3.31, p. 179); Table 1 (p. 165) and Table 2 (p. 179) are Kopei's two dictionaries.
- p. 180 Lemma 4.2 / Corollary 4.3 / Remark 4.4: explicit smooth foliated flows on the Reeb foliation of the solid torus and of S³, transverse off the compact torus leaf, with countably many closed transverse orbits (all of length 1/α by construction).
- p. 181 Lemma 4.9: a meromorphic function continuous across a compact leaf is constant on it.
- pp. 186–187 Proposition 4.22 / Theorem 4.23: a Riemann–Roch theorem for divisors on the Reeb foliation of S³, rk(O_D) − rk(Ω_{−D}) = deg D + χ(𝓕S³, h̄) with χ = 1.
- p. 189 [31]: Kopei cites Moore–Schochet **2nd edition** (2006) for the tangential Chern form / Hurder's appendix; the delivered r4-15b is the 1988 first edition.
