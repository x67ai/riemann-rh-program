## §3b. The answer from Moore–Schochet (2nd ed.), verbatim — Q2

**Correction to the program's pointer:** the tangential de Rham complex and its topology are in **Chapter III (Tangential Cohomology), printed pp. 55–58**, not Chapter II. ### 3b.1 Chapter II — the definitions (printed pp. 31–33; PDF 47–49; text layer + vision on pp. 31, 33)

p. 31: "The local picture of a foliated space is a topological space of the form L × N, where L is a copy of ℝ^p and N is a separable metric space, not necessarily a manifold. A **tangentially smooth function** f : L × N → ℝ is a continuous function with the following properties: (1) For each n ∈ N, the function f(·, n) : L → ℝ is smooth. (2) All partial derivatives of f in the L directions are continuous on L × N. We denote the tangentially smooth functions as C^∞_τ(L × N)." … "Let X be a separable metrizable space. A foliated chart (U, φ) is an open set U ⊆ X and a homeomorphism φ : U → φ(U) ⊆ L × N with φ(U) open in L × N. We define C^∞_τ(U) as the set of all functions f : U → ℝ such that φ^{-1}f ∈ C^∞_τ(L × N). A plaque P of X is a set of the form φ^{-1}(L × {n})."

p. 32: "Using [Candel and Conlon 2000, Lemma 11.2.9], we may assume without loss of generality that our atlas 𝒰 is regular; that is: (1) Given a foliated chart (U, φ) ∈ 𝒰, there exists a foliated chart (V, ψ) ∈ 𝒰 with Ū ⊆ T ⊆ V, T compact in V, and ψ|U = φ. … (2) The open cover {U_α} is locally finite. (3) Given foliated charts (U, φ) and (V, ψ) and plaque P ⊆ U, then P meets at most one plaque in V." … "**Definition 2.1.** A foliated space X of dimension p is a separable metrizable space equipped with a regular foliated atlas 𝒰 = {(U_α, φ_α)} such that whenever U_α ∩ U_β ≠ ∅ then the composition φ_α(U_α ∩ U_β) → U_α ∩ U_β → φ_β(U_α ∩ U_β) is tangentially smooth."

pp. 32–33: "In particular, we may take X_2 = ℝ or ℂ and let C^∞_τ(X) to denote the set of tangentially smooth (real or complex-valued) functions on the foliated space X. Note that C^∞_τ(X) is a commutative algebra, unital if X is compact, and **normed by the sup norm. It is almost never complete in that norm.**" (p. 33, verified by vision.)

p. 36 (PDF 52): the tangent bundle: "these coalesce to form a p-plane bundle over X, called the tangent bundle or foliation bundle of the foliated space and denoted p : FX → X." p. 37: "A vector bundle π : E → X of (real) dimension k over a foliated space X of dimension p is tangentially smooth if E has the structure of a foliated space of dimension p + k which is compatible with the local product structure of the bundle and if the map π : E → X is tangentially smooth. The tangent bundle is tangentially smooth. We let Γ_τ(E) or Γ_τ(X, E) denote the C^∞_τ(X)-module of tangentially smooth sections of the bundle E → X."

p. 38 (PDF 54), the only topology on function spaces in Ch. ### 3b.4 Plain-English reading for the f1-check question

**Is the C⁰ sup-norm among the seminorms defining the topology?** *The source says* (p. 57, p. 70): the topology is uniform convergence, on compact subsets of coordinate patches, of the coefficient functions a and all their tangential derivatives. The order-zero member of that family — uniform convergence of a itself on a compact subset K of a patch — is exactly the local sup-seminorm sup_K |a|. So, in the sense the f1-check note needs ("τ is finer than the sup-norm topology"), **yes**: convergence in Moore–Schochet's topology implies uniform convergence of the coefficient functions on compacta of every patch. *I infer* (elementary, not printed): on a **compact** X a finite regular atlas with compact plaque-closures covers X (p. 32 regular-atlas condition (1) gives Ū ⊆ T ⊆ V with T compact), any continuous fiber norm on Λ^k(F*) is comparable with the coefficient Euclidean norm on each compact chart piece, hence the global sup-norm ‖ω‖_∞ is dominated by finitely many of the defining seminorms and is a continuous seminorm for the topology. This is the same inference the note already makes for the manifold case (§4.1) and it carries over verbatim; nothing in Moore–Schochet obstructs it.

**Is it "the natural Fréchet topology"?** *The source says* nothing about Fréchet. *I infer*: with a countable family of seminorms (countably many patches of a regular locally finite atlas on a separable metrizable X, an exhaustion of each patch by countably many compacta, derivatives of all orders) the topology is that of a countable seminorm family, hence metrizable locally convex; completeness holds because uniform limits on compacta of C^∞-in-x families with all x-derivatives converging uniformly are again tangentially smooth (continuity in n of the limits is inherited from uniform convergence) — but **this is my inference, standard and easy, not a sentence in the book.** For the record: Deninger's phrase "the natural Fréchet topology" ([x-20] p. 14, p. 29) is Deninger's; Moore–Schochet's own words are "topological vector space" (p. 57) and "topologized by demanding … uniform convergence … on compact subsets" (pp. 57, 70). The program's record (`f1-check-C0-seminorm-s17.md` §4.2, §6) described the topology as "uniform convergence of tangential derivatives on compacta" — **that recall is CORRECT as printed**; its location was misremembered as Chapter II (it is Chapter III, p. 57, restated p. 70), and the edition on disk is the 2nd (so the citation should read "Moore–Schochet, 2nd ed., CUP 2006, Ch. III p. 57", with the 1988 page unknown from this file).

**Contradiction with the program's record?** None of substance. Two pointer corrections: (i) Chapter III, not II; (ii) the "sup norm" that Moore–Schochet do name explicitly (p. 33) is on C^∞_τ(X) as a *normed algebra*, with the remark that it is "almost never complete in that norm" — this is consistent with, and independent of, the finer topology on Ω^k_τ(X); it must not be quoted as "the topology on tangential forms is the sup norm."

### (a) Candel–Conlon, *Foliations I* (GSM 23, 2000) — read by vision

1. **Lemma 1.2.16, p. 29** (foliated manifolds): "*Every regular foliated atlas of codimension q is associated to a unique foliation 𝓕 of codimension q.*" Its proof contains the Epstein-2.2 ingredient, verbatim: "The main problem is to show that L is 2nd countable. Since each plaque is 2nd countable, the same will hold for L if we show that the set of 𝒰-plaques in L is at most countably infinite. Fix one such plaque P_0. By the definition of a regular, foliated atlas, P_0 meets only finitely many other plaques. That is, there are only finitely many plaque chains {P_0, P_1} of length 1. By induction on the length p of plaque chains that begin at P_0, we prove similarly that there are only finitely many of length ≤ p. Since every 𝒰-plaque in L is, by the definition of ∼, reached by a finite plaque chain starting at P_0, the assertion follows."
