### 1.3 MATERIAL — D3's clause (6), "is the on-line summand one-signed?", is decidable now, and the answer is NO

Rank 1's contract clause (6) (line 286) and stop line (v) (line 212) ask whether the M6 sign mechanism transfers to the μ-side datum. Two independent grounds say it does not.

**(a) Reader's derivation from standard facts.** Write ξ(s) = H(s)ζ(s) with H(s) = ½s(s−1)π^{−s/2}Γ(s/2), and Ξ(z) = ξ(½ + iz). Ξ is real on the real axis and even. At a simple on-line zero ρ = ½ + iγ we have ξ′(ρ) = −iΞ′(γ). Between consecutive simple real zeros Ξ changes sign, so Ξ′(γ) alternates in sign along the zeros. The residue of 1/ξ at ρ is 1/ξ′(ρ) = i/Ξ′(γ): purely imaginary, with alternating sign. The residue of 1/ζ is H(ρ)/ξ′(ρ): the same alternation rotated by the archimedean phase arg H(½ + iγ). With the M6 test g = f ⋆ f̃, ĝ(γ) = |h_f(γ)|² ≥ 0 on real γ (`separation-note.md` §0.1 line 20). So the on-line summand of the μ-side datum is ĝ(γ)·(residue). Its sign is set by the residue, and that sign is not fixed. The same functional-equation symmetry that makes the datum real also forces each off-line pair's contribution into the same line as the on-line terms. So no real-part or imaginary-part split separates them either (ξ′(1 − ρ̄) = −conj ξ′(ρ); this holds on-line and for any off-line pair alike). **No on-line sign property, so no refutation channel.**

**(b) The published object, opened this session.** A. M. Odlyzko and H. J. J. te Riele, "Disproof of the Mertens conjecture", J. reine angew. Math. 357 (1985). Fetched 2026-09-25 from `https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~odlyzko/doc/arch/mertens.disproof.pdf` (a DigiZeitschriften scan; the 2.3 MB PDF is saved in this session's tool-results folder, not in the project) and read as page images, pp. 138–141. p. 139: "In this paper we will disprove the Mertens conjecture by showing that lim sup M(x)x^{−1/2} > 1.06, lim inf M(x)x^{−1/2} < −1.009." p. 141, formula (2.3), Titchmarsh's formula under RH and simple zeros: M₀(x) = lim_k Σ_{|γ|<T_k} x^ρ/(ρζ′(ρ)) − 2 + Σ_n (−1)^{n−1}(2π/x)^{2n}/((2n)! nζ(2n+1)). Under RH, M(x)x^{−1/2} is an on-line residue sum, and the paper shows it takes values beyond ±1. **A negative (or positive) μ-side value certifies nothing about zeros off the line.** This is the nearest published object that D3's 10(n) line (line 209) should have named. It is also the published home of the μ-side's S3 content. p. 141: "the Riemann hypothesis and the simplicity of the zeros … follow from any one of the following three weaker hypotheses: i) lim sup M(x)x^{−1/2} ≤ A … ii) lim inf M(x)x^{−1/2} ≥ −A …". A one-sided bound on the μ-datum implies RH plus simple zeros. That is the classical (P)-shaped Möbius statement. p. 140 says it is expected to be false ("M(x)x^{−1/2} is expected to be unbounded").
