### r5-07 — Inaba, *Examples of exceptional minimal sets* (printed pp. 95–100, all by vision)

**What the article is (the source says, p. 95, opening paragraph, verbatim):** "This note consists of two unrelated topics about exceptional minimal sets in codimension one foliations. In §1, we prove that in a certain class of transversely piecewise linear, codimension one foliations of closed manifolds, every exceptional minimal set contains only finitely many semiproper leaves. In §2, we provide a method of constructing examples of exceptional minimal sets."

**I infer (flag for the record):** the row-7 description "the catalogue of known exceptional minimal sets in codimension one" is **not** what the article is. It is a six-page note with one finiteness theorem (§1) and one construction ("branched staircases", §2). It contains no catalogue, no list of examples with properties, and **no statement anywhere about the ends of any leaf** — the word "end" does not occur in the article except inside "endset" in the title of reference [9] (Takamura's thesis). What Warsaw [9] is cited for should be checked against the Warsaw text by the next stream (a01-12-warsaw.md does not record the sentence that cites [9]; see §4).

**§1 of the article — the PL finiteness theorem (p. 95, verbatim).** "Let k be a positive integer. We denote by PL^k_+(ℝ) the group of orientation preserving, piecewise linear homeomorphisms f of ℝ satisfying the following two properties: 1) f(0) and non-differentiable points of f are k-adic numbers, i.e. numbers of type p/k^q with p, q being integers. 2) Slopes of f are integral powers of k except at non-differentiable points. A codimension one foliation is *transversely-PL^k_+(ℝ)* if all holonomy transitions are restrictions of elements of PL^k_+(ℝ). A codimension one foliation is *without one-sided holonomy* if for every loop in a leaf, the associated holonomy germ is either trivial or nontrivial on both sides."
"**Theorem 1.** Let 𝓕 be a codimension one, transversely-PL^k_+(ℝ) foliation of a closed manifold M without one-sided holonomy. Let 𝓜 be an exceptional minimal set for 𝓕. Then the number of semiproper leaves in 𝓜 is finite."
Supporting facts (p. 96, verbatim): "**Theorem 2** [2]. For each k, there exists a homeomorphism h of ℝ such that h∘PL^k_+(ℝ)∘h^{−1} is contained in Diff^∞_+(ℝ)." ([2] = Ghys–Sergiescu, *Sur un groupe remarquable de difféomorphismes du cercle*, IHES preprint 1985.) "**Theorem 3** [3]. Let 𝓕 be a codimension one, C² foliation of a closed manifold without one-sided holonomy. Let 𝓜 be an exceptional minimal set for 𝓕. Then every semiproper leaf in 𝓜 has nontrivial holonomy." ([3] = Hector, *Ouvertes incompressibles et théorème de Denjoy–Poincaré*, C. R. Acad. Sci. Paris 274 (1972) 159–162.) "**Lemma 4.** Let 𝓕 and 𝓜 be as in the hypothesis of Theorem 1. Then every semiproper leaf in 𝓜 has nontrivial holonomy." The proof of Theorem 1 (pp. 96–97) counts the components of M − 𝓜 that are not foliated interval bundles via the finite set Σ_0 of non-differentiable points of the generating holonomy maps: "Since border leaves of U are semiproper leaves of 𝓜, it follows from Lemma 4 that they have nontrivial holonomy, say h. … Since h is a nontrivial piecewise linear homeomorphism of a closed interval J to itself, h must have a non-differentiable point in J. … This implies that the cardinality of 𝒰_1 does not exceed the cardinality of Σ_0. So 𝒰_1 is finite."
"**Remark 5** (p. 97, verbatim). In [8], Hector asked if an exceptional minimal set of a C² foliation has only a finite number of semiproper leaves. To the author's knowledge, no positive results have so far appeared about this question." ([8] = Schweitzer's problem list = r5-08; see below, Problem 28.1.)

**§2 of the article — branched staircases (pp. 97–99).** Data: an m × m matrix A = [a_ij] of nonnegative integers with (C1) "For every i, j, 1 ≤ i, j ≤ m, there is a positive integer p such that the (i,j)-th element of A^p is positive"; oriented closed intervals I_1, …, I_m and non-surjective orientation-preserving embeddings f^k_{ij}: I_j → I_i (k = 1, …, a_ij) with images R^k_{ij} satisfying (C2) the R^k_{ij} pairwise disjoint in I_i, (C3) ∂I_i contained in the union of the ∂R^k_{ij}, (C4) for each "admissible" sequence the nested intersection ⋂_{n≥1} f^{k(1)}_{i(1)i(2)} ∘ … ∘ f^{k(n)}_{i(n)i(n+1)}(I_{i(n+1)}) is a single point; compact oriented manifolds P_1, …, P_m with (C5) ∂P_j having 1 + Σ_i a_ij components ∂_j, ∂^k_{ji}, and (C6) orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms ψ^k_{ij}: ∂_j → ∂^k_{ij}. Then (p. 98, verbatim): "Now we define a manifold S with corner as the quotient space of the disjoint union of P_i × I_i, i = 1, …, m, by the identification of ∂_i × R^k_{ij} with ∂^k_{ij} × I_j as follows: (x, f^k_{ij}(t)) ∼ (ψ^k_{ij}(x), t), x ∈ ∂_i, t ∈ I_j. The product foliation of P_i × I_i induces a foliation 𝓕_S of S. We call (S, 𝓕_S) a *branched staircase*. It is easily seen from the conditions (C1) ∼ (C6) that (S, 𝓕_S) has a unique exceptional minimal set 𝓜_S." "**Proposition 6.** For each branched staircase (S, 𝓕_S), there exists a C^∞-foliated closed manifold (M, 𝓕) such that S is a submanifold-with-corner of M and that the restriction of 𝓕 to S is 𝓕_S." (Proof: "the same as the proof of Theorem 3.2 of [5]" = Inaba, *Reeb stability for noncompact leaves*, Topology 22 (1983) 105–118.)
Then (p. 99, verbatim): "The set of all admissible sequences corresponds bijectively with the union of I_j ∩ 𝓜_S (j = 1, …, m). Thus by using symbolic dynamics, we understand the structure of branched staircases fairly well. For example, we see that there exist infinitely many resilient leaves in 𝓜_S, etc, etc,… / We can check that many known examples of exceptional minimal sets (e.g. [7], [4], [6]) have neighborhoods diffeomorphic to branched staircases." "**Remark 7.** The notion of branched staircase is first introduced by M. Takamura in [9]. The content of this section is a generalization of his paper." ([9] = M. Takamura, *Reeb stability for leaves with uncountable endset*, Master thesis, Hokkaido Univ. 1984 (in Japanese).)

### r5-09 — Cantwell–Conlon, *Endsets of leaves* (Topology 21, 1982)

**Standing setting (p. 333, vision, verbatim):** "Let M be a closed, orientable n-manifold, C²-foliated by continuously orientable manifolds of dimension n − 1, and let L be a leaf. The space ℰ(L) of *ends* of L [1] is a compact, totally disconnected, metrizable space that is a topological invariant of the manifold L. If L has nonexponential growth and lies at *finite level k* (§0), then the derived set ℰ^{(k+1)}(L) is empty ([5], (3.6) and (3.7))." ([5] = *Nonexponential leaves at finite level*, TAMS 269 (1982) = r5-07c, agent a02's file.)

**Main theorems, verbatim (p. 333, vision):**
- "**Theorem A.** *Suppose* dim(M) = 3 *and* H¹(M; ℝ) ≠ 0. *Let* E *be an arbitrary compact, totally disconnected, metrizable space. Then there exists a* C^∞ *foliation of* M *by continuously orientable surfaces, such that some leaf* L *is nonexponential, lies at infinite level, and has endset* ℰ(L) *homeomorphic to* E."
- "**Theorem B.** *Let* M *be a closed, orientable 3-manifold,* L *a leaf of a transversely orientable foliation of* M *of smoothness class at least* C². *If* L *has nonexponential growth, then there is an integer* k ≥ 0 *such that* ℰ^{(k)}(L) *contains no planar end.*"
- "One easily concludes, *with no assumption of orientability and/or transverse orientability*, that **Corollary C.** *There are uncountably many diffeomorphism types of open surfaces that cannot be realized as leaves with nonexponential growth in any closed,* C²*-foliated 3-manifold.*"
- p. 345 (vision): "**Theorem A′.** *Let* M *be a closed, orientable 3-manifold with* H¹(M; ℝ) ≠ 0. *Let* L *be a totally nonplanar surface. Then there exists a* C^∞ *foliation of* M *by orientable surfaces, such that some leaf is nonexponential, lies at infinite level, and is diffeomorphic to* L." (Preceded by "(3.9) *Definition.* An open, orientable surface is totally nonplanar if each of its ends is nonplanar.")
- p. 350 (vision): "**(5.8) Definition.** An end e of a leaf L is at finite level k if the maximal level of any leaf in e-lim(L) is k. If no such integer k exists, e is at infinite level." "**(5.9) Theorem.** *If* L *is a nonexponential leaf, there is an integer* k ≥ 0 *such that no planar end of* L *is at level higher than* k." "By contrast, the nonplanar ends of infinite type produced in the constructive proof of Theorem A all lie at infinite level."
- pp. 350–351 (vision): "**(5.10) Theorem.** *If* L *is a nonexponential leaf, then one of the following holds:* (1) L *is a totally proper leaf, hence* L *has endset of finite type* (k − 1, n) *and the growth of* L *is exactly polynomial of degree* k *(here we agree that an empty endset has type* (−1, n)*);* (2) *for some* k ≥ 0, L *is a locally dense leaf homeomorphic to* N_k *or* N_k # N_k *(connected sum);* (3) L *is totally nonplanar;* (4) *for some* k ≥ 0, L *has infinitely many planar ends of type* k *and none of type* k + 1." (N_k, p. 350: "the only orientable surfaces with no handles such that ℰ^{(k)}(N_k) is a singleton".)
- p. 351 (vision): "**(5.11) Corollary.** *For each integer* k ≥ 1, *there exist diffeomorphism classes of surfaces that can be realized in suitable* C^∞*-foliated 3-manifolds as leaves with exactly polynomial growth of degree* k, *but not in any* C^r*-foliated 3-manifold,* 2 ≤ r ≤ ∞, *as leaves with some other nonexponential growth type.*" Closing (p. 351): "The following questions remain open. (1) Are there diffeomorphism types that are not compatible with exponential growth (as leaves, of course)? (2) Are there diffeomorphism types that cannot be realized as leaves at all?"

**Which leaves the theorems concern.** Every theorem is about **nonexponential-growth** leaves (A, A′ construct such leaves at infinite level; B, 5.9, 5.10 constrain such leaves), in dimension 3 (A, A′, B, C) — (5.9)–(5.10) are stated for "a nonexponential leaf" under the paper's standing hypotheses. The §0 review (p. 334, vision) is the paper's own statement of the level trichotomy for nonexponential leaves: "(a) L is totally proper at level k ≥ 0 …; (b) L is a leaf of a local minimal set X of type (ii) [open, locally dense] …; (c) L is at infinite level, in which case there are leaves at each finite level in L̄, these are totally proper leaves, and the union 𝒮(L) of these totally proper leaves (the *substructure* of L) is dense in L̄." **The source says (p. 334): for a nonexponential leaf at infinite level, the leaves at finite level in its closure are totally proper** — so, I infer, the closure of a nonexponential infinite-level leaf contains no exceptional LMS (an exceptional LMS is at finite level and is not a proper leaf). Every exceptional minimal set contains a resilient leaf, and leaves accumulating on a resilient leaf have exponential growth (r5-10 p. 259, "[C-C2, (3.2)]"), so the contract's L — which accumulates on the exceptional K — is outside the scope of this paper's theorems.

**Does any statement concern a non-semiproper leaf?** The words "semiproper"/"semi-proper" do not occur in the paper (text-layer sweep). Cases (b) and (c) of the §0 trichotomy and Theorems A, A′ do concern leaves that are not proper (locally dense leaves; leaves at infinite level, which "does not have a proper side" by Candel–Conlon I Lemma 8.3.23) — but always under nonexponential growth. **No statement concerns a leaf that accumulates on an exceptional (local) minimal set, non-semiproper or otherwise. Expected answer confirmed: none bears on the contract's leaf.** In particular the paper prints no relation between the endset of a leaf and an exceptional LMS in its closure; its one endset-versus-level statement, (5.9), is about *planar ends* of *nonexponential* leaves.

**For scout-F §3.3's sub-case wording ("L a non-proper leaf of an exceptional LMS at level ≥ 1 with K ⊂ L̄ at level 0"):** nothing here sharpens it; the paper's endset types (k, n), "type" of an end, and Definition (5.8) (level of an end) are tools a future stream could borrow, but no theorem here applies to exponential-growth leaves.

### r5-11 — Cass, *Minimal leaves in foliations* (TAMS 287, 1985)

**Abstract (p. 201, vision, verbatim):** "The paper defines a property of open Riemannian manifolds, called *quasi-homogeneity*. This property is quasi-isometry invariant and is shown to hold for any manifold which appears as a minimal leaf in a foliation. Examples are given of surfaces which are not quasi-homogeneous. One such is the well-known noncompact leaf of Reeb's foliation of S³. These surfaces have bounded geometry."

**What "minimal leaf" means there (p. 201, vision, verbatim):** "**Definition 0.3.** A *minimal set* in a foliation is a collection of leaves which is closed in the M-topology and has no proper subsets which are closed unions of leaves. A *minimal leaf* is a leaf in a minimal set." Setting (p. 201): "Let 𝓕 be a foliation of a compact manifold M^n by leaves of dimension p" — any codimension, any C^r (p. 202: "If the leaves of 𝓕 are C² smooth, then the normal bundle to the foliation will serve as n⃗. Even if the leaves are only C¹ smooth, there still exists such a complement n⃗."). Also p. 202: "**Definition 0.4.** If L ∈ 𝓕 and K_i is a sequence of compact subsets of L such that K_i ⊂ K_{i+1} and ⋃K_i = L, then the *asymptote of* L, denoted A(L), consists of the intersection of the closures in M of the sets L − K_i. / A(L) is a closed union of leaves, each point of which is approached by a sequence of points of L. A(L) may or may not contain L, according to which L is called 'improper' or 'proper'. In any case, Zorn's Lemma shows that A(L), if nonempty, must contain a minimal set, and so at least one minimal leaf."

**Main theorems, verbatim:** p. 204 (vision): "**Definition 2.1.** Suppose M is given a metric inducing a distance function d̂ on M, which we denote by d when it is restricted to a leaf. Then L is *recurrent* provided that for each ε > 0 there is a sufficiently large T such that L ⊂ B_{d̂}(B_d(x, T), ε) for all x ∈ L." "**Theorem 1.** L *is minimal if and only if* L *is recurrent.*" ("This theorem was stated and proved by Birkhoff for flows … Plante has shown the forward implication for the codimension one case (Topology **12**, p. 179).") p. 206 (vision): "**Definition 3.2.** Let L be a p-dimensional Riemannian manifold. L is called *quasi-homogeneous* provided there exist dilation bounds (k, K) such that: (1) For any positive r there is positive R satisfying: Any r-cover ball B̃(x, r) ⊂ L admits an immersion B̃(x, r) →^f B(y, R) into any ball B(y, R) ⊂ L, where all such f share dilation bounds (k, K). (2) For any fixed r-cover ball B̃(x, r) ⊂ L there is R′ so large that there is an endpoint-monic immersion B̃(x, r) → B(y, R′) into any ball B(y, R′) ⊂ L." "**Theorem 2.** *A minimal leaf must be quasi-homogeneous in the metric it inherits from* M."

**Ends of a named leaf (one line):** the paper's only end-statements are in **Example 4.1** (p. 208, vision): "There exists a codimension two foliation 𝓕 of a compact manifold M⁴ by surfaces such that: (1) 𝓕 has one compact leaf T, a torus. (4) At least one end of each leaf other than T has the quasi-isometry type of a cylinder R⁺ × S¹, with handles attached, spaced at intervals d_k, where the d_k are unbounded as k increases." — a codimension-two example whose non-minimal leaves are two-ended; **the paper says nothing about the ends of any minimal leaf, nothing about exceptional minimal sets, and nothing about codimension-one dynamics beyond citing Plante and Cantwell–Conlon (p. 202: "A leaf of linear growth, which in codimension one must be an 'infinite repetition' of a compact segment (Cantwell and Conlon), need not be a repetition in higher codimension"). Expected answer confirmed.** Bearing on face (a): the paper is about foliations of compact manifolds, not foliated spaces; "minimal leaf" is a leaf of a minimal set and the paper's invariant (quasi-homogeneity) is a metric-geometric restriction on such leaves, not an endset statement.
