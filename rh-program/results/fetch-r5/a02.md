#### (iv) The definitions, verbatim (§1(D), pp. 185–186, vision)

- **Local minimal set, p. 185:** "(D) *Local minimal sets and levels.* Let U ⊆ M be an open, 𝓕-saturated set. A minimal set of the foliation 𝓕|U is said to be a *local minimal set* of 𝓕. For smooth-leaved C⁰-foliations, such minimal sets need not exist, but (3.0) guarantees their existence for C²-foliations."
- **Intrinsic definition, p. 185:** "DEFINITION. Let X ⊆ M be a nonempty, 𝓕-saturated set. If X̄ − X is compact and if each leaf of X is dense in X̄, then X will be called a local minimal set of 𝓕." "REMARK. If X is as above and if U denotes the open, 𝓕-saturated set M − (X̄ − X), then X ⊆ U is a minimal set of 𝓕|U. Conversely, if U is an arbitrary, open, 𝓕-saturated set and if X ⊆ U is a minimal set of 𝓕|U, then X satisfies the above definition."
- **The three types, pp. 185–186:** "There are three types of local minimal sets: (a) every proper leaf is such a set; (b) a connected, open, 𝓕-saturated subset U ⊆ M, in which each leaf of 𝓕|U is dense in U, is a local minimal set of *locally dense type*; (c) a local minimal set X of neither type (a) nor (b) is said to be of *exceptional type*. In type (c), X is nowhere dense and meets each transverse, open arc in a set homeomorphic to an open subset of the Cantor set. Evidently, a local minimal set X either fails to meet a given compact, 𝓕-saturated set Y, or X ⊆ Y. Consequently, two local minimal sets either coincide or are disjoint."
- **Level, p. 186:** "DEFINITION. A minimal set of 𝓕, and each of its leaves, is said to be at level 0. A local minimal set X, and each of its leaves, is at level k > 0 if X̄ − X consists entirely of leaves at levels at most k − 1, at least one of which is at level k − 1."
- **Level of an end, p. 186:** "DEFINITION. An end e of a leaf L is at level k ≥ 0 if e-lim(L) is a union of leaves at levels at most k, at least one of which is at level k." Followed by the T³ Reeb-modification EXAMPLE (a locally dense LMS U at level 1 whose leaves have "a countable set of isolated ends, each asymptotic only to the toral leaf; hence these ends are totally proper at level 0. - **Substructure, p. 186:** "DEFINITION. If L is a leaf of 𝓕, the *substructure* S(L) is the union of all leaves of L̄ at levels strictly less than the level of L."
- **Ends (§1(C), pp. 184–185, vision):** "DEFINITION. The *limit set* of L is the set lim(L) = ⋂_{α∈A} W̄_α." "If L′ ⊂ lim(L) is a leaf, we say that L is *asymptotic* to L′ (or, *approaches* L′)." #### The bounded question, answered from r5-03

**ANSWER (r5-03): NONE for the endset; a structural dichotomy, not a relation, for the exceptional LMS.** Trans. AMS 265 (1981) prints **no statement relating the endset (or the number, type, or set of ends) of a leaf at infinite level to its level, or to anything else** — the word "end" does not occur in §5 "Leaves at infinite level" (pp. 198–199), and every end-theorem in the paper ((6.0)–(6.4), p. 186 Example) carries the hypothesis "totally proper" (end or leaf), which forces finite level. On the existence of an exceptional LMS in the closure of a leaf L at infinite level, the paper prints only the dichotomy of **(5.0)(a)** ("either all of the leaves of S(L) or only finitely many of them are totally proper"), sharpened by **(5.2)** and the **Remark after (5.1)** ("no LMS in L̄ can be of locally dense type. All must be either proper leaves or exceptional"). *I infer* (not the source): in the first horn no exceptional LMS lies in L̄ (an exceptional LMS in L̄ is at finite level by (4.0), so lies in S(L), and its leaves are not proper); in the second horn S(L) contains LMS at every level k ≥ 0 of which only finitely many are totally proper leaves, so all but finitely many of its LMS are exceptional or are proper leaves whose limit set contains a non-proper leaf of L̄ — and such a leaf lies in an exceptional LMS by the Remark — so **an exceptional LMS does lie in L̄ in the second horn**. Both horns are printed as possible; nothing is printed that ties either horn to the ends of L. **No statement here would name branch (P∞) beyond what adjudication §3 already records** (the printed structure of L̄: (5.0)(b)–(e) = Candel–Conlon I Lemmas 8.3.22–8.3.26). Nothing here forces or forbids one end for a non-semiproper leaf; the paper's only semiproperness result at infinite level is (5.0)(d)/(5.4): no leaf of Z = L̄ − S(L) — in particular L itself, and every leaf asymptotic to L (Remark p. 183) — has a proper side. That is the source of Candel–Conlon I Lemma 8.3.23 and of Warsaw Lemma 2.1's "No leaf at infinite level can have a proper side"; it is the implication semiproper ⇒ finite level already on the record (scout-O §2.2 (α)), printed here in its original form.

#### (iv) Definitions, verbatim

- p. 2: "DEFINITION. O(F) is the set of open, F-saturated subsets of M. B(F) is the set of Lebesgue measurable, F-saturated subsets of M."

- **p. 2: "DEFINITION. A subset X ⊆ M is a local minimal set (LMS) of F if it is a minimal set of F|U, some U ∈ O(F)."** ("Theorem 1 seems to date back to Hector's thesis [Hec 1]. A proof was discovered independently by the authors [C-C 3, (3.0)].")
- p. 2: "If U ∈ O(F), one shows that the union U₀ of all minimal sets of F|U is relatively closed in U. In particular, M₀ is compact and W₀ = M∖M₀ ∈ O(F). [Transcriber's note: the typescript prints "M₁ = M₀ ∪ W₀"; the intended set is M₀ ∪ (W₀)₀, the union of M₀ with the minimal sets of F|W₀ — a visible typo in the source, not corrected here.]

- **p. 3: "DEFINITION. A leaf L of F has level k ≥ 0 if L ⊆ M_k∖M_{k−1}. If L ⊆ M∖M_*, L has level ∞ and we set M_∞ = M∖M_*."**
- p. 3: "THEOREM 3. [C-C 3, §4] Let (M,F) satisfy our hypotheses. Then a leaf L has finite level if and only if L̄ is a finite union of LMS."
- p. 3: "DEFINITION. The substructure of a leaf L is S(L) = L̄ ∩ ⋃_{k<level(L)} M_k."
- **p. 3, REMARKS (the three kinds of LMS):** "There are three kinds of LMS. A proper leaf L is a LMS. Indeed, U = M∖(L̄∖L) ∈ O(F) and L ⊂ U is a minimal set of F|U. The remaining type is called exceptional and meets an arbitrary open, transverse arc (if at all) in an open subset of a Cantor set. Each of these LMS is an element of the σ-algebra B(F) defined earlier. So is M_k, 0 ≤ k < ∞ (by compactness), hence so is M_* and M_∞."
- p. 4: "DEFINITION. Let L be a leaf, [x,y) ⊂ M a transverse arc with x ∈ L. If L ∩ (x,y) ≠ ∅ and if some loop on L, based at x, defines a holonomy contraction of [x,y) to x, then L is a resilient leaf."

#### (ii) Every printed statement about endsets, verbatim

- p. 3 (after Theorem 3): "Consequently, the asymptotic behavior of a leaf at finite level is analysed by describing the way that the leaf approaches finitely many LMS. This description will involve the set of ends E(L), a compact, totally disconnected, metrizable space of ideal points of L at infinity. The endset is a topological invariant of L."
- **p. 3: "THEOREM 4. Let (M,F) satisfy our hypotheses. Let L have growth dominated by a polynomial of degree k. Then L has level ≤ k and the kᵗʰ derived subset E^k(L) ⊆ E(L) is empty."** "A slightly weaker version of this theorem was proven in [C-C 2] and gave the first nontrivial relation known between the growth and topology of a leaf. Its proof actually preceded and led to the general theory of levels."
- p. 6: "Let L be a totally proper leaf. Then L̄ ∩ M₀ is a finite union of compact leaves. Unless L = L̄, there are leaves at level 1 in L̄. Then the level-2 picture (Figure 1, p. 6) and p. 7: "Generally, if L′ ⊂ L̄ is a leaf at level k ≥ 1, it admits a compact, connected submanifold A such that each component B of L′∖A is a neighborhood of infinity in L′ and B winds in on a leaf L″ ⊂ L̄ at level at most k − 1. Again there is a compact juncture N ⊂ L″ and the corresponding 1-sided holonomy contraction f on L″ represents the Poincaré dual of N in H¹_c(L″). Here, a planar leaf L′ is winding in on a cylindrical leaf L″ with juncture N ⊂ L″ diffeomorphic to R. This picture can be realized in a C¹ foliation of S³ [C-C 1, §4(A)]."
- **p. 7: "THEOREM 8. Let (M,F) satisfy our hypotheses. Then a totally proper leaf L at level k ≥ 1 has exactly polynomial growth of degree k and E^{(k−1)}(L) is a finite, nonempty subset of E(L)."** "This is closely related to Theorem 4."

**These are the only end-statements in the paper.** Theorem 4 (growth dominated by a polynomial ⇒ level ≤ k and E^k(L) = ∅) and Theorem 8 (totally proper at level k ⇒ E^{(k−1)}(L) finite nonempty) are genuine printed relations between endset and level — **both at finite level**: Theorem 4's hypothesis forces finite level (its conclusion "level ≤ k"; cf. r5-03 p. 207 "If a nonexponential leaf is at infinite level, it cannot have growth dominated by a polynomial"), and Theorem 8's hypothesis "totally proper" forces finite level (Theorem 3 with p. 6). Neither says anything about a leaf at infinite level, nor about a non-proper leaf of an exceptional LMS.

#### The bounded question, answered from r5-07b

**ANSWER (r5-07b): NONE.** *The theory of levels* prints no relation between the endset of a leaf at infinite level and its level, and no statement about an exceptional LMS in the closure of such a leaf. On leaves at infinite level it prints Theorem 5 (the closure structure, = r5-03 (5.0)(b)–(c)), the "bewildering" remark, and the sentence that **"R² can be realized as a leaf at infinite level in every closed 3-manifold M and, for a suitable metric on M, this leaf is the hyperbolic plane [C-C 8, (5.2)]"** — the printed origin of Candel–Conlon I Example 8.3.20's sentence, here with the exact pointer to *Every surface is a leaf* (5.2) (file r5-06, agent a03). Nothing found would name branch (P∞) or move any face.

**One trap for the record (p. 1):** "There is also a local version of the theory of levels for open, foliated manifolds, making it possible to extend Duminy's theorem to that case [C-C 7]." — "Duminy's theorem" **here** is the Godbillon–Vey vanishing theorem (Theorem 6, p. 4: "Let M be closed and orientable. ### §3.3 r5-07c — Trans. AMS 269 (1982) 637–661, *Nonexponential leaves at finite level*

**Identity check (vision, PDF 1 and 25):** title page as in §1; last page folio 661 ends the References ([Ts2] Tsuchiya, *Growth and depth of leaves*, J. Fac. Sci. Univ. Tokyo Sect. IA Math. 26 (1979) 473–500) and carries the two St. Louis addresses. **Matches the citation exactly.**

**Conventions (p. 638, text layer):** "All foliations will be transversely oriented of codimension one on closed manifolds and, except in §7, of class at least C²." §1 (p. 638): "Suppose that the foliation 𝓕 of M has smoothness class C²."

**Abstract (p. 637, vision), verbatim:** "Previous examples of leaves with nonexponential and nonpolynomial growth (due to G. Hector) have occurred at infinite level. Here the same growth types are produced at finite level in open, saturated sets of leaves without holonomy. Such sets consist of leaves with only one or two locally dense ends, and it is shown that the exotic growth types only occur in the case of one locally dense end. Finally, C¹-foliations are produced with open, saturated sets as above in which the leaves have strictly fractional growth."

**The paper's own summary of the level theory (§1, pp. 638–639, vision):**
- "(1.1) DEFINITION. Minimal sets of 𝓕|U are called local (or relative [D]) minimal sets of 𝓕." (U ⊂ M open, connected, 𝓕-saturated.)
- "(1.2) THEOREM [C-C4]. For M, 𝓕, and U as above, and for L a leaf of 𝓕|U, L̄ ∩ U contains a finite, nonzero number of minimal sets of 𝓕|U."
- p. 639: "A proper leaf of 𝓕 is always a local minimal set. Open, 𝓕-saturated, connected sets U ⊂ M are local minimal sets if each leaf of 𝓕|U is dense in U (these are said to be of *locally dense type*). All other local minimal sets meet the leaves of 𝓛 in sets homeomorphic to open subsets of the Cantor set (and are said to be of *exceptional type*)."
- **"(1.3) DEFINITION. A minimal set of 𝓕 (and each of its leaves) is said to be at level 0. A local minimal set X of 𝓕 (and each of its leaves) is at level k > 0 if the highest level of any local minimal set in X̄ − X is k − 1. A leaf of 𝓕 that is not at any level k ≥ 0 is said to be at infinite level."** (A slightly different wording from r5-03 p. 186 — "highest level of any LMS in X̄ − X is k − 1" versus "X̄ − X consists entirely of leaves at levels at most k − 1, at least one of which is at level k − 1"; equivalent given r5-03 (4.0)/(4.7), which make X̄ − X a finite union of LMS. Not a contradiction; noted.)
- "(1.4) THEOREM [C-C4]. For each integer k ≥ 0, the union M_k of all leaves of 𝓕 at levels at most k is compact. Furthermore, for each leaf L of 𝓕 and each k ≥ 0, L̄ ∩ M_k is a finite, nonempty union of local minimal sets."
- **"(1.5) THEOREM [C-C4]. Let L be a leaf of 𝓕 at infinite level. Then, for each integer k ≥ 0, L̄ ∩ (M_k − M_{k−1}) ≠ ∅ (taking M_{−1} = ∅). If X = ⋃_{k≥0} L̄ ∩ M_k and Z = L̄ − X, then (1) X is dense in L̄; (2) Z is an uncountable union of leaves; (3) each leaf of Z is dense in L̄; (4) no leaf of Z has a proper side."** "From this it follows, in particular, that every local minimal set is at finite level."
- "(2.1) DEFINITION. Let L be a leaf of 𝓕. The substructure of L, denoted by S(L), is the union of all leaves F ⊂ L̄ such that F̄ ≠ L̄. Equivalently, S(L) is the union of all leaves of L̄ of level strictly less than the level of L. Evidently, S(L) is a union of local minimal sets, none of which is of the locally dense type."
- "(2.2) DEFINITION. If each leaf of S(L) is proper, then L is said to have *totally proper substructure*. If, in addition, L itself is proper, then L is said to be a *totally proper leaf*." (2.3): a totally proper end; (2.4): "B₀ spirals on F"; (2.5): "periodic of period F"; (2.6) THEOREM [C-C4]: "If e is a totally proper end of L, then e-lim(L) = F and e is periodic of period F." (2.7)–(2.9): the decomposition of a totally proper leaf at level k, exactly polynomial growth of degree k, type k − 1.

**The semiproper definition and the semiproper ⇒ finite level step, verbatim (p. 643, vision):**
- "(3.1) DEFINITION. Let L be a leaf of 𝓕, x ∈ L, J = [x,y) or (y,x] a subarc of a leaf of 𝓛, and suppose that a suitable loop on L at x defines a holonomy contraction g : J → J to x. If L ∩ int(J) ≠ ∅, then L is said to be a *resilient leaf*." "(3.2) THEOREM. Let F be a leaf of 𝓕 and suppose that F̄ contains a resilient leaf. Then F has exponential growth." "(3.3) THEOREM. Let X be a local minimal set of 𝓕 of exceptional type. Then there is a resilient leaf in X." "REMARKS. For (3.3), the requirement that 𝓕 be of class C² is essential (the C¹-counterexample being Denjoy's construction [Sc, Appendix]). For (3.2), it is enough that 𝓕 be of class C⁰."
- "(3.4) COROLLARY. If L is a leaf of 𝓕 with nonexponential growth, then L has totally proper substructure."
- **"(3.5) COROLLARY. If L has a proper side (we say that L is semiproper) and has nonexponential growth, then L is totally proper. PROOF. By part (4) of (1.5), L is at finite level, hence belongs to a local minimal set. This cannot be of locally dense type nor, by (3.3) and (3.2), can it be exceptional. Thus, L is proper; hence, by (3.4), L is totally proper."**
- "(3.6) COROLLARY. A semiproper, nonexponential leaf of 𝓕 at finite level k ≥ 0 must have exactly polynomial growth of degree k. It must also be a manifold of type k − 1."
- **"(3.7) THEOREM. Let L be a nonexponential leaf of 𝓕 at finite level. If L does not have a proper side, then L ⊂ U where U is a local minimal set of locally dense type, 𝓕|U has trivial holonomy, and the leaves of 𝓕|U are mutually diffeomorphic and have the same growth type as L. If L is at level k, then it is a manifold of type at most k. Finally, Ū − U is a finite union of totally proper leaves at levels at most k − 1, at least one of which is at level k − 1."**

**Main theorems, verbatim (vision):**
- **(5.1) THEOREM, p. 648:** "*Let M be a closed 3-manifold and let γ ∈ G. Then there is a C^∞-foliation 𝓕 of M containing a local minimal set U of locally dense type such that* (a) *Ū − U is a finite union of totally proper leaves;* (b) *𝓕|U has trivial holonomy, the leaves being mutually diffeomorphic;* (c) *each leaf of 𝓕|U has the growth type γ.*" (G = "the set of growth types of the functions m f_(r)(m), m f̃_(r)(m), and e^m, all r > 1" — "a continuous infinity of distinct quasipolynomial but nonpolynomial types ((4.11) and (4.12)) and a continuum of distinct nonexponential but nonquasipolynomial types ((4.15) and (4.16)), together with the exponential type.") **Does anything here constrain a leaf at infinite level or its ends? — NO (one paragraph).** The paper mentions infinite level only to summarize r5-03 ((1.5), a restatement of (5.0)(b)–(d) without the holonomy clause (e) and without the totally-proper dichotomy (a)) and to say that the exotic growth types "have occurred at infinite level" (Abstract; Introduction p. 637: "the interesting growth types (nonpolynomial and nonexponential) occur for leaves at infinite level"). Every theorem is stated for a leaf **at finite level** (title; (3.5)–(3.7); (5.1), (5.5), (7.1) construct locally dense LMS, which are at finite level by (1.5)'s corollary; §6 assumes "U ⊂ M is a local minimal set of locally dense type"). The end-statements — (6.1) one or two locally dense ends, (6.2) two locally dense ends ⇒ exactly polynomial growth of degree d + 2, the Abstract's "the exotic growth types only occur in the case of one locally dense end" — concern leaves of a **locally dense** LMS **without holonomy**, which is neither an exceptional LMS nor a leaf accumulating on one (r5-03 (5.1) Remark: no LMS in the closure of a leaf at infinite level is locally dense; (3.7) here: a nonexponential leaf without a proper side at finite level lies in such a U). So nothing constrains the endset of a leaf at infinite level, and nothing constrains a leaf of an exceptional LMS or one accumulating on it; the only sentence touching the contract's leaf is (3.5)'s proof step "By part (4) of (1.5), L is at finite level" — semiproper ⇒ finite level, the implication already on the record (scout-O §2.2 (α)), here as an explicit citation of r5-03 (5.0)(d). **The paper cannot name (P∞) or any other branch.** One further point for the record: (3.7) shows that a one-ended, non-semiproper leaf at *finite* level with *nonexponential* growth lies in a locally dense LMS and hence cannot accumulate on an exceptional LMS in the manner of (D5) — so a contract leaf in branch (P0) or (P1) must have exponential growth; this is an inference from (3.7)+(3.3)+(3.2) that I record for the next stream, not a verdict (and (D4)'s L ≅ D says nothing about growth).
