## §3. The answer from the source

### §3.1 r5-04 — *Generic leaves* (CMH 73, 1998)

**(a) The theorem behind Warsaw p. 227, verbatim (printed p. 329 = PDF 24, vision-checked).** The standing hypotheses of §9 are printed at the head of the section (printed p. 328, text layer; vision-checked at the top of p. 329 for continuity):

> "**9. Codimension one.** We indicate some applications of our results to closed, C²–foliated manifolds (M, 𝓕) of codimension one. It is assumed that M is orientable and that 𝓕 is transversely orientable."

Then (p. 329):

> "Let X ⊂ M be an exceptional minimal set. A celebrated, but unpublished, theorem of Duminy asserts that the semiproper leaves of 𝓕|X have a Cantor set of ends. If the foliation is real analytic, this result holds for all the leaves 𝓕|X. If the holonomy of 𝓕|X is generated by a Markov chain (all of the standard examples of exceptional minimal sets have this property), the authors have shown that all the leaves have a Cantor set of ends [3], but the general case remains open. The following is a small but interesting step in the right direction.
>
> **Theorem 9.1.** *If (X, 𝓕|X) is an exceptional minimal set of a foliated manifold (M, 𝓕) as above, there is a residual family G ⊆ X of leaves such that either every leaf in G has a Cantor set of ends or every leaf in G has exactly one end.*
>
> *Proof.* Since (X, 𝓕|X) is minimal and compact, every leaf is totally recurrent, so the family G can be chosen as in Theorem A. If the leaves in G are 2–ended, Theorem 8.1 provides a holonomy invariant probability measure on T. By minimality, supp µ = X ∩ T. The C² hypothesis allows us to apply a theorem of Sacksteder [12] according to which some leaf L of 𝓕|X supports a 2–sided holonomy contraction. In standard fashion, it follows that each of the infinitely many points x of L ∩ T has the same *positive* measure µ({x}), contradicting the fact that µ(T) = 1. □"

([3] = Cantwell–Conlon, *Leaves of Markov local minimal sets*, Publ. Mat. 33 (1989) 461–484 = r5-01; [12] = Sacksteder, *Foliations and pseudogroups*, Amer. J. Math. 87 (1965) 79–102; printed p. 336.)

**Every hypothesis of Theorem 9.1, as printed:** (i) M a closed manifold, orientable; (ii) 𝓕 of codimension one, C², transversely orientable ("as above" = the §9 preamble); (iii) X ⊂ M an exceptional minimal set, with (X, 𝓕|X) regarded as a compact foliated metric space (the paper's general setting, p. 306: transverse model S "totally disconnected (e.g., exceptional minimal sets in compact foliated manifolds of codimension one …)"). The proof uses: Theorem A (needs only "complete, foliated metric space" with a totally recurrent leaf — supplied by minimality plus compactness); Theorem 8.1 (needs a compact, leafwise C¹ lamination of a closed manifold with continuously oriented tangent bundle — p. 327; supplied by the C² foliation and the orientability assumptions); Sacksteder's theorem (needs C²). Nothing about the leaf dimension beyond p = dim M − 1; the 3-manifold case is not singled out.

**(b) The meaning of "generic" there — the source says.** *Topological (Baire) genericity, not measure.* Printed p. 307–308: "The term 'generic leaf' can be defined from the measure theoretic point of view, where it means 'µ–almost every leaf' relative to a suitable measure µ, or from the purely topological viewpoint, where it refers to a residual family of leaves." … "Recall that a subset Y ⊂ X is *meager* if it is contained in the countable union of closed, nowhere dense subsets of X. … The complement of a meager set, called a *residual* set, contains the countable intersection of open, dense subsets of X." … "In this paper, we take 'generic' in the topological sense" (p. 308, vision-checked). The two notions are explicitly contrasted on p. 308: "The two notions, of course, have no logical relation, as is illustrated by the existence of Cantor sets Y ⊂ ℝ of positive Lebesgue measure." So Warsaw p. 227's "generic … in the sense of [5]" = residual family of leaves of the compact foliated space (X, 𝓕|X) (Lemma 3.1, p. 312: residual saturated sets in X correspond to residual Γ-invariant sets in the transversal T); "in the sense of [7]" (Warsaw's [7] — not resolved here; I infer from this paper's [6] that it is Ghys, *Topologie des feuilles génériques*, Ann. of Math. 141 (1995), i.e. harmonic-measure-almost-every) is the measure sense, which THIS paper does not use in Theorem 9.1. **I infer**: Warsaw's "It is not hard to show that the generic leaf has one end or a Cantor set of ends" is, in the sense of [5], exactly Theorem 9.1; in the sense of [7] it would be the analogous statement for harmonic measure (Ghys's 0/1/2/Cantor with the 2-ended and 0-ended cases excluded by the same Sacksteder argument) — the paper does not print that measure-theoretic version; the sentence "the general case remains open" (p. 329) is the paper's own statement of what Theorem 9.1 does NOT do.

**(c) The general theorems Theorem 9.1 rests on, verbatim (printed p. 308 = PDF 3, vision-checked).**

> "**Theorem A.** *Let (X, 𝓕) be a complete, foliated metric space. If 𝓕 has a totally recurrent leaf, then there is a residual family G of totally recurrent leaves without holonomy such that one of the following holds:* (1) *every leaf in G has a Cantor set of ends;* (2) *every leaf in G has exactly two ends;* (3) *every leaf in G has exactly one end.* *If, in addition, the leaf dimension is p = 2, either all leaves L in G have genus 0, or all leaves L in G are orientable and have only nonplanar ends, or all leaves L in G have only nonorientable ends.*"

> "**Theorem B.** *If (X, 𝓕) is a complete, foliated metric space, a residual family G has the property that its leaves have 0, 1, 2, or a Cantor set of dense ends, the cardinality of ℰ_d(L) being constant as L varies over G. If the leaf dimension is 2, the dense ends of the leaves in G are either all planar, all orientable but nonplanar, or all nonorientable.*"

Setting (p. 306): "(X, 𝓕) a locally compact, separable, complete, foliated metric space … leaves are manifolds of some fixed dimension p, but transversely the foliation is modeled on a locally compact, separable, metric space S." "It is not assumed that X is compact, no differentiability is needed" (p. 308). **Definition 1.1** (p. 307): "An end e of a leaf L in a foliated space (X, 𝓕) is *dense* if every neighborhood of e in L is dense in X. The leaf L is *totally recurrent* if it is noncompact and every end of L is dense."

**Theorem 8.1** (p. 327, the instrument that kills the 2-ended case; hypotheses at the head of §8: "compact, leafwise C¹ laminations of a closed manifold M … We also require that there be a continuous orientation of the tangent bundle T(X, 𝓕) of the lamination"): "*If (X, 𝓕) has a totally recurrent leaf and the generic leaf is 2–ended, then there is a probability measure µ on T, invariant under the holonomy pseudogroup and supported in L̄ ∩ T, where L is a generic, 2–ended leaf.*" (Proof sketched via Sullivan's structure cycles / Goodman–Plante averaging sequences.)

**(d) The level-theoretic companions, verbatim (printed pp. 329–330 = PDF 24–25, vision-checked)** — these bear directly on the adjudication's branch (P∞):

> "**Theorem 9.2.** *If L is a leaf at level k < ∞, there is a residual family of leaves G ⊆ L̄ such that every leaf in G has 0, 1, 2, or a Cantor set of ends at level k, the cardinality of ℰ_k(F) being constant as F ranges over the leaves in G*" [no full stop printed].

> "If L is a leaf at infinite level, X = L̄ contains uncountably many leaves at infinite level, all asymptotic to L [1, §5]. This family of leaves is a residual subset of X. Indeed, the sets X ∩ M_k are compact and, being in the closure of L, they have empty interior in X, so the union of leaves at finite level in X is meager. Again, the ends at infinite level are exactly the dense ends relative to the foliated space (X, 𝓕|X) and every leaf at infinite level has such an end.
>
> **Theorem 9.3.** *If L is a leaf at infinite level, there is a residual family G ⊂ L̄ of leaves, every one of which has 1, 2, or a Cantor set of ends at infinite level, the cardinality of ℰ_∞(F) being constant as F ranges over the leaves in G.*"

Definitions used (p. 329, verbatim): "An end e of a leaf L is at level k ≤ ∞ if the maximal level of leaves in its asymptote A_e ⊆ M is k. We denote by ℰ_k(L) the set of ends of L at level k ≤ ∞." The filtration and "level" are recalled from [1] (= Cantwell–Conlon TAMS 265 (1981) = r5-03) with the C² hypothesis stated: "The fact that 𝓕|U has minimal sets, where U ⊆ M is any open, 𝓕–saturated subset, is a consequence of the C² hypothesis."

**(e) Is "one end" realized by an example, and under what conditions? — the source says.** Yes, in two places, neither of which is an exceptional minimal set of a codimension-one foliation.

1. **Example 10.3 (printed pp. 332–333):** the Anosov-type foliation of T₁(Σ) (Σ hyperbolic genus 2) by left cosets of the upper-triangular subgroup H ⊂ PSL(2, ℝ) — a *minimal foliated manifold* in which "most of these leaves survive as copies of the hyperbolic plane, but a countable infinity are hyperbolic cylinders and have nontrivial holonomy. Each leaf is everywhere dense, so (M, 𝓕) is a minimal foliated manifold and the planar leaves form the residual set G₀." Then (p. 333, vision-checked): "a simple modification of this foliated manifold produces an example in which the generic leaves are 1–ended with infinite genus. Simply drill out a small tubular neighborhood N of one of the circle fibers of T₁(Σ) … Replace N with a copy of K × S¹, foliated by copies of K, where K is either a handle or a crosscap. … the generic leaves (in either sense) are 1–ended with handles (respectively, crosscaps) clustering at that end." *Conditions:* this is a minimal codimension-one foliation of a closed 3-manifold whose minimal set is all of M — NOT an exceptional minimal set; so it realizes case (3) of Theorem A, not the one-ended alternative of Theorem 9.1.

2. **Example 10.4 (printed pp. 332–333 = PDF 27–28, vision-checked at PDF 28), verbatim in full** — the passage that bears on rows 4 AND 6 and on the adjudication's (P∞) branch:

> "**Example 10.4.** In [2], it was shown that, given an arbitrary closed 3–manifold M and an arbitrary noncompact, orientable surface L, there is a smooth foliation 𝓕 of M having a leaf diffeomorphic to L. This leaf eludes visual intuition by lying at *infinite level* in the foliation. By the theory of levels, X = L̄ ⊆ M is a compact foliated subspace containing leaves at every finite level, as well as uncountably many leaves at infinite level, each of the latter being asymptotic to every leaf in X [1, §5]. Generally, the leaf L will not be totally recurrent in X, but if we take L ≅ ℝ², then the fact that L has one end and is asymptotic to every leaf (including itself) in X does imply total recurrence. This leaf, being simply connected, also has trivial holonomy. By the proof of Proposition 6.3 and Corollary 6.4, the set of leaves with a Cantor set of ends must be meager. Otherwise, the totally recurrent leaf L without holonomy would have a Cantor set of ends. Then, by the proof of Proposition 6.5, the set of leaves with exactly two ends must be meager. Otherwise, the totally recurrent leaf L ⊂ G₀, having at most two ends, would have exactly two ends. Similarly, every leaf without holonomy has genus zero and it follows that the topologically generic leaf in (X, 𝓕|X) is the plane. We do not know whether these leaves lie in the support of a harmonic measure.
>
> Many other topological types can also occur, nongenerically, among the leaves of 𝓕|X. If H₂(M; ℝ) ≠ 0, the construction in [2] can be carried out so that X contains leaves at every finite depth. The well understood theory of leaves at finite depth (*cf.* [1, §6], where they are called 'totally proper' leaves) shows that there are at least countably many distinct topological types of noncompact leaves in X. It is also possible to carry out the construction in arbitrary M so that X contains an exceptional minimal set. By Duminy's (unpublished) theorem, X would then contain some leaves with a Cantor set of ends. Finally, the trick of modifying the foliation along a closed transveral [sic] through L produces a generic family of one ended leaves of infinite genus or one with infinitely many crosscaps. This example is not minimal."

([2] = Cantwell–Conlon, *Every surface is a leaf*, Topology 26 (1987) 265–285 = r5-06; [1] = TAMS 265 (1981) = r5-03; printed p. 336.)

*Conditions under which one end is realized here:* L ≅ ℝ² a plane leaf **at infinite level** of a C^∞ (the paper says "smooth") foliation of an **arbitrary closed 3-manifold**; X = L̄ is then a compact foliated subspace in which the topologically generic leaf is the plane (one end); X is **not minimal** ("This example is not minimal"), so this is NOT an instance of Theorem 9.1's one-ended alternative either. Whether the one-ended alternative of Theorem 9.1 is ever realized *inside an exceptional minimal set* is not decided by the paper: the source says (p. 329) "the general case remains open", and no example in §10 has an exceptional minimal set whose generic leaf is one-ended (Example 10.5, the Hirsch-type example, is minimal with generic Cantor-tree leaves; Examples 10.1–10.2 have generic 2-ended leaves and are not minimal or not codimension one in the C² manifold sense — 10.2 is a T²-bundle whose generic leaves are 2-ended; it is a foliation with a dense family of compact leaves, so not an exceptional minimal set).

**(f) What this settles for §2, in plain English.** *The source says:* for a C² transversely orientable codimension-one foliation of a closed orientable manifold and an exceptional minimal set X, a *residual* (Baire) family of leaves of X either all have a Cantor set of ends or all have exactly one end (Theorem 9.1); "generic" in Warsaw's "[5]" sense means residual, not measure; the paper prints no example realizing the one-ended alternative in an exceptional minimal set, and prints that the general question is open. So the "one end" alternative on Warsaw p. 227 is exactly as narrow as Theorem 9.1 makes it: it survives only because the Sacksteder/Theorem 8.1 argument kills the two-ended case and nothing printed kills the one-ended case. *I infer* (not printed): Theorem 9.1 is a statement about a residual family in X, so it constrains a single named leaf L ⊂ K (branch (P0)) only if L happens to be in G — it does not, by itself, say anything about one particular leaf; and it says nothing about a leaf outside X.
