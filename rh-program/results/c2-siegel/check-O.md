## Item 2 — Lemma C (the pole cap) and the thresholds: CLOSES, with three sharpenings

**Lemma C, re-derived.** Let w ∈ Σ_∞ and y ∈ [0, ½). Then (ŵ ∗ μ_y)(0) = ∫ŵ(s)μ_y(s)ds, since μ_y is even. The hypothesis w ≥ 0 gives |ŵ(ξ)| ≤ ŵ(0) (note §0.1, first bullet), and μ_y ≥ 0 with ∫μ_y = 1 (note Lemma P(i)–(ii)). Hence ∫ŵμ_y ≤ ŵ(0)∫μ_y = ŵ(0). Equality would force ŵ ≡ ŵ(0) on ℝ, because μ_y > 0 everywhere. That is impossible for ŵ ∈ L¹ unless w = 0. As y → ½, μ_y tends to a point mass and the share tends to 2ŵ(0) (dominated convergence, ŵ continuous and bounded). This is the scout's proof at lines 59–61. **The margin identity** margin = 2∫ŵμ_y − 2ŵ(0) − Arch_K(w) = −Arch_K(w) − 2∫μ_y(ŵ(0) − ŵ) is exact, and its second term is ≥ 0.

**Which hypothesis does what (the brief's question).**
- *The pole cap itself* uses only **w ≥ 0** (through |ŵ| ≤ ŵ(0)) and μ_y ≥ 0 of mass one. It needs **no archimedean hypothesis at all**, and it does not even use ŵ ≥ 0.
- *Uncertifiability* needs Arch_K(w) = ∫ŵ a_K ≥ 0. For a single element this follows from **ŵ ≥ 0 together with a_K ≥ 0 on supp ŵ**, that is, only where ŵ lives. For instance, a Fejér element with support in |s| ≤ T is uncertifiable as soon as a_K ≥ 0 on [0, T]. The **cone-wide** statement "no element of Σ_∞ certifies" needs a_K ≥ 0 at every point, because the cone contains elements whose ŵ concentrates at any chosen point. For this to hold at every point, the condition at s = 0 is necessary and sufficient, since a_K is minimal at s = 0 (see the closed form below). That condition is |D| ≥ D₁.
- The pointwise sign of A_K (threshold D₀) is **not** the relevant condition: the budget meets A_K only through the smoothed density a_K = μ₀ ∗ A_K. The scout says the same at lines 65 and 152, where it uses D₁ and not D₀.

The substitution v = 2τ, followed by Frullani's integral ∫₀^∞(e^{−v/2} − e^{−v})/v dv = log 2, gives
