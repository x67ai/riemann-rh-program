On Haglund's Conjecture 4 for the Riemann $\Xi$ approximants
Let $\Xi_k$ be the $k$-th partial sum of Riemann's expansion $\Xi = \sum_{n \ge 1}\Phi_n$ of the $\Xi$-function in incomplete gamma functions. Haglund conjectured (his Conjecture 4) that for every $k \ge 1$ the imaginary part of each non-real zero of $\Xi_k + t\Phi_{k+1}$ decreases as $t$ goes from $0$ to $1$. We prove that this pencil is $\Xi$ minus a level that is positive on the real axis and decreases with $t$; that real zeros stay real if and only if every real critical point of $\Xi_{k+1}/\Phi_{k+1}$ with value in $(0,1]$ is a non-degenerate maximum; and that a simple zero $\beta$ of $\Xi$ above the real axis with $\mathrm{Im}\,\Xi'(\beta) > 0$ would give, for every large $k$, a zero of the pencil whose imaginary part strictly increases. If the zeros of $\Xi$ are real and simple and the level is frozen at a constant, every non-real zero descends and lands at the top of its own lobe; this is known in substance. In floating point, not interval arithmetic, and with two independent programs for $k \le 3$, the imaginary part decreased at every step of every branch followed, in complete windows for $k \le 9$ and around the landings for $k = 15, 20, 26, 27, 35, 50$; the real-axis criterion holds on a grid of $[0, 4(k+2)^2 + 40]$ for $k \le 50$. The true, slowly varying level is treated heuristically.
