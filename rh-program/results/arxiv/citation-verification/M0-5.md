### CONTENT CLAIM — "semidirect product of the half-line by the monoid of positive integers acting by multiplication"

**CONFIRMED, and the acting monoid is N^x (positive integers), not R*_+.** Three primary quotations:

1. arXiv:1507.05818 abstract (arXiv API, verbatim):
   > "The obtained site is the semi-direct product of the Euclidean half-line by the action of the monoid
   > of positive integers by multiplication."

2. arXiv:1603.03191 abstract (arXiv API, verbatim):
   > "The obtained semiringed topos is the Grothendieck topos semi-direct product of the Euclidean half-line
   > and the monoid of positive integers acting by multiplication, endowed with the structure sheaf of
   > piecewise affine, convex functions with integral slopes."

3. The published C. R. note's abstract, retrieved from Crossref's JATS record for `10.1016/j.crma.2015.09.027`,
   spells the object out in MathML. Decoding the MathML, the abstract literally contains

   > "The obtained site  [0,∞) ⋊ N^×  ..."

   (the raw JATS shows `<mml:mo stretchy="false">[</mml:mo><mml:mn>0</mml:mn><mml:mo>,</mml:mo><mml:mo>∞</mml:mo>
   <mml:mo stretchy="false">)</mml:mo><mml:mo>⋊</mml:mo><mml:msup><mml:mi mathvariant="double-struck">N</mml:mi>
   <mml:mo>×</mml:mo></mml:msup>`).

So the load-bearing distinction resolves in favor of **N^x**. The half-line is `[0,∞)` (the "Euclidean
half-line"); R*_+ appears elsewhere in this circle of ideas (the scaling *flow*, and the maximal compact
subgroup quotient of the idele class group), but the semidirect-product factor defining the site is the
multiplicative monoid of positive integers.

### Verdict: CORRECTED

Two corrections to the report:
* the Selecta article's preprint is **arXiv:1603.03191**, not arXiv:1507.05818;
* "The Scaling Site" is a separate, published C. R. note that the report did not cite.

Also: the journal capitalizes the title as *Geometry of the scaling site* (lower-case s), and the C. R. note
as *The scaling site*; arXiv styles the note *The Scaling Site*. Follow the journals.

### CONTENT CLAIM — verdict CONFIRMED, with one hedge to respect

**LeClair-Mussardo — confirmed, and they say it almost in the report's own words.** From the arXiv:2307.01254
abstract, verbatim:

> "Moreover the idea that the validity of the GRH requires both the duality equation (i.e. the mapping
> s -> 1 - s) and the Euler product representation of the Dirichlet L-functions finds additional and novel
> support from the physical scattering model analysed in this paper. This is further illustrated by an
> explicit counterexample provided by the solutions of the Bethe Ansatz equations which employ the
> Davenport-Heilbronn function D(s), i.e. a function whose completion satisfies the duality equation
> χ(s) = χ(1-s) but that does not have an Euler product representation."

**França-LeClair — confirmed as a statement, but they explicitly decline to assert the causality.**
The paper has a whole section on it — §XIX, "COUNTEREXAMPLE OF DAVENPORT-HEILBRONN", p. 75 of the v2 PDF:

> "The Davenport-Heilbronn function has almost all the same properties of ζ, such as a functional equation,
> except that it has no Euler product formula. It is well known that such a function has zeros in the region
> Re(z) > 1 and zeros in the critical strip 0 <= Re(z) <= 1, of which infinitely many of them lie on the
> critical line Re(z) = 1/2, but it also has zeros off of the critical line, thus violating the RH.
> **Whether this is due to the absence of an Euler product is not understood, although this is likely to be
> the reason.**"

and, later, p. 83 (open question 4):

> "This is an appealing idea since the RH would then be related to the fact that there are no zeros with
> x >= 1, which follows from the Euler product formula. Functions such as the Davenport-Heilbronn function
> where the RH fails do not have an Euler product formula."

and p. 84 ("Note added 6", reporting on the 2023-24 work, i.e. the LeClair-Mussardo paper):

> "On the other hand, the Davenport-Heilbronn counter-example has no such unitary S-matrix since the linear
> combination of Dirichlet L-functions does not admit an Euler product, showing why it fails within this
> framework."

So the pairing the report describes is genuinely present in both papers. The only correction of substance:
**do not attribute to França-LeClair the claim that the Euler product is *known* to be what GRH needs** —
their §XIX says in terms that this is "not understood, although this is likely to be the reason".
LeClair-Mussardo is the paper that puts weight behind the claim ("finds additional and novel support").
A safe citing sentence: "the same reading — that the Euler product, and not the functional equation alone,
is what the hypothesis needs, with Davenport-Heilbronn as the standing counterexample — is argued in physics
vocabulary in [LM24, abstract] and discussed in [FL14/24, §XIX]."

### Verdict: CONFIRMED (fields corrected: França; JHEP journal-ref added)
