### CONTENT CLAIM (a) — "SS1 publishes the m1 note's SSSS3-7 end to end"

**VERDICT: CONFIRMED**, with one structural nuance worth carrying into the note.

Section 1, "Weil's work in the 1940s and 1950s", pp. 4-24. Inside it, the subsection "The
geometric proof of the Riemann hypothesis for curves" begins on p. 6 and runs the entire chain.
Page numbers are the printed page numbers of arXiv v1 / `pRH.pdf` (identical pagination):

* **p. 7** — "Elementary intersection theory"; Riemann-Roch for curves, then for a surface:
  "The Riemann-Roch theorem for a surface V states that, for all divisors D on V,
  l(D) - sup(D) + l(K_V - D) = p_a + 1 + 1/2 (D . D - K_V)"; then chi(O(D)) = chi(O_V) +
  1/2 (D.D - K), h^2(D) = h^0(K - D), sup(D) = h^1(D); then the adjunction formula
  K_C = (K_V + C).C.
  — this is the note's (IN1) Riemann-Roch, (IN2) Serre duality and (IN5) positivity, printed
  in exactly the combination the note uses.
* **p. 8** — subsection "The Hodge index theorem". Lemma 1.1: "For a divisor D and hyperplane
  section H, l(D) > 1 => (D . H) > 0", proved from "D_1 cap H = D_1 cap H', which is nonempty
  by dimension theory" — the note's (IN3), ample meets effective positively, in its hyperplane-
  section form.
  Theorem 1.2 (Hodge Index Theorem): "For a divisor D and hyperplane section H,
  (D . H) = 0 => (D . D) <= 0." Milne's proof is the note's SS3 and SS4 fused:
  > "To prove the contrapositive, it suffices to show that (D . D) > 0 => l(mD) > 1 for some
  > integer m, because then (D . H) = 1/m (mD . H) != 0 by (3) above. Hence, suppose that
  > (D . D) > 0. By the Riemann-Roch theorem
  >     l(mD) + l(K_V - mD) >= (D.D)/2 m^2 + lower powers of m."
  The note's SS3 ("effectivity criterion") is not a separately numbered lemma in Milne; it is
  the interior of this proof. That is the one structural difference, and it is a difference of
  packaging only.
* **p. 9** — Corollary 1.3 ("The intersection form on N(V) has index 1") and Corollary 1.4
  ("Let D be a divisor on V such that (D^2) > 0. If (D.D') = 0, then (D'^2) <= 0"): the note's
  SS5 signature statement. Then the subsection "The inequality of Castelnuovo-Severi", with
  Theorem 1.5: "Let D be a divisor on V; then (D^2) <= 2 d_1 d_2", proved in four lines —
  > "(C_1 + C_2)^2 = 2 > 0, (D - d_2 C_1 - d_1 C_2).(C_1 + C_2) = 0. Therefore, by the Hodge
  > index theorem, (D - d_2 C_1 - d_1 C_2)^2 <= 0. On expanding this out, we find that
  > D^2 <= 2 d_1 d_2."
  — the note's SS6, on V = C_1 x C_2, by the same auxiliary divisor.
* **p. 10** — Corollary 1.6 (the def(D)def(D') Cauchy-Schwarz form), Example 1.7
  (def(Gamma_f) = 2 g_2 deg(f) via adjunction), then the subsection "Proof of the Riemann
  hypothesis for curves", ending
  > "|(Delta . Gamma_pi) - q - 1| <= 2 g q^{1/2}. As (Delta . Gamma_pi) = number of points on C
  > rational over k_0, we obtain Riemann hypothesis for C_0."
  — the note's SS7 F_q degeneration clause, including its point-counts-as-output structure.
* **p. 10, Aside 1.8** — the closest thing in the paper to the note's non-circularity thesis:
  > "ASIDE 1.8. Note that, except for the last few lines, the proof is purely geometric and
  > takes place over an algebraically closed field. This is typical: study of the Riemann
  > hypothesis over finite fields suggests questions in algebraic geometry whose resolution
  > proves the hypothesis."

**On "makes the logical dependency explicit":** Milne states the dependency direction outright,
but in the History subsection rather than in the proof, and as a historical remark about
Grothendieck (p. 13):
> "In trying to understand the exact scope of the method of Mattuck and Tate, Grothendieck
> (1958a) stumbled on the Hodge index theorem. In particular, he showed that the
> Castenuovo-Severi-Weil inequality follows from a general statement, valid for all surfaces,
> which itself is a simple consequence of the Riemann-Roch theorem for surfaces."

(*sic*: Milne prints "Castenuovo", missing the l. Quote it as printed with [sic] or silently
correct — but do not silently "fix" it into a form that no longer matches the source if the
note is quoting.)

He also frames his own SS1 presentation as the modern, simplified one (p. 6, "Before describing
it, I present a simplified modern version of the proof") and closes the History subsection
(p. 13) with "The proof presented in the preceding subsection incorporates these
simplifications." So the paper does publish, as its own load-bearing narrative, that the chain
Riemann-Roch (surface) => Hodge index => Castelnuovo-Severi => Weil's bound is the one in use.
