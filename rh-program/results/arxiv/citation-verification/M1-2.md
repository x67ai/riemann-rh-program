## 1.3 Claim (a) — "his §2 itemizes the input list by number" — CONFIRMED, with one mapping error in the report

§2 of the paper (p. 210) opens:

> **2.** Nous allons déduire le théorème 1 d'une proposition auxiliaire, que nous énoncerons avec
> plus de précision qu'il ne sera nécessaire pour notre objet.

and then recalls, by number, exactly four things:

| Grothendieck | verbatim (p. 210) | his own name for it |
|---|---|---|
| (2.1) | `l(D) + l(K — D) ≧ ½ D(D — K) + χ(X)` | *"l'inégalité de Riemann-Roch"* |
| — | `Σ₀² (—1)ⁱ dim Hⁱ(X, O(D)) = ½ D(D — K) + χ(X)` | *"l'égalité de Riemann-Roch (qui se démontre maintenant de façon très élémentaire)"* |
| (2.2) | `dim H²(X, O(D)) = dim H⁰(X, O(K — D))` | *"le théorème de dualité de Serre"* |
| (2.3) | `l(D + D′) ≧ l(D′)  si  l(D) > 0` | *"l'inégalité évidente"* |
| (2.4) | `l(D) > 1 implique D · H > 0` | H *"une section hyperplane de X (dans une immersion projective de X)"* |

He states explicitly that (2.1) is derived from the RR *equality* together with (2.2):

> Rappelons que cette inégalité résulte de l'égalité de Riemann-Roch … et du théorème de dualité
> de Serre

and he proves (2.4) in place:

> car l(D) > 1 implique que D est équivalent à un diviseur strictement positif D′, et d'autre part
> on peut trouver une section hyperplane H′ équivalente à H n'ayant aucune composante commune avec
> D′, d'où D · H = D′H′ > 0.

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.** §2 is a numbered input list of exactly the m1 note's kind, published in
1958. This is a genuine content-cite and it earns its place.

**CORRECTION to the report.** Report line 397 maps *"(2.3) l(D+D′) ≥ l(D′) if l(D) > 0 = (IN5)"*.
That is wrong. The note's (IN5) is *"h⁰, h¹, h² ≥ 0, hence h⁰(D) + h²(D) ≥ χ(D)"*. Grothendieck's
(2.3) is a different trivial fact. (IN5) is in fact folded into his **(2.1)**: the passage from the
RR equality to the RR *inequality* is precisely "drop h¹ ≥ 0", plus Serre duality. The correct
mapping is:

* (2.1) = (IN1) + (IN2) + (IN5)
* (2.2) = (IN2)
* (2.3) = an additional elementary fact with **no** counterpart in the note's (IN1)-(IN7) list
* (2.4) = (IN3)

The executing session must not print the report's mapping as written.

## 1.4 Claim (b) — "his post-Theorem-1.1 remark is the note's own (IN6) scoping" — CONFIRMED verbatim

First, the numbering. His main theorem really is printed **"Théorème 1. 1."** (i.e. §1, item 1;
in running text he calls it *"le théorème 1"*). So citing it as "Theorem 1.1" is faithful to the
page. Verbatim, p. 208:

> **Théorème 1. 1.** (*Hodge-Segre-Bronowski*). *Soient X une surface projective non singulière,
> ℘ le groupe des classes de diviseurs sur X (pour l'équivalence linéaire), P la variété de Picard
> de X, et N = ℘/P le groupe de Néron-Sévéri de X. Soit E = N ⊗ R, considérons sur N la forme
> bilinéaire symétrique f déduite de l'application (D, D′) → D · D′ de ℘ × ℘ dans Z, et soit aussi
> f la forme bilinéaire sur E × E qui s'en déduit. Si E est de dimension n, alors f est non
> dégénérée de type (1, n — 1).*

The paragraph immediately following it — the "post-Theorem-1.1 remark" — reads, verbatim:

> Ce théorème semble utiliser, dans son énoncé, le théorème de Néron [5] impliquant que E est de
> dimension finie. **Il serait évident comment formuler le théorème 1 si on voulait ignorer le
> théorème de Néron.** Signalons tout de suite que le fait que f soit non dégénérée est un cas
> particulier d'un résultat de A. Weil [8], valable pour des variétés X de dimension quelconque.
> Quant à l'autre assertion du théorème 1, savoir que dans f intervient exactement un carré
> positif, [1] [6] en donnent des démonstrations assez simples, valables en toute caractéristique.

The sentence the report quotes is present **word for word**. And it is exactly the m1 note's (IN6)
scope note, which reads:

> Finite generation of Num(X) is the Néron-Severi theorem … it is consumed ONLY for ρ < ∞ in the
> signature statement §5, which sits off the load-bearing chain to §§6-7.

Grothendieck's point is the same one: the finite-dimensionality input enters the *statement*, not
the *proof*, and the statement can be reformulated to avoid it. His [5] is A. Néron, *Problèmes
arithmétiques et géométriques rattachés à la notion de rang d'une courbe dans un corps*, Bull. Soc.
Math. France **80** (1952), 101.

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.** Strongest single item in this cluster — a 1958 published precedent for the
note's own scoping caveat, in one quotable sentence.

**BONUS, and the executing session should take it:** the *same* paragraph independently confirms
MUST 5's content claim (see Item 2 below) and confirms that Grothendieck names the theorem
**"(Hodge-Segre-Bronowski)"** — three names, not two. Report MUST 5 lists only Segre and Bronowski.

## 1.5 Claim (c) — "Grothendieck's Prop. 2.1 needs NO positivity hypothesis on D" — CONFIRMED IN SUBSTANCE, but the report's wording overstates and must be fixed

Verbatim, p. 210:

> **Proposition 2. 1.** *Soit D un diviseur tel que D² > 0. Alors remplaçant au besoin D par — D,
> on aura l(— nD) = 0 pour tout entier n > 0, et alors pour tout diviseur D′, on aura
> l(D′ — nD) = 0 pour n assez grand, et l(D′ + nD) tend vers + ∞ quand n tend vers + ∞.*

The proof runs pp. 210-211 and closes *"ce qui montre bien que l(D′ + mD) tend vers + ∞ avec m, et
achève la démonstration de prop. 2. 1."*

So the hypotheses are, in full: X a nonsingular projective surface (standing assumption of the
paper), and `D² > 0`. **Nothing else.** No ampleness, no hyperplane section, no effectivity, and
no sign condition on D — indeed he explicitly allows himself to swap D for −D. Contrast the m1
note's §3 Lemma, which assumes *"H ample on X and … D with D² > 0 and D·H > 0"*. The report's
substantive point stands: Grothendieck's auxiliary proposition is strictly weaker in hypotheses.

**CORRECTION to the report's phrasing.** "needs **no** positivity hypothesis on D whatsoever"
(report line 451-452) is not accurate as English: `D² > 0` *is* a positivity hypothesis — on the
self-intersection number. The defensible sentence, and the one to print, is:

> Grothendieck's Prop. 2.1 assumes only D² > 0: no ampleness, no hyperplane section, no
> effectivity, and no sign condition on D itself (he replaces D by −D if need be).

## 1.6 Claim (d) — the hyperplane-section route — CONFIRMED as described, with a caveat the report omits

Grothendieck's proof of Theorem 1 (p. 211, immediately after Prop. 2.1), in his order:

1. Let H be a hyperplane section of X. Then `H² > 0` *"en vertu de (2.4)"* — because l(H) > 1 for
   a hyperplane section, so (2.4) applies. Hence *"la forme quadratique f sur E comporte au moins
   un carré positif."*
2. To show f has no *other* positive square, it suffices to show: if D·H = 0, then D² ≤ 0.
3. Suppose D·H = 0 but D² > 0. By Prop. 2.1 there is n with l(nD) > 1, whence by (2.4)
   `nD · H > 0`, *"contrairement à l'hypothèse D · H = 0, cqfd."*

Verbatim:

> Prouvons maintenant le théorème 1. Soit H une section hyperplane de X, on a alors H² > 0 en vertu
> de (2. 4), ce qui implique que la forme quadratique f sur E comporte au moins un carré positif.
> Pour montrer que f n'en a pas d'autres, il suffit de prouver que si D est un diviseur dont
> l'image dans E = N ⊗ R est orthogonale à celle de H, i. e. tel que D · H = 0, alors on a D² ≦ 0.
> Or si on avait D² > 0, alors en vertu de proposition 2. 1 il existerait un n tel que l(nD) > 1,
> d'où en vertu de (2. 4) nD · H > 0 contrairement à l'hypothèse D · H = 0, cqfd.

Three sentences, and it uses only (2.1)-(2.4) and Prop. 2.1. In note terms: **it never invokes
(IN4) ("D + nH ample for n ≫ 0") nor (IN7) (Nakai-Moishezon).** The positive direction comes from a
hyperplane section and (2.4); the negativity of its orthogonal complement comes from Prop. 2.1 fed
back into (2.4). The report's optional repair at line 960 — *"drop (IN4)+(IN7) in favor of
Grothendieck's hyperplane-section route"* — is therefore **well founded on the Grothendieck side.**

**CAVEAT the report does not state, and the executing session should weigh before acting.** The
note consumes (IN7) in **§6**, to certify that `H = ξ₁ + ξ₂` on C × C′ is ample. Grothendieck's
route does not make that need vanish; it converts it into "some positive multiple of ξ₁ + ξ₂ *is*
a hyperplane section", i.e. `m(ξ₁+ξ₂)` very ample — which is exactly the elementary Segre-embedding
route the note **already** flags inside (IN7) as an available alternative. So the swap is a
re-routing, not a deletion, and it should be presented as one. Two further small frictions:
(i) Grothendieck's (2.4) is proved using a moving step (*"on peut trouver une section hyperplane H′
équivalente à H n'ayant aucune composante commune avec D′"*) that is very slightly more than
(IN3) as the note literally states it; (ii) his Theorem 1 is stated on the whole Néron-Severi space
and so carries the Néron finiteness in its statement — the very thing his own remark tells you how
to scope away. **Recommendation: make the change, but bill it as "(IN7) replaced by the
Segre-embedding fact the note already names", not as "two inputs deleted".**

## 2.4 CONTENT — what each paper proves, and whether "any characteristic" survives

**Segre 1937** — from the zbMATH/JFM review (German, quoted in full so nothing is laundered):

> Der Satz von *Hodge*, daß die Signatur der quadratischen Form, die zu einer Basis der
> algebraischen Kurven auf einer algebraischen Fläche gehört …, genau ein Pluszeichen enthält,
> läßt sich auf **rein algebraisch-geometrischem Wege** beweisen. Er folgt nämlich ohne Mühe aus
> dem folgenden Hilfssatz: Eine virtuelle Kurve der Ordnung null auf einer algebraischen Fläche hat
> niemals positiven virtuellen Grad und den virtuellen Grad null nur, wenn sie ein Nullteiler ist …
> Aus diesem werden noch verschiedene andere Folgerungen gezogen, z. B. die Ungleichung
> b²[A²] + a²[B²] − 2ab[AB] ≦ 0 …

One sentence: **Segre proves Hodge's signature theorem — the quadratic form attached to a base of
curves on an algebraic surface has exactly one plus sign — by purely algebraic-geometric means,
via the lemma that a virtual curve of order zero never has positive virtual grade; he deduces the
Castelnuovo-Severi-type inequality b²[A²] + a²[B²] − 2ab[AB] ≤ 0 as a corollary.**

**Bronowski 1938** — from the zbMATH/JFM review:

> Die Arbeit gibt **zwei neue geometrische Beweise** für den *Du Val-Hodge*schen Satz … Verf. führt
> den Beweis auf den des folgenden Satzes zurück: Sind M, N zwei algebraische Kurven auf F, so gibt
> es zwei ganze Zahlen m, n, die nicht beide verschwinden derart, daß nM − mN nichtpositiven Grad
> besitzt. … Der zweite Beweis transformiert F so, daß zur Basis vier ausgezeichnete Kurven
> M₁, …, M₄ hinzutreten … und zeigt …, daß … der Grad von L₂ nicht als Summe von vier ganzen
> Quadraten darstellbar, also negativ ist.

One sentence: **Bronowski gives two new geometric proofs of the Du Val-Hodge theorem (one basis
curve of positive grade, the rest negative), reducing it to the statement that for any two curves
M, N on F some non-trivial combination nM − mN has non-positive grade.**

**Does "valid in any characteristic" survive? Partly — and it must be attributed, not asserted.**

* The two zbMATH reviews say **"rein algebraisch-geometrisch"** and **"geometrische Beweise"**.
  Neither review mentions characteristic at all. **The reviews do not support "any characteristic".**
* Milne, arXiv:1509.00797, footnote 16, says: *"The Hodge index theorem was first proved by analytic
  methods in Hodge 1937, and by algebraic methods in Segre 1937 and in Bronowski 1938."*
  **"Algebraic methods" — Milne does not say "any characteristic" either.**
* The **only** primary source that says it is Grothendieck, and he says it flatly, p. 208:
  *"Quant à l'autre assertion du théorème 1, savoir que dans f intervient exactement un carré
  positif, **[1] [6] en donnent des démonstrations assez simples, valables en toute
  caractéristique**."* — [1] = Bronowski, [6] = Segre.

**Verdict: PARTIALLY_VERIFIED.** The proofs are algebraic (confirmed three ways). "Valid in any
characteristic" is confirmed **as Grothendieck's 1958 assessment of them**, which is a strong and
citable source, but it is not the papers' own claim and it is not in either review. The note must
write it as attribution:

> algebraic proofs of the index theorem which Grothendieck records as *"valables en toute
> caractéristique"* [Grothendieck 1958, p. 208]

and not as "…valid in any characteristic", unqualified. The originals themselves were **not**
read this session (Segre in Annali di Mat. and Bronowski in JLMS are both paywalled); the
