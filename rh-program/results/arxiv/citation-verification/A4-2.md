### Verdict — CORRECTED

* **Corollary 3 with 0.8477 (RH) / 0.8486 (GRH): CONFIRMED, verbatim.**
* **Inequality (7) exists and is a per-multiplicity integrality inequality that converts an
  `N*` bound plus a simple-zeros bound into a distinct-zeros bound: CONFIRMED.**
* **The report's phrase "the published convex-programming form" is WRONG and must not be
  executed as written.** Display (7) contains no convex or semidefinite programming whatsoever.
  It is elementary integer arithmetic — `(m-2)(m-3)/m >= 2 * 1_{m=1}` for integers `m >= 1`,
  summed over zeros — and the authors themselves attribute the combination to *"an argument of
  Ghosh"*, not to their own optimization. The semidefinite programming in that paper lives in
  Section 3, where it is applied to the Cohn–Elkies-type auxiliary-function class that produces
  Theorem 1's constants 1.3208 / 1.3155. Writing "convex-programming form of (7)" would
  misdescribe the cited source.
* **Second correction, smaller but real: (7) is not the same inequality as A4's Lemma 4.2.**
  They are the same *device* at different integrality levels and with different weightings:
  - Chirre et al. (7) uses the **third** level `(m-2)(m-3) >= 0`, divided through by `m`, because
    in the zeta literature `N_d(T) = sum 1/m_rho` weights each zero by the reciprocal of its
    multiplicity.
  - A4 Lemma 4.2(a) uses the **second** level `(m-1)(m-2) >= 0` with **unweighted** atom counting
    (`N_d = #atoms`). A4's own remark after the lemma says so: *"(a) is the second integrality
    level `(m-1)(m-2) >= 0`"*.
  So the honest sentence is that (7) is the published instance of the same per-multiplicity
  integrality device, at the reciprocal-weighted third level.
