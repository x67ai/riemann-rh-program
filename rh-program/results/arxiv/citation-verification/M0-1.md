### Raw evidence — content claim

The first half of the claim is verbatim, twice. From the introduction (printed p. 2):

> "Defining a Λ-structure on a general scheme X takes some time, as it does on a general ring. But
> when X is flat over Z, there is a simple equivalent definition: **it is a commuting family of
> endomorphisms ψp : X → X, indexed by the set of prime numbers p, such that each ψp agrees with
> the p-th power Frobenius map on the special fiber X ×_{Spec Z} Spec F_p.** It is also true that
> any reduced Λ-scheme is flat over Z."

And again, as the operative statement, in **§1.1 "General Λ-spaces"** (printed p. 6):

> "If X is a flat algebraic space over Z, then Λ-structure on X is the same as a commuting family
> of endomorphisms ψp : X → X, one for each prime p, such that ψp agrees with the p-th power
> Frobenius map on X ×_{Spec Z} Spec F_p."

Also relevant, and quotable, for the "deeper base" framing (introduction, p. 2):

> "So for instance, F1 itself should be defined to be Z with its unique Λ-structure, where each ψp
> is the identity map. It is the initial object in the category of Λ-rings."

**The second half of the claim is NOT printed in this paper.** Exhaustive greps for
`ψm`, `ψn`, `ψmn`, `multiplicativ`, `unique factoriz`, `Adams`, `composition`, `◦ ψ` return no
statement of the identity ψ_m ψ_n = ψ_mn, and no statement that the primes are the primitive
indices by unique factorization. What the paper does contain, and all it contains, on that point:

* §2.6 (p. 12): "Since ψn on Gm is the n-th power map, T would have to be closed under the
  extraction of n-th roots" — the general-n operator used, on one example, in a way consistent
  with ψ_m ψ_n = ψ_mn.
* §6 (p. 22): "For each integer m ⩾ 1, let Im denote the kernel of ψm : B → B. **The Λ-ideals Im
  are ordered by divisibility on m,** and the ordering is cofinal."
* §7.1 (p. 25), the function-field generalization: "we use commuting endomorphisms ψs : X → X, one
  for each point s ∈ E, such that ψs agrees with the qs-power Frobenius operator on the fiber Xs."

So ψ_m for general m ⩾ 1 is used freely and its indexing is explicitly ordered by divisibility —
the multiplicative structure is presupposed, and its definition is deferred to Borger's reference
[7] ("The basic geometry of Witt vectors"), not restated here. The identity ψ_m ψ_n = ψ_mn is
standard and true of Λ-rings; it is simply not a sentence of arXiv:0906.3146.

**Content verdict: the (PF2)-side claim is CONFIRMED verbatim with a section number; the
(PF3)-side claim (composition law + primitivity by unique factorization) is REFUTED as a claim
about *this paper's text* — it is true mathematics but not printed here.**

> **Theorem 1.2** Let λ, λ′ ∈ R×₊ be such that λλ′ ∉ Q. The composition of the Frobenius

And in §7.4 "The composition Ψ(λ) ○ Ψ(λ′)" (p. 37):

> **Theorem 7.7** Let λ, λ′ ∈ R*₊ such that λλ′ ∉ Q. The composition of the Frobenius
> correspondences is then given by
>
>     Ψ(λ) ○ Ψ(λ′) = Ψ(λλ′)
>
> The same equality holds if λ and λ′ are rational. When λ, λ′ are irrational and λλ′ ∈ Q,
>
>     Ψ(λ) ○ Ψ(λ′) = Id_ε ○ Ψ(λλ′)
>
> where Id_ε is the tangential deformation of the identity correspondence.

Section 7 is titled "Composition of Frobenius correspondences" and its lead-in (p. 33) reads:
"The main result of this section is Theorem 7.7 which describes the composition law of Frobenius
correspondences ... Ψ(λ) ○ Ψ(λ′) = Ψ(λλ′)."

**One qualification the report omits and the note must not repeat.** The composition law is *not*
unconditionally Ψ(λ)Ψ(λ′) = Ψ(λλ′). It holds when λλ′ ∉ Q and when λ, λ′ are both rational; in the
remaining case (λ, λ′ irrational with λλ′ ∈ Q) one gets Id_ε ○ Ψ(λλ′), with Id_ε the *tangential
deformation* of the identity. Any sentence in `m0-axiom-note.md` that presents this as a clean
semigroup law is overstating a published theorem, which is exactly the sort of thing a referee
will catch.

"Realized as congruences in the square" is also verbatim (§1, p. 3):

> "Then, we describe the Frobenius correspondences as congruences on the square parametrized by
> positive real numbers λ ∈ R×₊. The remarkable fact to notice at this point is that while the
> arithmetic site is constructed as a combinatorial object of countable nature it possesses
> nonetheless a one parameter semigroup of 'correspondences' which can be viewed as congruences in
> the square of the site."

### Raw evidence — the four differentiators

**(a) Index set of the Frobenius family — CONFIRMED, with a nuance that matters.**
The Frobenius *correspondences on the square*, which is what Theorems 1.2 / 7.7 are about, are
indexed by λ ∈ R×₊: "congruences on the square parametrized by positive real numbers λ ∈ R×₊"
(§1, p. 3), and §6.3 is titled "The Frobenius correspondences on (N̂×, Z_max)" and constructs
"the Frobenius correspondence associated to a positive real number" (p. 27). R×₊ is divisible, so
the report's inference is sound *for the correspondences*.

But the paper also carries a genuinely N×-indexed Frobenius family, and the note must be worded so
that a referee cannot say the differentiator is false. §1.1, p. 4:

> "The operators Fr_k ∈ End(Z_min): N× → End(Z_min), k ↦ Fr_k(n) := kn are the analogues, in
> characteristic 1, of the Frobenius endomorphism in characteristic p > 1 and, in the
> multiplicative notation, they are defined by the rule Fr_k(x) = x^k."

and the abstract: "The underlying topological space of the arithmetic site is the topos of functors
from the multiplicative semigroup of non-zero natural numbers to the category of sets."

So: the *structure sheaf* carries an N×-action (not divisible, primes primitive); the *square*
carries an R×₊-indexed one-parameter semigroup of correspondences. The correct differentiator is
**"the Frobenius correspondences on the square — the objects whose composition law they prove —
are indexed by the divisible group R×₊, so no primitivity-at-the-primes condition is available at
the level where their composition theorem lives."** Writing simply "their index monoid is R×₊"
would be attackable.

**(b) No nef-polarization degree calculus — CONFIRMED by absence.**
`grep -c -i` over the full extracted text: `nef` → **0**, `polariz` → **0**,
`Riemann-Roch`/`Riemann–Roch` → **0**. The paper contains no degree, no polarization, and no
Riemann-Roch statement of any kind.

**(c) No effectivity axiom — CONFIRMED by absence.** `grep -c -i effectiv` → **0**.

**(d) Characteristic-one semiringed topos, not an adelic curve — CONFIRMED.**
Abstract: "The structure sheaf is made by semirings of characteristic one and is given globally by
the semifield of tropical integers. ... This square is a **semi-ringed topos** whose structure
sheaf involves Newton polygons." (Note the paper hyphenates: "semi-ringed topos"; `semiringed`
unhyphenated → 0 hits.) `characteristic 1` → 10 hits, `characteristic one` → 5.
`adelic` → 3 hits, all of them §2.4 "Adelic interpretation" describing the *points* of N̂× and
"the adele class space"; **the phrase "adelic curve" (Chen-Moriwaki sense) does not occur.**

**Content verdict: CONFIRMED.** Theorem numbers correct as given; all four differentiators
sustained, with differentiator (a) needing the sharper wording above and the composition law
needing its λλ′ ∈ Q exception carried.
