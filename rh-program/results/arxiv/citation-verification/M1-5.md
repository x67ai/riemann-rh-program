### Raw evidence — the CONTENT claim

I read SS3 and SS3.1 in full from the on-disk PDF (arXiv v1 pagination: SS3 opens at the foot of
printed p. 16 and runs through p. 17; SS3.1 occupies pp. 17-18. In the Springer chapter these fall
at roughly pp. 68-70 of 53-125).

**SS3, "The Riemann-Roch strategy", opening sentence (printed p. 16):**

> "In relation to the study of the zeros of the Riemann zeta function, the Riemann-Roch strategy
> consists in trading the question of the location of the zeros for the problem of proving the
> non-positivity of a certain quadratic form s(f, f) (see (12))."

**SS3, same paragraph (printed pp. 16-17) — the logical direction, stated for the function-field
model:**

> "In the function field case, this inequality derives from an argument of algebraic geometry in
> finite characteristic and the most conceptual proof was obtained by applying the Riemann-Roch
> formula on the square of the curve defining the function field [32]. In that case, the function f
> defines a divisor D on the surface, as a linear combination of Frobenius correspondences. Then,
> if one assumes the positivity of s(f, f) > 0 for some f, it is the existence part of the
> Riemann-Roch theorem which yields a contradiction."

**SS3.1, "The role of the existence part of the Riemann-Roch formula in characteristic one"
(printed p. 17), the equivalence they are working from:**

> "It is known ([9]) that the RH problem is equivalent to an inequality for real valued functions f
> on R*_+ of the form
>     RH  <=>  s(f, f) <= 0 ,  for all f with  int f(u) d*u = int f(u) du = 0.   (12)"

**SS3.1 (printed p. 18) — the sentence that carries the whole claim:**

> "The Riemann-Roch strategy seeks to obtain a contradiction by assuming that, contrary to (12),
> one has s(f, f) > 0, for some function f. The key missing step is provided by the implementation
> of a Riemann-Roch formula whose topological side is (1/2) D . D and to conclude from it that one
> can make the divisor D := int f(lambda) Psi_lambda d*lambda (or its opposite -D) effective."

Corroborating, from SS1 Introduction (printed p. 2), that the Riemann-Roch input is *missing*, i.e.
upstream and not yet available, rather than a consequence of anything:

> "However, in the process to formulate a Riemann-Roch theorem on the square of the Scaling Site
> one faces a substantial difficulty. The problem, which is still open at this time, has to do with
> an appropriate definition of the sheaf cohomology (as idempotent monoid) H^1 ..."

and from SS3 (printed p. 17):

> "Here we develop yet another approach which is based on the construction of a complex lift from a
> geometry in characteristic 1 to the complex world and the use of the tropicalization map. In
> Sect. 3.2, we explain how this tropical descent allows one, in the context of the Riemann-Roch
> problem, to prove the existence results in characteristic 1 from existence results in
> characteristic 0."

### Verdict — CONFIRMED

Both halves of the report's content claim hold, in the authors' own words:

* *"adopts exactly the route the note certifies"* — SS3 states the strategy as trading zero-location
  for non-positivity of s(f, f), obtained by Riemann-Roch on the square of the curve, following
  Mattuck-Tate and Grothendieck (their reference [32]). That is the note's certified route.
* *"states the same logical requirement in its own words"* — SS3.1's "The key missing step is
  provided by the implementation of a Riemann-Roch formula whose topological side is (1/2) D . D
  and to conclude from it that one can make the divisor D ... effective" is precisely the statement
  that the Riemann-Roch existence result is the *input*, upstream of RH; RH is the *output* via
  (12). SS1 independently records that this input is still open, which is only meaningful if it is
  not derivable from RH.

> "Castelnuovo's principal result is that this invariant may be calculated from the fundamental
> invariants attached to the family by the formula (*) z(D) = 1/2 sigma(D,D), where
> sigma(D,D) = 2n(D)nu(D) - gamma(D) is the 'Severi-Weil metric' on Div(T x C) ... **From (*) one
> obtains the inequality sigma(D,D) >= 0, which is often referred to as the 'Inequality of

### Verdict — CORRECTED (the report's caution flag is now removed)

  by two independent primary indexes (Crossref/De Gruyter and zbMATH). DOI **10.1515/crll.1984.352.24**
  confirmed and can be added; the report did not give one.
* The report's parenthetical *"(reference not independently verified; known via Milne footnote 14)"*
  **should be deleted** — it is now independently verified and no longer depends on Milne.
* Content, in one sentence: **yes, it gives an independent route in characteristic p.** Kani's paper
  redefines the equivalence defect so that Castelnuovo's formula z'(D) = (1/2) sigma(D,D) survives
  in characteristic != 0, and proves it by a method *"that does not leave the surface X = T x C"* —
  i.e. without the theta-divisor on the Jacobian (Castelnuovo/Weil) and without symmetric products
  and Schubert's formula (Igusa) — with the Castelnuovo-Severi inequality sigma(D,D) >= 0 falling
  out because z'(D) >= 0 by construction.
* Evidentiary caveat, stated so the executing session does not over-claim: the content sentence is
  established from the **zbMATH review**, not from Kani's own text (the article is behind
  De Gruyter; GDZ, EuDML and digizeitschriften all refused the PDF this session — 403 / JS shell).
  A zbMATH signed review is a strong editorial source but is not the paper. If the note quotes Kani
  directly, that quote must be re-checked against the article.
