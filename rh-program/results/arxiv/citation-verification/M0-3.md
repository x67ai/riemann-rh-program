### Content claim — what these papers actually prove, and how it relates to h^0_theta

Read: full text of both PDFs (arXiv v2 and v1 respectively).

**(a) 2205.01391 / Bull. Sci. Math. 187, Theorem 1.1, verbatim from the PDF:**

> **Theorem 1.1.** Let D be an Arakelov divisor on Spec Z [overline]. Then
> dim_{S[±1]} H^0(D) − dim_{S[±1]} H^1(D) = ceil'( deg'D + log'2 ) − 1_L.
> Here ceil'(x) denotes the odd function on R that agrees with the ceiling function on positive
> reals, and 1_L is the characteristic function of the exceptional set of finite Lebesgue measure
> which is the union of the intervals (log'(3^k/2), log'((3^k+1)/2)).

with deg'(D) := deg(D)/log 3. The cohomologies are S[±1]-modules obtained by applying an
extended Dold-Kan correspondence to the adelic short complex
`psi : Q x O(D) -> A_Q, psi(q,a) = q + a`, where O(D) is the compact adelic box attached to D;
the "absolute base" S[±1] = {0, ±1} is the global sections of the structure sheaf of the
compactification in Connes-Consani's Segal Gamma-ring formalism. **The dimensions are
integer-valued.**

**(b) 2306.00456 / C. R. Math. 362, Theorem 1.1, verbatim:**

> **Theorem 1.1.** Let D be an Arakelov divisor on Spec Z [overline]. Then
> dim_S H^0(D) − dim_S H^1(D) = ceil'( deg_2 D ) + 1,
> [deg_2 := deg/log 2]

and the paper lists exactly what changes: "1. The term 1_L involving the exceptional set L in the
earlier formula is now eliminated. 2. Formula (1.1) displays a perfect analogy with the
Riemann-Roch formula holding for curves of genus 0. 3. The canonical divisor K = −2{2} has
integral degree deg_2(K) = −2."

**h^0_theta is NOT theirs, and they say so.** From the introduction of (a), verbatim:

> "Another attempt to a Riemann-Roch formula for a number field was promoted two decades ago by
> G. van der Geer and R. Shoof [11] and more recently, in the work of J.B. Bost [1]. In this
> approach one starts from the functional equation in terms of the theta function and rewrites
> that equation as a Riemann-Roch formula for the log-theta number, which is thus promoted to the
> status of a dimension. **This view of the log-theta number as a dimension remains virtual for
> the obvious reason that it outputs real numbers rather than integers.** An interpretation of
> these numbers as Murray-von Neumann dimensions could greatly improve their status as dimensions,
> but this step has not been achieved so far."

(Their [11] is `G. van der Geer, R. Schoof, Effectivity of Arakelov divisors and the theta divisor
of a number field, Selecta Math. (N.S.) 6 (2000), no. 4, 377-398` — the source of h^0_theta, and
already cited in the note as `\cite{vdGS}`. Their [1] is Bost's *Theta invariants of Euclidean
lattices...*, Progress in Mathematics 334.) Paper (b) does not mention theta, van der Geer,
Schoof or Bost at all — grep returns zero hits; its bibliography is five items, all
Connes-Consani, Dundas-Goodwillie-McCarthy, and Weil.

**Verdict on the report's content claim: PARTIALLY CONFIRMED, and the report's own wording
("cite and distinguish from h^0_theta") is the correct one — the brief's paraphrase
("these papers supply an h^0_theta") is wrong and must not be executed.**

What is true: SS7's flat "a substrate-level input no current object supplies" is overstated as an
*absolute*, because a Riemann-Roch theorem for divisors on the Arakelov compactification of
Spec Z, with two cohomologies, an *integer-valued* dimension, and Serre duality, is in print
(2023, strengthened 2024).

What is false: these papers do not supply h^0_theta, and they do not supply the
theta-effectivity bound the note actually names, h^0_theta(D) >= deg D + O(1). Their h^0 is a
*different* invariant (integer-valued, defined over the sphere spectrum, and only for Q / Spec Z
— not for a general number field), and their result is an *equality for the Euler characteristic*
chi(D) = h^0(D) − h^1(D); it yields no lower bound on h^0(D) alone without a separate vanishing
or duality input for H^1. On the note's own terms, therefore, the *DMV-violated M4-level input*
survives; what does not survive is the implication that no Riemann-Roch machinery over
Spec Z-bar exists at all.

**Referee-acceptable sentence to paraphrase into the note (both scope limits included):**

> Connes and Consani have since proved a Riemann-Roch theorem for Arakelov divisors on
> Spec Z-bar, in which the two cohomologies are modules over the sphere spectrum and carry
> *integer-valued* dimensions, with chi(D) = ceil(deg_2 D) + 1 in the 2024 form; this is a
> different invariant from the real-valued theta invariant h^0_theta of van der Geer-Schoof and
> Bost — Connes and Consani explicitly describe the log-theta number as a dimension that
> "remains virtual ... for the obvious reason that it outputs real numbers rather than integers"
> — and, being an Euler-characteristic identity for Spec Z alone, it does not supply the
> effectivity bound h^0_theta(D) >= deg D + O(1) that the M4-level input requires.

### Content claim — checked line by line

**Does chi_4 appear? Yes, and it is one of the paper's two worked cases.** Section 3 is titled
"Hilbert spaces associated with zeta(s) and L(s, chi_4)"; SS3.2 is "The Dirichlet L-function
L(s, chi_4)", and opens: *"Note that chi_4 is the real primitive Dirichlet character (mod 4)."*

**What the paper proves.** From the introduction, verbatim:

> "In this note, we shall indicate the difficulty of approaching the Riemann hypothesis by using
> de Branges' positivity conditions [2] [3] [4]. In fact, we shall give examples showing that
> de Branges' positivity conditions, **which imply the generalized Riemann hypothesis, are not
> satisfied** by defining functions of reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces associated with the
> Riemann zeta function zeta(s) and the Dirichlet L-function L(s, chi_4)."

Concretely, four counterexamples, all by explicit computation (MATHEMATICA; the code is in an
appendix):

* zeta, condition (3.1) [Re<F(z), F(z+i)>_{H(E)} >= 0]: at the 34th zero
  rho = 1/2 + i*111.0295355431696745..., they compute
  `−Re{xi'(rho) xi(1+rho)} = −5.389100507182945... x 10^{−69} < 0`, hence
  `Re{Ebar'(w)E(w+i)/2pi i} < 0`, so by their Theorem 1 the space H(E) with E(z) = xi(1−iz)
  **does not satisfy (3.1)**.
* zeta, condition (3.3) on the space F(W), W(z) = 1/xi(1−iz):
  `Re{xi(1+282i)/xi(2+282i)} = −0.000131957 < 0`, so **(3.3) fails**.
* chi_4, condition (3.7): at a zero rho = 1/2 + i*67.6369208635460683980549... of L(s, chi_4),
  `−Re{xi'(rho,chi_4) xi(1+rho,chi_4)} = −2.310349004993483456... x 10^{−45} < 0`, hence
  `Re{Ebar'_{chi_4}(w) E_{chi_4}(w+i)/2pi i} < 0` and, verbatim, *"by Theorem 1, we see that the
  Hilbert space of entire functions H(E_{chi_4}) with E_{chi_4}(z) = xi(1−iz, chi_4) does not
  satisfy the condition (3.7)."*
* chi_4, condition (3.8): `Re{xi(1+8714.2i, chi_4)/xi(2+8714.2i, chi_4)} = −0.000422340607 < 0`,
  hence *"the space F(W_{chi_4}) ... does not satisfy the condition (3.8)."*

Conclusion section, verbatim:

> "We have seen in section 3 the difficulty of approaching the generalized Riemann hypothesis by
> using de Branges type positivity conditions for reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces. **It is
> possible that these positivity conditions are too strong** for Hilbert spaces of entire
> functions associated with the Riemann zeta function and the Dirichlet L-functions."

**The Remark, which the note should not miss.** A closing Remark records that *"After he looked
at the manuscript of this paper, Peter Sarnak gave a proof for the statement that the space F(W)
does not satisfy the condition (3.3) ... and his argument involves no numerical calculations."*
Sarnak's argument uses the density of the values of log zeta(s) in 1/2 < Re s < 2
(Titchmarsh, Ch. XI) to produce s_0 with pi/2 < Im{log F(s_0)} < pi, F(s) = xi(s)/xi(s+1); and
*"By using a similar argument, Peter Sarnak also proved that the space F(W_chi) does not satisfy
the condition (3.8)"* for **any** Dirichlet character chi mod any r. So the failure is not a
numerical accident at one height — it is unconditional and universal over Dirichlet L-functions.

### Verdict on the content claim: **CONFIRMED IN PART, CORRECTED IN PART**

CONFIRMED: this is a published instance of a positivity criterion that would imply GRH being shot
down by exhibiting an object whose RH is believed true and for which the criterion fails; chi_4
(the nontrivial character mod 4) genuinely is one of the two witnesses, and it is used exactly as
the report says a witness is used.

CORRECTED, and this matters for how SS6.3 cites it: **the criterion also fails for zeta itself**
(the 34th zero; and Sarnak's numerics-free argument for the F(W) form). Conrey-Li is therefore
*not* an exemplar of a filter that separates zeta from chi_4 — which is what the note's SS6.3
does, with Lambda_{chi_4}(3) = −log 3 < 0 while zeta passes. Do not write "on the same witness
function chi_4" in a way that implies Conrey-Li's condition distinguishes the two; it does not.
The honest parallel is the *genre*: a sufficient-for-RH positivity condition, published as such,
falsified by direct computation against the very L-functions it was meant to cover. If SS6.3
wants the parallel narrower, the accurate statement is "including on L(s, chi_4)", not "on the
same witness function chi_4".

Also worth carrying: the paper is about **de Branges'** positivity conditions on reproducing
kernel Hilbert spaces H(E) and F(W) — not about Li's own coefficient positivity criterion
(lambda_n >= 0). A referee who knows the Li criterion will read "Conrey and Li, positivity" as the
latter unless the sentence says "de Branges' positivity conditions" explicitly. Say it explicitly.
