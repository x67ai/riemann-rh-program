### (R-a) The function-field rung (RH true)

**Do the sources build the same objects for a surface over F_q?** YES, at the page: the whole of s-15a is stated for B "a proper, smooth, connected curve over a finite field" as well as for Spec O_k (p. 3 §2; p. 11 §24); Theorem 40 carries the clause "at qˢ = 1, q² in positive characteristic" (p. 53); N = {qⁿ} in positive characteristic (p. 37); §53 is the positive-characteristic discussion of (∗) (pp. 81–83); Parshin's two papers are about curves over F_q by construction. The two-dimensional local fields are F_q((t₁))((t₂)) (t-04a p. 4 §2 with "finite last residue field").

**Does the formalism reproduce the functional equation of the surface's zeta function inside itself?** Continuation and FE are DERIVED FROM mean-periodicity of H (Theorem 48), and in positive characteristic mean-periodicity is deduced from "the rationality (as functions of q^{−s}) and the functional equations for the zeta function ζ_E(s) and zeta functions of global fields of positive characteristic" (p. 74 Remark 4) — i.e. FE is imported from the Grothendieck/Weil theory, then re-expressed. The formalism's own contribution on this rung is the theta formula and the identification of the boundary term. (Parshin, for CURVES over F_q, does derive continuation, FE and residues inside his formalism — u-07a Theorem p. 15 — but that is dimension one and by his own declaration not about zeros.)

**Is Weil–Deligne's RH for the surface derived INSIDE the formalism or imported?** IMPORTED, in the author's words: p. 81, "This section includes some material about hypothesis (∗) in positive characteristic, which is not such an interesting case since we already know the Riemann Hypothesis for ζ_E(s)"; Lemma 53 then proves (∗) FROM the absence of poles in 1 < Re s < 2 (that is, from RH) plus a pole-order condition; and p. 84 Remark 1: "In positive characteristic to show the real zeros part of the Riemann hypothesis seems to be as difficult as to show the full Riemann hypothesis, at least using the known methods. If so, the previous Corollary is not very useful in positive characteristic." Parshin: RH is "Weil's theorem" (t-35b p. 30).

**Observation that settles it:** the three quotations above (a source read at the page, §0 item 1). **Return: NO** — theorem-shaped sentence: *on the rung where RH is a theorem, the two-dimensional adelic formalism imports RH and derives its own positivity hypothesis (∗) from it (s-15a Lemma 53), so the class "sign conditions on the boundary function" cannot yield RH for the surface because on that rung the condition is a consequence of RH, not a source of it.* **Grade consequence:** dead-end on internal grounds for the RH question (the bottom rung has been run by the author and returns "import"). Refutation-shaped closes: YES would have read "the formalism derives \|α\| = q^{1/2} for the surface's zeta from (∗) proved independently" — no such proof exists in the sources (Lemma 53 is the converse); NO reads as above. **Price:** zero slots — decided by this read; a scout pair would re-read three pages. **Distance from upstream** for any object a scout would build: none is needed; nearest published object is s-15a §53 itself.

### (R-c) The S4 question (a new generator, or a Weil-positivity cone in new coordinates?)

**What the positivity is OF:** the sign of the fourth derivative of H(t) = h(e^{−t}), h the inverse Mellin transform of the zeta integral (p. 70); equivalently (char. 0) the single-sign property of Z_E(x) near 0, a Bessel-K series in the coefficients c(n²) (pp. 80–81). Not a measure, not a quadratic form, not a boundary function's positivity as such — a monotonicity of a transform of the zeta's own data. **What its positivity is equivalent to, from the source:** RH for ζ_k(s/2)ζ_E(s) (Theorem 54 Corollary and Remark 1) modulo continuation and the real-zeros part; and conversely (Lemma 53; Suzuki, Remark 2). **S4 class:** analytic — but it is not a generator in S4's sense (a mechanism producing positivity from an external source, as reflection positivity or complete monotonicity produce it from a structure): it is a criterion, a reparametrization of the conclusion. The one structural claim that could have made it a generator — that the two-dimensional boundary ∂T₀ FORCES the monotonicity — is stated as a heuristic ("expected to be related to the fact that the boundary of T₀ is very large", p. 85) and is nowhere proved; the proved monotonicities (Propositions 49–50) come from one-dimensional Riemann–Roch / the theta functional equation and from the order of the pole at s = 2, i.e. from the analytic data. Parshin's corpus contains no positivity at all (§1.3–1.4). **Return: NO** — *the positivity in these sources is not a new S4 generator: it is a sign condition on the inverse Mellin transform of the completed zeta function, equivalent to RH for that function; a Weil-positivity-class certificate in new coordinates would at least be a quadratic form in test functions, and this is not even that — it is III.13's equivalence level.* **Price:** zero (decided at the page). **Grade consequence:** dead-end on internal grounds for S4.

### (R-d) Parshin's holomorphic Tate–Iwasawa

**What "holomorphic version" buys, in the source's words:** "the curve over a finite field can be related to a complex manifold (in this case quite a simple one, a union of projective lines P¹(C) …) and we can apply the techniques of complex analysis. It turns out that the Poisson formula … transforms into the formula for the sum of residues at four points on the complex sphere P¹(C), and the Fourier transform turns into an involution on the same sphere, permuting these points" (u-07a p. 2); continuation, FE and residues become the solvability of a Cousin problem (Theorem p. 15); "the proof of the main theorem in our work is purely algebraic" (p. 2); Riemann's splitting of the domain of integration is not needed (Remark 1 p. 16). **Positivity:** none. **Doubled object:** none — the second adelic space is the dual of the divisor group, a union of P¹(C)'s (u-07a p. 2, p. 14), not a square of the curve; the classical square X × X appears only in t-35b §4.2 as the site of (Γ_g·Δ), imported from Serre. **Multiplicity-visibility (S3):** the residue calculus sees the roots of P(t) only as zeros of the rational function F (u-07a Theorem p. 15: F "is regular outside the points 0_χ, ∞_χ … z = 1, z = q^{−1}"), and t-35b's explicit formula (32) sums over "P(λ^{−1}) = 0" with multiplicity as in Weil's — nothing distinguishing a double root from a pair. ### (R-e) The S6 doubled-object question (III.20 / IV.10's rider)

**Does the two-dimensional adelic space of E stand to Spec Z (or to B) as C × C stands to C?** NO, at the page. A = A_{S₀} is the adelic space of the SURFACE E (a restricted product over points on curves of E, §25–29); it stands to E as the ordinary adeles stand to a curve — it is E's own adelic space, in dimension two. A × A is then the adelic space of E ⊔ E (a direct product), and its zeta integral is ζ_E² (Theorem 40) — two copies of the same zeta, not the zeta of E × E, which would be a fourfold over Z and is never formed. The only "squares" in the corpus are: (i) the local ζ(f_r, χ) = ζ_E(…)² (t-04a §18); (ii) Parshin's X × X over F_q with (Γ_g·Δ) (t-35b p. 35), the classical rung-1 object. **What comparison map do the sources supply?** t: T = (A × A)^× → J (K₂-deles), a symbol map "canonical modulo units" (p. 5, §36) — it compares the K₁ × K₁ structure with the K₂ structure of the SAME surface, not a square with a target; and the residue map A → A_{k(?)} at the residue level.
