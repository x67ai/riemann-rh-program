## §0 The question and the answer

**Question (the brief, verbatim in substance).** k = F_q, S a smooth geometrically connected curve over k, E its set of closed points. Borger's Λ_S-formalism defines a second square, S ×_{F₁^S} S := W_S*(S), the E-typical Witt space of S over itself (the Λ_S-analog of 0906.3146 §1.2's definition for Spec Z). Weil's proof of RH for S on the surface S ×_k S has a zero-dimensional half (Z) — (Γ_{Fr^N} · Δ) = N_N = #S(F_{q^N}) — and a quadratic-form half (Q) — (Δ·Δ) = 2 − 2g, (Γ_Fr·Γ_Fr) = q(2 − 2g), the fiber numbers, and the Castelnuovo–Severi inequality (D·D) ≤ 2d₁d₂ from the Hodge index theorem. Which of (Z), (Q) is expressed on W_S*(S) in the host's own terms (components, scheme-theoretic intersections, arithmetic degrees), and which lives only on S ×_k S?

**Answer, in the 10(c) shape — NO, as expected, with the sharper form proved.** *The quadratic-form half of Weil's proof cannot be expressed on the host W_S*(S) because the host is one-dimensional (Borger 1006.0092 Prop. 16.5(d)): its irreducible components Γ_n (one for each effective divisor n on S, §1.5) are not Cartier divisors, so no component has a self-intersection number, no line bundle O(Γ_n), no normal bundle, and no intersection form on which a Hodge-index inequality could be stated; its intrinsic pairing is defined between DISTINCT components only, and equals* deg(Γ_m ∩ Γ_n) = (1 + min(m_𝔪, n_𝔪))·deg(𝔪)·log q *when m and n differ at exactly one closed point 𝔪, and 0 when they differ at two or more (Theorem 3.3); these numbers are equivalent information to the zeta function Z(S, t) and to nothing beyond it (Theorem 4.2), so any inequality imposed on them is an inequality on Z(S, t)'s own coefficients — III.20 (B)'s "the generator never acts on the zeta's own explicit formula", here a rung-1 theorem — and the only expression of (Q) on the host is its conclusion. The zero-dimensional half IS expressed:* Σ_{|n| = N} deg(Γ_0 ∩ Γ_n) = log q · N_N = log q · (Γ_{Fr^N} · Δ) *for every N ≥ 1 (Theorem 3.4; checked on the g = 1 model y² = x³ + x + 1 over F₇ at N = 1, 2, 3, 4 against 5, 55, 380, 2475), and its generating series is* log q · t·Z′(S, t)/Z(S, t). *The passage from S ×_k S to the host is the comparison map (7.3.2) read at the page: the ghost component n maps onto the graph of Fr_q^{|n|}, |n| = Σ_𝔪 n_𝔪 deg 𝔪, and the index 0 onto the diagonal; it is not injective on components (two closed points of the same degree give distinct components with one image, Borger p. 25), so the host carries a finer index set than the surface — effective divisors, not integers — and it is exactly the single-point divisors that carry the nonzero pairings.* So: 1 of 2 halves expressed. ## §1 The definitions, fixed from the printed pages (each with its 10(n) line)

**1.1 The curve and its points.** k = F_q; S a smooth, geometrically connected curve over k, complete unless said otherwise; E the set of closed points of S; for 𝔪 ∈ E, deg 𝔪 := [O_S/𝔪 : k] and q_𝔪 := #(O_S/𝔪) = q^{deg 𝔪}; a_d := #{𝔪 ∈ E : deg 𝔪 = d}; N_N := #S(F_{q^N}) = Σ_{d | N} d·a_d. N^{(E)} := ⊕_E N (Borger 0801.1691 p. 2), the monoid of effective divisors on S; for n ∈ N^{(E)}, |n| := deg(n) := Σ_𝔪 n_𝔪 deg 𝔪 (this is Borger's deg(n), 0906.3146 p. 25, §7.3); e_𝔪 the basis vector; supp(n) := {𝔪 : n_𝔪 > 0}. For an affine open U = Spec R ⊆ S, R is a Dedekind domain and each 𝔪 ∈ E ∩ U is a maximal ideal of R with finite residue field: it is *supramaximal* in Borger's sense (0801.1691 §1.1 p. 6: "(a) R/𝔪 is a finite field, R_𝔪 is a discrete valuation ring, and 𝔪 is finitely presented as an R-module" — true here because S is smooth of dimension 1, so O_{S,𝔪} is a regular local ring of dimension 1, and R is noetherian; p. 6: "By far the most important example is a maximal ideal with finite residue field in a Dedekind domain"), and distinct maximal ideals are pairwise coprime as §1.2 p. 7 requires. *10(n):* the objects are Borger's; nothing new.

**1.2 The Λ_S-structure and the absolute point of F₁^S.** Read at the page: 0906.3146 §7.1 p. 24 — "Let S be a scheme of finite type over Z, and let E be a set of regular closed points of codimension 1. For each point s ∈ E, let q_s denote the cardinality of s. Let X be a flat algebraic space over S. We can then define a Λ_{S,E}-action on X just as we define Λ-actions, but now we use commuting endomorphisms ψ_s: X → X, one for each point s ∈ E, such that ψ_s agrees with the q_s-power Frobenius operator on the fiber X_s"; for E-flat rings, 0801.1691 §1.6 p. 8 (a Ψ_{R,E}-action such that id ⊗ ψ_α on R/𝔪_α ⊗_R A is x ↦ x^{q_α}) and §1.17 p. 12 ("a Λ_{R,E}-ring structure on an E-flat R-algebra A is the same as a commuting family of Frobenius lifts ψ_α"). **The identity family is a Λ_S-structure on S itself:** the fiber of S over s ∈ E is Spec(O_S/𝔪_s) = Spec F_{q_s}, on which the q_s-power map is the identity. Under it S is the initial Λ_S-space (an S-space with a Λ_S-action receives a unique Λ_S-map from S... more simply: O_S with ψ = id is a Λ_{R,E}-ring on every affine open, and by 0801.1691 §1.17 the structure is the coalgebra structure R → W_{R,E}(R) given by the constant vectors, r ↦ ⟨r, r, r, …⟩ — see 1.3). *10(n):* 0906.3146 §2.1 p. 8 prints this for Z ("The ring Z has a unique Λ-structure — each ψ_p is the identity. **1.3 The E-typical Witt vectors of S over itself, W_S(R) := W_{R,E}(R).** Read at the page, 0801.1691 p. 2: "Let N^{(E)} denote the commutative monoid ⊕_E N … Given an action of N^{(E)} on an R-algebra B, let ψ_𝔪 denote the endomorphism of B given by the 𝔪-th element of the standard basis of N^{(E)}. Let us say that such an action is a Λ_{R,E}-structure if for each 𝔪 ∈ E, the map ψ_𝔪 reduces to the Frobenius endomorphism x ↦ x^{[R:𝔪]} on B/𝔪B. Now, for any R-algebra A, the monoid N^{(E)} acts on A^{N^{(E)}} through its translation action on itself in the exponent. When A is E-flat, we define W^fl_{R,E}(A) to be the largest of the sub-R-algebras B ⊆ A^{N^{(E)}} having the properties that B is stable under the action of N^{(E)} and that the induced action on B is a Λ_{R,E}-structure." §1.8 p. 9 constructs it: U₀(A) = A^{N^{(E)}}, U_{i+1}(A) = {b ∈ U_i(A) | ψ_α(b) − b^{q_α} ∈ 𝔪_α U_i(A) for all α ∈ E}, W^fl_{R,E}(A) = ∩_i U_i(A) (1.8.1). §1.12 p. 11 defines W_{R,E} on all R-algebras by (1.12.1), and "Its restriction to Ring^fl_R agrees with W^fl_{R,E}"; §1.14 p. 12: "When A is E-flat, it is harmless to identify [the ghost map] w with the inclusion map." **1.5 The ghost components Γ_n.** For n ∈ N^{(E)}, Γ_n: S → W_S*(S) is the map dual to the n-th ghost projection w_n: W_S(R) → R on each affine piece (0906.3146 p. 25, §7.3: the composition ∐_{N^{(E)}} T → W_S(T) → S ×_k T "with the ghost map γ"). w_n is surjective (the constant vectors ⟨r, r, r, …⟩ lie in W_S(R): Theorem 3.1, or directly from p. 2 since constants are shift-invariant and r^{q_𝔪} ≡ r mod 𝔪), so Γ_n is a closed immersion at every finite level, an isomorphism of S onto an irreducible component of the host (1006.0092 p. 5: "W₁*(X) is the coequalizer … of the two maps X₀ ⇉ X ∐ X"; "the space W_n*(X) can be constructed by gluing n+1 copies of X together … along their fibers modulo p, …, pⁿ"; Thm. 17.3 p. 39 in the single-ideal case). Γ₀ is the identity component ("Γ₁" in IV.10's rider, whose indices are positive integers; Borger's index set starts at 0, 0801.1691 p. 1 "the shift in indexing is preferable"). **Definition.** For m ≠ n, Γ_m ∩ Γ_n := S ×_{W_S*(S)} S (the two maps Γ_m, Γ_n), a closed subscheme of S; on an affine piece it is Spec(R/I_{m,n}) with I_{m,n} := {a_m − a_n : a ∈ W_S(R)} — an ideal (the image of a group homomorphism; the identification R ⊗_{W} R = R/I_{m,n} holds because both maps are surjective: R ⊗_W R = W/(ker w_m + ker w_n) = R/w_m(ker w_n), and w_m(ker w_n) = {a_m − a_n} by the constant-vector shift a ↦ a − a_n·1). *10(n):* the components are printed (Borger, above); the intersection ideal I_{m,n} follows IV.10's rider R-b′ for Z (line 462), transported.

**1.6 The comparison map (7.3.2) with T = S, and what it does to each component.** Read at the page, 0906.3146 p. 25 §7.3: "let T be an affine space over S, the unit of the adjunction f_! ⊣ f* at v_!(T) is (7.3.1) v_!(T) → f*f_!v_!(T) = f*s_!(T) which, by definition, is a map (7.3.2) W_S(T) → S ×_k T … The composition ∐_{N^{(E)}} T → W_S(T) → S ×_k T with the ghost map γ is the map that, on the component n ∈ N^{(E)}, is simply (pr, Fr^n_T): T → S ×_k T where pr denotes the structure map T → S, and Fr^n_T is the Frobenius map defined on functions by x ↦ x^{q^{deg(n)}}, where deg(n) denotes Σ_𝔪 n_𝔪[O_S/𝔪 : k], the degree of the effective divisor corresponding to n. In particular, if T = S, then the image of this map is the union in S ×_k S of the graphs of all powers of the Frobenius map on S. These components are not disjoint. For instance, let x and y be two distinct closed points of S with the same residue field. Then the components of W_S(S) of indices x and y are distinct but have the same image in S ×_k S. So (7.3.1) is not a monomorphism." **Consequence (the orchestrator's reading, confirmed at the page).** With T = S (pr = id_S), c := (7.3.2) sends the component Γ_n onto the graph Γ_{Fr^{|n|}} = {(s, Fr_q^{|n|}(s))} ⊂ S ×_k S, where Fr_q is the q-power Frobenius of S over k, and |n| = deg(n) as in 1.1; in particular Γ₀ ↦ Δ. The orchestrator's phrasing "Π_𝔪 Fr_𝔪^{n_𝔪} = Fr_q^{Σ n_𝔪 deg 𝔪}" is the same map: Fr_𝔪 = Fr_q^{deg 𝔪} on functions (x ↦ x^{q_𝔪}, §7.2 p. 25). **Correction to the intuition, printed:** c is not injective on components — the host has one component per effective divisor n, the surface has one graph per integer N = |n|; c collapses every n of degree N onto Γ_{Fr^N}. Also printed (p. 25, §7.3 heading): "f is not an isomorphism of toposes"; Prop. 7.4 p. 26: the image of (7.3.2) is Zariski dense, by the ring map (7.4.1) R ⊗_k B → B × B × ⋯, a ⊗ b ↦ ⟨ab, ab^r, ab^{r²}, …⟩ (r the residue cardinality of a chosen maximal ideal). *10(n):* printed, with the one-line identification of the image of Γ_n as the writer's.

**1.7 The arithmetic degree.** For a finite k-scheme Z (a zero-dimensional scheme of finite type over k), deg Z := log #Γ(Z, O_Z) = log q · dim_k Γ(Z, O_Z) — the convention of IV.10's rider R-b′ (Durov arXiv:0704.2030 p. 63, Smirnov via Le Bruyn arXiv:1304.6532 p. 2, both as the rider records them; not re-opened here, `[record]`). On rung 1 it is log q times the ordinary degree [Z : k]; Weil's intersection number (Γ_{Fr^N} · Δ) = N_N is the ordinary degree of a transversal zero-dimensional intersection, so the host's numbers carry an extra factor log q relative to Weil's — the factor by which log p entered over Z. *10(n):* the convention is the record's; the rung-1 comparison "arithmetic degree = log q × k-degree" is a definition, not a theorem.
