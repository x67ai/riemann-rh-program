### P1 — Borger's Λ-ring geometry: Spec Z ×_{F₁} Spec Z as the Witt space W*(Spec Z)

**What the square IS, at the page.** Borger defines F₁-algebras as Λ-rings and F₁-spaces as spaces with an action of the big-Witt-vector monad W* (0906.3146 p. 6, §1.1: "a Λ-structure on a space X is by definition an action of W* on X"; "if X is an algebraic space, then the space W*(X) is ind-algebraic but typically not algebraic"). The base-change functor v*: Sp_{F₁} → Sp_Z strips the Λ-structure; its left adjoint v_! "sends X to its Witt space W*(X) with the natural Λ-structure" (p. 7, §1.2), and the paper then prints the definition the pool asks for: **"if one accepts the premise of this paper, the space Spec Z ×_{Spec F₁} Spec Z must be defined to be the Witt space W*(Spec Z)"** (p. 7). For an affine scheme "W*(Spec A) = colim_n Spec W_n(A)" (p. 6). Two further printed facts fix the object's shape: (i) "The ring Z has a unique Λ-structure — each ψ_p is the identity. Under this structure, it is the initial object in the category of Λ-rings. It is therefore reasonable to denote it F₁ and call Spec Z, viewed as a Λ-space, the absolute point" (p. 8, §2.1); (ii) "since the current version of our theory says nothing about the archimedean place of Q, it is hard to imagine this succeeding without further ideas. Even so, it should be done" (p. 5, on translating Weil's proof). The Λ-structure on a flat Z-scheme is "a commuting family of endomorphisms ψ_p: X → X, indexed by the set of prime numbers p, such that each ψ_p agrees with the p-th power Frobenius map on the special fiber" (p. 2); Borger–de Smit state the axioms as "(1) ψ_p is a Frobenius lift at p for each p ∈ P. (2) ψ_p∘ψ_q = ψ_q∘ψ_p for all p, q ∈ P" (1809.02295 p. 2), the composite ψ_{pq} being ψ_p∘ψ_q by definition of the monoid action of Id_P.

**(a) III.20's four items.**
- Item 1 (the doubled object): EXHIBITED (0906.3146 p. 7) — W*(Spec Z) = colim_n Spec W_n(Z), an ind-affine Z-space with Λ-structure, the fiber product being taken in the topos Sp_{F₁} of Λ-spaces over the absolute point. Why the fiber product over Spec Z is not it: over Spec Z the product is Spec Z itself (Z is initial); the second dimension comes from the Witt coordinates. But the *curve* of which this is the square is the absolute point (p. 8): in Borger's dictionary "Spec Z viewed as a Λ-space" — Spec Z with ψ_p = id — is Spec F₁, so the object whose self-product W*(Spec Z) is has no points but the one, and no self-maps but the identity (a Λ-morphism Spec Z → Spec Z over F₁ is a ring map Z → Z, of which there is one).
