## 2. What the paper defines, page by page

- **p. 1:** Frames "the problem of the absolute point": a "more mythical object P … with a natural morphism π_X : X → P … so that global invariants of X have an interpretations in terms of a version of direct image". Spec(Z) "has dimension 1 with respect to the Zariski topology and at least 3 with respect to the etale topology". The program is called "Arakelov geometry modulo n". It introduces F_{1ⁿ}: "we can think if a scheme X contains n-th roots of unity, then it is defined over F_{1ⁿ}, so that we have a morphism p_X : X → Spec(F_{1ⁿ}). (1)". It also says "F_{1ⁿ} does not exist in a rigorous sense".
- **p. 2:** The aim is "to make sense of the cohomological invariants obtained via the direct image of the morphism (1)", chiefly "det(Rp_{X*}(F)) for a coherent sheaf F on X". "vector spaces over F_{1ⁿ} are sets with a free action of the group μ_n". The Riemann–Roch problem "involves … residues modulo n of the numbers of integer points in some polyhedra". The "direct images of Chern classes (i.e., Deligne's torsors ⟨L, M⟩) involve n-power residue symbols, and well-definedness of our determinantal formalism implies a very natural proof of the reciprocity law". Section plan: §1 absolute fields; §2 homological algebra; §3 "the cohomological invariants of arithmetic curves … 'resultant torsors' ⟨L, M⟩ … The existence of a good theory of such torsors is equivalent to the reciprocity law of class field theory"; §4 "'modulo n compactifications' of arithmetic curves"; §5 "cohomological domain".
- **p. 3:** §1.1: a vector space over F₁ is a set, GL_n(F₁) = S_n, and "det … for F = F₁ is the sign homomorphism". §1.2 polynomial rings over F₁: GL_d(F₁[t]) is "the full braid group B_d", and the pure braid group is the analog of the congruence subgroup. Also Drinfeld's covering and elliptic F_q[t]-modules. (1.3) opens "Algebraic extensions of F₁".
- **p. 4:** "for any n we have one such extension of degree n. Denote it F_{1ⁿ} … say that F_{1ⁿ} is the monoid {0} ∪ μ_n". "We regard F₁ as the absolute point of the category of schemes, so that every scheme is defined over F₁. We say that a scheme X is defined over F_{1ⁿ}, if the ring of regular functions on X contains nth roots of 1", in accord with Iwasawa theory "where adding roots of unity to a number field replaces the extension of base field for a curve". ## 5. What else the paper bears on

**The free slot β.** The paper's analogy places F_{1ⁿ} in the role that a constant-field extension plays for a curve. It cites "adding roots of unity to a number field replaces the extension of base field for a curve" (p. 4) and "adjoining roots of unity is analogous to the extension of the base field" (Weil, Iwasawa, p. 2). So in the paper's own dictionary, the F_{1ⁿ} tower corresponds to the rung-1 constant field k (and its extensions F_{qⁿ}), not to a base *below* F₁.

It supplies none of A6's structure: no factorization, no f* or f_!, no β-spaces, no product over F_{1ⁿ}. Its "vector spaces" are pointed free μ_n-sets (p. 4), and the authors say F_{1ⁿ} "does not exist in a rigorous sense" (p. 1). As a candidate for A6's β, the paper offers a named symbol and a linear algebra over it (pp. 4–9), not a base category.

**Lorscheid's description** ("calculate cohomological invariants of arithmetic curves in terms of cohomology over F_{1ⁿ}", 1801.05337 p. 3, as SPEC §3 quotes it) is **confirmed** at the page. p. 2: "In Section 3 we define the cohomological invariants of arithmetic curves"; p. 13: "H⁰(X̄, L) = M_L ∩ B_L. This is a vector space over F = F_{1ⁿ}".

One refinement, from the copy: the "cohomology" printed is H⁰ plus an acyclicity condition (pp. 13–14). There is no H¹ and no higher cohomology in the copy.

**Other connections, at the page only:**
- The paper's pairing is a reciprocity (Hilbert-symbol-type) torsor, and its authors model it on linking numbers in the "3-fold" Spec Z (pp. 9–10). It bears on the SPEC's A12 (cohomology of Y and its coefficients) only negatively: its coefficients are μ_n and Z/n (pp. 11–12, 14), and it lives on X, not on a Y.
- Nothing in the copy touches ζ, its zeros, positivity, or the III.20 items 2–3.
